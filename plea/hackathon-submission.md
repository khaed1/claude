## PLEA + imd/acc

**One line:** a meme token you can always buy but can only sell by convincing a panel of 30 IMD oracle judges, from a wallet or by sending the plea as a plain transaction. The best pleas win IMD and PLEA prizes each season, and every trade pays 0.5% back to the trader as staked IMD.

### The problem
Meme tokens are a race to dump. Holders sell at the first green candle, nothing rewards them for staying, and the trading around a token does nothing for IMD holders.

### What we built
**PLEA** is a sell-gated meme token inspired by TokenWorks' CabalCoin (2025), rebuilt on IMD.
- **Buying is open.** Anyone buys PLEA in the PLEA/IMD Uniswap v4 pool. The hook (a fork of POOL4's CappedBurnHook) is the only liquidity provider: it keeps an inventory cap, burns trims and rebuilds an IMD buy wall below the price.
- **Selling needs a plea.** A holder writes up to 280 bytes explaining why they should be allowed to sell. The contract computes a fact score (0–45 {run 7; 0–55 before}: share sold, buy-weighted hold time, profit or loss, 24h price; {mainnet} profit costs points slowly, at a loss 13 down to 4 above 1,000%, and PLEA received from the airdrop or a prize counts as held since launch at the middle of that table), and the Cabal, a panel of 30 IMD oracle judges, scores the plea 0–45. The sell passes when the judges' score reaches the seller's need (71 − fact score {run 7}; serious pleas score about 33, so a fresh buyer selling up to 20% {mainnet; 15% on run 7} at a loss needs 37 and a week-old holder 30; on real mainnet panels a strong letter scored 37 and ordinary serious pleas 28–35, while a large same-day sale at a profit cannot pass) with 17 of 30 agreeing. An approval opens a 7-minute sell window; a denial starts a 4-hour wait with one appeal. A plea must be between 1% and 50% of the balance (or 2.5M PLEA), so a plea for a few wei can't farm season points, and a plea that can't pass is refused before it is paid for.
- **Pleading by letter.** Anyone can plead straight from a wallet or Etherscan by sending a transaction whose input data is the plea text to the ownerless **Letterbox**, or by calling `letter(text)` on the Write Contract tab. `APPEAL <text>`, `SELL` and `NAME <name>` are commands. A letter pleads 20% of the writer's PLEA {mainnet; 15% on run 7, 20% before}, held in escrow; the fees are paid in PLEA in the same transaction (the gate sells just enough PLEA for the 0.5 IMD oracle fee and a 0.25 IMD runner tip, at most the hourly price snapshot +10%). An approved letter **sells itself**: anyone calls `deliver` (at least 95% of the snapshot value), the whole sale goes to the writer, the points are recorded and the caller earns the tip; every other ending returns the escrow and the tip. (The site's "sell automatically" box works the same and pays a 0.35 IMD auto-sell fee into the season's prize pot from a delivered sale; letters pay none {run 7}.) An `APPEAL` letter keeps the original plea's share of the balance, so the fee sale cannot push it into a worse share band. `status(address)` and `leaderboard()` answer in plain sentences on Etherscan. No Letterbox function ID can be the start of valid text (a test checks the ABI), so a letter can never call a function by accident.
- **Seasons.** 15-day seasons (5 hours on Sepolia) run back to back while the Cabal lives. Every approved plea earns its score as points (each wallet's best three count). At the end of a season the top 25 wallets with at least 60 points split the prize pot in IMD, shares falling in a straight line from 4.5% (first) to 3.5% (25th) {run 5; runs 2–4: top 10, 25/18/14/11/9/7/6/4/3/3 %}, plus a **PLEA bonus**: 10,000,000 PLEA per season for the first six seasons {run 5; runs 2–4: 5,000,000}, held by the Seasons contract from launch, the unpaid rest burned. The owner of PLEA can't play: no points, prizes or bonus, and ownership moves in two steps so nobody can be made the owner and stripped of a prize. {run 7} **Seat multiplier:** a seller who holds an IMD seat NFT (the identity.md NFT) when a plea is recorded earns 1.2× its score as points: IMD's own agents and their operators get a head start on the leaderboard.
- **Objection!** {run 5} After an approval, other holders have 20 minutes to object (0.5 IMD oracle fee + 0.35 IMD stake). A second panel of 30 re-scores the plea reading the gate's own question, the real original score and the objection as untrusted text. The plea's season points can only fall: the recorded score becomes the lowest any panel gave, and a re-score more than 2 below the need (the panels' own noise) leaves no points at all (the stake comes back); otherwise the stake grows the season's prize pot. Two rulings per plea at most, from different wallets. The sale is never touched, and rulings never reset the dead-man. Objections are IMD oracle demand too: every one is a paid 30-seat panel.
- **Cashback to the right wallet.** 0.5% of every trade goes to the trader as staked IMD through imd/acc, following imd/acc's trader rule: the wallet named in the swap's hookData, else the wallet the router acts for (read from the router's `msgSender()`), else a plain-wallet sender; never a router, the Stacker or the vault. A trade with no resolvable trader goes through without cashback. Nothing waits on a keeper: `rebalance()` pays no tip.
- **The Wall** shows every plea, its verdict stamp, score and points.
- **Dead-man.** If the Cabal gives no verdict for 33 hours it dies for good, and PLEA trades freely forever. Nobody has to trigger it: "alive" is computed on chain. The last season ends at the death, and what is left in the prize pot goes to the buy wall.

**imd/acc** is trading cashback paid in staked IMD, for any IMD project. A shared, ownerless `Stacker.credit(trader, amount)` deposits IMD into the sIMD vault in the trader's name. Projects fund it from fees they already charge, so traders pay nothing extra. PLEA is its first integration. The mainnet Stacker is live (launch #1409).

### How it uses IMD
1. **The IMD oracle is the game.** Every plea is a paid IMD oracle request through Intake v2 (0.5 IMD), with a numeric answer. The signed score comes back through the Intake callback and is checked on chain (EIP-712 signature, panel size, quorum, score range); a refused or split panel is cleared at once through Intake v2's failure callback. The signer and Intake are immutable. Every sell attempt and every shot at the leaderboard is IMD demand.
   - **The Cabal's rules, version 2**, were calibrated with 13 live 30-seat panels on Sepolia: an honest "weigh my honesty, not my timing" went from a signed 0 (version 1 read it as manipulation) to 33; a threat is a unanimous 0; jokes, poems and other languages score on craft; a shill scores 20; content rules for hate, harassment and doxxing, while the writer's own hardship is allowed. In run 4, 6 of 6 fresh pleas got verdicts within 3–6 minutes (scores 32–35, one 0 for a text that posed as the owner), while both appeals that named their previous score split the panel (7 of 28 and 4 of 29 agreed) and got no verdict. **Version 3** (fix round) tells the judges the real previous score and need in the appeal question, and says that naming the earlier verdict is not manipulation.
2. **IMD is the pool's pair and the prize.** PLEA trades only against IMD, the hook keeps an IMD buy wall, and season prizes are paid in IMD.
3. **Cashback in sIMD.** 0.5% of every PLEA trade is deposited into the IMD staking vault for the trader through imd/acc, in the same swap.
4. **Launched through IMD from our own repository, four times.** PLEA v10 and test runs 2, 3 and 4 were written in a public Foundry repo and launched with `launch.open` + `repoUrl`/`baseCommit`: IMD's swarm audited the imported code, adapted and tested it, ran four specialist audits and a judge, then deployed it. In run 2 the audit found a real high-severity bug in our wall guard and fixed it before deploying. In run 3 the judge left three low findings, which we fixed for run 4. In run 4 the import audit found two lows in the new code and IMD's own adapt step fixed them (one better than our fix); the judge left two lows, both in the fix round. imd/acc and PLEA v2 were IMD swarm launches too (#1129, #1294, #1148, #1409).

**Fees per trade (2.25% on mainnet, all paid in the same swap; 2.0% on runs 7–8.5, 1.75% on runs 2–6):** 0.5% sIMD cashback to the trader · 1% owner (0.75% on runs 7–8.5, 0.5% on runs 2–6) · 0.25% season prize pot · 0.25% pool liquidity · 0.25% of the PLEA burned.

### Known risks and admin powers
- **Selling may be refused.** That's the game; the dead-man guarantees an exit if the Cabal goes quiet for 33 hours.
- **Judges are AI agents.** Scores are subjective. A split panel gives no verdict: the plea is cleared at once by the Intake's failure callback (a letter's escrow and tip come back; a hand plea's fee is spent) and the wallet can try again. Appeals that name their previous score split the panel in run 4; rules v3 addresses it.
- **Points must be recorded.** The oracle callback only stores the score (it has 200k gas on mainnet), so points are recorded by a separate call that anyone can make (`record`, or `closeSeason` with a list of pleas); a letter's `deliver` records them in the same transaction.
- **Owner powers:** an add-only transfer allowlist (empty today), a one-time Merkle root for the 12% distributor (the swarm's 10% and a 2% airdrop), and opening trading early with `seed()` (anyone can once START passes). The owner can't change the oracle signer, the Intake, fees, timings or prizes, and can't win anything; the fix round also excludes the launch owner (the hook's fee beneficiary) after a two-step handover, while a merely nominated owner keeps their prize.
- **The 10% swarm share** is minted to our own Merkle distributor: IMD builds no reward tree for a contracts-only launch, so the list of seats and accepted work comes from IMD's records and the owner sets the root once.
- **Testnet only:** seasons last 5 hours on Sepolia so a full cycle can be shown (15 days on mainnet; the contracts refuse shorter timings on chain id 1). TestSIMD's owner can pause it to test the cashback fallback. The test owner wallet is testnet-only; mainnet will use a fresh wallet.
- IMD's audit reports are in the launch repositories; agent reviews are not a full security audit.
