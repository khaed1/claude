What it is: a sell-gated meme token on Uniswap v4. Anyone can buy. Selling needs a plea: an oracle panel (IMD, 30 judges, quorum 17) scores the plea 0-45, and it passes when the score reaches a need of 69 minus an on-chain fact score (0-41). An approved plea is one sale, within 7 minutes, through the gate. 33 hours without a verdict kills the gate for good and PLEA then trades freely. Nine contracts in src/, 3,332 nSLOC.

Spec and the agreed invariants: https://github.com/khaed1/plea/blob/6ffcf6639bc15cda64fc58d358b00a57b92a7375/audit/THREAT-MODEL.md (numbers in bold are design decisions, not findings). What it does: The gate runs the plea lifecycle: submitSell and appeal (and the Letterbox's submitFor and appealFor), the fact score (share of the balance at most 20%: 16, at most 35%: 9, more: 3; held 7/3/1 days: 3/2/1; at a loss 13, then a profit table 12/10/8/6/4; the 24h trend from the hook's hourly checkpoints 9/5/0; a PLEA-weighted mean for PLEA no buy record covers) and the need = 69 - fact (CannotPass above 45), the 0.5 IMD oracle fee paid to IMD's Intake, the oracle callbacks with an EIP-712 / ERC-1271 attestation check, the 7-minute approval window, cooldowns, a 20-minute expiry, the 33-hour dead-man, the approved sale through the hook (with an exact-output fee swap for letters), and a UTF-8 text validator that decodes code points and refuses control, format, separator, variation-selector and default-ignorable characters and marker prefixes. Trusted: the oracle signer, and the Intake's writer, which pairs an answer with its request (accepted risk L85-I-1 in FINDINGS).

Please look hardest at: Anything that lets a plea pass with a lower need, sell more or more often than approved, revive the Cabal or kill it early, replay or re-address an answer, block other sellers, or get the fee back; the new pass mark and holding points (every bound and rounding that could have assumed the old ones); the attestation verification; the validator's UTF-8 decoding and its refused ranges; the fact score's edges and rounding.
