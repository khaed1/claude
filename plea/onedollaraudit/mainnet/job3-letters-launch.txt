What it does: Letters: a holder writes a letter (plain text, e.g. from Etherscan's message page). The Letterbox escrows 20% of their PLEA (within the gate's 2.5M cap), buys the 0.5 IMD oracle fee and the 0.25 IMD runner tip by selling a slice of the writer's PLEA (at most the hourly snapshot + 10%, at most 10% of the balance), files the plea through the gate, and on approval anyone delivers the sale (a 95% floor after the hook's 2.25% fees) for the tip. settle returns the escrow and the tip when no sale can come. Letters can appeal (same share as the original, never below the gate's minimum). The site's auto-sell box uses the same escrow and adds a 0.35 IMD fee to the season pot on a delivered sale. It also keeps names and builds the status and leaderboard text.

The token and the launch: PLEA is an ERC-20 (Ownable2Step) whose transfers are restricted while the Cabal lives: only the gate's pull, the court escrow and exempt senders move tokens. IMD's factory deploys PLEA, CabalGate, Seasons, PleaDistributor and PleaLaunch in one transaction (launch.json). PleaLaunch then deploys the hook (CREATE2 at a flag-bearing address, salt mined off chain), the Letterbox and the Tribunal against keccak256 code hashes committed at launch, mints the fixed split (82% hook, 6% Seasons, 12% distributor) and seeds trading (the owner any time, anyone from START; on chain 1 seed checks 15-day seasons, the 33-hour dead-man and the 14,000 IMD market cap). PleaDistributor is a Merkle distributor whose root is set once.

Please look hardest at: Letters: escrow accounting (every PLEA and IMD it holds must belong to an open letter); the fee path's bounds with IMD as both pool currency and oracle fee; races between delivery, settle and expiry; the appeal's same-share clamp; reentrancy through the hook and the gate; anyone moving or locking someone else's escrow; text building. Token and launch: ways around the transfer restriction (approvals, transferFrom, the PoolManager, ERC-6909 claims, exempt senders); the one-shot init and the code-hash commitments; front-running a deploy or the seed; the mint split; the distributor's root and claims; launch.json's arguments against the constructors.

Please rate each finding by severity and give a concrete path: who calls what, in which state, and what they gain or what breaks. Gas notes are welcome but secondary.
