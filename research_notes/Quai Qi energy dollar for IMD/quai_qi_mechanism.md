# Quai Network's Qi "energy dollar": exact mechanism and observed behaviour (state as of 13 Oct 2026)

Scope note: Quai's own materials (docs.qu.ai, qu.ai blog, 2024 Primer PDF, go-quai source) are the primary sources for the mechanism. Independent material on Qi is thin; the only non-Quai outlets found that discuss Qi at any length (Disruption Banking, Messari, TheStreet) are largely descriptive or promotional. Qi has no listing on CoinGecko/CoinMarketCap; the only market price found is a thinly traded DEX pool. Dates of individual claims are marked throughout. go-quai source quotes are from the `main` branch as fetched 13 Oct 2026 via raw.githubusercontent.com (`params/protocol_params.go`, `consensus/misc/rewards.go`, `core/types/utxo.go`).

Terminology used by the code: Qi's smallest unit is a "Qit" (= 0.001 Qi); Quai's smallest unit is an "It" (10^18 Its = 1 Quai, Ether-style). Blocks are 5-second "zone" blocks unless "prime block" is stated.

## Key Question 1: What exactly is Qi pegged to (hash/difficulty, energy, or cost of production)? Formulas for Qi and Quai block rewards.

### Takeaway
Qi is not pegged to anything in a collateral or redemption sense; Quai's Primer says so explicitly. Mechanically, the per-block Qi emission is a linear function of block difficulty (expected hashes per block) divided by a scheduled constant 1/k_Qi, so one Qi always costs a roughly fixed number of hashes to mine (about 8×10^12 hashes per Qi; marketed as "100 MH/s for a day = 1 Qi"), with a built-in 2.69-year "doubling" decay of k_Qi meant to offset hardware efficiency gains so the hash anchor approximates an energy anchor. Quai's emission is logarithmic in difficulty and its scale factor k_Quai is actively steered by a controller.

### Cited Findings
- Primer (2024, p.21-22): "The Qi reward function is linearly proportional to difficulty. The Quai reward function is proportional to the binary logarithm of difficulty." and "There is no fixed peg, backing nor collateralization, between or underlying either the Quai or Qi coins." — [Quai Primer PDF, Oct 2024](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)
- Primer formulas verbatim: "BlockReward QUAI = kQUAI * log₂ (Difficulty)" ; "BlockReward QI = kQI * Difficulty" ; "Difficulty is the expected number of hashes needed to mine a block at the current difficulty target value." — [Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)
- Primer on why this is "hash-pegged": "Quai rewards are issued in proportion to the 'bits' of difficulty, approximately represented by the number of leading zeros in the target value... As such, the percentage growth of Quai supply trends asymptotically toward zero overtime. Qi rewards are issued in direct proportion to 'hashes' of difficulty, tied to the hashrate, or more specifically to the expected number of hashes needed to mine a block at the current difficulty target. Supply is inflationary at a higher or lower rate depending on market demand." — [Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)
- Primer on k_Qi: "kQI is the variable that converts the scale of the difficulty measure into an appropriate magnitude for the actual number of Qi tokens offered in the block reward. It also has a decaying component to account for the projected growth in processing power over time, so that the Qi block rewards more closely reflect the energy cost of mining, rather than the increasing efficiency of computing power." — [Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)
- Primer on the stated goal: "Qi is meant to have stable purchasing power, connected to the cost of energy, and act as a non-fiat dollar alternative that is used as decentralized crypto cash." — [Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)
- Docs (Qi emissions page): "Block Reward_{Qi} ∝ (Difficulty)"; proportionality constant "currently set to 8.0 × 10^9"; "Supply_{Qi} = ∑(Emissions_{Qi}) ± ∑(Conversions_{Qi})"; Qi has "an accommodative supply that expands with demand". — [docs.qu.ai Qi Emissions](https://docs.qu.ai/learn/tokenomics/qi-emissions)

- Docs token-dynamics page wording: Qi value is "loosely tied to the cost of generating a hash and thus the cost of energy" and difficulty is "a reliable measure of energy or electricity pricing"; "Doubling difficulty doubles Qi rewards". — [docs.qu.ai Token Dynamics](https://docs.qu.ai/learn/tokenomics/token-dynamics/token-dynamics)
- Team spokesman "Dr. K" (Karl Kreder), quoted Dec 2025: "The value of Qi is always related to the cost of production." — [Disruption Banking, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.disruptionbanking.com/2025/12/22/bitcoin-news-how-quai-networks-christmas-fork-brings-renewed-confidence-to-bitcoin-miners/)

### Inferences
- The peg target is, precisely, hashes: 8×10^9 hashes per Qit (0.001 Qi) ⇒ 8×10^12 hashes per Qi at activation; 100 MH/s × 86,400 s = 8.64×10^12 hashes, which matches the "100 MH/s per day ≈ 1 Qi" shorthand. "Energy" and "cost of production" enter only via the assumption that hashes cost roughly constant energy once the 2.69-year efficiency decay is applied. Nothing in the protocol measures energy prices or hardware costs.
- Because 1/k_Qi grows (Qi per hash falls) on a fixed calendar, Qi per Qi of mining becomes scarcer over time by design, independent of actual hardware trends; if real efficiency gains differ from the 2.69-year doubling assumption, the hash anchor and the intended energy anchor diverge.
- The post-KawPow fork changes (SHA-256/Scrypt share normalisation and the 3×10^11 difficulty floor subtracted from log2 D) mean "difficulty" in both formulas is now a synthetic KawPoW-equivalent figure, not a raw native difficulty.

### Gaps
- The docs block-rewards page still says "The exact calculus for these constants/variables will be shared publicly closer to Mainnet launch"; the code is the only full statement of the k_Qi schedule. No official post-launch document was found reconciling the 26,000,000 pre-activation value with the 8×10^9 live value.
- Found no primary source for how `ForkAwareKawPowEquivalentDifficulty` numerically maps SHA-256 and Scrypt share work to KawPoW hashes (only the code's adjustment formula comments), so the "hashes per Qi" statement is exact only for native KawPoW work.

## Key Question 2: How does Quai/Qi conversion work (the "controller")? Rate, slip/fee, lag, direction, miner-choice signal.

### Takeaway
Conversion is two-way, protocol-native burn-and-mint at the ratio of the current Quai and Qi block rewards (x Quai : y Qi). The ratio is steered by a controller that adjusts k_Quai (the "ExchangeRate" header field) each block with gain α = 1/1000 toward the point where miners are indifferent between taking Quai or Qi, using the last 4,000 blocks of miner reward choices as the signal. Conversions carry a minimum 20-30 bps slip, a cubic volume-based discount up to 100% (hard floor: the user must receive ≥10% of value), a directional "K-Quai discount", a 2-week lock on output, a 100-Quai minimum flow and a 2× cap on flow growth. The code's `main` branch now contains a `KQuaiFreezeForkBlock` (prime block 2,237,000, the Sept 2026 fork) that "freezes the controller at its parent value until a future fork explicitly resumes or replaces it".

### Cited Findings
- Primer: "a fully automated two-way conversion mechanism is built into the protocol. This enables all users to convert Quai to Qi or Qi to Quai at the current protocol exchange rate. When conversions take place the protocol burns the input token and mints the output token." and "if the current potential block reward amount is x Quai coins and y Qi coins, conversions can be done at an exchange rate of x:y." Footnote: "there is a cooldown lock period of ~ two weeks on the new coins received through the native protocol conversion mechanism." — [Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)
- Primer on the controller objective: "The objective of this controller, and its resultant effect on the kQUAI variable, is to push the system back toward an equilibrium, defined as when the market value of the block reward in Quai tokens is equal to the market value of the block reward in Qi tokens, and both are equal to the cost of mining a block (such that miners are economically indifferent as to whether they select/receive their reward in either Quai or Qi...)". Tactically: "when there is more Quai selected by miners as block rewards and minted via conversion, the market value of the block reward in Quai is greater than that in Qi, and as such kQUAI must be adjusted upward such that there will be fewer Quai coins per block (remember that kQUAI is part of a negative exponent within the Quai block reward function)." — [Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)
- Primer on miner choice as signal: "Miners elect to receive either Quai or Qi as a block reward. They can change their selection on each block. ### Inferences
- The "10% conversion fee" sometimes mentioned is not a fee: it is the floor on the discount (user receives at least 10% of input value). The baseline cost of a small conversion is the 20 bps cubic minimum plus the 30 bps minimum slip tolerance; large conversions relative to the 4,000-block mean flow are penalised cubically up to 100%.
- The "lag" has three components: 4,000-block (≈5.6 h) EMA/regression windows on difficulty and miner choice, α = 0.001 per-block gain on k_Quai (so a full adjustment takes on the order of thousands of blocks), and a 2-week lock on conversion output. This makes arbitrage against external markets slow and risky, which is presumably the intent.
- The repeated hard-coded k_Quai resets (50% cut, ×30, ×60, "reset to baseline", and now a freeze) indicate the controller did not behave as designed under the KawPow/merged-mining regime; the Sept 2026 code freezes it entirely. Since the Qi reward is still linear in difficulty, freezing k_Quai means the Quai:Qi conversion ratio now moves only with difficulty and fees, not with miner preference.

### Gaps
- The v0.56.0 release notes do not mention the controller freeze; only the code comment does. Could not confirm from an official communication whether the freeze is in force on mainnet or what a "future fork" will replace it with.
- The exact regression used to compute `x_b_star` (β0, β1 from the 4,000-block token-choice set) lives in code not fetched (likely `core/` or `consensus/` beyond rewards.go). The docs' formula images were not machine-readable.
- Did not find any dataset of on-chain conversion volumes or the historical k_Quai path.

## Key Question 3: Fixed parameters — block times, Qi denominations, supply caps, inflation, who mints/burns.

### Takeaway
Zone block time is 5 s (difficulty retargets on a rolling 720-block average with factor 40); Qi is a scriptless UTXO ledger with 15 fixed denominations from 0.001 Qi to 1,000,000 Qi (the 2024 Primer listed 17; the code has 15); Qi has no supply cap and zero genesis supply, inflating only via miner reward choice and net conversions; only block rewards (miner choice) and protocol conversion mint Qi, and only conversion burns it.

### Inferences
- Qi inflation has no formula-level ceiling; it is bounded only by hashrate (Qi/block ∝ difficulty), miner willingness to take Qi over Quai, and the scheduled 1/k_Qi decay. Net Qi→Quai conversions are the only deflationary channel.
- The denomination table shrank from 17 (Primer) to 15 (code): 0.25 Qi and 50 Qi were dropped. The Primer is therefore out of date on this point.

## Key Question 6: Team, funding, architecture (only as needed to understand Qi).

### Takeaway
Quai is built by Dominant Strategies (Austin, TX; founders Alan Orwick, Jonathan Downing, Karl Kreder "Dr. K", Yanni Georghiades, Sriram Vishwanath; UT Austin alumni), with ~$15M raised (Polychain-led $8M 2022, Alumni Ventures $2M 2022, $5M strategic round Aug 2024). The chain is a hierarchy of merged-mined PoW chains (Prime → Regions → Zones) using "Proof-of-Entropy-Minima" fork choice; Qi lives on a scriptless UTXO ledger parallel to the EVM Quai ledger, both minted by the same block rewards; since Dec 2025 block PoW is KawPoW with SHA-256/Scrypt merged-mining shares, which is why Qi's "difficulty" is now a KawPoW-equivalent synthetic.

### Cited Findings
- Founders: Dominant Strategies "was started by Alan Orwick, Jonathan Downing, Karl Kreder, Yanni Georghiades, and Sriram Vishwanath"; team are "alumni of the University of Texas at Austin, where they met and later co-authored the Quai Network whitepaper." — [Gate Learn, What is Quai Network](https://www.gate.com/learn/articles/what-is-quai-network/789)
