# Theory, history and critique of energy-backed / hash-pegged currencies (as of 13 Oct 2026)

Scope note: these notes cover the intellectual lineage of "energy money" and hash-cost anchoring, prior hash/difficulty-linked products, the empirical record of Bitcoin hashprice 2020–2026, the theoretical status of "production cost as anchor", critiques and defences of Quai's Qi, and lessons from other non-USD "stable" units. Quai's own marketing/doc claims are labelled **[Quai claim]** throughout and kept separate from independent analysis. All figures carry dates.

---

## KQ1. Origins: where the "hash/energy as price anchor" idea came from

### Takeaway
The lineage runs from Depression-era energy-money (Ford/Edison 1921, Technocracy Inc.'s energy certificates 1930s), through Hayek/Graham commodity-reserve money (1937–44), to Szabo's "unforgeable costliness" (2002) and Satoshi's Aug 2010 remark that price gravitates to production cost — with Satoshi himself adding the caveat that once issuance is small relative to supply, price dictates cost, not the reverse. None of the pre-crypto proposals were ever implemented; Satoshi's remark is a loose-equilibrium observation, not a stability claim.

### Cited Findings
- **Satoshi, 7 Aug 2010** (bitcointalk thread "Bitcoin minting is thermodynamically perverse", topic 721, msg 8114): "The price of any commodity tends to gravitate toward the production cost. If the price is below cost, then production slows down. If the price is above cost, profit can be made by generating and selling more. At the same time, the increased production would increase the difficulty, pushing the cost of generating towards the price. In later years, when new coin generation is a small percentage of the existing supply, market price will dictate the cost of production more than the other way around." — thread/post confirmed at [bitcointalk topic 721, msg 8114, 7 Aug 2010](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=721.msg8114#msg8114); wording as quoted by [Capriole, "Bitcoin's Production Cost", 13 Dec 2019](https://capriole.com/bitcoins-production-cost/). In the same 7 Aug 2010 post Satoshi wrote "The marginal cost of gold mining tends to stay near the price of gold" and that the utility of exchange would exceed the electricity cost ("not having Bitcoin is a net waste") — [bitcointalk](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=721.msg8114#msg8114); [Decrypt, Apr 2021](https://decrypt.co/62338/satoshi-nakamotos-view-on-bitcoins-energy-consumption-resurfaces).
- **Henry Ford / Thomas Edison, Dec 1921**: Ford proposed financing the Muscle Shoals (Tennessee River) hydro project by issuing ~$40 million of US currency backed by neither bonds nor gold, arguing the site's ~1,000,000 horsepower was the best security; Ford: "This energy is wealth in a productive form." Economist Steve Keen disputes the modern reading that Ford/Edison proposed energy as the *unit* of money and notes Ford's anti-gold stance had antisemitic overtones — [Steve Keen, "Ford and Edison: the anti-gold bugs"](https://profstevekeen.substack.com/p/ford-and-edison-the-anti-gold-bugs). Secondary crypto-press sources quote a Dec 1921 New-York Tribune interview: "Under the energy currency system the standard would be a certain amount of energy exerted for one hour that would be equal to USD 1" — [Cryptonews](https://cryptonews.com/exclusives/henry-ford-s-energy-standard-a-100-year-old-bitcoin-predicti-8313.htm); Ford withdrew his Muscle Shoals bid in 1924 and the dam was finished by the TVA — [Navexa](https://www.navexa.com/the-benchmark/edison-and-ford-invented-bitcoin-in-1921-2026-02-26). (Primary Tribune text not located; treat quote as secondhand.)
- **Technocracy Inc. energy certificates (1930s)**: Howard Scott (Technical Alliance, 1919; Technocracy Inc. peak 1932–33) built the movement on an "energy theory of value"; the 1938 pamphlet *The Energy Certificate* specified certificates denominated in ergs/joules, equal per-capita allotments, non-transferable, spendable only at verified vendors, voided on spend (no circulation or hoarding), expiring on a set date. Contemporary critics: Foster & Browder said pricing by the erg merely swapped one arbitrary unit for another; Paul Blanshard (1933) said technocrats had not worked out how energy accounting would function — [Wikipedia, Technocracy movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocracy_movement); Scott's energy-certificate proposal also summarised at [SJSU (Watkins)](https://sjsu.edu/faculty/watkins/technocracy.htm).
- **Buckminster Fuller**: *Critical Path* (1981) discusses "cosmic costing" (pricing petroleum at ~$1 million/gallon by the electricity cost of nature's energy input) and "cosmic-energy income" vs "savings account" — [CS Monitor, 11 Jul 1997](https://csmonitor.com/1997/0711/071197.edit.edit.3.html); [Artforum (Furján), citing Critical Path p. xvii](https://www.artforum.com/features/helene-furjan-217172/). The specific "Kilowatt Dollar" attribution appears only in a Bitcoin newsletter — [Onramp, 24 Jul 2025](https://onrampbitcoin.com/newsletter/7-24-25-roundup-the-energy-standard-a-vision-realized) — and could not be verified against the book.
- **Hayek, "A Commodity Reserve Currency" (1943)**: a rule-bound currency redeemable in a basket of storable commodities, building on Benjamin Graham (1937 buffer stocks; 1944 *World Commodities and World Currency*) and Frank Graham; Keynes and Friedman rejected it on practical grounds (warehousing/perishables), Kaldor supported it — [AIER, "Hayek's Commodity Reserve Currency"](https://aier.org/article/hayeks-commodity-reserve-currency/); [Perry, "Monetary economics of Benjamin Graham"](https://sites.bu.edu/perry/files/2019/04/monetary_economics_benjamin_graham_revised.pdf). Key difference from hash-pegs: Hayek/Graham money was *redeemable* in the stored basket; a hash-peg has nothing in a warehouse.
- **Nick Szabo, "Shelling Out" (2002)**: collectibles are valuable because "unforgeably costly" and the cost is recoverable because "the unforgeably costly commodity repeatedly adds value by enabling beneficial wealth transfers"; cost must be verifiable by the recipient — [Nakamoto Institute, Shelling Out](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/shelling-out/). Szabo's Bit Gold (2005) applied proof-of-work to create digital unforgeable costliness — [Nakamoto Institute, Bit Gold](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/bit-gold/). Note Szabo argues costliness makes something *hard to forge*, not that its price *equals* its cost.
- **Difficulty/hashrate derivatives as the modern descendant**: Luxor launched a cash-settled hashprice NDF in Oct/Nov 2022 — [Hashrate Index, 23 Nov 2022](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/evaluating-luxors-hashprice-ndf-with-historical-data/) — and US exchange-traded hashrate futures with Bitnomial (1 PH contracts, monthly, settled to Luxor Hashprice Index with a 144-block fee average) in March 2024 — [Bitnomial, 19 Mar 2024](https://bitnomial.com/news/2024-03-19/hashrate-futures/); [CoinDesk, 19 Mar 2024](https://coindesk.com/business/2024/03/19/luxor-looks-to-help-bitcoin-miners-hedge-halving-risk-with-new-hashrate-futures/).

### Inferences
- Every pre-crypto "energy money" was either a *redeemable* claim (Hayek/Graham) or a *non-circulating ration* (Technocracy). Qi is neither: it is an unbacked, freely tradable token whose supply is tied to work done. The historical precedents therefore do not establish that an unbacked energy-cost unit can hold value.
- Satoshi's own sentence ("market price will dictate the cost of production more than the other way around") is the earliest statement of the central objection to hash-pegs: once the stock dominates the flow, causality runs price→cost.

### Gaps
- I found no writing by Paul Sztorc on hashrate/difficulty derivatives despite two targeted searches; his connection to this idea is unverified.
- Primary 1921 New-York Tribune text of the Ford interview not retrieved.

## KQ2. Prior attempts at hash- or difficulty-linked assets and what happened to them

### Takeaway
Hashrate tokens (BTCST, pBTC35A) fell 90%+ and were delisted or halted within ~2 years; they were claims on *mining revenue*, which tracks hashprice, and hashprice fell. Difficulty derivatives exist (Luxor NDFs, Bitnomial futures) precisely because hashprice is volatile. Cost-of-production models (Edwards/Capriole) are used as *floor indicators*, not pegs, and carry wide error bands. No earlier hash-pegged *stablecoin* proposal was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Binance BTCST (Bitcoin Standard Hashrate Token)**: launched Dec 2020 on BSC via Binance Launchpool; each token = 0.1 TH/s at 60 W/TH — [Crowdfund Insider, Jan 2021](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2021/01/171037-binance-launchpool-introduces-bitcoin-hashrate-token-as-cryptocurrency-market-cap-surpasses-1-trillion-mark/). Binance delisted BTCST (with MITH, TRIBE, REP) effective 22 Dec 2022 — [TokenInsight](https://tokeninsight.com/en/news/binance-will-delist-mith-tribe-rep-and-btcst); withdrawals ended 1 Sep 2024 with balances force-converted to USDC — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/binance-sets-deadline-for-withdrawal-of-delisted-monero-and-other-tokens/amp). Price fell ~97% in 2022 ($22.13 → $0.72) after ~-66% in 2021; now ~$0.009 — [CoinCodex](https://coincodex.com/crypto/bitcoin-standard-hashrate-token). Token valuation was explicitly "discounted cash flow of the underlying mining power" — [CoinCodex](https://coincodex.com/crypto/bitcoin-standard-hashrate-token).
- **Poolin pBTC35A**: 1 TH/s at 35 J/TH, launched ~Dec 2020 — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/90847/poolin-bitcoin-hashrate-token-pow-defi). Payouts halted June 2021 when Xinjiang/Sichuan shut mining; Poolin promised <60-day pause for miner migration — [The Block, Jun 2021](https://www.theblock.co/linked/109600); partial resumption at ~39,900 TH/s later — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/poolin-mining-pool-partially-resumes-payouts-on-hashrate-tokens/). Lesson: "hash-backing" is jurisdiction-specific physical capacity that can be switched off by policy.
- **Luxor hashprice NDF (launched Oct 2022)**: cash-settled; in its first month 30-day contracts traded from 0.1% premium to 4.5% discount to prior-day hashprice; backtest over 1,827 days found volatility rises with tenor and sell-side losses are unbounded while buy-side losses cap at 100% — [Hashrate Index, 23 Nov 2022](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/evaluating-luxors-hashprice-ndf-with-historical-data/). Jan 2023: contracts mostly sold by miners locking in hashprice, bought by speculators betting on falling difficulty/rising BTC — [Hashrate Index](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/january-luxor-hashrate-derivatives-recap/).
- **Bitnomial/Luxor hashrate futures (Mar 2024)**: CFTC-regulated DCO, 1 PH monthly contracts, settlement uses 144-block moving average — [Bitnomial](https://bitnomial.com/news/2024-03-19/hashrate-futures/); [CFTC filing Dec 2024](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules12202412459.pdf). Luxor's Head of Derivatives: "Hashrate has one of the highest volatilities of major commodities" — [CoinDesk, 19 Mar 2024](https://coindesk.com/business/2024/03/19/luxor-looks-to-help-bitcoin-miners-hedge-halving-risk-with-new-hashrate-futures/). OTC forward volume reportedly >$380m notional in 2025 (vs ~$65m in 2024), cumulative >$500m by 2026 — single secondary source, unverified — [Spark glossary](https://www.spark.money/glossary/hash-rate-derivative). Coin Metrics: no "robust, market-wide accepted methodology for hedging mining operation uncertainties" — [Coin Metrics SOTN](https://coinmetrics.substack.com/p/coin-metrics-state-of-the-network-15b).
- **Charles Edwards / Capriole "Energy Value"**: fair value = f(hashrate × J/GH energy input, supply growth); 2019 energy value ~$11,500 vs $7,200 price; 2024 "fairly priced" at ~$70k; Aug 2025 $145k–168k vs $116k market — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-energy-value-metric-puts-btc-s-fair-value-at-47k-analyst); [Capriole, Energy-Value Equivalence](https://capriole.com/bitcoin-energy-value-equivalence). Price has ranged −70% to +600% vs Energy Value — [Cryptonomist, Feb 2020](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2020/02/17/bitcoin-energy-value/). Capriole's Production Cost = electricity cost ÷ 0.60 assumed elec-share; electricity cost "has been a floor" with price "very close" in Nov 2018; largest hashrate drops 47% (2011), 37% (Dec 2018) — [Capriole, Dec 2019](https://capriole.com/bitcoins-production-cost/). Edwards concedes his trade "assumes the price would stop at fair value, which it never has" — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-deep-value-over-btc-price-nears-70k).
- **Chia**: Bram Cohen's claims were about *lower energy use* (proof of space and time), not price stability; he predicted buying new drives to farm "would lose you money" (2018) — [BREAKER](https://breakermag.com/bram-cohen-chia-green-cryptocurrency-kill-bitcoin/); instead Chia farming drove 12 TB drive prices up 59% in China (2021) — [Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/cryptocurrency/bittorrents-inventor-has-created-a-new-greener-cryptocurrency-chia-that-could-push-up-the-cost-of-hard-drives/); [TNW, May 2021](https://thenextweb.com/news/bittorrent-inventors-green-chia-cryptocurrency-may-not-be-great-for-the-environment-syndication).
- **Earlier hash-pegged stablecoin proposals**: a 2024 cryptoeconomics survey footnotes proposals "making the amount of mining rewards variable, dependent on the results of difficulty adjustment" (unnamed) and cites a 2018 study finding Bitcoin difficulty adjustment does not affect price fluctuation — [arXiv 2407.15715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.15715). d'Avernas, Maurin & Vandeweyer (2022): without an external anchor, stablecoins may have no value even with full commitment — [FDIC paper](https://www.fdic.gov/analysis/cfr/bank-research-conference/annual-21st/papers/davernas-paper.pdf).

### Inferences
- Hashrate tokens are the closest real-world test of "a token worth a fixed quantity of hashpower": both flagship products lost >90% in USD within two years because the USD value of a hash fell (see KQ3). A fixed-hash unit is therefore *not* a USD-stable unit — it is a short-difficulty, long-BTC position.
- The existence and growth of a hedging market for hashprice is itself evidence that miners regard hashprice as a volatile exposure to be laid off, not a stable numeraire.

### Gaps
- No independent source explaining Bitdeer/BitFuFu cloud-hashrate product performance for retail holders; BitFuFu's cloud-mining revenue fell 73.6% YoY in Q2 2026 ($94.3m → $24.9m) — [Cryptorank](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/39b83-bitfufu-q2-2026-revenue-falls-bitcoin-mining) — but that is company revenue, not product returns.
- No pre-2023 explicit "difficulty-pegged stablecoin" whitepaper surfaced in three searches.

## KQ3. Why hashprice is NOT stable in USD: the 2020–2026 record with numbers

### Takeaway
Bitcoin USD hashprice has moved more than 10× peak-to-trough in six years ($412/PH/day in Oct 2021 to ~$27.7 in Jun 2026), with the 2024 halving alone halving it, and difficulty climbing from ~20T to ~156T. Electricity costs vary 4–5× across regions and fleet efficiency has improved from ~30 J/TH to <10 J/TH, so "cost of a hash" is neither a single number nor a stable one.

### Cited Findings
**Hashprice (USD per PH/s per day; 1 PH = 1,000 TH):**
- 19/20 Oct 2021: all-time high $0.41275/TH/day (~$412/PH/day), weeks before BTC's ATH; 2021 average $0.31/TH/day (~$310/PH) — [Hashrate Index 2021 Year-End Report](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/hashrate-index-2021-year-end-report-the-year-of-the-greath-hashrate-migration/).

Glassnode put the Oct 2022 low at $66,500/EH (=$66.5/PH) with BTC ~$19,400 — [Investing.com](https://investing.com/news/cryptocurrency-news/bitcoins-hash-price-reaches-an-alltime-low-of-66k-per-exahash-2919991).

### Inferences
- Peak-to-trough USD hashprice 2021→2026: $412 → ~$27.7/PH/day ≈ −93%. A token defined as "X hashes" would have lost ~93% of USD value over that window even with zero BTC-price exposure error, because difficulty rose ~8× while BTC price only ~1.5–2×.
- Three independent drivers move "cost of a hash": (i) hardware efficiency (3× improvement S19j Pro→S23 in ~5 years), (ii) electricity price (4–5× dispersion across live mining regions), (iii) capital/opex share (~40% of all-in). "Energy per hash" is therefore not a constant, and "USD per hash" even less so.
- For Quai specifically: its network is GPU-mined on KawPow (network ~315 GH/s on 25 Jul 2026 — [Kryptex](https://pool.kryptex.com/quai-kawpow/hashrate)) and also merge-mined via SHA-256 and Scrypt pools ([Kryptex quai-sha256](https://pool.kryptex.com/quai-sha256/hashrate); [quai-scrypt](https://pool.kryptex.com/quai-scrypt/hashrate)). An RTX 3080 12GB does ~42 MH/s KawPow at ~250 W — [Kryptex](https://pool.kryptex.com/device/gpu/nvidia/nvidia-geforce-rtx-3080-12gb). My arithmetic: the quai.org reference "100 MH/s per day = 1 Qi" ([quai.org](https://www.quai.org/)) therefore implies ~2.4 such GPUs ≈ 0.6 kW × 24 h ≈ 14 kWh per Qi, i.e. ~$0.70 of electricity at 5 ¢/kWh, ~$1.40 at 10 ¢, ~$2.90 at 20 ¢ — before hardware, and before noting that merge-mined SHA-256/Scrypt hashes have utterly different energy content. The "energy content" of one Qi is thus ambiguous by at least 4× on electricity price alone.

## KQ4. In what sense can a hash-pegged unit be "stable"? Does production cost anchor from below, and what breaks it?

### Takeaway
A hash-pegged unit can at best be stable in *hashes* (and loosely in joules, if hardware efficiency is netted out); it is not stable in purchasing power. Production cost is at most a *soft floor* that only operates while miners are willing to exit, and difficulty adjustment re-sets "cost" to whatever price is — the academic literature finds causality runs price→hashrate with 1–6 week lags, so cost follows price rather than anchoring it.

### Cited Findings
- **Causality price→hashrate**: "a robust finding across multiple studies is a unidirectional Granger causality running from price to hashrate, with lags typically ranging from one to six weeks" — [arXiv 2508.06071, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06071). JRFM 2020: causality "always unidirectional going from the bitcoin price to the hashrate", lags 1–6 weeks, analogous to oil price→rig count — [MDPI JRFM 13(11):263](https://mdpi.com/1911-8074/13/11/263/html). Dissent: a Vilnius Tech study found insufficient evidence that price affects hashrate — [Vilnius Tech 2023](https://vilniustech.lt/files/5169/258/12/0_0/vvf_2023%20-%20032.pdf).
- **Prat & Walter (JPE 2021, 129(8):2415–2452)**: structural model in which free entry caps mining revenue; BTC/USD price forecasts hashrate; "a significant share of mining rewards was invested in mining equipment and the seigniorage income of miners was limited" — [IDEAS/JPE](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/doi10.1086-714445.html); [CESifo WP 6865](https://www.cesifo.org/en/publications/2018/working-paper/equilibrium-model-market-bitcoin-mining).
- **Difficulty vs price (Perrenod, 11 Jun 2025)**: full 2013–2025 sample: price leads difficulty in "core" regime (Granger significant 2–8 wks, strongest at 4); in "bubble" regimes difficulty appeared to lead price (3–7 wks), but "difficulty leading price is not demonstrated in the bubble zone for the last 5 years" (May 2020–May 2025), when max difficulty adjustment ratio stayed <1.1 — [Perrenod Substack](https://stephenperrenod.substack.com/p/does-difficulty-lead-bitcoin-price).
- **The floor is soft**: Edwards' own production-cost floor estimate was ~$20,260 at the 2022 lows, with 2018 and 2020 bottoms "touching" the lower boundary — [Bitcoinist](https://bitcoinist.com/why-bitcoin-production-cost-is-a-likely-bottom/amp/); Capriole says price "never quite reaches" electrical cost but came "very close" Nov 2018 — [Capriole](https://capriole.com/bitcoins-production-cost/). A 2025 analysis notes that if inefficient miners capitulate, hashrate declines, reducing Energy Value and easing difficulty for survivors — i.e., the "floor" moves down with price — [Unlock-BC](https://unlock-bc.com/en/bitcoin-energy-value-miners-profitability).
- **Mechanics of the ceiling/floor**: "price sits in hashprice's numerator and difficulty in the denominator. Rising prices attract machines, and about two weeks later difficulty rises and competes the margin away" — [Hoge.gg](https://hoge.gg/hashprice-2026-difficulty-ceiling-miner-recovery/). Bitcoin difficulty retarget capped at 4× per period; Quai instead uses rolling per-block adjustment — [Quai primer PDF, Oct 2024](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf) **[Quai claim]**.
- **Unbacked tokens**: "without an external anchor, stablecoins may have no value even with full commitment, similarly to fiat money" — d'Avernas, Maurin & Vandeweyer 2022, [FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/analysis/cfr/bank-research-conference/annual-21st/papers/davernas-paper.pdf).
- **Satoshi's caveat** (above): "market price will dictate the cost of production more than the other way around" once issuance is small relative to supply — [bitcointalk 7 Aug 2010](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=721.msg8114#msg8114).
- **Quai's own concession on the axis of stability** **[Quai claim]**: Qi "isn't pegged to an exact dollar value but rather a worldly good represented through energy expenditure"; value is "loosely tied to the cost of generating a hash"; stability "depends on rapid supply adjustments in response to changes in demand" — [Quai blog, 29 Aug 2024](https://www.qu.ai/blog/a-new-era-of-stablecoin-qi-the-energy-dollar). Primer: "Qi is meant to have stable purchasing power, connected to the cost of energy, and act as a non-fiat dollar" and "There is no fixed peg, backing nor collateralization" — [Quai primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf). Docs: each Qi "represents approximately the same amount of energy, regardless of when it was mined" and energy backing creates an "intrinsic value floor" — [Quai docs, Qi emissions](https://docs.qu.ai/learn/tokenomics/qi-emissions).

### Inferences
- A unit defined by hashes is stable only in hashes. To be stable in *energy* it must correct for efficiency gains; Quai's k_QI "decaying component to account for projected growth in processing power" ([primer](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)) is a *forecast* of Moore's-law-like improvement — if realised efficiency gains differ from the schedule, the "energy per Qi" drifts. Edwards' model had the same problem (assumed improvement rate fell from >400%/yr in 2015 to 50% in 2019 — [Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2020/02/17/bitcoin-energy-value/)).
- Production cost gives a one-sided, leaky floor: (a) it binds only when marginal miners actually exit; (b) exit lowers difficulty, which lowers the cost per coin, so the floor chases the price down; (c) sunk hardware means miners keep hashing below all-in cost as long as price > electricity, so the effective floor is cash cost (~40% below all-in by Capriole's 60% assumption). Nothing caps price from above except *new* issuance, which in Qi's design is throttled by miners' *choice* to take Qi — meaning the ceiling is a behavioural assumption, not a mechanical one.
- Because difficulty follows price with weeks of lag, an energy unit's "cost anchor" is endogenous to the market price of the asset being mined. In a dual-token system the relevant price is the *Quai* price (miners compare Quai-vs-Qi reward value), so Qi's "cost" is downstream of Quai's market price — the reflexivity point in KQ5.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed test of difficulty→price specifically (only hashrate), and no academic paper models a dual-token miner-choice system like Quai's.

## KQ5. Critiques of Quai's Qi specifically, and Quai's defences

### Takeaway
Independent, substantive analysis of Qi is thin: the only non-promotional assessment found (Disruption Banking, Sep 2026) concludes Qi is "a working design with yet to be proven economics" that "has not shown stability under load", with ~249k Qi in circulation against a ~$9m QUAI market cap. Known structural attack surfaces — miner choice as the price signal, Quai-price reflexivity, and difficulty following price — are visible in Quai's own primer but no third party has published a formal analysis of them. No reliable Qi/USD price series exists in public aggregators.

### Cited Findings
**Mechanism as described by Quai [Quai claim]:**
- BlockReward_QUAI = k_QUAI · log₂(Difficulty); BlockReward_QI = k_QI · Difficulty; Difficulty = expected hashes per block. k_QUAI is updated "such that the market value of the block reward in Quai tokens is equal to the market value of the block reward in Qi tokens, and both are equal to the cost of mining a block (such that miners are economically indifferent...)". k_QI has "a decaying component to account for the projected growth in processing power over time". "Exact function values for K_QUAI and K_QI will be publicly finalized prior to mainnet." Miners elect Quai or Qi each block; anyone can burn one and mint the other "at the current block reward ratio"; ~two-week cooldown on rewards and conversions; optional lock-up multipliers — [Quai primer PDF, 14 Oct 2024](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf). Docs give the Qi proportionality constant "currently set to 8.0 × 10⁹" — [Quai docs, Qi emissions](https://docs.qu.ai/learn/tokenomics/qi-emissions). Miner preference parameter 0–1 (default 0.5 = 50/50) — [Quai blog, 22 Oct 2024](https://www.qu.ai/blog/maximizing-mining-strategy-in-quai-network-choosing-between-quai-and-qi-tokens); [Quai on X, Oct 2024](https://x.com/QuaiNetwork/status/1848525333072368077). - Quai's own blog describes a *timing trade*: convert Quai→Qi at a difficulty peak (~415bn), mine Qi as difficulty falls to ~344bn (Quai emissions +~1%, Qi emissions +~20%), convert back and "end with more Quai than at the start" — [Quai blog, 22 Oct 2024](https://www.qu.ai/blog/maximizing-mining-strategy-in-quai-network-choosing-between-quai-and-qi-tokens). ### Inferences (structural attack surfaces — my analysis, grounded in Quai's own documents)
- **Miner-choice as price oracle**: k_QUAI is tuned from the *observed miner Quai/Qi election* so that both rewards are "equal to the cost of mining a block". The protocol has no external price feed; the miner vote *is* the oracle. A miner coalition (or a single large merge-mining pool — Quai's SHA-256/Scrypt merge-mining means Bitcoin/Litecoin pools can contribute hash at ~zero marginal cost) can therefore steer the ratio, and Quai's own Oct 2024 blog documents a profitable difficulty-timing loop. No public formal analysis of collusion thresholds was found.
- **Reflexivity**: the "cost of mining a block" that both rewards are supposed to equal is itself denominated in market value of Quai (or Qi). If Quai's USD price falls (it fell ~98% from Feb 2025 ATH to Sep 2026 ATL per DefiLlama), rational miners switch to whichever token has higher market value, and arbitrageurs burn the cheaper token into the dearer one; the "energy anchor" is thus a function of Quai's price, not an independent reference. Disruption Banking's "supply vs implied price" test is the right empirical check and has not been run publicly.
- **Difficulty follows price**: because hashrate (hence difficulty, hence Qi emission per block) responds to the USD value of rewards with 1–6 week lags (KQ4), a falling Quai price lowers difficulty, which lowers Qi per block — reducing Qi supply growth, but also reducing the "energy per Qi" the design claims is constant. Conversely in a Quai rally, difficulty and Qi emission expand. Qi supply is therefore pro-cyclical with Quai speculation, the opposite of what a stabiliser needs.
- **Thin float**: 249k Qi (Jul 2026) against a ~$9–11m QUAI market cap means the mechanism has never been stress-tested at scale; any observed "stability" so far is uninformative.

### Gaps
- No economist, Messari analyst, or crypto researcher was found publishing a named, formal critique of Qi (searches on Reddit, X, Medium, Messari with terms "critique", "flawed", "reflexivity", "manipulate" returned only Quai material). This absence is itself a finding: as of Oct 2026 the design has not attracted adversarial review.
- No Quai rebuttal to manipulation/reflexivity concerns was found; the primer's only adversarial-behaviour discussion is that lock-up multipliers add "disincentives to the possibility of miners acting adversarially".
- Final k_QUAI/k_QI functions and the conversion slippage schedule were not retrievable from public docs (404).
- No dated Qi/USD time series: the WQI pool snapshot is undated and thin.

## KQ6. Lessons from other non-USD "stable" units (Ampleforth, RAI, flatcoins, compute dollars)

### Takeaway
Every non-$1 unit tried so far has stayed tiny: AMPL (CPI-adjusted 2019 dollar) ~$1.23–1.28 with a market cap in the single-digit millions to tens of millions in mid-2026; RAI (floating redemption price) ~550k supply with a persistently negative redemption rate and a founder-admitted design mistake; Nuon and SPOT have no visible traction. The consistent lesson is that users will not hold or price in a unit whose value drifts from $1 unless they are paid to, and designers who refused a hard peg later regretted the resulting adoption gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Ampleforth (AMPL)**: targets one CPI-adjusted 2019 US dollar; daily rebase if deviation >5%, proportional supply change — [Chainlink case study](https://chain.link/case-studies/ampleforth); [MixBytes](https://www.mixbytes.io/blog/security-of-algorithmic-stablecoins). Founder resisted the "stablecoin" label; Axios called it a "chillcoin" that targets a dollar "loosely" — [Axios, 18 May 2022](https://www.axios.com/2022/05/18/ampleforth-crypto-stablecoin-native). Mid-2026 price ~$1.23–1.28; market cap estimates range $3.7m (BingX, 1 Aug 2026) / $4.05m (Coinranking, 21 Jul 2026) to $35m (Blockspot, 10 Sep 2026) due to differing supply figures — [BingX](https://bingx.com/price/ampleforth); [Coinranking](https://coinranking.com/coin/_cgMK4FnC+ampleforth-ampl/historical-data); [Blockspot](https://blockspot.io/coin/ampleforth-ampl/price-prediction/). ATH $4.05 (Jul 2020) — [Coinlore](https://www.coinlore.com/coin/ampleforth/historical-data).
- **RAI (Reflexer)**: ETH-only collateral, floating redemption price set by a PID controller. Founder Ameen Soleimani called the ETH-only choice a "mistake" (staking yield makes borrowing RAI costly) requiring a restart — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/rai-regrets-mistake-eth). Vitalik Buterin netted ~$92k shorting RAI over 7 months; Soleimani cited it as evidence of design flaw; redemption price fell 7.6% from 15 May 2022 to early 2023 — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/205078/wsiew@theblock.co); [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/vitalik-buterin-earned-92000-from-rai-token-trades/). Negative redemption rates of about −10%/yr were "quite common" (2023) — [PhD Capital comments](https://phdcapital.substack.com/p/rai-and-lusd-stablecoins-built-for/comments). Circulating supply ~550k (2026) vs ~2.05m earlier; ATL $1.75 on 5 Jun 2026 — [CoinGecko RAI](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/rai/usd); [The Big Whale](https://app.thebigwhale.io/tokens/rai). Original Reflexer contributors forked to HAI on Optimism (20 Feb 2024) with DAO governance and multi-collateral — [The Block, 6 Feb 2024](https://www.theblock.co/post/276100/stablecoin-protocol-hai-to-have-airdrop-ahead-of-mainnet-launch-on-optimism). RAI "remained much less volatile than most free-floating cryptocurrencies, including ether" — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/we-were-idiots-and-we-were-poor-then-from-mid-2020-to-now-suddenly-were-right-ameen-soleimani).
- **Nuon (Laguna Labs)**: "first flatcoin pegged to the cost of living", testnet Oct 2022, Arbitrum mainnet Feb 2023, Truflation oracle, liquidates least-collateralised positions on depeg — [Benzinga, Oct 2022](https://benzinga.com/markets/cryptocurrency/22/10/29389763/laguna-labs-launches-the-worlds-first-flatcoin-pegged-to-the-cost-of-living); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/nuon-flatcoin-targets-inflation). No post-2023 status or supply data found.
- **SPOT (Ampleforth)**: "a different kind of stablecoin that isn't pegged to anything", redeemable claim on AMPL-collateral tranches; Coinbase Ventures invested $1m — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/193230/ampleforth-developer-debuts-ethereum-based-inflation-resistant-token). No supply data retrieved.
- **Flatcoin critique**: flatcoins face an oracle problem (CPI data is centralised BLS data) and are exposed to Terra-style reflexive runs — [Cointelegraph opinion](https://cointelegraph.com/opinion/flatcoiners-should-take-a-cue-from-terrausd-s-fate). Quai's own blog makes the same oracle critique of flatcoins ("still rely on centralized oracles") — [Quai blog, 29 Aug 2024](https://www.qu.ai/blog/a-new-era-of-stablecoin-qi-the-energy-dollar) **[Quai claim]** — while substituting miner behaviour as its oracle (KQ5).
- **"Compute dollars"**: STAB3L proposes a benchmark "Standard Unit of Compute" index and a token pegged to 1 SUC — [Village.ai, STAB3L](https://village.ai/company/stab3l); a research proposal defines Compute Hours = (Performance/Reference Performance) × Operational Hours — [Moonlight review, "Commodification of Compute"](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/commodification-of-compute). USD.AI (Permian Labs) is a *USD-pegged* stablecoin collateralised by GPU-hardware loans, not a compute unit; critics flag hardware depreciation and falling rental yields — [Lex Sokolin / Fintech Blueprint](https://lex.substack.com/p/ai-gpu-backed-stablecoins-blend-ai); [Aave governance ARFC](https://governance.aave.com/t/arfc-onboard-usdai-susdai-to-aave-v3-arbitrum-instance/23260). None has traction as a unit of account.

### Inferences
- Across AMPL, RAI, Nuon, SPOT, the pattern is: (1) the unit holds value *roughly* but drifts from $1; (2) no merchant, exchange, or DeFi venue prices in it; (3) holders bear an opportunity cost (negative RAI redemption rate; AMPL rebase dilution) and leave; (4) the team either pivots to a harder peg/multi-collateral (HAI) or the product stagnates at a few million dollars. A hash-denominated Qi inherits all four problems plus a reference (hashprice) that is far more volatile in USD than CPI or ETH-PID outputs (KQ3: −93% peak-to-trough 2021–2026).
- The "compute dollar" projects show the same unsolved problem Qi has: compute/energy is not a homogeneous commodity (different chips, regions, efficiencies), so the index itself must be designed and governed — reintroducing the oracle/governance Quai says it avoids.
- Practical lesson for a stablecoin designer: if the goal is adoption as money, the unit must be $1 (or a widely quoted fiat). If the goal is an inflation-hedged or energy-indexed *asset*, call it that, expect a small, speculative holder base, and build in a redemption or collateral mechanism — the unbacked variants (AMPL, Qi) have no mechanical floor at all.

### Gaps
- Current Nuon and SPOT supply/market-cap figures not found.
- No academic adoption study of non-USD stable units was located.
