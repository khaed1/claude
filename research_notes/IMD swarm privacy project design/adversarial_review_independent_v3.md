## Working findings (appended in reading order)

### Batch 1 (read through line ~680: summaries, circuit, Pool, Registry tables). Raw findings, to be ranked later.

**F-SCR-1 (real, high). The "one to two days to refuse stolen funds" window is really 0 to 1 day, and a thief chooses 12 seconds.**
A batch for epoch E is "computed from chain data at the epoch's first block", `upTo` = "highest deposit id made before the epoch's first block", and flags count only "before epochStart". Thief deposits in the last block of epoch E-1 (block s_E - 1). Batch E covers it using data frozen one block later. Batch E is accepted in s_E+4,800..s_E+7,199. The deposit is 7,200 blocks old at s_E+7,199 and is activated there. Anything screeners learn during the whole of epoch E (theft reported, flags posted, list updated) can only enter batch E+1, which cannot be accepted before s_E+12,000. The 7,200-block age rule adds nothing because the covering batch is already fixed. Screeners' real reaction time = (s_E - deposit block) = 1 block. Summary sections ("Screeners get one to two days to refuse stolen funds", "with one to two days for screeners to refuse") are false as specified.
Fix: `upTo` must be the highest deposit id made at least 7,200 blocks BEFORE the epoch's first block (or Pool.activate requires `depositBlock + 7200 <= startBlock(epoch of last applied batch)`; the block is already in the record). Cost: activation latency becomes 2-3 days.

**F-SCR-2 (real, high). `screenedThrough` is unbounded and "may only rise": one captured (or buggy) accepted batch with `upTo = 2^64-1` switches screening off for good.**
`apply` checks only that the batch hashes to an accepted one. Nothing compares `upTo` with `Pool.depositCount`. `isApproved` = `id <= screenedThrough` and bit clear, bits default clear, so after such a batch every future deposit is approved the moment it is 7,200 blocks old; later honest batches can only set refusal bits after the fact, and a deposit made just after an epoch start is activatable (b+7,200) before the first batch that could refuse it is accepted (>= s+12,000). The spec's claim is that capture "lets a bad deposit through"; in fact a single captured epoch is permanent. Same hole from a plain off-by-N bug in the batch program. Fix: registry snapshots `Pool.depositCount()` at the first registry call of each epoch (or pool exposes `countAtBlock`), and `apply` requires `upTo < snapshot[epoch]`.

**F-SCR-3 (real, high). Capturing two thirds is worth far more than "a bad deposit": it is exclusive control of who may enter, hence of the whole 45% user emission and of 100% of screener pay.**
A 2/3 cartel (a) reveals a batch that differs from the honest one in any byte (`dataCid` is free) so honest revealers are unpaid and after 7 accepted batches are evictable ("missed 7 accepted batches") and need 30 epochs to return; (b) sets refusal bits on every deposit except its own, fills >= 1/4 of each cap with its own money and so collects the entire reward emission (45% of supply) plus the 15% screener stream plus 40% of fees. No slashing, no remedy, immutable. One address already holds 77 seats; with the first-week rule those 77 are all active at epoch 7 with no 30-day wait, and it has 2/3 unless more than 38 independent seats register in week one. For an outsider with 15 honest screeners: 30 seats = 21 ETH = $44,700 of resalable capital.
Fix (within fixed decisions): pay only for batches whose `dataCid`/words are independent of free fields is impossible on-chain; what can be done: (i) remove the first-week shortcut or make week-one registrations count only up to a small per-holder... (not enforceable) -> at least make "missed accepted batches" NOT an eviction ground (a minority that reveals every day is live, by definition); (ii) refusal should need an on-chain reason code and be time-limited (a refusal bit expires unless renewed), so default-deny-all costs 96-word batches for ever; (iii) say it in the risk section.

**F-SCR-4 (real, medium-high). A one-third-plus-one minority blocks deposits for ever, is never evictable, and it PAYS.**
Blockers commit and reveal a junk hash every epoch (2 cheap txs per seat per day). They "revealed", so the 7-epoch no-reveal ground never applies; no batch is accepted, so "missed 7 accepted batches" never applies either. The spec's recovery ("dropped from the count after seven days") only works against silent screeners. With 15 honest: 8 seats = 5.6 ETH = $11,900 resalable + refundable stakes. Contrary to "earns them nothing": whoever is already inside the pool keeps the whole emission undiluted (the index divisor is max(weighted, floor), so incumbents holding a quarter of the cap collect 100% of emission while entry is shut). Also every blocked day burns that day's share of the screeners' 15% ("stays locked for good"), starving honest screeners until fewer than seven remain.
Fix: evict on "no reveal matching the plurality hash in 7 epochs where the plurality reached one half of the snapshot"; and lower the acceptance threshold for an epoch to 2/3 of those who REVEALED once 7 epochs pass with no accepted batch (bounded below by seven); roll the unpaid token share forward instead of locking it.

**F-SCR-5 (real, medium). Commit-reveal does not stop copying; it stops copying from the chain only.**
The batch is a deterministic function of public data. Every screener is paid only if two thirds agree, so every screener profits from publishing its batch hash off-chain during the 4,800-block commit window, and a lazy seat commits `keccak(publishedHash, ownSeatId, ownSalt)`. One whale computes once and commits 77 times. The redesign's claim "a screener cannot copy another's answer" is false; quorum still measures one node (or one public feed, whose operator can then omit a refusal bit).
Fix: none on-chain that is cheap; be honest in the text; make the node software refuse to gossip batch hashes before reveal (weak); or require each commit to include a per-seat proof-of-computation (e.g. hash of batch salted INSIDE the preimage of words keyed by seatId, i.e. each screener reveals `H(words, seatId)` so a copier needs the full batch, not the hash) - still shareable.

**F-SCR-6 (real, medium). Example criteria: flag quorum is an absolute 3, so 3 seats (2.1 ETH = $4,470, resalable) can have ANY deposit, or every deposit, refused by the honest two thirds.**
"flags: quorum 3" and "deny: flagged(depositor) || flagged(anyAddressWithin(depositor, 2 hops, 7d))". Honest nodes are deterministic and must refuse. Flag an exchange hot wallet and every depositor funded from it within 7 days is refused. `flag(seatId, data)` stores nothing and costs one log. This is a 3-seat veto, far below the "one third to delay" the spec claims. Fix: quorum as a fraction of snapActive (>= 1/3), flags expire, flags apply to the depositor only (no hop expansion).

**F-SCR-7 (ambiguity with lethal default). "At most 96 words per batch" has no stated truncation rule.**
If the deterministic batch has more than 96 changed words, either apply() can never run (deposits stall while the backlog exists) or the program truncates; if it truncates while still advancing `upTo` past an unlisted dirty word, refused deposits become approved by default. Attacker without seats: ~97 "hold" deposits (>10 ETH from a fresh address, refunded at once, capital recycled) spaced one per bitmap word needs ~24,600 filler deposits per day = ~2.6e9 gas = ~1.3 ETH/day at 0.5 gwei. With F-SCR-6, three seats do it for the price of one log.
Fix: specify: `upTo` is lowered to just below the first dirty word that does not fit; words sorted ascending.

**F-SCR-8 (ambiguity with lethal default). Eviction ground "has not revealed for 7 epochs" has no stated starting point.**
A screener registered at epoch r cannot commit until r+30 (or 7). If the counter runs from registration (lastReveal = 0 or r), every new screener is evictable at r+7, before it has ever been allowed to reveal: nobody can ever join after week one, and first-week registrants from epoch 0 are evictable in the first block of epoch 7. Anyone can call evict. Must be specified as `max(lastReveal, activeFrom)`.

**F-SCR-9 (ambiguity). `activeCount` cannot change by passage of time.** "Counts from 30 epochs later" needs a per-epoch join delta folded in at the snapshot, or a first-commit increment. With a first-commit increment, a block of newly eligible seats is in the tally but not in `snapActive` for its first epoch: with snapActive = 7, five fresh sybil seats reach 2/3 alone. Spec must state the mechanism.

**F-SCR-10 (real, low-medium). Pay settlement only "on commit" with a 16-epoch ring: a screener that reveals and then exits, is evicted, or is offline 16 epochs loses that pay and the money is stranded in the registry for ever** unless requestExit/evict/withdrawPay also settle. Not stated.

**F-SCR-11 (real, low). apply() is unpaid** (up to 96 fresh SSTOREs ~ 2.1M gas). Works only because a depositor wants in. A thief benefits when the next (refusing) batch is NOT applied; honest screeners have no paid duty to apply it.

**F-SCR-12 (trust stated elsewhere, not in the registry spec; residual gaps).** `ownerOf` on the IMD seat contract is called in register, commit, reveal and evict. The identity section (not the build specification) says the seat NFT "is not a proxy" and its `owner()` is the CREATE2 proxy, so no key. CHECKED AND ACCEPTED as stated. Residual, unstated: whether seat supply is capped at 2,000 in code; whether a seat can be burned (`ownerOf` then reverts: `evict` on the "changed hands" ground and `commit` must not brick on a revert); escrow marketplaces and wrappers change `ownerOf`, so listing a seat for sale on an escrow venue lets anyone evict the screener (30-epoch re-entry). The registry spec should restate the seat contract's address, code hash and these properties as a pinned assumption.

**F-SCR-13 (ambiguity). "Any two honest nodes get the same batch"** needs canonical encoding of the batch, of the dataCid file (CID version, chunker, codec, JSON canonical form), the hop-tracing data source (internal transfers? token transfers? which client?), and what "on-chain" bitmap state the words are diffed against when the previous batch is accepted but not yet applied. None is stated. Divergence = no quorum, or minority evicted.

**F-CIR-1 (no soundness break found in constraints 1-14 as worded, but gaps a proof needs).**
- `earned` and the 64-bit remainder are not in the range list of constraint 3. Soundness survives only because constraint 12 equates it with 96-bit quantities and weight*X < p - 2^162; this must be written, not inferred.
- Binding of `leafIndex` to the path selectors: constraint 3 lists "leafIndex fits in 32 bits" and "path selectors are bits" as if separate signals. If a builder keeps them separate, `pos` in the nullifier is free and every note can be spent 2^32 times. Must say: selectors ARE the bits of leafIndex.
- Constraint 13 leaves `publicAsset` free when `publicAmount` = 0, and the contract is only said to check it "against the asset list". The recipient in `ext` is likewise free on a private transfer. Both are 1-in-N tagging channels unless the pool forces them to zero. Asset id 0 must not be a real asset if "zero when nothing leaves" is to mean anything.
- Proof obligation 3 says "at most", constraint 12 says "add up to" (equality). With equality a wallet cannot drop reward; fine, but if earned ever exceeded 4*(2^96-1) the note would be unspendable. Bound depends on supply*10^decimals < 2^96 (to check against parameter table).
- Padding inputs: value 0, accrued 0, everything else free (index, rho, d, leafIndex, slot). They carry nothing of value; their nullifier is an arbitrary hash under the prover's own nk and is stored for ever (state growth paid by the sender). I could not make a padding input create value, reward, or burn another user's nullifier (nk is derived from sk in-circuit).
- Both inputs share `sk` and `asset` (single private signals) - OK. But the two inputs may have different `d`; fine.
- Domain separation: commitment H3(packed, index, oc) vs ownerCommit H3(TAG_OC, ...) separated only because packed >= TAG_NOTE*2^200; requires TAG_NOTE != 0 and tags < 2^200 - state it. Leaf H4(C0..C3) vs H4(TAG_PK..)/H4(TAG_NF..) separated only because a commitment cannot equal a small tag; deposit leaf H4(commitment,0,0,0) - "0" must not equal any tag value used as first input... (first input is commitment, fine). OK.
- Reward note C[3]: value comes only from `claim`, bound by constraint 12. Not creatable from nothing. OK.
- Reward gaming with index: constraint 7 blocks spending against a checkpoint older than the note. Outputs take index = gRef <= G(now). I could not overpay: every unit of weighted value covers a contiguous G-interval inside [G(activation), G(withdrawal)] during which it is counted in `weighted`. Claim "total paid <= released" holds in exact arithmetic and all roundings go the right way.

**F-RWD-1 (ambiguity, liveness). The checkpoint roll is under-specified.** `spend` "rolls the checkpoint if a 300-block boundary has passed"; the index is advanced only "on every activation and every spend with a public amount". Not stated: (a) does the roll advance `g` to the current block first, or copy a stale `g`; (b) if two or more boundaries passed, is it one roll or several; (c) does `activate` roll. Under (b)="several" plus (a)="advance to now", after 600 quiet blocks gPrev = gCur = a value nobody could know before the transaction lands => every submitted proof fails => the roll reverts with it => no spend can ever succeed (permanent deadlock of the whole pool; `checkpoint()` is only a view, there is no standalone poke). Under (a)="stale copy", a withdrawer after a quiet period silently forfeits all reward since the last activation/withdrawal. A freshly activated note (exact index > gCur) cannot be spent until someone else's spend rolls a later checkpoint; in an empty pool the owner must first send a dummy two-padding spend. Must be specified exactly and given a public `roll()`.

**F-RWD-2 (ambiguity, possible permanent defect). `shielded[TOKEN]` vs `tokenIn/tokenOut`.** Claims are private so `shielded[TOKEN]` cannot count reward notes. `spend` "lowers the balances": with checked arithmetic, withdrawing a claimed reward when `shielded[TOKEN]` < amount reverts; if nobody ever deposits the token, no reward can ever leave. If instead rewards are allowed to eat `shielded[TOKEN]`, the token cap accounting is wrong. Also not stated whether `tokenIn` is gross or net of the entry fee (gross would let 0.2% of every token deposit be withdrawn twice, out of the reward budget). Also order: the token limit check is listed BEFORE "advances the reward index", i.e. against a stale `released`.

**F-POOL-1 (real, medium). 64-root window can be flushed with fee-less padding-only spends.** A spend with two padding inputs needs no funds and pays no fee. 64 such spends inserted between a user's proof and its landing invalidate it. ~64 x ~0.6M gas = 38M gas per flush; one flush per ~5 blocks blocks every exit: ~1.1 ETH/hour at 0.5 gwei ($2,400/hour). Cheap enough to hold exits shut for the hours that matter (a depeg, an exploit announcement). Fix: keep 64 roots but ALSO accept any root at a 300-block checkpoint boundary for the current and previous checkpoint (two extra slots).

**F-POOL-2 (real, medium). Cap squatting is still nearly free and is paid for by the emission.** Counting the cap on activated value prices squatting at the 0.2% entry fee: 25 ETH x 3 assets = 0.15 ETH = $320 at step 1, and the squatter collects that step's whole emission (25/3175 of 45% of supply = 0.354% of supply = ~$450 at the 48,000 IMD valuation). Pending deposits are unlimited and free, so the squatter pre-positions deposits and activates in the first block of each cap step.

**F-POOL-3 (real, low-medium). Relay fee as a shielded note roughly doubles the user's gas cost.** A relayer receives n small notes; turning them into ETH takes n-1 merge spends plus an exit, each costing about the gas the fee was meant to cover, plus 0.2% and a 0.005 ETH minimum exit. Break-even fee is >= 2x gas. The relayer also learns the asset and the fee (it must open C[2] to check it), which the design table implies is hidden.

### Batch 2 (lines ~670-811: FeeDistributor, Distribution, Token, state machines, gas, deployment, canary)

**F-LAUNCH-1 (real, high). `launch()` "reverts if that is not enough" = a free, indefinite veto on the whole token launch.**
The token address is known before it exists. Attacker creates the 1% token/IMD pool, initialises it above the start price and mints an IMD-only position just above the start tick (an IMD-only mint never calls `balanceOf` on the code-less token, so it works). Size: more IMD than the "bounded number of tokens" can absorb at ~start price (480 IMD = $1,270 per 1% of supply of bound; 12,000 IMD = $31,700 even if the bound is the entire 25%). `launch()` must sell down through that liquidity, cannot, and reverts. Because it reverts, the attacker's IMD is never touched: cost = capital parked, withdrawable at will. Before launch nobody else holds a single token (all supply is in Distribution; vesting starts at launch), so nobody else can sell into the bids to clear them. No launch => no 45% in the pool, no 15% in the registry, no token to stake => no screener can register in "the first week" => no deposit can ever be activated; meanwhile the genesis-based clocks (cap steps, eight emission steps, deposit window) keep running in immutable code. Kills the canary the same way.
Also internal contradiction: invariant "afterwards the five shares add up to the whole supply" vs "sells a bounded number of tokens" then "adds 25% of supply" - where do the sold tokens come from and where does the IMD received go? Unstated.
Fix (keeps one-sided locked position from 48,000 IMD): never revert. Sell tokens into whatever bids sit above the start price with `sqrtPriceLimit = start`; if the limit is reached, mint the remainder one-sided; if not, mint the planned liquidity L two-sided at the price reached using the IMD just received (selling into bids at or above the curve always yields at least L*(sqrtP - sqrtP0) IMD, so it always closes); surplus IMD goes to the buyback balance. The blocker then simply becomes the first buyer at a premium. Alternative: create the token inside `launch()` with a CREATE2 salt taken from `prevrandao`, so its address is unknown until that block.

**F-LAUNCH-2 (real, medium). Whoever calls `launch()` is the uncontested first buyer.** Same transaction: launch, then buy the bottom of the curve. 4,000 IMD ($10,560) takes 25% of the pool's tokens (6.25% of supply) and moves price to 1.78x; 12,000 IMD ($31,680) takes half (12.5% of supply) at 4x. Every screener must then buy its stake from that sniper's price level inside the first week or wait 30 epochs. The fixed decision is the one-sided pool; the realisation ("Anyone, once", in one transaction, all 25% live at once) hands the snipe to one bundle.

**F-FEE-1 (real, medium-high if mis-built; ambiguity). "IMD-side reserves at the current price" is undefined, and the obvious implementation (`IMD.balanceOf(uniPool)`) voids the sandwich bound.**
An IMD-only range position below the current tick adds to the pool's IMD balance and adds zero depth at the price. Attacker in one transaction: flash IMD, mint out-of-range bids (inflates "reserves" at no risk), front-run buy, call `buyback()` (step = min(IMD held, 0.5% x inflated balance) = everything held), back-run sell, burn the position. Worked example at 25% bought (virtual IMD reserve 16,000): distributor holding 2,000 IMD, attacker front-runs with 16,000 IMD -> attacker nets ~1,180 IMD (59% of the buyback), ~335 IMD of fees recycle. Safe definitions: the Distribution position's own real IMD, L_pos*(sqrtP - sqrtP_start), or in-range virtual L*sqrtP. Must be specified; the canary script would not notice.

**F-FEE-2 (real, medium). The bound is per step; the queue is public and the drift is cumulative.** The distributor's IMD balance B and the step rule are public, so the total price rise ((1+B/x)^2) over the next B/(0.005*y) steps is known to everyone the moment `BasketSold` fires. Buy when the basket sells, call `buyback()` yourself every 25 blocks, sell after the last step. With B = 10% of virtual reserves and an attacker position equal to the virtual reserve, the attacker takes ~55% of B net of the 2% round-trip fee (constant-product arithmetic: receives 1.0756x for x). The spec's claim covers only the atomic sandwich of a single step. It bites once daily basket proceeds exceed the fee rate times virtual reserves (1% x 12,000/(1-f) IMD = 160 IMD at 25% bought, i.e. fee income above ~$2,100/day... 20% share above ~$420/day).
Fix: cap the IMD that may ENTER the buyback queue per day at fee x virtual reserves, or spread each basket sale's IMD over a long fixed drip (e.g. 50,400 blocks) so that queued IMD is never large against depth.

**F-FEE-3 (real, low-medium). "That IMD stays in the pool as bids for good" is true only for the locked position's share of in-range liquidity.** Any third-party in-range LP (including a JIT range order placed one tick above price immediately before calling `buyback()`) is the counterparty for its pro-rata share: it sells tokens to the distributor at spot with no slippage, keeps the IMD and the 1% fee, and withdraws. Large holders (founder, swarm, a sniper) get a slippage-free exit paid by the 20%.

**F-FEE-4 (real, medium-low). Auction price has a one-sale memory; lumps go to MEV.** Price is for "the whole basket" and never starts above 2x the last price paid, while the basket's value jumps whenever fees are swept (once per 7,200 blocks per asset, by anyone, atomically with `buy`). Any fee lump larger than twice the previous sale (first busy day after a quiet week; one 1,000 ETH exit = 0.4 ETH to the basket) is sold at <= 2x the stale price to whoever back-runs it; the difference is bid away to the block builder, not to the buyback. Fix: release swept fees into the sellable basket linearly over 50,400 blocks, like the staker stream.
- Buying an empty basket: costs the current price in IMD, resets start to 2x paid. Raising the start by repeated buys costs the attacker the price each time and delays sales by at most a day (linear fall to the floor in 7,200 blocks whatever the start); but a start >= 7,200 x value makes the price fall through the whole profitable band inside one block, so the sale goes to a single-block race at about the floor. Cost to the griefer ~3,600 x basket value: not economic. No real problem.
- Naming a subset: buyer pays the full price for the subset, the rest stays. No extraction. Not stated: what happens when a NAMED asset's transfer fails (revert vs skip) - invariant 6 implies skip, which silently takes the buyer's IMD.
- Not stated: relation between `floor` and `minimum` start; whether `buy` with an empty `assets` list is allowed (it is the cheapest way to reset the clock).

**F-FEE-5 (real, low). Staker stream.** (a) If `totalStaked` falls to zero mid-stream, standard reward-per-token accounting strands the remaining stream in the contract for ever (the "nothing staked -> basket" rule is applied only at `notify`). (b) Anyone can call `sweepFees` every 7,200 blocks with dust, restarting the 50,400-block stream each time: payout becomes an exponential tail (time constant 7 days) rather than "evenly over seven days"; no theft. (c) rate = amount/50,400 truncates to zero for small 6-decimal amounts unless scaled. (d) Invariant 2 omits staked tokens for the token asset. (e) First staker with 1 wei takes the whole 40% until others arrive (expected).

**F-DEP-1 (ambiguity). The Deployer as described is circular.** "Constructor fixes the six creation-code hashes" and "each constructor receives the others' addresses": if addresses are constructor arguments inside the hashed code, the hashes depend on the Deployer's address, which depends on the hashes. Constructors must derive sibling addresses from `msg.sender` and the nonce. Also unstated: `deploy` must revert unless the created address equals the planned one (a failed CREATE that does not revert burns the nonce and bricks the address plan); whether hashes cover constructor arguments (if not, anyone picks the founder address); which contract defines "genesis block" and how the registry learns it if it is created before the pool; and that Pool must not treat a call to a not-yet-deployed Verifier as success (six separate transactions leave a gap).

**F-CAN-1 (real, medium). The canary checklist cannot be completed as funded, and the "same build" is not the same bytecode.**
- "An activation that fails at a full cap" needs `shielded` at the cap. First activation is ~day 8; the canary cap doubles every 7 days from 25 ETH, so the cap is already 50 ETH at the first possible activation and 800 ETH by day 35. Funding is "at least 10 ETH". The line is unreachable unless some asset has a tiny cap.

Founder and swarm vesting (two years) are not compressed, so end-of-vest arithmetic is never exercised; nor is the registry's 30-epoch path unless scripted; nor a >96-word batch; nor a real failed transfer (issuer freeze); nor adversarial liquidity in the pre-created pool (only a wrong price is listed); nor `evict` for missed batches, pay after exit, ring wrap by an absent screener.
- Needs at least seven real seat holders to buy and stake a worthless canary token inside its first week, or nothing can be activated before day 30+.

**F-WAL-1 (real, medium). A wallet cannot be restored from chain data alone.**
- `Spent` does not carry `gRef`, and no event is emitted when a checkpoint rolls. Every note created by a spend has `index = gRef`; without it the commitment cannot be recomputed and `earned` cannot be proved. It is recoverable only from transaction calldata (not from logs/snapshots; not trivially when the call came through a relayer contract or bundler).
- Deposits publish only `ownerCommit`; "rho is 248 random bits". A wallet restored from its seed cannot find rho for an activated deposit it has not yet spent -> the note is lost. rho (and d) for deposits must be derived deterministically from the seed and this must be normative.
- The relay fee note C[2] has no ciphertext on-chain ("opened to the relayer off-chain"): a relayer that loses its local database loses every unmerged fee note.
- The reward note C[3] ("derived by its owner"): `claim` is in no ciphertext; it is derivable only if the wallet convention fixes the accrued sent to outputs 0 and 2. The 80-byte payload layout is not defined at all.
- `Activated` is fine (leafIndex, commitment, index, net). `Deposited` lacks the block number used in the record hash (available from the log's block, OK).
