# Unsolved Problems of On-Chain Privacy (status as of 3 November 2026)

Scope: a sourced inventory of why crypto privacy tools are not used and why they fail, across technical, UX, economic, legal and social dimensions. Every statistic is dated. Where I could not find a reliable source, the item is listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

Research-method note: most "primary" quantitative sources on anonymity sets are academic papers (2022–2025); legal status comes from law-firm client alerts and court reporting; censorship data from live dashboards (mevwatch.info) snapshotted 2 Nov 2026. Several heavily-cited secondary pages (shattered.io, tech-insider.org, coinledger) were judged low-reliability and are not relied upon.

---

## KQ1. Anonymity-set problems (fragmentation, low usage, timing/amount correlation, last mile, address reuse, KYC-exchange linkage)

### Takeaway
The cryptography of mixers like Tornado Cash has not been broken; the anonymity set is. Measured leakage comes almost entirely from user behaviour (address reuse, deposit/withdraw from the same wallet, matching multi-denomination patterns, timing/FIFO ordering, wallet-software fingerprints), and the best academic estimates put 37–44% of Tornado deposits as "compromised" (2021 data) and 5–35% of withdrawals as linkable to deposits (2025 cross-chain study). The pool is also a single entity's attack surface: newer pools (Privacy Pools v2) have sub-$1M TVL and only a few hundred deposits, so the nominal anonymity set is tiny before any heuristics are applied.

### Cited Findings
- Tutela (Jan 2022, Ethereum data Aug 2015–Oct 2021; ~97,365 Tornado deposits, 83,782 withdrawals): 42.8k deposits (~44%) were flagged as compromised by at least one heuristic. By heuristic: exact address match 18.6k (~19%), linked ETH addresses 18.9k (~19%), multi-denomination 16.2k (~17%), unique gas price 102, TORN mining 358. Average anonymity-set reduction across pools 37% (±15%); cDAI and WBTC pools "heavily compromised", USDC less so. — [Tutela, arXiv 2201.06811](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.06811v1)
- Cristodaro/Kramer/Tessone (Oct 2025, Ethereum + BNB Chain + Polygon): 5.1–12.6% of Tornado withdrawals traceable to deposits via address-reuse and transactional-linkage heuristics; a FIFO temporal-matching heuristic adds a further 15–22 percentage points; the heuristics link >$2.3B of withdrawals to identifiable deposits. Leakage "comparable" across all three chains, which the authors attribute to "chain-agnostic user misbehaviour" rather than protocol flaws. CAVEAT: v3 of the paper was withdrawn by the author on 18 Nov 2025 "due to mistakes in the references"; a corrected version was promised. Treat numbers as provisional. — [arXiv 2510.09433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09433)
- "Attacking Anonymity Set in Tornado Cash via Wallet Fingerprints" (ACM SAC 2025): withdrawals sent from a particular wallet software can be matched to deposits made with the same software, and are heuristically linkable if the deposit precedes the withdrawal. (Full PDF returned 403; finding from abstract/search summary only.) — [ACM DL 10.1145/3672608.3707896](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3672608.3707896)
- "Analysis of Address Linkability in Tornado Cash on Ethereum" (2022) proposes three heuristic clustering rules that reduce the effective anonymity set. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358013690_Analysis_of_Address_Linkability_in_Tornado_Cash_on_Ethereum)
- Basic anonymity-set arithmetic: each linked deposit/withdrawal pair removes one member; with 3 users and one link, the chance of guessing the remaining pair goes to 1/2. — [Tutela](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.06811)
- Privacy Pools v2 (Ethereum, L2BEAT snapshot early Nov 2026): TVL $510K; 470 total deposits; 363 in last 30 days; only 2 active relayers in 30 days. L2BEAT warns "the small anonymity set limits the ratings". Per-asset deposits (total): ppETH 230, ppUSDC 146, ppUSDT 76, raw ETH 18. The anonymity set definition counts only ASP-approved deposits of the same asset whose notes still hold at least the withdrawn amount, i.e. fragmented by asset, by amount and by ASP approval. — [L2BEAT Privacy Pools v2](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)
- Privacy Pools usage as of Nov 2025: "$6 million in volume across more than 1,500 users" (press release). — [NatLawReview / 0xbow seed round](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/0xbow-closes-35m-round-compliant-crypto-privacy-technology-following)
- L2BEAT chain-analyst risk list for Privacy Pools v2 (what still links deposits to withdrawals even with a sound ZK design): a note's label passes to later payees; timing against ASP root updates; unusual amounts; yield-share counts; gas fingerprints. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)
- Railgun: an analyst argues the anonymity set remains small relative to volume processed and is a structural limit (secondary analysis). Railgun TVL ~$110M as of 2 Nov 2026 (DefiLlama). — [DefiLlama Railgun](https://defillama.com/protocol/railgun); [Learning Pill analysis](https://thelearningpill.substack.com/p/this-privacy-protocol-is-about-to)
- Tornado Cash historical TVL peaked around $800M+ (DefiLlama shows $812.75M at one snapshot); current pool depth is far lower. — [DefiLlama Tornado](https://defillama.com/protocol/tornado-cash?hideMcapChart=true)
- Zcash as counter-example of what "default-on" does to the set: shielded share of transactions reported at ~6% (2021 paper), ~8% early 2024, 18% Oct 2025, >30% of supply shielded by May 2026, with a 59.3% shielded-transaction share in Feb 2026 attributed to wallets (Zashi/Zodl) routing users to shielded by default via Unified Addresses. Metrics differ between sources (share of supply vs share of txs) and some sources are promotional. — [arXiv 2105.02793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02793); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/why-30-of-zcash-supply-is-now-in-the-shielded-pool/); [CoinDesk Research](https://www.coindesk.com/research/encryption-supremacy-zcash-and-privacy-in-the-age-of-scale)

### Inferences
- Fixed denominations (Tornado) cause multi-denomination fingerprints; arbitrary amounts (Railgun, Privacy Pools v2) cause "unusual amount" fingerprints. Neither eliminates amount correlation; only in-pool transfers that never exit solve it.
- Per-asset, per-chain, per-ASP, per-denomination pools fragment the set multiplicatively: Privacy Pools v2 is deployed on 7 chains with 3 assets each, yet has 470 total deposits on mainnet.
- "Chain-agnostic user misbehaviour" means the fix is wallet-side (forced fresh addresses, randomized delays, no self-withdrawal) rather than protocol-side.
- The "last mile" (withdraw → KYC exchange) is the largest practical deanonymizer but there is little public quantification; the Tutela "linked ETH addresses" heuristic (19%) is the closest proxy.

### Gaps
- No public 2025–2026 quantification of the "deposit from KYC exchange / withdraw to KYC exchange" linkage rate specifically; Chainalysis does not publish it.
- No measured timing-correlation success rate for Railgun or Privacy Pools (only Tornado studies).
- Could not retrieve full text of the ACM SAC 2025 wallet-fingerprint paper (403).
- The 2510.09433 cross-chain paper is withdrawn pending correction; per-chain numbers unavailable.

## KQ2. Metadata leaks outside the chain (RPC, wallet telemetry, relayers, explorers, frontends, WalletConnect)

### Takeaway
The dominant wallet (MetaMask) routes through Infura by default and, since Nov 2022, ConsenSys's policy states Infura collects IP address and wallet address on transaction requests; Vitalik's April 2025 roadmap explicitly lists "privacy of RPC reads" and network-level anonymity as unsolved, with only TEE-based RPC as a short-term fix and PIR "not yet efficient enough". L2BEAT's threat model for the newest pool (Privacy Pools v2) says the default Alchemy RPC can time the sign-in account against withdrawals and that the single operator can cross-correlate ASP, relayer and RPC logs. No confirmed public case of an RPC-log subpoena was found.

### Cited Findings
- 23–24 Nov 2022: ConsenSys privacy-policy update stated that when a MetaMask user uses Infura as default RPC, "it will collect your IP address and your Ethereum wallet address whenever you make a transaction"; read-only requests were described as not storing wallet addresses. MetaMask's co-founder reportedly said MetaMask cannot stop logging IPs entirely because any RPC service will see the IP. — [The Block, Nov 2022](https://www.theblock.co/post/189717/consensys-says-it-collects-ip-addresses-of-metamask-users-via-infura); [CoinDesk, Dec 2022](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2022/12/06/consensys-to-update-metamask-crypto-wallet-in-response-to-privacy-backlash)
- An Infura privacy statement (undated, per search result) says IP and wallet address relating to a transaction "are not stored together or in a way that allows our systems to associate those two pieces of data" — this conflicts with the 2022 reporting; current practice unverified. — [web3wagmi Infura guide](https://blog.web3wagmi.com/infura-guide)
- Aug 2022: Infura and Alchemy stopped serving RPC requests to Tornado Cash contracts after OFAC designation, meaning MetaMask under default settings could not reach Tornado; users had to switch RPC endpoints. — [Cointelegraph, Aug 2022](https://cointelegraph.com/news/alchemy-and-infura-block-access-to-tornado-cash-as-vitalik-buterin-weighs-in-on-debate)
- Vitalik, "A maximally simple L1 privacy roadmap" (Apr 2025): names four areas including "privacy of RPC reads" and "network-level anonymization"; proposes TEE-based RPC (Automata cited, "needs verification and hardening") as short-term; PIR "not yet efficient enough for large datasets"; a TEE+PIR hybrid for 2^20-sized state regions; wallets using multiple RPC nodes, ideally one per dapp, optionally via mixnet. — [Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459)
- In the same thread, SCBuergel argues the RPC focus is too narrow: wallets also leak to gas-price services, price feeds, transaction simulation, scam-check services, gasless-tx services, cross-chain balance services, multisig signing, indexers and token-icon fetches, and calls for a general-purpose anonymous transport layer. kdenhartog warns per-app addresses can recreate third-party-cookie-style cross-origin tracking unless send-to-self is private by default. — [Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459)
- L2BEAT Privacy Pools v2 "network observer" risk: "The default Alchemy RPC sees the account at sign-in and can time it against withdrawals. Privacy requires a local node and an IP-hiding route for all traffic." "Privileged insider" risk: the relayer sees recipients and IPs; "0xbow can pair sessions across its ASP, relayer, and Alchemy logs." — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)
- Tornado relayer trust: relayers pay gas so the fresh recipient never needs ETH, but "if a relayer were to act maliciously, they could potentially disclose information about the user." — [Bitquery Tornado explainer](https://bitquery.io/blog/tornado-cash)
- Zcash light clients: ZIP 307 requires streaming 580 bytes per output to the client; wallets have historically trial-decrypted every shielded output and the detection server learns which client is syncing; proposals (PIR, "Oblivious Synchronization" in Tachyon) aim to let a light client query a server "without the server knowing which account or funds you are querying". — [ZIP 307](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0307); [ZecSec](https://zecsec.com/posts/making-zcash-light-wallets-faster-and-more-private/); [ChainCatcher Zcash core team dialogue](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2301230)
- The 2023 FinCEN proposed CVC-mixing rule (withdrawn Oct 2026) would have required covered institutions to report wallet identifiers, transaction identifiers and IP addresses for mixing transactions, showing regulators view IP metadata as a key linkage. — [The Block, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-05-fincen-drops-crypto-mixing-rule-self-hosted-wallet-proposal-417690)

### Inferences
- Any project whose frontend defaults to a commercial RPC (Alchemy/Infura) hands a timing oracle to a third party; "private RPC" is a hard dependency, not an option.
- A single operator running ASP + relayer + frontend + RPC key (0xbow's current setup) is a complete deanonymization stack if logs are retained or subpoenaed.
- Light-client/PIR/TEE solutions are all pre-production as of 2026; the designer must assume RPC-level leakage exists and mitigate behaviourally (Tor/mixnet, multiple RPCs, local node).

### Gaps
- No confirmed public record of a law-enforcement subpoena to Infura/Alchemy for IP↔address logs; an advocacy site asserts it without evidence. Treat as unverified.
- No current (2026) statement of Infura's actual retention period for IP+address pairs.
- No data on WalletConnect relay logging or block-explorer (Etherscan) query logging practices.

## KQ3. Gas and fee payment (fresh addresses, relayers as trust/legal/censorship point, paymasters, relayer MEV)

### Takeaway
Paying gas from a fresh address is the unsolved "bootstrapping" problem that every privacy system routes around with a relayer/broadcaster/paymaster — which then becomes a trust point (sees recipient + IP), a censorship point (can refuse), a legal point (the operator of a relayer is the party most exposed to §1960 theories), and historically an MEV target (front-runnable fee capture). Vitalik's roadmap proposes FOCIL + EIP-7701 so privacy protocols can work "without relays or public broadcasters", but in the same thread Aztec's engineer asked how gas gets paid in a relayer-free model and the thread "does not answer this."

### Cited Findings
- Early Tornado implementation note: "the relayer is frontrunnable. When relayer submits a transaction someone can see it in tx pool and frontrun it with higher gas price to get the fee and drain relayer funds." Workarounds: bid almost the whole fee into gas, or hardcode a single relayer. Production Tornado bound recipient and relayer fee inside the ZK proof so a copied tx cannot redirect funds. — [kendricktan/tornado-mixer](https://github.com/kendricktan/tornado-mixer); [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/blog/tornado-cash)
- Tornado relayer economics (TORN governance): anyone can become a relayer by staking TORN in the Registry; minimum stake set by governance; fees paid in TORN from the relayer's staked balance into a StakingReward contract and distributed to locked governance token holders; listing amount, registry fee and minimum balance all governance-configurable. — [Tornado governance docs mirror](https://git.hackliberty.org/TornadoCash/tornado-governance/src/commit/dcd946754e454ba065b693321c756107b8c783c4/contracts/v3-relayer-registry/ProposalChanges.md); [staking.md](https://git.hackliberty.org/TornadoCash/docs/src/branch/en/general/token/staking.md)
- Privacy Pools v2 relayer model (L2BEAT, Nov 2026): 0xbow's relayer is the only one offered in the app; others require building your own client; the relayer quotes a fee per request taken from the withdrawn asset; "the relayer can refuse a withdrawal, which forces self-relaying from a public wallet"; the relayer sees each exit's recipient and requesting IP. Only 2 active relayers in 30 days. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)
- Railgun fee model: 0.25% on shield/unshield, free internal transfers; gas paid directly or through a "broadcaster" fee; 30-day protocol fees ~$323K–$356K (Nov 2026 DefiLlama snapshots). — [DefiLlama Railgun](https://defillama.com/protocol/railgun); [Baltex comparison](https://baltex.io/blog/ecosystem/aztec-vs-railgun-privacy-fees-2026-comparison)
- Kohaku (EF wallet SDK): kohaku-eth/railgun v0.0.1-alpha.21 "supports 4337 mempool relaying for private transactions", i.e. using the ERC-4337 alt-mempool/bundlers instead of a bespoke relayer. — [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/ethereum-foundation-s-kohaku-launches-privacy-integration-sdk-for-wallets)
- Stealth addresses: fresh stealth addresses hold no ETH; implementations rely on ERC-4337 paymasters (e.g. Labyrinth lets users spend notes "without ETH or gas tokens" via paymaster, which can take fees in the transferred asset) or on bundling the ETH send with the announcement via AA/EIP-7702; "these approaches are not turnkey yet." ERC-4337 spec: the paymaster still pays gas, so sponsorship shifts cost rather than removing it. — [Openfort stealth address deep dive](https://www.openfort.io/blog/stealth-addresses); [EIP-4337](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4337)
- Vitalik roadmap item 4: FOCIL and EIP-7701 "would let privacy protocols work without relays or public broadcasters and improve censorship resistance"; iAmMichaelConnor (Aztec) "asked how gas gets paid in a relayer-free model. The thread does not answer this." — [Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459)
- Legal exposure of relayer operators: in Storm, DOJ proceeded under §1960(b)(1)(C) (transmitting funds known to be criminal), and Galeotti (Aug 2025) said new (b)(1)(C) charges against developers of "truly decentralized, non-custodial software that automates peer-to-peer transfers" will not be approved, but "retaining control, even in limited ways (front ends, upgrades, oracles, or other gateways), raises risk." A fee-earning relayer is an operated service, not pure code. — [Alston & Bird, Sept 2025](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/doj-illicit-finance-enforcement-defi)

### Inferences
- Every current design has a "gas oracle": someone who learns (recipient, IP, time) in exchange for paying gas. Decentralising relayers (Tornado's registry) created a token-staking dependency; centralising them (0xbow) creates a single censorship/log point.
- 4337 bundlers are a partial improvement (permissionless, many operators) but bundlers also see the UserOp and sender IP; the alt-mempool is not an anonymity network.
- Designer options: (a) in-protocol fee deduction with permissionless broadcaster set and proof-bound recipient (Railgun/Tornado pattern); (b) 4337 paymaster paid in the shielded asset; (c) protocol-level FOCIL/7701 (not shipped as of Nov 2026).

### Gaps
- No empirical 2025–2026 data on relayer front-running or relayer censorship incidents in production Railgun/Privacy Pools.
- No source on how Railgun broadcaster fees are split or whether broadcasters are under-provisioned.
- No shipped FOCIL/EIP-7701 as of Nov 2026 (status of inclusion in Glamsterdam/Hegotá not confirmed in sources retrieved).

## KQ4. Censorship points (frontends, RPC blacklisting, builder/relay censorship, stablecoin freezes, exchange "taint" freezing)

### Takeaway
The August 2022 Tornado sanction demonstrated every off-chain chokepoint at once: GitHub removed the repo and contributor accounts, Infura/Alchemy refused RPC to the contracts, Circle froze USDC in the pool, and dYdX/OKX blocked accounts (including users with only indirect exposure). On-chain, OFAC-filtering MEV-Boost relays delivered ~15.5% of payloads on 2 Nov 2026 (down from 51–63% in late 2022), so block-level censorship is now a delay rather than a block, but the issuer-freeze vector has grown: in the Oct 2026 Binance hack Tether/Circle/Ripple froze ~$1.8–3B within days. For a privacy project, stablecoin freezes and exchange taint-scoring are the most consequential and least controllable censorship points.

### Cited Findings
- 8 Aug 2022 OFAC designation listed 44 Tornado smart-contract addresses; GitHub took down the Tornado organization and contributors' personal accounts; Infura and Alchemy stopped answering RPC requests to Tornado; Circle froze USDC; the frontend survived on IPFS and users pointed MetaMask at alternative RPCs. — [Cointelegraph, Aug 2022](https://cointelegraph.com/news/alchemy-and-infura-block-access-to-tornado-cash-as-vitalik-buterin-weighs-in-on-debate); [Forklog explainer](https://forklog.com/en/what-is-the-tornado-cash-mixer-and-why-was-it-sanctioned/); [dev.to IPFS guide](https://dev.to/0xalita/how-to-use-tornado-cash-ipfs-with-working-rpc-nodes-to-withdraw-ethbnbdaimatic-5fhh)
- Circle froze >75,000 USDC across sanctioned addresses on 8 Aug 2022 (sources disagree: 44 vs 81 addresses), including the Tornado USDC pool itself, meaning ordinary depositors could not withdraw. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/162172/circle-freezes-usdc-funds-in-tornado-cashs-us-treasury-sanctioned-wallets); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/circle-blacklists-all-tornado-cash-eth-addresses-effectively-freezing-usdc/)
- dYdX (10 Aug 2022): blocked accounts holding Tornado-linked funds based on a compliance vendor's flags; "This sudden influx of flags affected many account holders that never directly engaged with Tornado Cash"; later unblocked some. — [Cointelegraph, Aug 2022](https://cointelegraph.com/news/dydx-confirms-blocking-and-unblocking-some-accounts-linked-to-tornado-cash)
- OKX (2022): any deposit from or withdrawal to Tornado would lead to account termination; users feared "dusting" attacks where a third party sends Tornado-tainted funds to get an account banned. — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/19665)
- Binance returned frozen BTC to a user only after the user "promised" not to use CoinJoin (Wasabi) again (reported 2019/2020). — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-returns-frozen-btc-after-user-promises-not-to-use-coinjoin)
- Builder/relay censorship: shortly after the Merge (Oct–Nov 2022), 51% then 63% of blocks were reported OFAC-compliant; Flashbots relay produced 19,436 consecutive post-Merge blocks that all excluded Tornado txs. — [Cointelegraph, 2022](https://cointelegraph.com/news/51-of-ethereum-blocks-are-now-compliant-with-ofac-standards-raising-censorship-concerns); [The Block, 2022](https://www.theblock.co/post/180158/63-of-ethereum-transaction-blocks-are-now-ofac-compliant); [CoinDesk, Oct 2022](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2022/10/05/whats-going-on-with-ethereums-mev-boost)
- mevwatch.info snapshot 2 Nov 2026: OFAC-filtering relays delivered ~15.5% of MEV-Boost payloads in last 24h (2,807 of 18,052; bloXroute Regulated 13.8%, Flashbots 1.8%); "down 20% over the last 30 days"; 30-day trough 12.5% on 30 Oct 2026. Methodology caveats: payload stats from relayscan.io; a block can count for multiple relays; censorship classification is editorial. A separate 1 Nov 2026 snapshot read 16.2%. — [mevwatch.info](https://www.mevwatch.info/)
- Builder concentration: Titan and Quasar reportedly produced 73% of MEV-Boost blocks (Oct 2026, citing stake.fish) — source is low-reliability (shattered.io); flagged, not relied upon. — [shattered.io](https://shattered.io/titan-quasar-73-percent-mev-boost-blocks-builder-concentration-2026/)
- Oct 2026 Binance hack ($6.3–6.4B): Binance said $2.54B frozen; Tether/Circle/Ripple reportedly froze ~$3B (another report: $1.79B USDT+USDC); Elliptic reported the attackers used CoinJoin for the first time (~28 BTC). Figures are preliminary and conflicting. — [Yahoo/Reuters](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/binance-says-2-54-billion-005100392.html); [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/technology/binance-freezes-assets-security-breach-october-30-2026/); [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2026/10/29/is-binance-being-hacked-billions-leave-hot-wallets-as-tether-and-circle-freeze-funds/); [CryptoTimes, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/11/02/binance-hackers-use-coinjoin-for-first-time-2-85b-funds-frozen/)
- Chainalysis 2026: stablecoins were ~84% of all flows to illicit addresses in 2025 — i.e. the asset class privacy users most want to shield is the one issuers can freeze. — [Chainalysis 2026 intro](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2026-crypto-crime-report-introduction/)
- Privacy Pools v2 ragequit (the only exit if the ASP rejects a deposit) "publishes the owner, amount, label, and commitment, which can link the note to its creating transaction"; the multisig can also block ragequit by lowering tree capacity. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)
- Vitalik roadmap: FOCIL (fork-choice enforced inclusion lists) is the proposed L1 fix for builder censorship. — [Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459)

### Inferences
- Block-level censorship has fallen from a majority to a minority of relays, so inclusion is delayed by a slot or two, not blocked; the live threats are (1) freezable assets inside the pool and (2) exchange off-ramp scoring.
- "Taint" scoring is vendor-driven and hits indirect exposure (dYdX case); a privacy pool that mixes with any illicit deposit poisons all withdrawers at the exchange layer. This is the economic rationale for ASP-style screening, at the cost of a centralised gatekeeper.
- Holding USDC/USDT inside a pool contract creates a single freeze target (Circle froze the Tornado USDC pool); designs should either avoid issuer-controlled assets or accept that risk explicitly.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025–2026 user reports of Coinbase/Kraken freezing accounts specifically for Railgun or Privacy Pools exposure; only 2022 Tornado-era incidents (dYdX, OKX) and the Binance/CoinJoin incident were found.
- No authoritative 2026 stat on what share of block builders (as opposed to relays) filter OFAC addresses.
- No source confirming whether the USDC frozen in the Tornado pool in 2022 was released after the March 2025 delisting.

## KQ5. Legal and regulatory (Storm, Samourai, Blanche memo, DOJ DeFi outline, CLARITY/BRCA, FinCEN, OFAC, EU AMLR, FATF, UK/Japan) and design choices that reduce developer risk

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026 the US legal position is: (1) Storm stands convicted (Aug 2025) of §1960 conspiracy with a Rule 29 acquittal motion pending since the April 2026 hearing and a retrial on laundering/sanctions counts set for 26 Apr 2027; (2) Samourai's founders are serving 5 and 4 years after July 2025 guilty pleas; (3) DOJ policy (Blanche memo Apr 2025, Galeotti Aug 2025) says "writing code without ill intent is not a crime" and that §1960(b)(1)(C) charges against "truly decentralized, non-custodial" developers "will not be approved" — but this is prosecutorial discretion, not law; (4) the statutory fix (CLARITY Act §604 / BRCA) failed Senate cloture 49–50 on 15 Sep 2026; (5) FinCEN withdrew its mixing rule (Oct 2026) and OFAC delisted Tornado (Mar 2025) but Semenov remains sanctioned. The EU AMLR bans CASPs from servicing anonymous accounts/privacy coins from 1 Jul 2027. The consistent design signal from DOJ statements and the BRCA text is: no custody or control of funds, no fee-earning operated service, no upgradeable/admin-keyed contracts, no operated frontend/relayer.

### Cited Findings
**US v. Storm (Tornado Cash)**
- 6 Aug 2025: jury convicted Storm of conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money transmitting business; hung on conspiracy to commit money laundering and conspiracy to violate IEEPA/North Korea sanctions. — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2025/08/06/roman-storm-guilty-of-unlicensed-money-transmitting-conspiracy-in-partial-verdict); [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/storm-jurors-reach-split-verdict-in-criminal-trial/)
- Two months before trial, in line with the Blanche memo, DOJ dropped its §1960(b)(1)(B) (licensing) theory and proceeded only under §1960(b)(1)(C) (transmitting funds known to be derived from crime). — [The Rage](https://www.therage.co/doj-crypto-mixers-memo/)
- Rule 29 motion (filed Sept/Oct 2025; argued ~9 Apr 2026) argues "publishing software is not the same thing as transmitting money"; DOJ relies on the Bitcoin Fog (Sterlingov) appellate decision for venue; Judge Failla had not ruled as of early Oct 2026 and pushed the retrial to 26 Apr 2027 (pretrial 5 Feb–20 Apr 2027). ### Inferences (design choices that reduce developer legal risk)
- The through-line of Blanche, Galeotti, BRCA §604 and the Storm/Samourai distinction is control and custody: immutable contracts, no admin/upgrade keys, no pauser, no operated relayer, no fee flowing to developers, no hosted frontend, no coordinator server. Privacy Pools v2 currently violates several of these (2/4 Safe with no-delay upgrade/pause, single operator relayer, hosted site, 20% yield cut) per L2BEAT.
- "Knowledge" is the (b)(1)(C) trigger; operating any component that gives developers knowledge of specific illicit flows (ASP screening, relayer logs, support channels) converts a code publisher into someone who "learned of illicit activity"; Galeotti says doing nothing then invites scrutiny. Either do not operate it, or operate it with documented screening.
- DOJ policy can be reversed by the next administration; the statutory safe harbor did not pass; the Storm precedent (conviction on (b)(1)(C) despite no custody) still stands pending Rule 29. Design for the worst case, not the memo.
- EU 2027 rules bite at the CASP interface, so an EU user's problem is on/off-ramping, not using the protocol; UK is scrutiny not ban; Japan is a ban at exchanges.

## KQ6. UX problems (proving time, note management/backup, sync/scanning, mobile, wallet support, DeFi composability, ERC-20/NFT privacy, "privacy is an app not a default", cross-chain leaks)

### Takeaway
The UX failure that research most consistently identifies is "privacy as a separate app": Zcash shielded usage sat at ~6–8% until wallets made shielded the default, after which it rose to 30–59% (2025–2026). The hard engineering problems underneath are note discovery/scanning (linear in chain activity; Aztec projected 1.2B notes/year), per-app address isolation conflicting with composable DeFi (raised but unresolved in Vitalik's thread), nullifier-key rotation, and the fact that every fresh address needs gas. The EF's answer in 2026 is Kohaku, an SDK to put Railgun/Privacy Pools inside existing wallets, still alpha.

### Cited Findings
- Vitalik (Apr 2025): privacy tools should live inside existing wallets with a "shielded balance" and "send from shielded balance" option "ideally on by default"; users "should not need a separate privacy wallet"; one-address-per-app by default "brings real convenience costs"; private send-to-self must be default or per-app addresses are "unworkable". — [Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459)
- Unresolved in that thread: nullifier keypair rotation is hard (nullifiers derive from the old key; detaching ownership risks double-spends; needs constraint-heavy proofs); complex DeFi flows spanning many contracts may need one shared address, so per-"DeFi session" addresses were proposed; privacy wallets may need separate snark-friendly secrets; funding new wallets per app causes drop-off (mpeyfuss). — [Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459)
- Zcash: a 2021 usability paper found only ~6% of transactions shielded and attributed it to poor tooling; shielded share rose to 18% (Oct 2025), >30% of supply (May 2026), 59.3% of transactions (Feb 2026) after Zashi/Zodl defaulted to shielded via Unified Addresses; public tx counts stayed flat (~8,500/day). CoinDesk field test: default shielding works with latency "within seconds to under a minute". — [arXiv 2105.02793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02793); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/why-30-of-zcash-supply-is-now-in-the-shielded-pool/); [CoinDesk Research](https://www.coindesk.com/research/inside-zcash-encrypted-money-at-planetary-scale)
- Note discovery: Zcash wallets trial-decrypt every shielded output; cost "grows linearly with network activity"; scanning nullifiers since last sync is linear in the gap; light clients must download 580 bytes/output (ZIP 307). Aztec's RFP: trial decryption "becomes increasingly less feasible as note volume grows", est. ~1.2B notes/year at target throughput; alternatives are delegated trial decryption and tag hopping. Proposed fixes (PIR, Tachyon "Oblivious Synchronization", indexed Merkle trees for O(log n) spent checks) are proposals, not shipped. — [Aztec note discovery RFP](https://forum.aztec.network/t/request-for-proposals-note-discovery-protocol/2584); [Aztec fast note decrypting](https://forum.aztec.network/t/fast-note-decrypting/5417); [ZIP 307](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0307); [Zcash forum accumulator thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/accumulator-for-zcash-ztreexo/57011); [CoinDesk on Tachyon](https://www.coindesk.com/research/building-the-zcash-machine-tachyon-and-quantum-readiness)
- Stealth addresses (ERC-5564): recipient must scan many announcements (compute, sync time, bandwidth); the 1-byte view tag cuts scanning ~6x at a cost of 128→124-bit security margin (single blog source); spam-triggered scans are a known issue; fresh addresses have no gas. — [Openfort](https://www.openfort.io/blog/stealth-addresses); [BingX on Feb 2026 ERC-5564 rollout post](https://bingx.com/en/news/post/ethereum-to-roll-out-erc-stealth-addresses-outlined-feb)
- Kohaku (EF): SDK released 25 May 2026 to let any wallet integrate Railgun, Tornado Cash and Privacy Pools; Railgun module alpha with 4337 relaying; Privacy Pools and Tornado integrations "in development"/alpha; stealth-address modules listed as future; Ambire reportedly integrated Railgun via Kohaku (Sept 2026). — [DexTools](https://www.dextools.io/news/ethereum-foundation-kohaku-sdk-privacy-wallets-2026); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/ethereum-foundation-s-kohaku-launches-privacy-integration-sdk-for-wallets); [Blockworks 0xResearch](https://blockworks.com/newsletter/0xresearch/issue/post_b6d8cfdd-c353-4548-8b36-635e9b795f12)
- Composability workaround: Railgun's atomic unshield-swap-reshield via 0x integration; Privacy Pools v2 withdraw-and-swap via LI.FI with route bound into the proof. — [Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459); [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)
- Privacy Pools v2 supports only ETH, USDC, USDT (plus Aave yield wrappers) on mainnet — no arbitrary ERC-20s or NFTs. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)
- Privacy Pools v2 is deployed on Ethereum, BNB Chain, Citrea, Optimism, Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood Chain as separate pools (L2BEAT tracks only Ethereum), i.e. no shared cross-chain anonymity set. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)
- Reputation conflict: MacBudkowski argues public activity builds on-chain reputation that defends social apps against bots, so full privacy weakens it; proposes UX asking users whether to make actions public. — [Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459)

### Inferences
- The strongest empirical evidence that defaults, not cryptography, drive adoption is Zcash's 2025–2026 jump after wallet-level default shielding.
- Note backup is a distinct unsolved UX problem: shielded balances live in encrypted notes that must be rediscovered by scanning; losing the viewing/scanning key or server means a costly resync. (Inference from ZIP 307/Aztec sources; no direct "note backup failure" statistic found.)
- Cross-chain bridging of shielded funds necessarily unshields at the bridge, and each chain has its own small pool; cross-chain privacy is effectively unsolved.

- No concrete 2026 benchmark figures for client-side proving time on mobile for Railgun, Privacy Pools or Aztec were found.
- No usage statistics for Kohaku-integrated wallets.
- No source quantifying user drop-off from "fund a fresh address first" flows in privacy apps specifically.
- No sourced evidence on NFT privacy solutions or their absence beyond the Privacy Pools asset list.

## KQ7. Economic problems (revenue, token incentives, relayer provisioning, liquidity bootstrapping, public-good framing, ASP business model)

### Takeaway
Only Railgun shows meaningful protocol revenue (~$4M/yr run-rate, declining from ~$1.4M/quarter in Q1 2025 to ~$0.9M in Q3 2026), funded by a 0.25% shield/unshield fee. Tornado's TORN-staked relayer registry suffered a $2.17M governance attack (May 2023) and TORN now has ~$32M market cap. Privacy Pools v2 earns from a 20% cut of Aave yield on wrapped deposits and reserves the right to charge a vetting fee of up to 99.99%; the ASP itself has no published fee. Pools are chronically thin (Privacy Pools $510K TVL; 2 active relayers), illustrating the under-provisioning and liquidity-bootstrapping problems.

### Cited Findings
- Railgun (DefiLlama, 2 Nov 2026): TVL ~$110M; 30-day fees ~$323K (all booked as revenue); trailing-year pace ~$4.1M; quarterly gross revenue fell from ~$1.4M (Q1 2025) to ~$880K (Q3 2026 to date); holders' revenue share raised from 2% to 4.2% of treasury after 29 Jul 2026, distributed fortnightly; fee 0.25% in/out, free internal. — [DefiLlama Railgun](https://defillama.com/protocol/railgun)

- 0xbow / Privacy Pools: $3.5M seed (Starbloom Capital, Nov 2025); "operates without access to user funds or transaction data"; $6M volume / 1,500 users at that date. — [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/0xbow-closes-35m-round-compliant-crypto-privacy-technology-following)

### Inferences
- A fee on shield/unshield (Railgun) is the only proven revenue model, and it is pro-cyclical and shrinking; it also creates the "fees to developers" fact pattern that DOJ control analysis treats as risk-raising.
- Yield-on-deposits (Privacy Pools v2 via Aave) is an attempt to pay users to stay in the pool (enlarging the set) while funding the operator — but it ties the pool to a freezable, upgradeable DeFi dependency and adds "yield-share count" fingerprints.
- Token-staked relayer registries (Tornado) proved attackable via governance and tie relayer liquidity to a volatile token.
- ASP-as-business (screening fee) has no public evidence of revenue; the model is pre-product.

## KQ8. Social / reputational ("mixer = criminal" stigma, illicit share, Lazarus usage)

### Takeaway
The quantitative stigma case rests on 2022–2023 Chainalysis data (illicit inflows to mixers $1.0B in 2022 → $504M in 2023; ~1/3 of YoMix inflows from Lazarus-linked hacks) and on the Tornado/Blender/Sinbad sanctions. Chainalysis's 2026 report does not break out a mixer share at all; TRM reportedly found mixer activity down 37% and bridge laundering up 66% in 2025 (secondhand). Illicit activity is <1% of all crypto volume, and stablecoins (not mixers) carry 84% of illicit flows; the regulator's own 2026 reversal (FinCEN citing lawful privacy use) suggests the narrative is shifting, but exchange taint-scoring still operationalises the stigma.

### Cited Findings

## KQ9. Emerging threats (AI-assisted chain analysis; quantum threats to Groth16/KZG/BN254 and stealth ECDH; harvest-now-decrypt-later)

### Takeaway
Chainalysis publicly says it does not use ML to decide wallet control (Aug 2026) and launched "blockchain intelligence agents" (Mar 2026) for automation, so the near-term AI threat is cheaper, broader application of existing heuristics rather than new linkage power. The quantum threat is twofold and often conflated: (a) soundness — a CRQC breaks pairing-based Groth16/KZG on BN254/BLS12-381 (forged proofs); (b) confidentiality — HNDL against ECDH-based stealth addresses and published viewing keys. L2BEAT already rates Privacy Pools v2 "bad" on the future-adversary axis: "everything is exposed... if elliptic-curve cryptography is broken." EF's PQ roadmap targets ~2029 for L1 with STARK-based migration and no dated completion.

### Inferences
- For privacy specifically, HNDL matters more than soundness: an adversary who archives today's chain (encrypted notes, stealth-address ephemeral pubkeys, published viewing keys) can retroactively link everything once ECDLP falls; this cannot be patched later. Hash-based commitments and symmetric/PQ-KEM note encryption would be needed now, at a cost in proof size and gas.
- Groth16's trusted setup (Privacy Pools: 61/597 contributions; Tornado similar) is a separate, non-quantum soundness risk that STARKs also remove.
- AI's realistic effect is to lower the cost of running the Tutela/FIFO/wallet-fingerprint heuristics at scale and across chains, raising the baseline deanonymization rate from the ~5–45% measured in academic studies.
