## Key Question 1: Exactly how do deposits, withdrawals, relayers, and the anonymity set work, and where do users leak identity in practice?

### Takeaway
Tornado Cash Classic is a set of immutable, fixed-denomination pools: a deposit inserts a Pedersen-hash commitment of (nullifier, secret) into a height-20 MiMC Merkle tree; a withdrawal is a Groth16 proof of membership plus a nullifier hash, with recipient/relayer/fee bound into the proof, so the contract pays out to any address without an on-chain link. The anonymity set is formally "every unspent deposit in that pool," but in practice users leak identity through address reuse, deposit/withdrawal timing (FIFO), multi-denomination portfolio matching, gas-price fingerprints, relayer/RPC metadata, and compromised frontends.

### Cited Findings

**Protocol design (primary source: whitepaper v1.4, 17 Dec 2019, authors Pertsev, Semenov, Storm)**
- Deposit: user "Generate[s] two random numbers k, r ∈ B^248 and computes C = H1(k||r)" where H1 is a Pedersen hash; the contract adds C as a leaf to "a Merkle tree of height 20, where each non-leaf node hashes its 2 children with H2," H2 being MiMC in Feistel/sponge mode. k is called the nullifier, r the randomness/secret. — [Tornado Cash Privacy Solution v1.4 whitepaper (PDF mirror)](https://berkeley-defi.github.io/assets/material/Tornado%20Cash%20Whitepaper.pdf)
- Withdrawal statement proved in zero knowledge, with public values (R, h, A, f, t): "I KNOW k, r ∈ B^248, l ∈ B^16, O ∈ Zp^16 SUCH THAT h = H1(k) AND O is the opening of H2(k||r) at position l to R," where R = Merkle root, h = nullifier hash, A = recipient, f = fee, t = relayer address; "A and f are included into the context of the statement" (i.e., bound into the proof so it cannot be front-run/redirected). Proof system: ZK-SNARK [Gro16] (Groth16) with a trusted-setup keypair. — [whitepaper](https://berkeley-defi.github.io/assets/material/Tornado%20Cash%20Whitepaper.pdf)
- Contract behaviour: "stores the last n = 100 root values in the history array"; on withdrawal "verifies the alleged proof P against the submitted public values (R, h, A, f, t)... releases (N − f) ETH to address A and fee f ETH to the Relayer address t"; double-spend prevented "by checking that the nullifier hash from the proof has not appeared before and if so, adds it to the list of nullifier hashes." — [whitepaper](https://berkeley-defi.github.io/assets/material/Tornado%20Cash%20Whitepaper.pdf)
- Relayer model as specified: "The N ETH is withdrawn through a Relayer with f Ether sent as a fee to the Relayer address t and (N − f) to the designated recipient. The value f and t is chosen by the sender... the withdraw transaction is initiated by the Relayer and it pays the Gas fee that is supposed to be covered by f." Alternative: recipient self-withdraws with f = 0 but must already hold ETH for gas. — [whitepaper](https://berkeley-defi.github.io/assets/material/Tornado%20Cash%20Whitepaper.pdf)
- Security claims include: "No coin can be withdrawn twice"; "The proof is binding: one can not use the same proof with a different nullifier hash, another recipient address, or a new fee amount"; primitives have "at least 126-bit security (except for the BN254 curve where the discrete logarithm problem has something like 100-bit security)"; and the anonymity statement: "For each withdrawal every deposit since the last moment when the contract has zero Ether till the formation of the root in the proof can be a potential coin, though some coins are more likely to be withdrawn depending on the user behaviour." — [whitepaper](https://berkeley-defi.github.io/assets/material/Tornado%20Cash%20Whitepaper.pdf)
- Implementation: circuits in circom/circomlib (iden3), proofs via SnarkJS / websnark; tornado-core README lists deposit gas 1,088,354 (43,381 + 50,859 × tree_depth), withdraw gas 301,233, 28,271 circuit constraints, ~10.2 s proof time; audited by ABDK Consulting ("no critical issues"). — [tornado-core README (GitHub)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tornadocash/tornado-core/master/README.md)
- Trusted setup: Groth16 (websnark), circuit-specific Phase 2 on top of Perpetual Powers of Tau; Phase 1 had 30 contributions, Phase 2 had 1,114 contributions over 10 days, completed May 2020; pools immutable since 2020-05-18 ("No one can pause, upgrade, or block them"). L2BEAT rates the trusted-setup risk "yellow (medium)". — [L2BEAT Tornado Cash privacy review](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- Fixed-denomination pools (Ethereum mainnet, per L2BEAT): ETH 0.1 / 1 / 10 / 100; DAI 100 / 1k / 10k / 100k; WBTC 0.1 / 1 / 10; USDT 100 / 1k; USDC 100 / 1k; cDAI 5k / 50k / 500k / 5M. Pools charge no protocol fee; relayers take their fee from the withdrawal. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- Historic multi-chain deployments: ETH, DAI, cDAI, USDC, USDT, WBTC on Ethereum; BNB on BSC; MATIC on Polygon; xDAI on Gnosis; AVAX on Avalanche; ETH on Optimism and Arbitrum. — [Tornado Cash intro (dev.to, 2022)](https://dev.to/tornadocash/introduction-to-tornado-cash-3hhe)
- Fifth Circuit's description of the architecture (useful because it is a court's findings of fact): developers "initially retained the ability to update the pool smart contracts' codes"; after the 2020 ceremony "at least twenty smart contracts—including the pool smart contracts—became irreversibly immutable." Relayers "never have custody over users' Ether, as the smart contract ensures that withdrawn Ether are only ever sent to the user's withdrawal account." A separate mutable relayer-registry contract "collects a fee from the relayer and pays it to the TORN token holders who have locked their TORN into the mutable governance smart contract." "The use of relayers—and the mutable relayer-registry smart contract—is entirely optional." OFAC's designation covered "37 Tornado Cash smart contracts (including at least twenty immutable smart contracts)." — [Van Loon v. Dep't of the Treasury, No. 23-50669 (5th Cir. Nov. 26, 2024)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)
- Relayer registry (introduced by governance proposal 10, deployed 2023-06-04 per L2BEAT): anyone can relay by staking TORN; docs cite a 300 TORN minimum to be listed on the UI, with a later doc giving a 40 TORN minimum locked balance to stay "active"; registry fee described as 0.3% of relayer fee, burned from the relayer's stake. (Docs mirror reachable only via search snippet.) — [Tornado docs mirror, staking/relayer pages (snippet)](https://git.hackliberty.org/TornadoCash/docs/raw/branch/en/general/token/staking.md); [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- Government's figure on relayer usage: the indictment alleges "98% of users used the optional relayer network," which the co-founders "manually whitelisted until March 2022." — [The Block, Apr 2024 (DOJ opposition to motion to dismiss)](https://www.theblock.co/post/291286/doj-says-roman-semenov-did-much-more-than-just-write-code-in-response-to-defenses-dismissal-motion)
- Compliance tool ("proof of origin"): user feeds the deposit note into the tool, which "generates a PDF report showing the source of their tokens" and "can selectively reverse the unlinking for third parties who need traceability." If the note is spent, the report includes the withdrawal tx hash and destination. It is optional and user-initiated; nothing is possible without the note. — [Coin Center, "How does Tornado Cash actually work?"](https://coincenter.org/how-does-tornado-cash-actually-work/); [L2BEAT compliance section](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- Anonymity mining / TORN: until December 2021, deposits in ETH, DAI, cDAI and WBTC pools earned TORN ("anonymity mining"); TORN holders govern parameters. Governance (per L2BEAT): proposal threshold 1,000 TORN, quorum 100,000 TORN, 5-day vote, then timelock (sources on the page conflict: 2 days vs 7 days). DAO-controlled components: RelayerRegistry, FeeManager, InstanceRegistry, TornadoTreesV2, staking rewards, TORN token, router pool list, and the app's IPFS hash. — [dev.to intro](https://dev.to/tornadocash/introduction-to-tornado-cash-3hhe); [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- Fifth Circuit on TORN/DAO: "There are currently 1.5 million TORN tokens in circulation... The DAO can only vote to implement new projects and change certain optional Tornado Cash features. It cannot vote on or make any changes to immutable smart contracts, such as the pool smart contracts." — [Van Loon opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)

**Tornado Nova (arbitrary amounts, shielded transfers)**
- Nova launched as a beta in December 2021 on Gnosis Chain (then xDai) "allowing both arbitrary amounts & shielded transfers"; "shielded transfers can be sent to any chosen address as long as this address is also registered in Tornado Cash Nova"; ETH bridged from mainnet via Omnibridge. — [Tornado Cash Medium (snippet; page returned 403)](https://tornado-cash.medium.com/8df92d93c37c); [dev.to intro](https://dev.to/tornadocash/introduction-to-tornado-cash-3hhe)
- Repository README ("Tornado Pool"): "experimental version that supports depositing arbitrary amounts and making internal (shielded) transfers"; runs on L2 (xDai) with an ETH→WETH bridge; "Contracts can be upgraded by Tornado Cash governance" (i.e., NOT immutable, unlike Classic pools); beta deposit cap 1 ETH; L1 withdrawals must exceed 0.05 ETH to prevent bridge spam; audited by Igor Gulamov (Zeropool). — [tornado-nova README (GitHub)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tornadocash/tornado-nova/master/README.md)
- Beta-era docs: deposits "limited to 1 ETH/transaction", withdrawals in "four predetermined amounts (0.1, 0.3, 0.5 & 1 ETH)", and Nova "has a low relayer count, is in an experimental state and regularly faces issues with Omnibridge because of bridge limits and validator censorship." — [Tornado docs mirror (snippet)](https://git.hackliberty.org/TornadoCash/docs/blame/commit/d9f672f7dea5a8de7d4e7455b324b0bc372096ee/tornado-cash-nova/fund-and-withdraw-on-nova.md)
- Usage: in early 2022 Nova held ~32% of Ethereum-ecosystem privacy-protocol transaction share (Aztec 33%, Umbra 21%); after the August 2022 sanctions, daily transactions fell 97% on Tornado Classic and 91% on Nova. — [Cambridge CCAF, Feb 2026](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2026/crypto-privacy-after-sanctions-the-return-of-coin-mixers/)

**Frontend, hosting, and operational leaks**
- Frontend is pinned at the ENS name tornadocash.eth (IPFS hash as of L2BEAT snapshot: bafybeigduniojgqrz7e4vntsgmovdli3oi7a2pnzzvz2gfxlvj3yqwk75a, source commit 17c54da), reachable via eth.limo or a local IPFS node; builds are reproducible from a named commit. The app downloads per-pool deposit caches, so RPC/Graph services learn which pool but not the note; the Tornado CLI "fee checks bypass Tor and expose the user's IP to the RPC." L2BEAT rates "future adversary" risk as warning: an elliptic-curve break would shrink candidate sets and make on-chain note backups readable. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- January–February 2024 frontend backdoor: malicious JavaScript hidden in a governance proposal (submitted Jan 1, 2024) was live ~2 months on the IPFS-hosted frontend, sending users' deposit notes to an external server (L2BEAT dates it 2024-01-02 to proposal 52's fix). — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/backdoor-discovered-in-ipfs-version-of-tornado-cash/); [CoinDesk, Feb 26 2024](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/business/2024/02/26/tornado-cash-reportedly-suffers-backend-exploit-user-deposits-at-risk); [L2BEAT timeline](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- At delisting (Mar 21, 2025) ZachXBT warned "the main frontend is still compromised"; community members circulated hardened IPFS hashes. — [Bitcoin.com, Mar 22 2025](https://news.bitcoin.com/ofac-unshackles-tornado-cash-but-security-hawks-warn-frontend-remains-a-minefield)
- The Fifth Circuit noted gas fees as the inherent leak that relayers exist to fix: "those transactions aren't fully anonymized because they still incur gas fees." — [Van Loon opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because f, t, and A are bound into the SNARK's public inputs, a relayer cannot steal or redirect funds; its only power is to decline to relay (censorship) and to observe the withdrawer's IP/metadata. This is why relayers are simultaneously the privacy-critical component (fresh address needs gas) and the legally exposed one (a fee-earning service operated by identifiable parties).
- The mutable periphery (relayer registry, governance, IPFS hash) is where every documented operational compromise happened (May 2023 governance takeover, Jan 2024 note-leaking frontend, June 2026 fake treasury proposal), not the immutable pools.

## Key Question 2: What are the measured anonymity-set sizes and the empirical rate of linkable deposits/withdrawals?

### Takeaway
Four independent studies converge on the same picture: advertised anonymity sets are overstated, and simple on-chain heuristics (address reuse, linked addresses, portfolio matching, timing) link roughly 10–35% of withdrawals to deposits and shrink effective anonymity sets by ~37% on average, with small-denomination pools (0.1 ETH) worst affected (up to ~49–60% matchable with FIFO timing).

### Cited Findings
- **Tutela (Wu et al., arXiv 2201.06811, Jan 2022):** five Tornado Cash heuristics: (1) address match (same address deposits and withdraws from a pool), (2) unique gas price (pre-EIP-1559), (3) linked ETH addresses (deposit and withdrawal addresses with ≥3 non-Tornado transactions between them), (4) multi-denomination portfolio match within 24-hour windows, (5) TORN anonymity-mining points revealing the number of blocks a deposit sat in the pool. Dataset: ~97,365 deposits and 83,782 withdrawals through Oct 1, 2021. Results: ~42.8K of 97.3K deposits "potentially compromised" (address match 18.6K; linked addresses 18.9K; multi-denomination 16.2K; gas price 102; TORN mining 358); "the anonymity set shrinks by 37% (±15%) across pools," with cDAI and WBTC pools most compromised and USDC least. Authors concede false positives. — [Tutela paper (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.06811); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.06811)
- **Wang, Chaliasos et al., "On How Zero-Knowledge Proof Blockchain Mixers Improve, and Worsen User Privacy" (WWW '23; arXiv 2201.09035):** mixers' anonymity-set claims are "mostly inaccurate"; five heuristics combined increase an adversary's probability of linking a withdrawer to the correct depositor by 51.94% on Tornado Cash (Ethereum) and 108.63% on Typhoon.Network (BSC); identified 105 DeFi attackers using ZKP mixers; anonymity mining "does not necessarily improve the quality of a mixer's anonymity set" because it "attracts privacy-ignorant users" who reuse addresses. Reported post-sanction drop: total daily TC deposits decreased by 83% (search snippet, v2). — [arXiv 2201.09035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09035v1); [WWW'23 PDF](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~livshits/papers/pdf/www23.pdf)
- **Béres, Seres, Benczúr, Quintyne-Collins, "Blockchain is Watching You" (arXiv 2005.14051, 2020):** claims "strong heuristics to link the mixing parties" plus time-of-day and gas-price profiling for pairing addresses of the same user. The arXiv abstract page did not expose the numeric results; the full PDF was not fetched. — [arXiv 2005.14051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14051v2)
- **Cristodaro, Kraner, Tessone, "Clustering Deposit and Withdrawal Activity in Tornado Cash: A Cross-Chain Analysis" (arXiv 2510.09433, Oct 2025):** dataset through August 2024: Ethereum 183,508 deposits / 174,583 withdrawals; BSC 232,088 / 224,062; Polygon 17,774 / 17,088. Address reuse + transactional linkage trace 5.1–12.6% of withdrawals; adding FIFO temporal matching adds 15–22 points, reaching 34.7% on Ethereum and Polygon and 21.1% on BSC. Per pool: Ethereum 0.1 ETH pool 10% (address reuse), 12% (transactional), 49% (FIFO) and "over 60%" matchable overall; Polygon 100,000 MATIC 38.7%. Over $2.3 billion in withdrawals linked (Heuristic 1 alone: >$1.1B on Ethereum). Authors call the FIFO heuristic "speculative"; no precision/false-positive rate is measured. — [arXiv 2510.09433 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09433v1)
- **L2BEAT's framing of anonymity sets:** "Each pool's set is the unspent deposits of the same token and amount"; the 30-day metric counts only past deposits; mitigation advice: use large pools, vary timing, wait before exiting, use fresh addresses, withdraw through a relayer. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- **Whale concentration shrinks effective anonymity:** In November 2025, ≥21 PulseX/Richard Heart-linked addresses sent >116,000 ETH (>$393M) into Tornado Cash, roughly a third of then-current deposits; MetaMask's Taylor Monahan: "it appears a few big 'whales' are all dumping into Tornado Cash," which makes it harder for smaller users to hide. — [DL News, Nov 7 2025](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/privacy-protocol-tornado-cash-volumes-hit-record-high/)
- **Depositor profile shift after sanctions (CCAF):** depositors funded by centralized exchanges fell from 14% to under 3%; unlabelled funding sources rose from 76% to 95%; fast depositors (<24h from wallet creation) skew toward profiles the authors associate with illicit use. — [Cambridge CCAF, Feb 24 2026](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2026/crypto-privacy-after-sanctions-the-return-of-coin-mixers/)
- **Chainalysis as relayer:** Chainalysis told the court in a July 2025 filing it had operated "a relayer node" for Tornado Cash "for a period in 2022" and earned fees; prosecutors said there was no evidence Storm knew. (Relevance: a relayer sees withdrawer IP/metadata and timing.) — [The Block, Aug 26 2026](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-08-26-tornado-cash-roman-storm-retrial-april-2027-412761); [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/storm-says-chainalysis-fees-tornado-cash/)

### Inferences
- The heuristic linkage rates (10–35%) are lower bounds on what a chain-analytics firm with off-chain data (exchange KYC, relayer logs, RPC IPs) can achieve; Tutela explicitly notes off-chain data "could enable more powerful de-anonymization attacks."
- Fixed denominations eliminate amount correlation but push users into multi-pool portfolios, which Tutela's multi-denomination heuristic (16.2K deposits) exploits; arbitrary-amount designs (Nova) trade this for amount-correlation risk unless amounts are hidden (Nova hides them in-pool).

### Gaps
- Béres et al.'s exact numeric linkage rates were not retrieved (only abstract).
- No study reports a measured false-positive rate for the heuristics; all authors acknowledge this.
- No Chainalysis/Elliptic/TRM 2025–2026 report with Tornado-specific anonymity-set measurements was found; TRM's 2023 blog gave post-sanction illicit share roughly doubling while total illicit volume fell ~77% (snippet only). — [TRM Labs (snippet)](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/tornado-cash-volume-dramatically-reduced-post-sanctions-but-illicit-actors-are-still-using-the-mixer)

## Key Question 3: What is the precise legal status of Tornado Cash and its developers as of late 2026?

### Takeaway
As of November 2026: the protocol itself is not sanctioned (OFAC delisted it Mar 21, 2025 after the Fifth Circuit held immutable smart contracts are not "property," and a Texas district court permanently enjoined re-sanctioning on Apr 28, 2025); Roman Storm stands convicted (Aug 6, 2025) of one count of conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money-transmitting business (max 5 years), unsentenced, with a Rule 29 acquittal motion pending since Oct 2025 and a retrial on the hung money-laundering and sanctions counts set for April 26, 2027; Alexey Pertsev's Dutch conviction (64 months, May 14, 2024) is on appeal with additional investigation ordered Oct 2025 and no appellate verdict yet; Roman Semenov remains indicted, at large, and on the SDN list.

### Cited Findings

**OFAC sanctions (Aug 8, 2022)**
- OFAC designated Tornado Cash under E.O. 13694 (as amended), citing >$7 billion laundered since 2019, including >$455 million stolen by the Lazarus Group, >$96 million from the June 24, 2022 Harmony Bridge heist and ≥$7.8 million from the Aug 2, 2022 Nomad heist; "Tornado Cash has repeatedly failed to impose effective controls." — [Treasury press release jy0916](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy0916)
- Same day GitHub removed the repositories and suspended developer accounts; Circle froze ~$75,000 USDC; Pertsev was arrested in Amsterdam Aug 10, 2022. — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Cash)
- Aug 23, 2023: Storm and Semenov indicted in SDNY for conspiracy to commit money laundering, sanctions violations (IEEPA), and operating an unlicensed money-transmitting business; Storm arrested in Washington State; OFAC sanctioned Semenov the same day. — [Treasury press release jy1702](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1702); [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/economy/founders-of-crypto-mixer-arrested-sanctioned-after-u-s-cracks-down)

**Van Loon v. Treasury (Fifth Circuit, Nov 26, 2024)**
- Panel: Jones, Willett, Engelhardt; opinion by Judge Willett. Holding: "Tornado Cash's immutable smart contracts (the lines of privacy-enabling software code) are not the 'property' of a foreign national or entity, meaning (1) they cannot be blocked under IEEPA, and (2) OFAC overstepped its congressionally defined authority." "Perhaps Congress will update IEEPA, enacted during the Carter Administration, to target modern technologies like crypto-mixing software." — [Van Loon opinion (PDF)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)
- Reasoning: "property has a plain meaning: It is capable of being owned"; "The immutable smart contracts at issue in this appeal are not property because they are not capable of being owned"; rejecting the district court's vending-machine analogy ("a vending machine has an owner... can unplug, move, or... destroy the vending machine"); "the immutable smart contracts are not property because they are not ownable, not contracts, and not services." Reversed and remanded with instructions to grant partial summary judgment under the APA. — [Van Loon opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)
- The opinion explicitly leaves mutable contracts open and declined Chevron-style deference post-Loper Bright. — [Ballard Spahr via JD Supra](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/fifth-circuit-rejects-ofac-designation-3248351); [Bracewell](https://bracewell.com/resources/an-immutable-force-meets-an-immovable-object-ofacs-crypto-loss-at-the-fifth-circuit)

**Delisting and injunction (March–April 2025)**
- Mar 21, 2025: Treasury "exercised [its] discretion to remove the economic sanctions against Tornado Cash," citing review of "novel legal and policy issues raised by use of financial sanctions" and stating it "will continue to monitor closely any transactions that may benefit malicious cyber actors or the DPRK." — [Treasury press release sb0057](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0057)
- Federal Register notice (scheduled Mar 26, 2025) records removal from the SDN list on Mar 21, 2025. — [Federal Register 2025-05096](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2025-05096.pdf)
- Semenov was NOT delisted; his SDN entry lost the cyber tag but retains the DPRK tag. — [MyToken summary](https://mytoken.io/news/495519.html); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/ofac-sanctions-removal-tornado-cash)
- Apr 28, 2025: Judge Robert Pitman (W.D. Tex.) held the case not moot ("capable of repetition while evading review"; "OFAC does not suggest they will not sanction Tornado Cash again") and entered judgment that OFAC is "permanently enjoined from enforcing" the sanctions on the immutable contracts. — [CoinDesk via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/tornado-cash-t-sanctioned-again-184331930.html); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/us-treasury-ofac-cant-restore-tornado-cash-sanctions); [CourtListener docket entry 106 (W.D. ## Key Question 5: What did Tornado Nova add and why did it see less use?

### Takeaway
Nova (Dec 2021 beta) added arbitrary amounts and in-pool shielded transfers between registered users on Gnosis Chain, but it was upgradeable by governance (not immutable), bridge-dependent, capped at 1 ETH, thinly relayed, and launched eight months before sanctions crushed its activity by 91%; it never recovered share.

### Cited Findings
- Features: "arbitrary amounts & shielded transfers"; "shielded transfers can be sent to any chosen address as long as this address is also registered in Tornado Cash Nova"; runs on Gnosis Chain "as a Layer2 to optimize speed and cost" with ETH bridged via Omnibridge. — [Tornado Cash Medium (snippet)](https://tornado-cash.medium.com/8df92d93c37c); [docs mirror (snippet)](https://git.hackliberty.org/TornadoCash/docs/blame/commit/d9f672f7dea5a8de7d4e7455b324b0bc372096ee/tornado-cash-nova/fund-and-withdraw-on-nova.md)
- Design/ops constraints: contracts "can be upgraded by Tornado Cash governance"; beta deposit cap 1 ETH; L1 withdrawal minimum 0.05 ETH; audit by Zeropool's Igor Gulamov; docs warned of "low relayer count," "experimental state," and recurring Omnibridge problems "because of bridge limits and validator censorship." — [tornado-nova README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tornadocash/tornado-nova/master/README.md); [docs mirror (snippet)](https://git.hackliberty.org/TornadoCash/docs/blame/commit/d9f672f7dea5a8de7d4e7455b324b0bc372096ee/tornado-cash-nova/fund-and-withdraw-on-nova.md)
- Usage: Nova reached ~32% of Ethereum-ecosystem privacy transactions in early 2022 (comparable to Aztec's 33%), then fell 91% in daily transactions after Aug 2022 sanctions; by 2025 Railgun (a shielded-balance design similar in spirit to Nova) held ~70% share while Tornado (Classic) held 25%; Nova is not listed separately in 2025 shares. — [Cambridge CCAF](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2026/crypto-privacy-after-sanctions-the-return-of-coin-mixers/)
- The Fifth Circuit's property holding is explicitly limited to immutable contracts and leaves mutable ones (which Nova's are) open to sanction. — [Van Loon opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf); [Ballard Spahr](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/fifth-circuit-rejects-ofac-designation-3248351)

### Inferences
- Nova's low adoption is over-determined: (a) Gnosis Chain liquidity and bridge friction, (b) 1 ETH cap during beta, (c) few relayers, (d) sanctions arriving within 8 months, and (e) the main privacy demand (whales parking large ETH) was better served by the immutable 100 ETH Classic pool. Railgun later captured the "arbitrary amount, shielded balance" niche on mainnet.
- Legally, Nova is in a worse position than Classic: upgradeable-by-DAO contracts are closer to "ownable/controllable" property under Van Loon's reasoning.

- No source quantifies Nova's TVL or transaction counts in 2025–2026, or confirms whether the Omnibridge path still functions; L2BEAT tracks only Ethereum mainnet Classic pools.

## Key Question 6: What concrete design mistakes would a successor need to avoid (legal and technical)?

### Takeaway
The record points to a consistent set of lessons: keep the privacy core immutable and un-ownable (that is what won Van Loon), but recognize that everything around it — a developer-run frontend, a whitelisted/registered relayer network, protocol fees routed to a token, governance that can be captured, and public statements showing knowledge of specific criminal flows — is what produced the Storm conviction and the Pertsev conviction; technically, fixed pools without timing/address hygiene leak 10–35% of links, and the mutable periphery was the vector for every real compromise.

### Cited Findings
- Immutability was the decisive legal fact for sanctions: the court held immutable contracts "not ownable, not contracts, and not services," contrasting them with mutable contracts that "may be changed" and noting "once a smart contract becomes immutable, no one can reclaim control over it." — [Van Loon opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)
- Criminal exposure came from the service layer, not the code: prosecutors alleged the founders ran and paid to host the UI most users used, manually whitelisted relayers until March 2022 (98% of users relayed), profited via "control, with others, of key components of the integrated Tornado Cash service," refused AML, and privately acknowledged specific Lazarus flows ("guys we are fucked"). — [The Block, Apr 2024](https://www.theblock.co/post/291286/doj-says-roman-semenov-did-much-more-than-just-write-code-in-response-to-defenses-dismissal-motion)
- Mayer Brown's post-verdict read: "The jury was unpersuaded by the argument that Storm lacked control over users, which may reflect an expectation that developers exert some oversight"; developers "should get legal advice on whether they are money transmitting businesses." — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/the-tornado-cash-trials-mixed-verdict-implications-for-developer-liability)
- DOJ's stated safe harbor (discretionary): software "truly decentralised and solely automates peer to peer transactions" where the third party lacks "custody and control over user assets," and "merely writing code without ill intent"; intent-based charges remain. — [DL News on Galeotti](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/regulation/devs-of-decentralised-protocols-wont-be-prosecuted-doj-says/)
- The Dutch court rejected "it is only a tool": "it was Tornado Cash that executed the concealing and disguising activities," i.e., the court treated the protocol itself as performing laundering acts and the developer as responsible for its design and for not acting on suspicion. — [CoinDesk on Pertsev verdict](https://dev.coindesk.com/policy/2024/05/14/tornado-cash-developer-alexey-pertsev-found-guilty-of-money-laundering); [Blockworks](https://www.blockworks.com/news/alexey-pertsev-tornado-cash-guilty)
- Relayers as metadata and legal liability: relayers see withdrawer requests off-chain; Chainalysis ran one in 2022 and earned fees; the registry routes fees to TORN stakers, which the Fifth Circuit described as fee-collecting mutable contracts. — [The Block, Aug 2026](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-08-26-tornado-cash-roman-storm-retrial-april-2027-412761); [Van Loon opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)
- Anonymity mining backfired: TORN mining both (a) attracted privacy-ignorant users who reuse addresses and (b) leaked the block-count of each deposit when points were claimed (Tutela heuristic 5; Wang et al.). — [Tutela](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.06811); [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09035v1)
- Governance capture: May 2023 fake proposal with self-destruct/redeploy trick; Jan 2024 proposal that pointed the IPFS hash to a note-exfiltrating frontend; June 2026 fake treasury proposal with a look-alike address — all via the DAO's control over mutable periphery. — [Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-tornado-cash-hack-may-2023); [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/backdoor-discovered-in-ipfs-version-of-tornado-cash/); [L2BEAT timeline](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)
- Empirical linkage: 34.7% of Ethereum withdrawals linkable with three heuristics; 0.1 ETH pool >60% matchable; anonymity sets shrink 37% ± 15% on average. — [Cristodaro et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09433v1); [Tutela](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.06811)
- Whale domination of pools reduces everyone's privacy (Nov 2025 Heart-linked deposits ≈ 1/3 of deposits). — [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/privacy-protocol-tornado-cash-volumes-hit-record-high/)
- Successor approaches: Privacy Pools' association sets let users prove membership in a curated "clean" set and "ragequit" if excluded, designed by Buterin/Illum et al. as the explicit alternative to Tornado's all-or-nothing anonymity; 0xbow's Proof of Association retrofits this to legacy Tornado notes. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/privacy-pools-launches-ethereum-support-vitalik-buterin); [Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/news/cryptocurrency-news/show/658687-0xbow-launches-tool/)
- Rationale for fixed denominations (from the protocol spec): a single fixed N per pool means every unspent deposit is a candidate for every withdrawal ("every deposit since the last moment when the contract has zero Ether... can be a potential coin"); amounts therefore carry no information. — [whitepaper](https://berkeley-defi.github.io/assets/material/Tornado%20Cash%20Whitepaper.pdf)
- Trusted-setup dependency: Groth16 circuit-specific setup; compromise of either phase's toxic waste enables forged proofs; L2BEAT rates this medium risk. — [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)

### Inferences (design lessons a successor should draw)
- Legal: (1) ship immutable core contracts with no admin key and no developer-controlled fee switch; (2) do not operate the canonical frontend, hosting, or relayer whitelist as the developer; (3) avoid a token whose value derives from protocol fees paid by withdrawals (this is the "commercial enterprise" nexus); (4) avoid any mechanism (registry, staking, treasury) that gives a DAO ongoing control over user-facing components, because mutable components are sanctionable under Van Loon's own carve-out; (5) implement a user-driven, selective-disclosure/association mechanism so legitimate users can exit a tainted set, which both reduces illicit utility and gives a non-intent defense; (6) document and act on sanctions screening in a way that is real rather than cosmetic (the "ineffective UI OFAC screen" was used as evidence of bad faith).
- Technical: (1) eliminate the need for relayers (gas abstraction via account abstraction/paymasters or in-pool fee payment) or make relaying permissionless and client-selected over Tor; (2) discourage address reuse and immediate withdrawals in the client (enforced delays, randomized timing hints); (3) hide amounts and avoid multi-pool portfolios (shielded-balance/UTXO designs like Nova/Railgun) while keeping the anonymity set unified; (4) never let governance change the frontend hash without a verifiable, time-locked, multi-party review; (5) avoid anonymity-mining incentives that leak deposit age; (6) prefer universal/transparent setups over circuit-specific Groth16 ceremonies; (7) keep deposit notes off-chain and never offer on-chain note backups.

### Gaps
- No single retrospective from the Tornado Cash core team or DAO articulating "lessons learned" was found; the lessons above are synthesized from court findings, academic results, and successor designs.
- No source quantifies how much relay volume is concentrated among a few relayers today, which would bear on how much real metadata exposure exists.
