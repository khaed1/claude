This note continues `design_review_v3.md`. It records the owner's decisions taken in session on 11 November 2026, the fourth review (of revision 4.3) and the design decisions that follow. The specification is the authority once a revision is merged; this note says why.

## 2. What the relay-fee decision does to the circuit

The decision is a removal. Revision 4.3 already had the case "relayer paid in the spend's own asset by a wallet that holds a single note" (the fee asset equal to the spent asset, the fee input empty). The owner's choice makes that the only case and deletes the rest: the third input, its nullifier, the fee change output, the public `feeAsset`, the reward term for the fee note, the flags `hasFee` and `same`, and the rule that let value cross between two sides.

Decisions taken while writing it down, and why:

- **The leaf stays a bundle of four.** A spend now has three commitments (recipient, change, reward). The leaf is still `H4(C0, C1, C2, 0)`. A bundle of three under `H3` would save about 16,700 gas per insert, but a two-bit slot selector would then have one illegal value that needs a new constraint, and position arithmetic would change. The four-slot bundle with a zero in the last slot is what activation leaves already look like, and the tree and membership text of 4.3 stay as they were.
- **One public asset.** `publicAsset` is the asset of both the public amount and the relay fee. It is zero exactly when both are zero. The circuit ties it to the real asset with `(publicAmount + relayFee) × (asset − publicAsset) = 0`; neither term can wrap because both are range-checked to 96 bits.
- **The two public amounts appear in the conservation equation only as a sum.** What keeps a proof for one split from verifying for another is that each has its own range check, so each has its own column in the constraint system. This is written into the specification next to lemma 10, because a builder who drops a range check on a public input as redundant (the contract checks sizes anyway) would make the two interchangeable.
- **Relaying an asset now always depends on the frozen conversion rate** for every asset but ETH. With the ceiling at about seven times the gas of a spend in ETH and a factor of eight for other assets, relaying in an asset stops working if its price in ETH falls to about a fiftieth of the frozen rate. Its owner can then still send the spend from a public address. Stated as a risk.

## 3. Fourth review, of revisions 4.3 and 4.4

Two reviewers, each given only a plain-text rendering of the page and told not to open anything else in the repository. `adversarial_review_r43_mechanisms_v4.md` (registry, launch, fees, canary; ids Q-1 to Q-17) read revision 4.3. `adversarial_review_r44_core_v4.md` (circuit, notes, pool; ids P-1 to P-8) read revision 4.4, because the fee note it would otherwise have reviewed no longer existed. A first attempt at both was lost to a container restart before either had recorded a finding.

Result: neither area passes. No critical fault. One certain high (Q-1). Three that are high or worse under one of two readings (Q-2, Q-3, P-1). Eleven medium, ten low, 28 contradictions between sections, 46 points a builder would have had to guess. I re-ran the mechanisms reviewer's strike simulation (`strikes_fix.py`): as written a group of 14 in 30 blocks 42.5% of days for ever; with a miss at three strikes, credit only on days with an accepted batch and removal at 21 it is 29.9%, and 33.0% for 50 of 101.

What held, by both: value and reward conservation, the split between `publicAmount` and `relayFee` (lemma 10 is right), `extHash`, the ledgers, `owed`, `START`, `roll`, every figure of arithmetic on the page, the salt loop and launch gas, the address derivation, the canary schedule, the stream scales, the auction and the buyback.

## 4. Decisions for revision 4.5, and why

The owner chose to keep patching, so every finding gets a fix or a stated reason. A removal is used where the removal is the fix. Nothing the owner decided is reopened; where a reviewer proposed cutting something the owner confirmed on 11 November (refund by others, the 90-day bar), it is kept and repaired.

### Registry

| Decision | Reason | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| `flag` and `signalCriteria` can be called for any registered seat whose holder still owns it, active or still waiting. The criteria count flags from seats in the epoch's snapshot, whenever in the last 14 epochs they were posted | Nobody is active before epoch 31, and that epoch's batch reads flags only up to the end of epoch 30. The first batch covers a month of deposits and could contain no flag, so about $153,000 of known-stolen, unlisted funds could fill the opening caps | Q-1 |
| One permanent record per epoch (`epochs[e]`: snapshot, lead hash and count, accepted hash, revealers, applied, pay per head). The 16-slot ring, its tags and its 16-epoch deadline are removed. Nothing "closes" an epoch after it ends | "Closing the previous epoch" had no meaning after a day with no call. Read literally the first quiet day divided by zero and every registry function reverted for good. The lead hash, accepted hash and applied flag were single variables | Q-2, Q-6 |
| `apply` runs only in the last four hours of the epoch, after reveals close, and fixes that epoch's pay itself | At that moment the number of revealers is final, so pay per head can be written once. It also means the screener whose reveal completes the two thirds no longer holds the first place by construction (Q-17). Cost: the earliest activation moves from 36 to 44 hours after a deposit | Q-2, Q-6, Q-17 |
| `reserved[asset]`: pay fixed for an epoch and not yet credited. `fund` books the balance less everything accounted | That money was in neither `pot` nor `owed`. Under one reading `fund` counted it twice and the shortfall came out of stakes | Q-6 |
| The roll (first call of an epoch) does one thing: `activeCount += joining`, `joining = 0`, write the snapshot. `activate` requires `activeFrom` to be zero. Ending a record lowers `joining` or `activeCount` according to `activeFrom`. The snapshot never moves during an epoch | A screener that left while in `joining` was folded in anyway, and `activate` had no once-only guard. Eight phantom screeners against 15 real ones make two thirds unreachable for good | Q-3 |
| A record cannot be ended (exit, eviction, replacement by a buyer) in an epoch in which it has revealed, and cannot exit after committing that day | Its pay for that epoch is fixed only at `apply`. Ending it earlier stranded the pay. The price is a day's delay for an eviction | Q-6, guess 4 |
| Settlement is bounded at seven epochs: a record with no reveal in seven active epochs is ended for silence inside settlement itself, so no loop is longer | Without the ring a screener could be unsettled for years. Since each commit settles, a gap over seven epochs means seven epochs of silence, which ends the record anyway | Q-2, Q-6 |
| Strikes: +3 for each epoch with a majority hash that the screener did not reveal; −1 for each epoch in which it revealed the majority hash and a batch was accepted; removal at 21. An even split is stated as a deadlock with no penalty | With +2/−1 and credit on blocked days, members who rest on a blocked day lose a strike. The bound was the group's share of seats, not one day in three. The owner confirmed "a week of blocking, or more than one day in three"; these constants are what deliver it (7 × 3 = 21) | Q-4 |
| Strikes and the bar belong to the seat (`seats[seatId]`), survive exit, eviction, sale and registration, and any settlement that finds 21 strikes ends and bars the record whoever called | The bar was set only by `evict` on two grounds. Exiting first, or moving the seat to a second wallet, reset strikes and set no bar | Q-7 |
| The distributor sends the screeners' 40% to the registry while `Registry.fundable()`: a batch applied in the last seven epochs and commits still open. The pot carries to the next applied batch | The 4.3 rule (this epoch or the one before) did not do what the page said: fees swept on a blocked day still reached the pot, and it gave stakers a reason to want batches to fail. A blocked day now delays pay and removes none; strikes are the penalty | Q-5 |
| `unbonding[holder][releaseEpoch]` | One total per holder with a clock that restarted let anyone delay a holder's whole stake by evicting one stale record | Q-13 |
| Example criteria: a flag by a fifth of the snapshot (at least three seats) holds a deposit until it is eight days old; by more than half it refuses. Holds are defined by the deposit's age, not by a count of batches. `fundedByListed` is taken over the larger of the inflow and the deposit. The file in force is the last to have reached two thirds. A list that cannot be read stops batches | A majority quorum over the whole snapshot let seats that do nothing vote against every flag. Holds counted in batches stretched under a blockade past the 21 days. Dust from a listed address held an old address's deposit. The file could fall back to the frozen one | Q-8, Q-9, Q-11 |
| A batch lists at most 64 words that set a bit, leaving 32 for words that clear | 96 held deposits a day in 96 words left no room to lift anyone else's hold | Q-10 |
| `swarmPaid`, capped at 10% of supply | A list that sums to more than 10% was paid out of the founder's share | Q-14 |
| Stated, not fixed: removing a blocking third from a registry of seven to ten stops it (Q-12); an issuer that freezes the registry or the distributor stops that asset's fee flow (Q-15); the auction floor is a fixed number of IMD and a thawed backlog sells at a stale price (Q-16); whoever lands the first transaction after hour 20 takes the first place at an opening (Q-17) | Each follows from a decided rule or has no fix that is smaller than the fault | |

Not adopted: removing refund by others (cut 1) and the bar (cut 3), because the owner confirmed both on 11 November; paying all fees in IMD (cut 5), because payment in the asset received is decided. Refund by others keeps its contract rule (refused now, and at least 21 days old). The page now says that it tests age and not time refused, the criteria define holds by age so that none outlasts day 8, and the node software does not signal a file whose holds run past a deposit age of 14 days.

### Circuit, notes and pool

| Decision | Reason | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Every public input is below `p`. The pool rejects a call in which any is not, before the proof is checked, and the verifier returns false for such an input whatever a reference verifier does | Nothing said so. If the verifier were made to agree with a reference that reduces inputs, `N + p` would pass as a fresh nullifier and each note could be spent five or six times | P-1 |
| The diversifier `d` is removed. `ownerPk = H3(TAG_PK, spk, nk)`, one per wallet | The address did not carry `d`, yet the payer had to write it into ciphertext 0. Under one reading received notes were unspendable after a restore. `d` hid nothing on-chain that `rho` does not hide, and the page already said it did nothing for addresses | P-3 |
| Lemma 11 is split. 11a (machine-checked): the frozen witness generator satisfies the constraint system for every input that meets a list of integer conditions, none of which mentions a circuit signal. 11b (contract property): in every reachable state honest spends meet those conditions. Lemmas 3 and 9 reworded. A paired test and a second named assumption added | As written lemma 11 was false over the stated ranges (800 ETH with an index of 2^88 gives `earned` near 2^103), rested on a property of the Pool, and its easy repair let a too-tight range check through | P-4 |
| A refund is paid like every other ETH payment: a failed 50,000-gas call is credited to `owed` | It was the one payment with neither fallback nor gas. Under the draft Glamsterdam prices one fresh storage slot in a depositing contract's receiving code costs more than the allowance | P-5 |
| The last-64-roots window is removed. A proof names one checkpoint: `rootCur` with `gCur`, or `rootPrev` with `gPrev` | The wallet rule (always prove against the checkpoint) and the state table (spend in the next transaction against a recent root) could not both be followed, and no conforming wallet used the window. A note is spendable after the next hourly checkpoint | P-8 |
| `deadline` in `extHash`, and one comparison in `spend` | A relayer handed a proof could hold it and land it later, after the wallet had proved again for another relayer | P-7 |
| Every output has an `ownerCommit` from a derived or fresh `rho`, and ciphertext 0 is always a real encryption | With a constant in an unused output anyone could tell a claim from a payment and read the asset of an owner-sent spend | P-6 |
| The relay-fee ceiling keeps its formula, which the owner confirmed. The page stops saying "a few times the gas cost" and says what it is: 0.002 ETH plus about seven times the base-fee cost of a spend, eight times that for other assets, with no daily limit. Standard sizes are the default and the relay fee is a second, capped way out that a user can aim at their own address, paying the 0.2%. The wallet refuses a quote above three times the current gas cost | At the page's own base fee the ceiling is 46 times the gas cost for ETH and 372 times for other assets. Four descriptions were false. No funds are at risk and it harms only the user who does it. It is also how dust leaves | P-2 |
| Weights are scaled by 10^6 (IMD 10^6, ETH about 8.07 × 10^8, USDT and USDC about 3.79 × 10^17) | With IMD at weight 1 the other weights round to whole multiples of it: one part in 800 today, 6% if IMD rose a hundredfold before the freeze | guess 18 |

Checked while deciding: with weights scaled by 10^6 the product in constraint 9 is at most about 2^245 against a modulus of 2^253.6, and the reward index moves by about 1.4 × 10^8 a second, so nothing is lost to rounding. The largest index falls from about 2^75 to about 2^55 on mainnet.
