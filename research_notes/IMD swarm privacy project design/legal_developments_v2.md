## 6. Item 5: EU

Verified against the Official Journal text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 [P] https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401624 (OJ L, 19.6.2024):
- Article 79(1), verbatim: "Credit institutions, financial institutions and crypto-asset service providers shall be prohibited from keeping anonymous bank and payment accounts, anonymous passbooks, anonymous safe-deposit boxes or anonymous crypto-asset accounts as well as any account otherwise allowing for the anonymisation of the customer account holder or the anonymisation or increased obfuscation of transactions, including through anonymity-enhancing coins."
- Article 90: "It shall apply from 10 July 2027, except in relation to obliged entities referred to in Article 3, points (3)(n) and (o), to which it shall apply from 10 July 2029." Those points are football agents and professional football clubs. The 2029 date in Article 79(3) concerns bearer shares.
- CORRECTION. Article 2(1)(25): "'anonymity-enhancing coins' means crypto-assets that have built-in features designed to make crypto-asset transfer information anonymous, either systematically or optionally". The term is defined in the Regulation, and "optionally" is in the definition.
- Article 79 contains no delegation for technical standards or guidelines on anonymity-enhancing coins. Its only reference to standards (paragraph 2) concerns anonymous prepaid cards.
- Recital 160: "That prohibition does not apply to providers of hardware and software or providers of self-hosted wallets insofar as they do not possess access to or control over those crypto-asset wallets."
- Article 40: CASPs must assess and mitigate risk for transfers to or from a self-hosted address; "By 10 July 2027, AMLA shall issue guidelines to specify the mitigating measures".
- Text search of the Regulation: zero occurrences of "mixer", "tumbler", "viewing key", "selective disclosure", "smart contract", "decentralised".
- EUR-Lex document record: "In force"; no amending act listed; only language corrigenda (ES/FI/SV, LV, HU, CS). [P] https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:32024R1624

MiCA, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, Article 76(3), verified [P] https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1114 : "The operating rules of the trading platform for crypto-assets shall prevent the admission to trading of crypto-assets that have an inbuilt anonymisation function unless the holders of those crypto-assets and their transaction history can be identified by the crypto-asset service providers operating a trading platform for crypto-assets."

AMLA (the AML/CFT standard-setter since the mandate moved from the EBA)
- Public consultations page: 11 consultations, all closed, none on Article 79, anonymity-enhancing coins or the Article 40 self-hosted address guidelines. Most recent: draft RTS on a central AML/CFT database, 7 Oct to 3 Nov 2026. [P] https://www.amla.europa.eu/policy/public-consultations_en
- Regulatory instruments tracker, "Last update: 7 October 2026": no instrument listed under AMLR Article 79 or Article 40(2). The page says the overview "is not exhaustive". [P] https://www.amla.europa.eu/policy/regulatory-instruments_en
- 1 Oct 2026: AMLA submitted three final draft RTS to the Commission, including customer due diligence (Article 28(1)). A text search of the 28(1) final report finds no occurrence of "self-hosted", "anonym", "privacy", "mixer" or "obfuscat". [P] https://www.amla.europa.eu/press-release-amla-finalises-key-standards-private-sector_en
- NEW, 7 Oct 2026: AMLA response to the Commission's MiCA review consultation. On DeFi: "effective control may arise from a concentration of governance tokens or the ability to pause or modify smart contracts"; AMLA recommends that "the legal framework should define the term 'DeFi arrangement' and establish common criteria for determining whether a person or entity performs, provides or exercises effective control", and that "consideration could also be given to requiring CASPs that facilitate access to the arrangement to apply appropriate risk-mitigation measures." It does not mention mixers, privacy coins or Article 76(3). [P] https://www.amla.europa.eu/amla-identifies-amlcft-priorities-review-mica_en

EBA (still applicable until AMLA instruments replace them, per the AMLA tracker)
- EBA/GL/2024/01, 16 Jan 2024, ML/TF Risk Factors Guidelines extended to CASPs. Risk-increasing product features "which facilitate anonymity such as, but not limited to, mixers or tumblers, obfuscated ledger technology, ring signatures, stealth addresses, ring confidential transactions, atomic swaps and non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs." Also lists transactions with "platforms that aim to obfuscate transactions and facilitate anonymity such as mixer or tumbler platforms" and with self-hosted addresses. [P] https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-01/a3e89f4f-fbf3-4bd6-9e07-35f3243555b3/Final%20Amending%20%20Guidelines%20on%20MLTF%20Risk%20Factors.pdf

Answer to "does any official text address self-custody software, mixers, privacy pools, or selective disclosure / viewing keys?"
- Self-custody software: yes, AMLR recital 160 and Article 40.
- Mixers and zero-knowledge proofs: yes, as risk factors for CASPs, in EBA/GL/2024/01. Not in the AMLR text.
- Selective disclosure: not by name. The closest is the identification condition in MiCA Article 76(3).
- Privacy pools, viewing keys: no official EU text found that names them.
- No November 2026 EU news on Article 79 found. No proposal to postpone the 10 July 2027 date found (search only).

- GC26/7, DeFi guidance: "This guidance does not create new regulatory requirements." [P] https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/guidance-consultations/gc26-7-decentralised-finance-guidance ; PDF https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/guidance-consultation/gc26-7.pdf
- CP26/36, Cryptoasset Perimeter Guidance (October 2026). It covers a new statutory exclusion (Regulated Activities Order article 9Z2A) from "arranging deals in qualifying cryptoassets" for an unauthorised person who "merely provides a technical service" giving access to a third party's services or to "a decentralised protocol". The FCA says the exclusion "does not provide a general exclusion for services described as technological, technical, automated, software-based or connected with decentralised finance", and that a service is more likely to qualify where it "responds to instructions initiated by" the user and the provider "transmits [the user's] instructions without exercising qualitative judgement about their content or destination". On decentralisation: "an activity is not outside the regulatory perimeter merely because some or all of the functions involved are performed automatically." The paper does not use the words relayer, mixer or privacy. [P] https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consultation/cp26-36.pdf

Relayers specifically
- FinCEN FIN-2019-G001 (9 May 2019) remains posted on fincen.gov with no rescission or supersession note. [P] page: https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/application-fincens-regulations-certain-business-models ; [P] PDF: https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf . Key sentences, section 4.5.1:
  - Definition: "Providers of anonymizing services, commonly referred to as 'mixers' or 'tumblers,' are either persons that accept CVCs and retransmit them in a manner designed to prevent others from tracing the transmission back to its source (anonymizing services provider), or suppliers of software a transmittor would use for the same purpose (anonymizing software provider)."
  - 4.5.1(a): "An anonymizing services provider is a money transmitter under FinCEN regulations."
  - 4.5.1(b): "An anonymizing software provider is not a money transmitter. FinCEN regulations exempt from the definition of money transmitter those persons providing 'the delivery, communication, or network access services used by a money transmitter to support money transmission services.' This is because suppliers of tools (communications, hardware, or software) that may be utilized in money transmission, like anonymizing software, are engaged in trade and not money transmission."
  - Section 4.4: "when DApps perform money transmission, the definition of money transmitter will apply to the DApp, the owners/operators of the DApp, or both."
  - The guidance does not contain the word "relayer" (text search: zero occurrences of "relay").
- No 2025-2026 FinCEN guidance, enforcement action or court ruling was found that classifies a fee-earning relayer or broadcaster as a money transmitter, or that says it is not one. Status: NOT VERIFIED either way; searches only.
- What the 2026 court record does contain is party argument, not a holding, at the 9 Apr 2026 Rule 29 hearing (Dkt. 290, filed 5 May 2026, link above):
  - Government (AUSA Arad): "on the withdrawal side, it's even clearer that Tornado Cash is the one making the transfer because, there, it's the relayer itself that writes to Blockchain", describing "a Tornado Cash relayer, which is part of the cohort operating Tornado Cash for the purpose of transferring money from one place to another for a profit". Also: "the presence of relayers was the icing on the cake that made every transaction that went through Tornado Cash truly secret".
  - The Court, questioning the Government: "I'm not sure I saw, with respect to the relayer registry as distinguished from the Chainalysis oracle, conversations suggesting that they knew and they wanted the relayer registry to aid bad actors".
  - Defense: "the relayers were never alleged to be coconspirators, they're independent third actors. Mr. Storm never earned any fees ... as relayers".
  - Defense letter of 30 Apr 2026 (Dkt. 289): the Government "does not dispute that relayers were third parties who set and collected their own fees, which were not shared with the Peppersec founders, and that relayers were optional." [P] https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.604938/gov.uscourts.nysd.604938.289.0.pdf
