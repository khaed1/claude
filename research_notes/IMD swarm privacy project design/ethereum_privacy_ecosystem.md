## Key Question 1: Shielded pools / mixers on Ethereum L1 and L2s — mechanism, status, metrics, lessons

### Takeaway
Three live shielded-pool designs dominate Ethereum L1 in late 2026: Tornado Cash (fixed-denomination mixer, ~$794M TVL, no compliance layer, delisted but developers still in criminal proceedings), Railgun (variable-amount UTXO shielded ledger, ~$110M TVL, ~$5B cumulative volume, opt-in Private Proofs of Innocence), and 0xbow Privacy Pools (ASP-gated shielded pool, ~$9M TVL, v2 with private transfers launched October 2026 on seven chains). Almost every other L1/L2 shielded pool launched 2021–2025 is dead, dormant, paused, exploited, or sub-$1M TVL (Nocturne, Intmax, Hinkal, zkBob, Umbra as a pool, Labyrinth, Panther, Cyclone, Firn), which is the clearest whitespace signal: the market has consolidated to one uncompliant mixer and two "compliant" pools, with the "compliant" pools' TVL combined at roughly 15% of Tornado's.

**Railgun**
- Live on Ethereum mainnet since July 2021; audits by ABDK (07.2021), Zokyo (11.2021, 02.2023); Private Proofs of Innocence announced 11.2023; $1B cumulative volume reported May 2024; $5B+ cumulative private volume per a June 2026 snapshot. — [ethprivacy.tools Railgun deep dive](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/)
- Mechanism: shielding moves tokens into the Railgun contract; user receives a "0zk" address with separate spending and viewing keys; balances become encrypted notes in a shielded Merkle tree; each spend verified by zk-SNARK (Groth16/BN254); 0zk-to-0zk transfers can be any amount (no fixed denominations); "RelayAdapt" unshields, runs arbitrary contract calls (e.g., a swap), and re-shields in one tx. All assets share one Merkle tree, so the anonymity set grows with total shielded value; rare tokens have thin sets. — [ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/); Groth16/BN254 and 0.50% round-trip fee per [Permissionless taxonomy](https://permissionless-technologies.com/blog/privacy-landscape-2026)
- TVL snapshot (June 2026, DefiLlama cross-checked on-chain): ~$80.3M total shielded (~42,255 ETH); Ethereum $75.2M, Arbitrum $3.3M, BNB $1.1M, Polygon $0.7M; mainnet composition ETH $38.9M (48%), USDC $14.7M (18%), USDT $11.5M (14%), DAI $6.3M (8%), wBTC $1.8M, 147 tokens shielded; RAIL staked (~$62M) is governance, not shielded TVL. — [ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/)
- A separate measurement study put shield volume at $5.06B across Ethereum, Arbitrum, Polygon and BNB as of May 12, 2026. — [search aggregate; see also baltex.io comparison](https://baltex.io/blog/ecosystem/aztec-vs-railgun-privacy-fees-2026-comparison)
- Broadcasters (Railgun's relayer network): self-federated over the Waku messaging network with no central server; broadcasters submit unshield transactions and pay gas, reimbursed in any accepted token; anyone can run one; the reference node enforces a PPOI for every relayed transaction; Waku published a Railgun case study on 26 April 2024. — [ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/)
- Railgun docs state the broadcaster "cannot read the contents of the interaction, apart from the gas amount paid to it," and users can switch broadcasters if one refuses a transaction. — [Railgun docs, Community Broadcasters](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/learn/privacy-system/community-broadcasters)
- Railway wallet: free, open-source, non-custodial, desktop/iOS/Android; shielding, private transfers, private swaps via 0x aggregation; built-in PPOI; "zero activity logs" claim. — [ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/)
- Private Proofs of Innocence (PPOI) design: list providers are Elliptic, ScamSniffer, PureFi, SlowMist, and the Chainalysis Sanctions Oracle; anyone can publish a list; wallets can combine providers; providers see only public on-chain data and receive nothing from wallets. After shielding there is a 1-hour "Unshield-Only Standby Period" in supporting wallets during which tokens can only be unshielded back to the origin address (gives list providers time to update). A separate blinded ZK proof is generated per list provider showing the tokens are not in that provider's dataset; recursive SNARKs carry the proof forward through later private transfers so recipients inherit it. Anyone can run a PPOI node; nodes sync proof data IPFS-style; broadcasters run PPOI nodes. Tokens can always be unshielded back to origin regardless of PPOI status. — [Railgun docs, Private Proofs of Innocence](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/assurance/private-proofs-of-innocence.md)
- Railgun denied North Korea (Lazarus) use as it neared $1B volume (2024-era coverage). — [EconoTimes](https://www.econotimes.com/Railgun-Denies-North-Korean-Use-Approaches-1B-Volume-Amid-Vitalik-Buterins-Praise-1675811)
- Ethereum Foundation added Railgun to Kohaku on Oct 8–10, 2025; RAIL rose ~300% to a market value near $300M — "the fastest price increase the token has experienced since its 2021 debut." — [DL News, 10 Oct 2025](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/railgun-token-soars-as-ethereum-foundation-integrates-protocol-into-kohaku/)
- Railgun V3 "announced" as of April 2026 (no mainnet date found). — [Permissionless taxonomy](https://permissionless-technologies.com/blog/privacy-landscape-2026)
- Criticisms/lessons (per ethprivacy.tools): shield/unshield events are public, so timing and amount correlation at the edges is the main analysis surface; round-trips in quick succession weaken privacy; no "official" wallet (users trust the frontend); broadcasters charge fees selected automatically by the wallet. — [ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/)
- L2Beat rates Railgun as a "shielded ledger" on Ethereum, "run by no operator; governed by RAIL stakers," with a 7-day exit window. — [L2Beat Privacy](https://l2beat.com/privacy)

- ASP mechanics (docs): the ASP "maintains the current set of approved deposit labels" and "determines withdrawal eligibility"; approved labels are represented as a Merkle root; each update is a record with root + IPFS hash of off-chain data + timestamp; only authorized "postmen" accounts can submit updates; contract keeps a circular buffer of historical roots; withdrawals validate against the latest root. Depositors whose funds are not approved can "ragequit" (exit publicly to the origin address, called a "complaint exit path"); labels can be removed during a wind-down. Screening criteria, data sources, latency, fees and multi-ASP support are **not** specified in the docs. — [Privacy Pools docs: ASP layer](https://docs.privacypools.com/layers/asp); [Privacy Pools docs overview](https://docs.privacypools.com/)

### Inferences
- The anonymity-set data shows a stark bimodal market: Tornado (uncompliant, ~$794M) vs. everything else combined (<~$130M). The "compliant pool" thesis has roughly 14% of Tornado's TVL after 18 months of EF endorsement, Vitalik deposits and Kohaku integration — demand for compliance-gated privacy is real but small, and the delisting of Tornado removed the regulatory moat the compliant pools were designed around.
- Railgun's edge-correlation weakness (public shield/unshield, amount/timing) and Privacy Pools' single-operator ASP with unspecified screening criteria are the two most-cited design gaps; neither has multiple ASPs or public screening rules.
- Projects that died shared traits: L2-first before L2s had users (Nocturne v1), novel-but-fragile proving systems (Intmax balance-proof bug), small TVL making exploits fatal (Hinkal), and lack of a wallet distribution channel. Survivors (Railgun, Privacy Pools) have their own wallet surface and an EF/Kohaku distribution path.
- Compliance-layer "flavours" now span a spectrum: exclusion proofs against public blocklists (Railgun PPOI, 5 providers), inclusion sets curated by one operator (0xbow ASP), KYC/attestation-at-entry (Panther, Hinkal, Intmax/Predicate), and threshold de-anonymization keys (Labyrinth) — the last two are the least adopted.

## Key Question 2: Stealth addresses — ERC-5564/6538, Umbra, Fluidkey, gas-from-fresh-address, scanning/announcer privacy costs

### Takeaway
Stealth addresses are the one Ethereum privacy primitive with a finished standard (ERC-5564 announcer + ERC-6538 registry, canonical singleton deployments), two long-running products (Umbra since 2021, ~25.5K deposits; Fluidkey since 2024, self-reported 30K+ users and $900M+ volume), and a growing set of integrators (Cloaked, COWL on Robinhood Chain, Kohaku). They solve recipient unlinkability only — "traceability is not broken" — and the gas-from-a-fresh-address problem is solved in practice by wrapping each stealth address in a Safe smart account with sponsored/ERC-4337 gas (Fluidkey, Labyrinth) or by an operator relayer (Umbra), both of which hand the operator a correlation vantage point.

### Cited Findings

**Standards**
- ERC-5564 defines a singleton Announcer deployed at the same address on every chain; view tags "reduce the parsing time by around 6x"; the view tag "only affects the privacy and not the secure generation of a stealth address" (one write-up: reduces security margin from 128 to 124 bits). ERC-6538 is an extension that lets registration happen "using only the users' signatures, without on-chain interaction." Real-world implementers cited: Umbra, Fluidkey, Railgun, Nocturne; COWL (Robinhood Chain) uses both canonical contracts. — [EIP-5564](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5564); [Openfort deep dive](https://www.openfort.io/blog/stealth-addresses); [COWL GitHub](https://github.com/cowlpro/cowl); [2077 Research](https://research.2077.xyz/erc-5564-erc-6358-unlocking-privacy-on-ethereum-with-stealth-addresses)
- Fake-announcement spam risk: anyone can post announcements to force extra scanning work; spam filters suggested, not deployed. — [TradingView/U.Today](https://www.tradingview.com/news/u_today:e39300db6094b:0-ethereum-to-integrate-erc-5564-in-push-for-privacy/)
- Fluidkey's "FK1" stealth-Safe scheme (scheme id 0x464B31) uses meta-address `P_spend ‖ configHash` (65 bytes) rather than the standard scheme-1 `P_spend ‖ P_view` (66 bytes); an ENSIP-30 stealth-address ENS resolver reference repo (with FluidkeyVerifier) is WIP, unaudited, undeployed. — [fluidkey/stealth-ens-resolver](https://github.com/fluidkey/stealth-ens-resolver)
- ScopeLift maintains a stealth-address SDK and contributed to the EIPs. — [ScopeLift stealth-address-sdk](https://github.com/ScopeLift/stealth-address-sdk)

**Vitalik's stealth-address writing (Jan 20, 2023)**
- "Hiding the fact that a transfer happened is impossible," so the goal is hiding the recipient. On gas: "That account has 0 ETH in it, and so there is no way to pay transaction fees"; funding from the main wallet links the two; ERC-4337 token paymasters "won't work, because those only work for fungible ERC20 tokens" (for NFTs). Options: ZK proof of funds elsewhere ("an extra hundreds of thousands of gas just for a single transfer"); specialized aggregators selling blinded Chaumian "tickets" who include the tx "in their bundle for free"; paymasters for the fungible case. Scanning: with a 1-byte view tag, "Bob only needs to do a single elliptic curve multiplication for each ephemeral public key," full derivation needed ~1/256 of the time. Quantum: elliptic curves are "vulnerable to quantum computers"; candidates are isogenies (some broken 2022), lattices (large keys), generic hash-based ZK constructions (need STARKs, large). — [Vitalik, "An incomplete guide to stealth addresses"](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/01/20/stealth.html)

**Umbra (ScopeLift)**
- Mechanism: register once (ERC-6538-style registry), receive payments at fresh addresses; 11,983 registered recipients form the ceiling of the anonymity set; "registrations are public, so a payment soon after registering can point to that registrant"; the effective set depends on waiting time (blend with everyone registered in a 30-day window). Risks: owner can raise an uncapped ETH toll on new payments; the operator can link wallet to stealth addresses; the hosted app's RPC (Alchemy) sees the payee address just before payment; indexer and relayer API are closed source; contracts immutable (infinite exit window); "future adversary" exposure if ECC is broken. — [L2Beat Umbra](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/umbra)
- Activity: all-time 25.5K deposits (~$143M); 30 days: 81 (~$1.14M); 7 days: 9 (~$158K); snapshot ~Oct 9, 2026. — [L2Beat Umbra](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/umbra)
- Earlier metrics: ~13,000 registered addresses, >53,000 transactions (2024 Optimism RetroPGF application); taxonomy cites "77,000+ stealth addresses generated" and a Trail of Bits audit (Apr 2026). — [Optimism RetroPGF 3](https://vote.optimism.io/retropgf/3/application/0x617e15a1edc68034cac85db6f0a4d3bb55418761ab7ba4fe26423c3806ff20e6); [Permissionless taxonomy](https://permissionless-technologies.com/blog/privacy-landscape-2026)
- Academic anonymity analysis concluded that "for the majority of Umbra payments, it is straightforward to establish the recipient." — [DeepAI: Anonymity Analysis of the Umbra Stealth Address Scheme](https://deepai.org/publication/anonymity-analysis-of-the-umbra-stealth-address-scheme-on-ethereum)
- Code open source. — [ScopeLift/umbra-protocol](https://github.com/ScopeLift/umbra-protocol)

**Fluidkey**
- Design: user signs a key-generation message with an existing EOA to derive a spending key and viewing key ("the private key pair never leaves the user's client and is not stored locally"); user shares a BIP-32 viewing node `m/5564'/N'` (currently N=0 for all users) with Fluidkey so Fluidkey can generate addresses and retrieve funds; each stealth account is a counterfactual 1/1 Safe smart account whose sole signer is a stealth EOA derived at `m/5564'/0'/8'/0'/0'/p'/n'`; Safe gives "gas sponsorship" and cross-chain address validity; deployment happens only at withdrawal; users get a static ENS name `username.fkey.id`/`.fkey.eth` that resolves (via an off-chain ENS resolver) to a new stealth address on every query; recovery possible without Fluidkey by replaying derivation. The docs do not spell out the paymaster flow, scanning mechanics, or what the server learns. — [Fluidkey technical walkthrough](https://docs.fluidkey.com/technical-documentation/technical-walkthrough/)
- Metrics: homepage claims 30,000+ users and $900M+ transfer volume (self-reported, undated); June 2026 coverage cites >24,000 users; BNB Chain added June 2026 making seven EVM networks (Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Polygon, Optimism, Gnosis, BSC). — [fluidkey.com](https://www.fluidkey.com/); [Crypto Briefing, June 2026](https://cryptobriefing.com/bnb-chain-fluidkey-private-payments/)
- Fluidkey's own docs say stealth addresses give unlinkability but "do not break fund traceability." — [Bankless on Fluidkey](https://www.bankless.com/read/fluidkey-bank-base); [Penchan explainer](https://penchan.co/en/crypto/fluidkey/what-is-fluidkey/)
- Crypto Briefing flags that US/EU AML treatment of stealth-address wallets is unsettled. — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/bnb-chain-fluidkey-private-payments/)
- Fluidkey publishes `fluidkey-stealth-account-kit` (core crypto for the interface/SDK). — [github.com/fluidkey](https://github.com/fluidkey)
- L2Beat lists Fluidkey ("optional yield and bank transfers") and Cloaked ("passkey login; Incognito balance in Privacy Pools") as stealth-address products with infinite exit windows — i.e., stealth-address wallets are now composing with shielded pools. — [L2Beat Privacy](https://l2beat.com/privacy)

**Gas-from-fresh-address solutions in practice**
- Labyrinth: user operations via ERC-4337 bundlers with a paymaster paid in the transferred asset. — [Medium stealth addresses write-up](https://simbro.medium.com/privacy-in-ethereum-stealth-addresses-f05016109010)
- Paymaster risks: undercalculated gas, incorrect ERC-20 handling, access-control failures that drain the paymaster deposit. — [OtterSec, 2 Dec 2025](https://osec.io/blog/2025-12-02-paymasters-evm/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/soken_team/unlocking-security-risks-in-erc-4337-paymasters-why-most-are-vulnerable-today-4gim)
- Kohaku's Railgun integration "features operational 4337 mempool relaying for private transactions." — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/blockchains/ethereum-foundation-kohaku-sdk-privacy-wallet-integration-bb4t52)

### Inferences
- Every production stealth-address system solves gas by introducing a party (Fluidkey server with the viewing node; Umbra relayer/indexer; a paymaster) that can see the mapping between stealth addresses and the recipient. The "privacy cost" of scanning has been shifted from client CPU to a trusted-operator metadata leak — Fluidkey's shared viewing node means the operator can enumerate all of a user's receive addresses by design.
- Umbra's activity (~9 deposits/week) vs Fluidkey's claimed volume suggests the winning UX is "stealth Safe + ENS name + bank rails," not "announcer + client scan."
- The ERC-5564 ecosystem is standardised but the registry-based anonymity set (~12K Umbra registrants) is small and public; a designer could get a larger set by piggybacking on an existing shielded pool (as Cloaked does with Privacy Pools) rather than a standalone registry.

### Gaps
- No independent verification of Fluidkey's 30K users / $900M volume; no Base-specific breakdown.
- Fluidkey docs do not document the paymaster/bundler provider or the server-side scanning design, so the exact server-side metadata leak is inferred rather than cited.
- No usage metrics for ERC-5564 announcer calls across chains; no count of wallets supporting ERC-6538 registry lookups.
- No 2026 ScopeLift funding/roadmap news for Umbra.

## Key Question 4: Proving and relaying infrastructure — who pays gas, prover markets, client-side proving, relayer centralization risk

### Takeaway
Gas for private transactions is paid by one of three patterns: (a) protocol-native relayers reimbursed in-pool (Railgun broadcasters over Waku, Umbra relayer), (b) ERC-4337 bundler + paymaster paid in the transferred ERC-20 (Labyrinth, Kohaku's Railgun relaying, Fluidkey's Safe sponsorship), or (c) the user's own public wallet (which leaks). Decentralized prover markets exist for rollup-scale proofs (Succinct: 6M+ proofs in 2025, 25–31 provers; Boundless: mainnet Sept 2025, RISC Zero hosted service shut Dec 2025) but are not yet used by consumer privacy wallets, because client-side proving on phones is now feasible for small circuits (multi-second UltraHonk proofs via Mopro; iPhone OOM still a known failure mode).

### Cited Findings

**Who pays gas**
- Railgun: broadcasters submit unshield txs, pay gas, are reimbursed in any accepted token, run over Waku with no central server, enforce PPOI, see only the gas amount; wallets auto-select a broadcaster; users can switch if refused. — [ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/); [Railgun docs](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/learn/privacy-system/community-broadcasters)

### Inferences
- No production privacy wallet outsources its spend proofs to Succinct/Boundless; those markets serve rollups. Client-side proving is "good enough" for ~1–10 s circuits on flagship phones but memory-bound on iOS, which is why Railgun/Privacy Pools keep Groth16/PLONK circuits small and why Kohaku lists "faster proving" for recovery as a goal.
- The relayer layer is where metadata and legal exposure concentrate: Railgun's answer (permissionless broadcasters over Waku + PPOI enforcement at the relayer) is the most decentralized shipped design; 4337 bundler+paymaster is the most interoperable but adds a bundler/paymaster operator who sees the userOp.

## Key Question 5: Compliance primitives — proof of innocence, ASPs, selective disclosure, viewing keys, screening oracles, uptake

### Takeaway
Five compliance patterns are deployed on Ethereum in 2026: exclusion proofs against multiple public blocklists with a 1-hour standby (Railgun PPOI, using Elliptic/ScamSniffer/PureFi/SlowMist/Chainalysis Sanctions Oracle); single-operator inclusion sets with ragequit (0xbow ASP); attestation/KYC-at-entry (Panther via AMLBot/PureFi, Intmax via Predicate AVS, Hinkal KYT); per-account viewing keys (Railgun, Hinkal, Zcash-style, now standardised in L2Beat's risk model); and governed escrow/threshold de-anonymization (Panther Forensic Data Escrow, Labyrinth revokers). Measured institutional uptake of any of these is essentially unreported; the Chainalysis sanctions oracle (free, on-chain since March 2022) is the one screening primitive that privacy protocols actually consume.

### Cited Findings
- Railgun PPOI: see Q1 — five list providers, anyone can publish a list, 1-hour unshield-only standby, blinded per-provider exclusion proofs carried recursively; providers get no wallet data. — [Railgun PPOI docs](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/assurance/private-proofs-of-innocence.md)
- 0xbow ASP: Merkle root of approved labels posted by authorized "postmen," IPFS pointer to off-chain data, history buffer, ragequit as the "complaint exit path"; approval can be revoked later; screening criteria unpublished; one operator (0xbow); v2 promises "no global viewing keys or universal backdoors" and no KYC. — [Privacy Pools ASP docs](https://docs.privacypools.com/layers/asp); [EtherWorld v2](https://etherworld.co/privacy-pools-v2-brings-private-payments-onchain/); [PANews](https://panews.io/articles/m29oozp5)
- Privacy Pools paper authorship includes Chainalysis's chief scientist Jacob Illum — the ASP concept was co-designed with a screening vendor. — [PANews](https://panews.io/articles/m29oozp5)
- Chainalysis released a free on-chain sanctions oracle contract plus API in March 2022; a secondary source lists deployments on Ethereum, Polygon, Arbitrum, Optimism, Avalanche, BNB Chain, Celo (unverified against Chainalysis docs). — [Decrypt, Mar 2022](https://decrypt.co/94808/crypto-chainalysis-sanctions-screening-tools-defi-daos); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/chainalysis-launches-free-sanctions-screening-tools/); [spark.money glossary](https://www.spark.money/glossary/compliance-oracle)

### Inferences
- Railgun's PPOI is the only shipped design with pluggable, multiple list providers; 0xbow's ASP is single-operator with secret criteria — a multi-ASP or user-selectable-ASP design (as the 2023 paper envisioned) has not been built by anyone found, which is a concrete whitespace.
- "Selective disclosure" in practice means per-account viewing keys (all-or-nothing); per-transaction or per-counterparty disclosure is described in papers/SIP docs but not shipped in any Ethereum product found.
- Uptake evidence is anecdotal: ASP-gated TVL ~$9M, Railgun ~$110M, versus Tornado ~$794M. Compliance features have not yet pulled meaningful institutional volume; the institutional track (ZKsync Prividium, EthSystems, Zama) is being built as permissioned/FHE rather than as compliance-wrapped public pools.
