## Findings in detail (in reading order; ids C-1 to C-27; the ranked summary and the other required sections are at the end)

### Batch 1 (read so far: lines 1-785; overview, pool design table, screening, token, both reviews, conventions, notes, circuit, lemmas, Pool contract tables)

**C-1. Ambiguity, dangerous reading is critical. Nothing in the circuit stops the ETH fee input (input 2) being the same note as input 0 or 1, and the two contract passages disagree on whether the contract checks it.**
- Text. `[#b-circuit]` public-input table: "nf[0], nf[1] | ... | The spent set. Both must be new and different" and "nf[2] | ... | The spent set when non-zero. Must be zero exactly when relayFee is zero". `[#b-contracts]` Pool `spend` row: "the nullifiers are unused and distinct". `[#b-circuit]`: "What the circuit leaves to the contract: ... that the nullifiers are new and distinct".
- Mechanism. With `asset = ETH`, a prover can give the same ETH note N (value v) as input 0 and as input 2. Constraints 4, 5, 6 are satisfied for both (same position, same rho), so nf[2] = nf[0]. Constraint 11 then lets the prover take v out twice: `publicAmount = v` from the asset side and `relayFee = v` from the fee side (relayer = the attacker's own address). If a builder implements the table literally (nf0 != nf1; nf2 looked up in the spent set, all lookups before any marks), nf2 == nf0 passes and every ETH note doubles per spend until `shielded[ETH]` is empty. The `spend` row says "distinct", which read as pairwise-over-three closes it; the table row for nf[2] does not say it. None of the ten lemmas covers it (it is a contract duty) and no invariant in the Pool list states it.
- Second half of the same gap. "Marks the nullifiers": if a builder marks `spent[0] = true` on the first unrelayed spend (nf[2] = 0), every later unrelayed spend fails the "unused" check. Self-sent spends are the fallback when no relayer serves, so that reading removes the fallback for good.
- Fix. In the table and the `spend` row state: "nf[0], nf[1] and, when non-zero, nf[2] are pairwise different and none is in the spent set; nf[2] = 0 is never looked up and never marked; nf[0] and nf[1] must be non-zero". Add it to the Pool invariants and to the proof list as a contract-level property ("no note position is consumed twice in one call").

**C-2. Medium. Lemma 3 ("one nullifier per note") is too weak as worded: a circuit with a free `nk` or free `rho` inside the nullifier satisfies it.**
- Text `[#b-circuit]` "What the proofs must show": "One nullifier per note. The position inside each nullifier is the position proved in the tree, so a note has exactly one nullifier and a nullifier names one position."
- The checkable part is only "the position inside the nullifier is the proved position". The "so ..." does not follow from it. A circuit whose nullifier is `H4(TAG_NF, nk', pos, rho')` with `nk'` or `rho'` a free private input (not the `nk` inside `ownerPk`, not the `rho` inside `ownerCommit`) has the right position in every nullifier and lets one note be spent any number of times with fresh `nk'`/`rho'`. That circuit also passes lemma 1 (owner derived from sk), 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9. Only the top statement ("every constraint above holds") excludes it, and the page's own argument for having lemmas is that the top statement alone is not trusted.
- Fix. Word lemma 3 as: "For a non-empty input, the nullifier is a function of the note's position, of the `rho` inside its `ownerCommit` and of the `nk` inside its `ownerPk`, and of nothing else. Two accepted proofs that spend the same position publish the same nullifier. A non-empty input always publishes its nullifier (nf[2] is non-zero whenever input 2 is non-empty)."

**C-3. Medium. Lemma 8 ("nothing is left free") is false for the circuit as specified, so the proof job must change the statement, and the page does not say to what.**
- Text: "Nothing is left free. Given the private inputs, every public output is determined."
- As specified: `extHash` depends on no private input (constraint 14 only makes it "take part in one constraint"); `gRef` is itself an input and is not a function of the private inputs listed; `root` is unconstrained when all three inputs are empty (constraint 5 skips the equality); `publicAsset` is unconstrained when `publicAmount = 0` (constraint 13). So four of the 13 are free in some or all cases, by design, and the contract pins them. A prover job cannot discharge the lemma as written. It will either weaken it silently or stop. The danger is the silent weakening: "determined" might be restricted to C[0..3] only, leaving nf free.
- Fix. Replace with the exact list: "nf[0..2], C[0..3], publicAmount and relayFee are functions of the private inputs and gRef. root is determined whenever at least one input is non-empty. publicAsset is determined whenever publicAmount != 0. extHash and gRef are free in the circuit and are fixed by the contract." Also state the circom-level form that tools check: every intermediate signal is determined by the input signals.

**C-4. Medium. Lemma 10 ("non-malleability") and the top statement are not properties of a constraint system, so "machine-checked against the constraint system" cannot produce them; a builder has to guess what to prove.**
- Text: "The launch gate requires the top statement and each lemma to be machine-checked against the constraint system that goes into the ceremony". Top statement: "For every proof the verifier accepts, there exist a key sk and up to three notes such that ...". Lemma 10: "Changing any one of the 13 public inputs of an accepted proof makes it rejected."
- "For every proof the verifier accepts there exist ..." is Groth16 knowledge soundness. It depends on the ceremony output and on a group-model assumption, not on the R1CS. What can be machine-checked is "every satisfying assignment of the R1CS satisfies relation R". Lemma 10 is likewise a proof-system property (and Groth16 proofs can be re-randomised by anyone: the same public inputs accept under many different proof bytes, which is harmless here only because the contract never uses the proof bytes as an identifier). The R1CS-level fact that supports it is "each of the 13 public signals occurs in a constraint that survives the compiler", and the key-level fact is "none of the 14 IC points of the final verifying key is the identity". Neither is stated.
- Also not stated: how the hash is modelled in the proofs. Lemmas 1 and 3 ("knowing nk and spk is not enough", "exactly one nullifier") hold only under collision and preimage resistance of Poseidon, which is a compression function and so is not injective. The formal statements need an explicit axiom ("no two distinct inputs with equal output are known") and the page should say so, otherwise two proof jobs will formalise different things.
- Fix. Split the list in two: (a) R1CS-level statements, machine-checked; (b) assumptions carried by Groth16, the ceremony and Poseidon, named and not machine-checked. Replace lemma 10 by the two checkable facts above plus a test on the deployed verifier that flipping each public input rejects.

**C-5. High. No lemma and no launch-gate item asks that honest spends are accepted (completeness). With immutable contracts an over-constrained circuit locks notes for good, and nothing on the page would catch it.**
- Text: all ten lemmas and the top statement are of the form "accepted implies sound". `[#b-circuit]` constraint 3: "Every value, accrued, claim, earned, publicAmount and relayFee fits in 96 bits. gRef and each input's index fit in 90 bits." Constraint 8 fixes `earned` as a quotient that must fit 96 bits.
- Why it matters here and not only in general. Several constraints reject honest states if a parameter or an implementation detail is off: (i) `earned` must fit 96 bits, i.e. `weight x value x (gRef - index) < 2^160`; whether that always holds depends on the weight scale and on g's range (checked in a later batch against the parameter table); (ii) `gRef` must fit 90 bits, so if `g` ever reaches 2^90 on-chain no proof can be made against any checkpoint and every note in every asset is frozen for ever; the Pool invariant says "g ... stays under 2^90" but not what enforces it; (iii) a builder who implements "empty" for inputs 0 and 1 slightly differently, or forgets that an all-empty spend must still pass, removes a spend type. A canary run exercises typical values, not the ends of the schedule in real time.
- Fix. Add a lemma "Completeness: for every state the Pool can reach (every note the Pool can create, any g up to the proven maximum, any checkpoint pair), the honest witness satisfies the constraint system", with the reachable ranges taken from the parameter table. Add the supporting arithmetic lemma to the contract proofs: "g < 2^90 for every reachable sequence of calls up to the end of emission", with the bound derived from the constants.

**C-6. Low. No lemma says that input 2 is an ETH note; lemma 4 names the asset of outputs only.**
- Text: "No value is created, per asset. Outputs 0 and 1 carry the inputs' asset, output 2 carries ETH, and the sums balance over the integers with the public amount and the relay fee."
- A circuit that took input 2's asset id as a free private input would turn 10^18 base units of any 18-decimal asset (one reward token, worth a fraction of a cent at the starting valuation, or one IMD) into a 1 ETH relay fee paid to the attacker's relayer address, until `shielded[ETH]` is empty. Lemma 5's "the weight of its own asset" excludes that circuit only indirectly (weights differ), and only if the formaliser reads "its own asset" for input 2 as ETH. Constraint 4 does say "the ETH id for input 2", so the top statement covers it.
- Fix. Lemma 4: "Inputs 0 and 1 and outputs 0 and 1 carry `asset`; input 2 and output 2 carry ETH; ...". Lemma 2 should also define "non-empty" in the lemma itself (value or accrued non-zero), since a circuit that tests `value == 0` alone passes the lemma as worded and admits forged `accrued`.


### Batch 2 (whole page now read once, lines 1-2016)

**C-7. High. The relay fee is an uncapped ETH exit that pays no 0.2% and obeys no standard size. Any holder of shielded ETH can leave with any amount, fee-free, by naming their own address as "relayer".**
- Text. `[#b-circuit]` public inputs: "relayFee | ETH paid to the relayer, in wei. Zero for a self-sent spend | The pool's ETH balance" (the only check). Constraint 11: "Input value 2 equals output value 2 plus relayFee." `[#b-contracts]` `spend`: "for a public amount, requires a standard size and enough balance ..., lowers the balance, keeps amount / 500 as the exit fee; for a relay fee, lowers the ETH balance ... Last, pays the recipient and the relayer named in ext." `spend` is callable by "Anyone" and the relayer is just an address inside `ext`. `[#parameters]`: "Relay fee | public, in ETH | to confirm | Paid from a shielded ETH note in the same proof, to the relayer named in the call". No passage bounds the amount (searched every occurrence of "relay fee"/"relayFee").
- Mechanism. Alice holds a 7.3134 ETH note. She builds a spend with that note as input 2, `relayFee = 7.3134 ETH`, `relayer =` a fresh address of hers, `publicAmount = 0`, and sends it from any funded address (or through a helper contract that forwards the fee). The pool lowers `shielded[ETH]` and pays 7.3134 ETH. No exit fee is kept and no size check runs. Cost to her: one spend's gas, the same as an honest withdrawal. A relayer service can offer this to others by naming a splitter contract as relayer.
- What breaks. (a) The owner's fixed "0.2% out": for ETH it becomes optional, so the distributor, stakers, screeners and buyback get the entry half only on ETH. (b) "Funds leave only in standard sizes" (`#overview`, `#lifecycle`, design table "Exits: Public withdrawals only in a 1, 2, 5 series"; `#b-contracts` invariant list) is false for ETH: arbitrary amounts leave and can be matched to deposits by amount, and every user who takes the cheaper path is removed from the standard-size crowd. (c) "A remainder under 0.005 ETH ... cannot leave by itself" (`#decisions`) is false (harmless, but shows the path was not considered). (d) `#visibility` "the ETH fee ... says nothing about what was moved" stops being true once the fee field carries withdrawals.
- Fix (keeps every fixed decision). Bound the fee in the contract by something an attacker cannot set for free: `relayFee <= (K x block.basefee + TIP) x GAS_CEILING`, with build constants such as K = 3, TIP = 2 gwei, GAS_CEILING = 2,000,000. `block.basefee` is burned, so even a block builder cannot inflate it cheaply (do not use `tx.gasprice`: a builder pays the tip to itself). Then the most that can leave through the fee field is a small multiple of the gas the same transaction burns, which is never worth doing to save 0.2%. State the bound in the circuit table ("Checked by the pool against"), the `spend` row, the invariants and the wallet rule (a quote above the bound is rejected before proving). The wallet must leave margin for a falling base fee between proof and inclusion.

**C-8. Medium. The page does not say what `spend` does when the payment to the recipient or relayer fails, and each reading hurts someone. With "revert", any user can make a relayer burn about 1.1M gas for about 30k of their own, repeatedly, with the same proof.**
- Text. `[#b-contracts]` `spend`: "Last, pays the recipient and the relayer named in ext." `[#b-conventions]`: "ETH is sent with call, never transfer." Nothing on gas forwarded, on failure, or on order between the two payments. `#visibility`: "A relayer refuses or delays | ... | A short delay" is the only relayer failure considered; `[#spec-fees]` says the relayer's margin is "about 10%".
- Reading 1, failure reverts the spend. Attacker owns a note of the smallest size and a fee note. Recipient is a contract of theirs whose `receive` reverts when a storage flag is set. The relayer simulates (flag clear, passes) and broadcasts. The attacker sees it and sets the flag in front (about 27k gas). The relayer's transaction runs the whole spend (roll, proof check, 33 Poseidon calls, three nullifier writes) and reverts at the last step: about 1.1M gas today, 1.5M under Glamsterdam, lost. The attacker's notes are unspent and the proof is still valid, so the next relayer gets the same. Ratio about 40 to 1. Relaying is described as near-zero margin, so a few dollars a day empties the 0.15 ETH floats or pushes relayers to refuse every ETH withdrawal to an address with code (which includes Safes and EIP-7702 accounts). Without relayers users send their own spends and show an address.
- Reading 2, failure is ignored. `shielded[ETH]` is already lowered and the ETH stays in the contract with no ledger entry: the user's withdrawal is lost for good.
- Also undefined: gas forwarded. With all gas forwarded and the recipient paid first, a recipient can burn the relayer's whole gas limit.
- Fix. Pay the relayer first. Pay the recipient with a fixed gas allowance (a build constant large enough for a Safe, for example 50,000). If that call fails, do not revert: credit `owed[recipient] += amount` and add `claimOwed(address)` callable by anyone that sends to that address only. Add the slot to storage, to the invariant "No function sends an asset anywhere except" and to the gas table. Token transfers that fail (frozen recipient) should still revert, since only the issuer can cause that between simulation and inclusion.
- Related low-cost grief that remains by design and should be written into the relayer software rules: the same nullifiers proved for two relayers. The second transaction reverts at the nullifier check after paying intrinsic gas and about 1.9 KB of calldata. The wallet's own retry rule ("pick again and prove again" in `[#spec-mesh]`) produces exactly this when the first relayer was slow and not dead.

**C-9. Medium (ambiguity; dangerous reading freezes every note). Reward weights are given only as ratios "per whole unit". The integer weights the circuit and pool must share, the decimals of IMD, and the proof that g stays under 2^90 are not on the page, and the answer changes by a factor of hundreds between plausible encodings.**
- Text. `[#parameters]`: "Reward weights | ETH 1 : USDT 1/2,000 : USDC 1/2,000 : IMD about 1/800 : token 0 | decided | Per whole unit". `[#b-conventions]`: "Reward index | One number g for the whole pool, at most 90 bits, fixed-point with 64 fractional bits" and "The list and each asset's weight are constants in the circuit and the pool". `[#b-circuit]` constraint 3: "gRef and each input's index fit in 90 bits". Pool invariant: "g never decreases and stays under 2^90". `[#b-deploy]`: canary constants are "starting caps of 0.5 ETH, 1,000 USDT, 1,000 USDC and 50 IMD" with the same supply.
- Arithmetic (mine). Per base unit the ratios are ETH 1 : USDT 5x10^8 : IMD 1/800 if IMD has 18 decimals, so integer weights need a scale S on ETH of at least about 800. The page never states S or IMD's decimals. The largest g can get is when the pool stays under the floor for the whole schedule (true for at least the first 33 days, when nothing can be activated, and for an unused pool for ever): g_max / 2^64 = 8 steps x 4 x E / (sum of step multipliers x W0), where E = 45% of supply in base units and W0 = weighted capacity at step 0. With supply 10^9, the recommended stop (multipliers sum to 127), W0 = 78.1 ETH-equivalent:
  - S = 800: g_max about 2^74.8. Fine.
  - S = 1 (possible if IMD's weight is handled as a fraction, or IMD has fewer decimals): g_max about 2^84.5. Fine, 5.5 bits of room.
  - S = 1 and supply at the page's stated limit of 79 billion: g_max about 2^90.8. Over.
  - S = 1 on the canary (caps 50 times smaller, same supply): g_max about 2^90.1. Over, in a deployment that is meant to prove the build.
- What happens at 2^90 is not specified. If the pool lets g pass 2^90, every later checkpoint fails the circuit's 90-bit range check and no note in any asset can ever be spent. If the pool reverts, `roll` reverts and so do `activate` and `spend`. Only a clamp is safe, and the page does not say clamp.
- The same constants decide two more bounds the lemmas rely on: constraint 8's left side is below p only if `2 x weight + weightEth < about 2^67` (USDT's weight is 5x10^8 x S, so S must stay under about 10^10); and `earned` fits 96 bits only because a note's weighted value never exceeds `weighted`, which is a Pool invariant the page does not list.
- Fix. Put the integer weight table in `[#parameters]` (one integer per asset per base unit, with each asset's decimals), add a derived row "largest possible g" for the real build and for the canary with the working shown, make "g clamps at 2^90 - 1 and emission stops" the specified behaviour, and add the Pool invariant "for each asset, the sum of live note values is at most shielded[asset]" that the 96-bit bound on `earned` depends on.


### Batch 3 (second pass over notes, restore, refund rule, privacy)

**C-10. Medium. "Anyone may refund a refused deposit" turns every temporary refusal into a permanent bounce, and the page relies on temporary refusals in three places.**
- Text. `[#b-contracts]` Pool `refund`: "The depositor at any time before activation. Anyone, while the registry marks the deposit refused". `[#b-states]` diagram: "Refused --> Covered: a later batch clears it" and "Refused --> Refunded: refund, by anyone, to the depositor". `#screening`: "A refusal can be added or lifted until the moment of activation" and the example criteria: `{ "hold": "amount(deposit) > 10 ETH-equivalent && firstFunded(depositor) < 72h", "epochs": 3 }` with "flags ... expireAfterEpochs: 14". Registry `isRefused(id)`: "The deposit's bit is set". The Batch carries only `upTo` and the refusal bitmap.
- Mechanism. A "hold" has no state of its own in the registry. It can only be encoded as the refusal bit set for some epochs and cleared later (the other encoding, keeping `upTo` below the held deposit, stalls every deposit behind it, so one refundable large deposit from a fresh address every three days would stop all activations; the page does not say which encoding is meant). While the bit is set, anyone calls `refund(id, record)`; the record is public (`Deposited` event plus the block time). Cost: about 40k gas. The deposit is gone before the clearing batch arrives. The caller can also wait for the clearing batch to be accepted and front-run its `apply`. So "a refusal that a later batch clears" (a canary line) is reachable only when nobody bothers.
- Who has a motive. Anyone already inside (rewards are shared by weight, and the page says so: "blockers already inside the pool keep the rewards to themselves"); anyone queuing for cap space at a full cap; a third of screeners, who can raise a flag quorum, have the deposit bounced, and let the flag lapse.
- What they gain. The victim must deposit again and wait the full 1d16h to 2d16h again. Large fresh deposits (the ones a hold targets) can be kept out for as long as the hold rule keeps matching.
- Fix. Keep the rule but give it an age: "anyone may refund a deposit that is refused and at least N days old", with N a build constant longer than the longest hold and the flag expiry (for example 21 days). Refused funds still do not sit in the pool for long, and a transient refusal can clear. State in `#screening` how a hold is encoded in a batch.

**C-11. Medium. "Many unlinkable addresses" is not supported by the address format: every address of a wallet carries the same announced encryption key, and the announcement is a public transaction from some Ethereum address.**
- Text. `[#b-notes]`: "ownerPk = H4(TAG_PK, spk, nk, d) // d: a diversifier, so one wallet can hand out many unlinkable addresses" and "Address a payer needs: ownerPk (32 bytes) and the recipient's X-Wing public key (1,216 bytes). Short form: ownerPk and keccak256(key). The key is announced once through Pool.announce and read from the public snapshots." `[#b-contracts]`: "announce(keys) | Anyone | Emits an encryption key".
- Mechanism. Two addresses from one wallet have different `ownerPk` and the same key (or key hash). Any two payers who compare notes, or anyone who sees two published addresses, link them at once. There is no key-private diversification for a KEM public key, so the alternative is one X-Wing key per address: one `announce` (about 50k gas, 1,216 bytes) and one more trial decryption per spend per address. Second leak: `KeysAnnounced` is emitted by a transaction whose sender is public. A user who announces from their own account ties every address they ever hand out to that account, for every payer who looks the key up. No relayer path exists for `announce` (relayers are paid only through `spend`).
- Fix. Either drop the claim and say "addresses of one wallet are linkable by their encryption key", or specify per-address keys (derived from the seed and `d`, each announced once, with a wallet cap on how many are scanned). In both cases specify who sends `announce`: recommend that the wallet sends it from the depositing address before the first deposit and tells the user that the key, and so the shielded address, is then tied to that account for anyone who is given the address; or let `spend` carry an optional announcement so a relayer can send it.

**C-12. Medium. A user who is paid inside the pool and holds no shielded ETH cannot make any relayed spend. The page rates the fee-note requirement "Low" and says the wallet "keeps a fee note topped up", which a new recipient cannot do.**
- Text. `#risks`: "A relayed spend needs a small shielded ETH note for the fee, and the fee amount is public | Low | The wallet keeps a fee note topped up." `[#b-circuit]`: input 2 "Nullifier of the ETH note that pays the relayer"; constraint 4 puts all three inputs under one `sk`. Ciphertext 0 describes output 0 only (`[#b-notes]`), so a payer cannot also hand the recipient an ETH note in the same spend.
- Mechanism. Bob receives 500 USDC privately. To move or withdraw it through a relayer he needs an ETH note under his own key. His options: (a) deposit ETH from a public account and wait 1d16h to 2d16h, which shows an account of his as a pool user at that moment and needs ETH on that account; (b) send the spend himself, which shows his account as the sender of that withdrawal; (c) ask the payer to send a second spend with an ETH note. Revision 4.1 (fee in the asset moved) did not have this hole; 4.2 trades it for cheaper relaying and does not say so.
- Fix inside the fixed decisions. Either let output 2's owner be described in ciphertext 0 (add 12 bytes: "fee change value", so a payer can gift the fee change to the recipient in the same spend; the format has 16 memo bytes and 20 spare bytes in ciphertext 1 but none in ciphertext 0), or state the limitation in `#lifecycle` and `#risks` at its real weight and make "send a first ETH note with the first payment" a wallet default.

**C-13. Low. The disclosure receipt cannot do what it says for this pool without revealing `nk`, which is wallet-wide.**
- Text. `[#spec-disclose]`: "NoteOpening[] notes; // per note: asset, value, owner key, randomness, leaf index, Merkle path ... The recipient recomputes each commitment from its opening, checks it sits in the pool's tree, and checks the nullifier on-chain ... A receipt is narrower than a viewing key because it covers one transaction."
- Mechanism. Here `nullifier = H4(TAG_NF, nk, pos, rho)`. To check a nullifier the reader needs `nk` (and `spk`, `d` to tie `nk` to `ownerPk`). `nk` is the same for every note of the wallet. A reader who holds `nk` and has ever paid the user (so knows that note's `rho` and position) can compute its nullifier and see when it is spent; with `spk` it can also test whether any `ownerPk` it meets belongs to the same wallet. That is wider than one transaction.
- Fix. For this pool the receipt opens commitments only, and proves "this nullifier belongs to this opened note" with a small separate zero-knowledge proof made off-chain (no ceremony needed if a transparent system is used), or omits the nullifier check and says so.

**C-14. Low. Which root and which checkpoint a wallet proves against is public and is left to the wallet; a natural policy tells observers that a spend consumes a note made in the last hour.**
- Text. `[#b-states]`: "A proof built against a checkpoint root and gCur stays valid until two more rolls have happened"; public inputs `root` ("The last 64 roots and the roots saved at the last two checkpoints") and `gRef` ("gCur or gPrev").
- Mechanism. A wallet that prefers the checkpoint root (longer validity) must fall back to a recent root when an input is newer than the checkpoint (its own change from the previous withdrawal, or a payment just received). A spend whose root is not a checkpoint root then says "one of my inputs was created since the last roll". In a pool with a few spends an hour that is a handful of candidate leaves, and it links the steps of a withdrawal that is split into several standard sizes, which the page says the wallet does routinely. `gRef = gPrev` likewise marks a proof built before the last roll.
- Fix. A wallet rule in the spec: always prove against the newest root and `gCur`, or always against the checkpoint pair and wait for the next roll before spending anything newer. Never mix.

**C-15. Low. "No claim to observe" holds for the conversion step only; withdrawing the reward token is public and bounds value times time.**
- Text. `#overview`: "The reward is collected inside the proof when a note is spent, so there is no claim to observe." `#token`: "nobody can tell a claim from a payment. Tornado's reward scheme leaked deposit age at exactly that step."
- Mechanism. Reward tokens are useful only outside the pool. A token withdrawal of standard size X (1,000 to 100,000,000) shows that the spender held at least X of reward, which at a known rate (251 or 504 tokens per ETH-equivalent per day) means at least X / rate ETH-days. Deposits and their ages are public, so this narrows the set of depositors the withdrawal could belong to, the same kind of leak as Tornado's, coarser by the 1-2-5 sizes. Private transfers of token notes blur it only if a market for shielded tokens exists.
- Fix. Say it on the page, and have the wallet warn before withdrawing reward tokens to an address linked to other exits, and prefer many small sizes over one large.

**C-16. Ambiguity (dangerous reading: wallets that cannot restore each other's notes; no working view-only wallet). The derivation of everything from "the seed" is named but not specified.**
- Text. `[#b-notes]`: "rho and d for a deposit come from a keyed hash of the seed and a per-wallet deposit counter ... rho for outputs 1, 2 and 3 of a spend comes from a keyed hash of the seed, the spend's first nullifier and the slot"; ciphertext 1 "sealed with a key from the seed and the first nullifier"; "nk ... safe to give to a view-only wallet".
- Missing: how `sk` and the X-Wing key come from the seed; the hash, key and domain string of each "keyed hash"; the `d` used for a wallet's own outputs 1 to 3; how far past the last hit a restore keeps trying deposit counters; how an empty input's `rho`, `leafIndex` and `slot` are chosen (must be fresh randomness: a deterministic choice repeats a dummy nullifier after a restore and the spend reverts as "used"); the AEAD and nonce of ciphertext 1; the HPKE KDF, AEAD and `info` string (only the KEM id 0x647A is given). A view-only wallet needs `spk`, `nk`, the decapsulation key, and the keys behind ciphertext 1 and the output `rho`s. If those last two are derived from `sk` the view-only wallet cannot follow change notes and shows a wrong balance after the first spend; if from the seed directly, the page must name a separate "view root". "nk is safe to give" is true (nothing can be spent or blocked with it) and is not enough to view anything.
- Fix. A derivation table in `[#b-notes]`: seed -> spend root (`sk`), view root (ciphertext-1 keys, output `rho`s, deposit `rho`/`d`), X-Wing seed; exact functions and labels; gap limit; `d` rule for own outputs; dummy-input rule. Reusing a deposit counter after a partial restore repeats `ownerCommit` on-chain and links two depositing accounts, so the gap rule matters for privacy too.


### Batch 4 (third pass: fee-note bootstrap, schedule arithmetic, smaller items)

**C-17. Medium. Inside one spend the fee side and the asset side cannot exchange value, even when the asset is ETH. A wallet whose only ETH note is its deposit can pay relayers for ever and can never move or withdraw anything through one.**
- Text. `[#b-circuit]` constraint 11: "Input values 0 and 1 add up to output values 0 and 1 plus publicAmount. Input value 2 equals output value 2 plus relayFee." Public inputs: nf[2] "Must be zero exactly when relayFee is zero". `#lifecycle`: "The relayer is paid in ETH out of a small shielded ETH note you keep for the purpose."
- Mechanism. Carol deposits 1 ETH and activates. She holds one note. For a relayed withdrawal she needs it as input 0 (to fund `publicAmount`) and needs a different non-empty ETH note as input 2 (the same note in both places is the double use of C-1). If she puts her one note in as input 2 with empty inputs 0 and 1, the only non-zero output is output 2: one note again, smaller by the fee. No relayed spend ever gives her a second note. Her ways out are a second public deposit (a second 0.2%, about 830k gas, another two-day wait) or sending one spend herself to split the note, which shows her account as the sender of a pool transaction. The page never says how the first fee note comes to exist. Together with C-12 this means the relay path does not work for a new depositor or a new recipient until they have done something public a second time.
- Fix (circuit, so before the ceremony). When `asset` is ETH, replace the two equations of constraint 11 by their sum: `in0 + in1 + in2 = out0 + out1 + out2 + publicAmount + relayFee`. A selector on `asset == ETH` costs a handful of constraints. Input 2 still has to be non-empty when a fee is paid, so nothing new is revealed. Carol then uses her one note as input 2 and gets a withdrawal, change and a fee note out of one relayed spend, and any ETH spend can top the fee note up. Add the case to lemma 4 and to the canary list ("a relayed spend by a wallet that holds a single ETH note").

**C-18. Low. Three more gaps in the lemma list.**
- Lemma 9 ("No sum or product in constraints 8, 11 and 12 can reach the field modulus") leaves out the other places where an integer reading is assumed: the `packed` word in constraints 4, 9 and 10 (fields must not overlap and the word must be below p, which is what makes a commitment bind asset, value and accrued), the emptiness test `value + 2^96 x accrued` in constraint 5, `4 x leafIndex + slot` in constraint 6, and `gRef - index` in constraint 7. They hold today because of the ranges in constraint 3; the lemma should name them so a later edit to a range cannot pass.
- Lemma 10 speaks of "any one of the 13 public inputs". Groth16's check is linear in the public inputs, so the property that matters is about any change to the vector, not one coordinate (two inputs that entered every constraint only through their sum could be changed together). The checkable form is "the 14 IC points of the verifying key are pairwise distinct, none is the identity, and the public columns of the constraint system are linearly independent".
- Nothing states that all eleven statements are non-vacuous. See C-5: a circuit that accepts nothing satisfies every one of them.

**C-19. Ambiguity (dangerous reading: `roll` reverts for ever some years out, which freezes every spend). The schedule arithmetic has no stated end.**
- Text. `#pool` design table: caps "doubling every 90 days in code. Recommended: stop after five doublings". `[#parameters]`: "Caps ... to confirm ... Recommended stop". `[#b-contracts]`: "floor = a quarter of Σ weight[asset] × cap(asset) at the current step"; "g += seconds × emission(step) × 2^64 / max(weighted, floor(step)) // per cap step crossed"; "emission ... zero afterwards"; storage `g ... uint128`, `weighted ... uint128`; "roll ... advances the reward index to now"; "Activation, refund, roll and spend have no end date."
- If the owner does not confirm the stop, `cap = start × 2^step` grows without limit. A builder who computes `floor(step)` or `cap(asset)` for the current step on every `roll` (the formula as written does, even when emission is zero) hits a checked-arithmetic overflow once `weight × cap × 2^step` passes the integer type: about step 58 for a uint128 cap in wei (14 years), later for uint256. From then on `roll` reverts, and `spend` and `activate` call `roll` first. Nothing can be withdrawn. Even with the stop, the page should say that the step used in every formula is clamped.
- Also unstated: the initial `gTime` (must be GENESIS; a zero start makes the first advance count 56 years of seconds), the initial `window`, `gCur`, `rootCur` (zero or the empty-tree root), and what Pool calls do before GENESIS.
- Fix. One sentence in the reward-index box: "step = min(floor((now − GENESIS) / 90 days), LAST_STEP) in every formula; after the eighth step the index is never advanced again and `roll` only rotates the checkpoint roots". List the initial values of every Pool storage variable.

**C-20. Low (ambiguity). The pool "does not check ciphertexts" and also "every spend publishes the same 1,312 bytes". The length is not said to be enforced.**
- Text `[#b-notes]`: "Every spend publishes the same 1,312 bytes whatever it does" and "The pool does not check ciphertexts."
- If lengths are free, a spend's size becomes a fingerprint (wallet versions, memo use) and a relayer can be handed arbitrary data to publish. Fix: `spend` requires `ct0.length == 1216` and `ct1.length == 96`, stated in the `spend` row and counted in the gas model.

**C-21. Low. The verifying-key check is a short list for a step the page calls the cause of two drained verifiers.**
- Text `[#b-contracts]` Verifier: "A separate script checks the key itself: gamma and delta distinct, every point on the curve, 14 input points."
- Missing from the list: G2 points in the correct subgroup (on-curve is not enough for G2 on BN254); alpha, beta, gamma, delta not the identity and delta, gamma not the generator they start as; the 14 input points pairwise distinct and none the identity (this is the key-level half of lemma 10); the key recomputed from the final ceremony file by a second tool; the contribution chain verified from the first file to the last.

**C-22. Low. `roll` inside `spend` makes one case of proof expiry and one cost unpredictable; both need a wallet and relayer rule.**
- Text `[#b-contracts]`: "spend ... In this order. Calls roll." and "roll ... One roll per call". `[#b-states]`: "A proof built against a checkpoint root and gCur stays valid until two more rolls have happened".
- "Two more rolls" can be minutes apart, not an hour: a proof built in a window where no checkpoint has been taken yet uses the old `gCur`; if someone rolls later in that window and the spend lands just after the hour turns, the spend's own `roll` pushes its `gRef` out and it reverts. Anyone can cause the first of those rolls for about 60k gas. It only hits spends in flight across the boundary, so it is a nuisance. The rule to write down: if no checkpoint exists for the current window, the wallet (or relayer) calls `roll` first and proves against the new `gCur`. Second, the first transaction of each window pays for the checkpoint (five or six storage writes, 25k to 70k gas depending on the rule set), which a relayer cannot know when it quotes; the quote should assume it.

**C-23. Ambiguity. How the Pool learns the token's address, and what it does with the token asset before launch, is not in the Pool section.**
- Text. `[#b-deploy]`: "No address is passed to a constructor: each contract works out the others' addresses from the Deployer's address and the fixed order". `[#b-contracts]` Distribution: "Creates the Token with CREATE2 and a salt taken from the block's prevrandao and the caller, so its address does not exist and cannot be known before this block ... token(), pool(), liquidity() | Views | Read by the other contracts. Zero before launch". Pool storage has no token slot; `deposit` is open to "Anyone, until the deposit window closes".
- A builder must decide: read `Distribution.token()` on every token operation (an extra cold call in every token deposit, withdrawal and sweep, not in the gas model) or cache it once; and reject asset 5 while it is zero. The wrapper that "accepts tokens returning no value" must also require code at the target, or a call to the zero address "succeeds" and only the arrived-amount check stands between that and a phantom token deposit. The arrived-amount check does stop it as written; say so, and say the token asset reverts before launch.

**C-24. Numbers that do not agree (not security).**
- Emission length. `#token`: "over two years" and "Emissions run two years"; `[#parameters]`: "Emission | 8 steps of 90 days" and `[#b-contracts]` "spread over eight cap steps" = 720 days. Founder and swarm vesting and the screeners' shares are 730.
- Which schedule the reward figures use. `#decisions`: "The build specification is written to the recommended value" (stop after five doublings). `#token`: "about 0.55% a year on a full pool, and four times that on a pool a quarter full or less"; `#sim`: "251 tokens", the unlock table (users 0.18% at month 3, 45% at 24), "half of them arrive in the last 90 days", cap rows "540 to 630 | 1,600 ETH" and "630 to 720 | 3,200 ETH", "about 21,900 IMD" over two years. All of these are the no-stop schedule (I re-ran `reports/sim/murmuration_economics.py`: its main tables are labelled "v4 default", and the stop is a separate "recommended variant" giving 504 tokens and 1.09%). Under the schedule the build is written to, a full pool pays about 1.09% a year at the start price, users have 0.35% after three months, and the last three steps pay 25.2% each. Only one bullet in `#sim` says so.
- First emissions. Nothing can be activated for about 33 days after launch (`[#b-deploy]`), and launch cannot be before GENESIS, so at least 37% of the first step's emission is never released. It is 0.3% of the users' share with the stop and less without; the page does not mention it where it says the 45% goes to users.
- Activation gas. The parts on the page give 32 × 18,624 + 51,826 = 647,794 for the insert, plus 35,117 for the commitment, 21,000 base, the registry call, and 10 to 14 storage overwrites when the call also rolls: about 780k to 800k today, against "about 750,000". Within "about", and worth re-stating once measured.

**C-25. Low (edge of my scope). Outgoing transfers are not measured, only incoming ones.**
- Text `#review` other findings: "The pool checks the amount that arrived." `[#b-contracts]` `sweepFees`: "Sends the asset's accrued fees to the distributor and calls its notify"; FeeDistributor `notify(asset, amount)`.
- If Tether switches its fee on, a sweep of `amount` USDT delivers less than `amount` to the distributor, and `notify` books the full figure. The distributor is then short in USDT for its last claimants. Fix: `notify` books its own balance increase, not the argument. (The Pool's own books stay right, as the page says.)


**C-26. High. Fee notes live under the ETH cap. When the ETH cap is full, which the page itself expects, no newcomer can get a fee note, so holders of USDT, USDC and IMD who arrive later cannot use a relayer at all.**
- Text. `[#b-contracts]` `activate`: "For a capped asset, requires shielded + value to fit under the cap." `deposit`: "a value of at least the asset's smallest exit size". `#risks`: "A 25 ETH cap can be filled by one depositor" and "Whoever fills a cap first collects that step's rewards for the price of the 0.2% entry fee, and others cannot get in until the next step". `[#b-circuit]`: the relay fee is paid only from a shielded ETH note under the spender's own key.
- Mechanism. A farmer (or an attacker who wants this outcome) activates 25 ETH on the first day activation is possible. Cost: 0.05 ETH of entry fee, and it earns the ETH share of rewards. From then on every `activate` of ETH reverts, including a 0.005 ETH deposit meant only as a fee note. Dave deposits and activates 5,000 USDC (the USDC cap has room). He has no ETH note and cannot get one: not by deposit (cap), not from his own notes (C-17), not from a payer unless that payer sends a separate ETH spend (C-12). Every spend he makes must be sent from a public account of his, which ties that account to each withdrawal. The space that relay fees free up is about 0.00025 ETH per relayed spend, so roughly twenty relayed spends by others make room for one smallest deposit. At each doubling the same party can refill first.
- Why High. It is the expected state of the pool, not a corner. It removes the relay path, and with it sender privacy, for exactly the users the stablecoin listing is meant to attract, and it is cheap to cause on purpose (the capital comes back).
- Fix (no fixed decision touched). A separate small lane for fee notes: ETH deposits of the smallest two or three sizes are admitted above the cap up to a second ceiling (for example 5% of the ETH cap, one per depositing address per day). The loss bound moves from the cap to 1.05 times the cap. Combine with C-17's merged conservation for ETH and C-12's fee-note gift so that the first fee note can also arrive privately.

**Corrections to earlier batches.** C-9: the scale S on ETH must stay under about 2x10^11 (not 10^10) for constraint 8 to stay below p when the asset is a stablecoin. C-19: with a uint128 the overflow comes between step 54 and step 64 depending on the weight scale, that is 13 to 16 years after GENESIS. C-3: if "public output" is read as only nf[0..2] and C[0..3], the lemma is true for the specified circuit; the page calls all 13 "public inputs" and never says which are outputs, so the finding stands as an ambiguity and is graded Low. C-17: a wallet can avoid the problem today by making two deposits (the amount and a separate fee note) at the cost of one more deposit and activation; the page should say so if the circuit is not changed.


**C-27. Low (first graded Medium; lowered after I measured the leak, see the last line of the mechanism). Ciphertext 1 is sealed "with a key from the seed and the first nullifier". A retried spend has the same first nullifier, so two different records are sealed under one key, and the first one is already in someone else's hands.**
- Text. `[#b-notes]`: "Ciphertext 1, 96 bytes, is the sender's own record, sealed with a key from the seed and the first nullifier: the value and accrued of output 1 (12 and 12), the value and accrued of output 2 (12 and 12), the claim in output 3 (12), and 20 spare bytes, with a 16-byte tag." 80 bytes of record plus a 16-byte tag leaves no room for a nonce. `[#spec-mesh]`: "on refusal or timeout: ... pick again and prove again". `extHash` names the relayer, and `relayFee` is that relayer's quote, so a retry is a new proof with the same inputs, the same nf[0], and normally a different fee, hence a different "value of output 2".
- Mechanism. The first relayer holds ciphertext A from the attempt it refused or sat on. The retry lands with ciphertext B. With any stream-based AEAD and a fixed nonce (the only reading the 96 bytes allow), A xor B equals record A xor record B. The fields that did not change cancel to zero. The fee-change field gives (F − feeA) xor (F − feeB) with both fees public. That reveals the carry chain of the subtraction, and with it each bit of the hidden value at every position where the carry and the difference disagree. If the user also changed the amount, the change value leaks the same way. If the first attempt was mined and reverted (stale checkpoint, expired root, the grief in C-8), its calldata is public and everyone can do this, not only the relayer. The one-time authentication key is reused as well.
- How much leaks (measured with a short script, `xorleak.py` in my scratch folder). For a fee retry with two quotes about 5% apart: 14 of the 55 bits of the fee-change value, all in the low 45 bits. That is a fingerprint of the fee note, not its size. For a retry that changes a withdrawal from 2 ETH to 1 ETH: 25 of 61 bits of the change value, reaching the top bits. More retries leak more. It is a real misuse of the cipher with a limited leak, hence Low.
- Fix. Derive the key from the seed, the first nullifier and the three commitments C[1], C[2], C[3] (all in the `Spent` event, so restore still works), or seal with a nonce-misuse-resistant mode (a synthetic-IV construction fits 80 + 16 bytes exactly and leaks only whether two records are equal). Say which in `[#b-notes]`.

## Final sections

Grades below are final and replace the grade in a detail entry where they differ (C-3 and C-4 are Low here). Counts: 0 certain critical; 3 high (C-7, C-26, C-5); 6 medium (C-17, C-12, C-8, C-10, C-11, C-2); 6 ambiguities, two of them with a critical or pool-freezing dangerous reading (C-1, C-9, then C-16, C-19, C-20, C-23); 11 low (C-3, C-4, C-6, C-13, C-14, C-15, C-18, C-21, C-22, C-25, C-27). ### 1. The most serious findings, ranked by damage times likelihood

1. **C-7, High, certain as designed. The relay fee is an ETH exit with no 0.2%, no standard size and no limit.** `relayFee` is checked only against "The pool's ETH balance", and the relayer is any address named in `ext`. Any holder of shielded ETH can leave with any amount, fee-free, by naming an address of their own as relayer. It defeats two of the owner's fixed decisions for ETH (0.2% out, exits in standard sizes) and the page's headline "funds leave only in standard sizes". Fix: the contract bounds `relayFee` by a multiple of `block.basefee` times a gas ceiling plus a small tip allowance; stated in the table, the `spend` row, the invariants and the wallet rules.
2. **C-1, ambiguity with a critical reading. Input 2 can be the same ETH note as input 0 or 1, and only one of two contract passages says the contract must stop it.** The circuit permits it (same position, same nullifier). The public-input table says nf[2] is checked against "The spent set when non-zero"; the `spend` row says "unused and distinct". A check-all-then-mark-all implementation of the table doubles every ETH note per spend until `shielded[ETH]` is gone. Fix: state pairwise distinctness over the three nullifiers and "zero is never looked up or marked" in both places and in the invariants, and also add the two inequalities to the circuit (a few constraints), since the circuit is the part that can never be corrected.
3. **C-26, High, expected state of the pool. Fee notes sit under the ETH cap, so a full ETH cap shuts newcomers in every asset out of relaying.** The page expects one depositor to fill the 25 ETH cap. After that no ETH deposit of any size can be activated, so a later USDT, USDC or IMD depositor cannot obtain a fee note, and every spend they make shows one of their accounts. Cheap to cause on purpose: the capital comes back and earns rewards. No fix is free. Options, in my order of preference: (a) for withdrawals only, where the asset is public anyway, let the relayer be paid a public amount of the withdrawn asset out of the shielded remainder (not out of the payout, so payouts stay uniform), charged the 0.2% and bounded to a fraction of the size; this changes the circuit and must be settled before the ceremony; (b) a small cap-exempt lane for fee-size ETH deposits, which stops this being the default state but can itself be filled for about $90 of gas; (c) at the least, say on the page that with a full ETH cap newcomers in other assets have no private path.
4. **C-5, High. Every statement in the proof list is satisfied by a circuit that accepts nothing, and no statement asks that honest spends are accepted.** All eleven are "if accepted, then sound". The page's own risk table names "statements that are true of anything" as the known failure of agent-written proofs, and extra constraints make soundness proofs easier. In an immutable pool an over-constrained corner (a range that is one bit short, an edge of the reward division, a spend type nobody tried) is funds locked for good. The canary covers typical spends, not the reachable extremes. Fix: a completeness lemma over the reachable state space, with the ranges taken from the parameter table, plus the contract-side arithmetic lemma that g stays under its bound.
5. **C-17 with C-12, Medium, certain. The first fee note cannot be made privately.** Value cannot pass between the fee side and the asset side of a spend even when the asset is ETH, so a wallet with one ETH note can pay relayers and move nothing; and a user who was paid in another asset has no ETH note at all and cannot be given one in the same spend. Both need a second public step (another deposit, or a self-sent spend). Fix: when `asset` is ETH, replace the two conservation equations by their sum (circuit change, a few constraints); let a payer direct the fee change (output 2) to the recipient using a memo convention so the recipient can find it; write the two-deposit rule into the wallet if the circuit stays as it is.
6. **C-9, ambiguity with a pool-freezing reading. The integer reward weights, IMD's decimals and the proof that g stays under 2^90 are not on the page.** With the stated ratios and a one-billion supply, the worst-case g is about 2^74.8 if ETH's weight is scaled by 800 and about 2^84.5 if it is 1; at the page's own supply limit of 79 billion with scale 1 it is 2^90.8, and on the canary (caps fifty times smaller, same supply) with scale 1 it is 2^90.1. What the pool does at 2^90 is unspecified, and two of the three possible behaviours stop every spend for ever. Fix: publish the integer table, the derived maximum of g for the real build and the canary, and "g clamps and emission stops" as the rule.
7. **C-8, Medium. What `spend` does when paying the recipient fails is unspecified; under "revert" a user can make a relayer burn about 1.1M gas for about 30k of their own, repeatedly with one proof; under "ignore" the withdrawal is lost.** Relayers are described as near-zero margin. Fix: pay the relayer first, pay the recipient with a fixed gas allowance, and on failure credit an `owed` balance that anyone can push to that address later.
8. **C-10, Medium. Anyone can refund a deposit while its refusal bit is set, so a hold or a refusal that would have been lifted becomes a permanent bounce for about 45k gas.** Those already inside, and anyone queuing at a full cap, have a motive. Fix: third-party refund only for a refused deposit older than a build constant (longer than the longest hold and the flag expiry); state how a hold is encoded in a batch.
9. **C-11, Medium. "Many unlinkable addresses" does not hold: all addresses of a wallet carry the same announced X-Wing key, and the announcement is a public transaction.** Fix: per-address keys or drop the claim; say who sends `announce` and what that reveals.
10. **C-2, Medium. Lemma 3 proves only that the position inside the nullifier is the proved one.** A circuit with a free `nk` or `rho` in the nullifier passes it, and passes every other lemma, and allows unlimited double spends; only the top statement excludes it. Fix: reword to bind `nk` to `ownerPk` and `rho` to `ownerCommit`, and to require that a non-empty input always publishes its nullifier.

Advances the reward index to now, computes the commitment with that exact index". When the activation is the first call of its window the roll has just set `gCur` to that same index, so the note is spendable at once. The canary line "of a freshly activated note before and after a roll" does not say whether "before" must fail.

11. Exit sizes settled or not. `#pool` design table lists "1 to 1,000,000 USDT or USDC" as the design and gives no token sizes; `[#parameters]`: "Still to confirm: 1 to 1,000,000 USDT or USDC, and 1,000 to 100,000,000 of the token"; the `#decisions` row mentions the stablecoin sizes as open and omits the token's.
12. State left behind. `[#b-gas]`: "Activate | ... | None that lasts. Tree writes overwrite existing slots" against the storage table's `roots[64]` and `filled[32]`, whose first writes (the first 64 inserts, and each level's first fill) create slots at 22,100 or 110,020 gas. Small, but it moves the early activation and spend figures.
13. Who can be seen. `#lifecycle`: "Observers see that a transaction happened and nothing about who" and `#visibility` against `[#b-gas]` "Spend sent by its owner" and `relayFee` "Zero for a self-sent spend": a self-sent spend shows its sender, and C-26, C-17 and C-12 make self-sending the only path for many users.

### 5. Claims on the page that I checked and found correct

1. Total reward claimable cannot exceed `released`. By induction over notes: any value that earns over an interval was counted in `weighted` for that whole interval (activation adds to `weighted` at the instant the note's exact index is taken; outputs start at the same `gRef` where their inputs stopped; value that leaves stops earning at `gRef`, which is never later than the moment `weighted` drops). The index is rounded down, `earned` is rounded down, `released` is rounded up. It rests on one unstated invariant: for each asset the sum of live note values is at most `shielded[asset]`.
2. "Checkpoints always round against the spender", and the loss is small: only value that leaves loses reward, for at most about two hours.
3. A proof against a checkpoint root and `gCur` stays valid until two more rolls; filling the 64-root window cannot block exits for longer than the wait for the next roll. Sustained flushing costs the attacker about 70M gas per flush.
4. An activated note becomes spendable no later than the next roll, including after emissions end (equality passes constraint 7) and after any idle period (one roll is enough).
5. The zero conventions close as specified: nf[2] = 0 exactly when input 2 is empty exactly when `relayFee` = 0; a fee input cannot carry another asset or skip its nullifier if constraints 4 and 6 are implemented as written.
6. An empty input carries nothing: zero value, zero accrued, zero earned. A note with value zero and accrued non-zero is not empty and must prove membership.
7. The position inside the nullifier defeats duplicate commitments (two identical commitments at two positions are two notes with two nullifiers), and a sender who knows `rho` and the position cannot compute the recipient's nullifier without `nk`.
8. Spending needs `sk`; `nk` and `spk` are not enough. A relayer or copier cannot change recipient, relayer, fee, amounts or ciphertexts (all are public inputs or inside `extHash`, which also binds chain id and pool address).
9. Domain separation: no H3 or H4 use can collide with another by construction (tags 1 to 5 first, `packed` at or above 6 x 2^200, leaf bundles start with a hash output). `packed` fields do not overlap and stay below p.
10. Bit widths of constraints 11 and 12 (sums under 2^99) and of constraint 8 (left side under p whenever 2 x weight + weightEth is under about 2^67.6; right side under 2^161). With a one-billion supply the real pool's g stays under 2^85 for every weight scale.
11. Sizes: 1,120 + 80 + 16 = 1,216; payload fields sum to 80; ciphertext 1's fields sum to 80 plus 16; 1,312 in total; 13 public inputs and 14 key points; 2^96 / 10^18 is about 79 billion.
12. The constraint estimate: my own count from hash sizes (about 243, 264 and 300 constraints for H2, H3, H4) and range checks gives about 31,800.
13. Pool ledgers close for deposit, activation, refund, exit, relay fee and sweep; unactivated deposits cannot be reached by a spend; a token spend is limited to `tokenIn + released − tokenOut`.
14. Activation timing: one day sixteen hours to two days sixteen hours, from the epoch rule and the reveal window.
15. Gas arithmetic that can be redone from the page: 32 x 18,624 + 51,826 = 647,794; 182,441 + 13 x 6,626 = 268,579; 1,312 x 24 = 31,488; 3 x 97,920 = 293,760; 24,576 bytes at 200 and 1,530 gas give 5.36M and 38.2M with calldata; 96 slots give 2.12M and 10.56M of which 1.16M is execution; every dollar figure at 0.2 gwei and $2,130.
16. The simulation: re-ran `murmuration_economics.py`; its output matches every table on the page (251 and 504 tokens, 0.55% and 1.09%, 0.39%, 5.9%, 50.2%, the IMD share table, the one-sided range table). Also: 17 ETH sizes, at most 16 withdrawals under 1,000 ETH, $5.1M at the stop, about $41M without.
17. Harvest-now-decrypt-later: note contents are hidden by the hybrid ciphertext, a seed-derived symmetric key, and hashes with a 31-byte `rho`; the Groth16 proof leaks nothing about the witness even to a quantum reader. Only soundness is at risk, as the page says.
18. The canary reaches the largest g the code can see (its caps are 50 times smaller for the same supply), so it does exercise the index range, which is also why C-9 shows up there first.
