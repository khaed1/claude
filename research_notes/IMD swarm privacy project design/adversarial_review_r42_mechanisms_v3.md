### M-1. Launch can be front-run by anyone who can order a transaction ahead of it, not only by the block builder (high)

Text. `[#b-contracts]` Distribution: "launch() | Anyone, once, from GENESIS | Creates the Token with CREATE2 and a salt taken from the block's prevrandao and the caller, so its address does not exist and cannot be known before this block. ... If someone in the same block has already created it at a higher price, sells at most a small bounded number of tokens param with the starting price as the limit ... and reverts the whole call if the price is still above the start." `[#token]` The public pool: "The token's address now does not exist and cannot be computed before the launch block, so there is nothing to prepare against. A block builder can still interfere inside that one block, which delays a launch attempt and cannot prevent the next." `[#review2]`: "Only someone building that same block can still interfere, and that costs them each time and delays one attempt."

Mechanism. The salt is a function of two values that every transaction in the launch block can read or be told: `block.prevrandao` (an opcode) and the caller (visible in the pending transaction). So an attacker does not need to know anything before the block. He deploys, once, a helper contract with one function `jam(caller)` that (1) computes the salt from `block.prevrandao` and `caller`, (2) derives the CREATE2 address from the Distribution address and the token init-code hash, both public, (3) calls `UniswapV3Factory.createPool(token, IMD, 10000)`, (4) calls `initialize` at a price far above the start, and (5) mints IMD-only bids above the starting price worth a little more than the "small bounded number of tokens". Checked against the local Uniswap source: `createPool` (`UniswapV3Factory.sol` line 35) has no access control and takes any address, `initialize` (`UniswapV3Pool.sol` line 271) is open to anyone once, and `mint` takes any recipient.

The helper call works in whatever block it lands in. Getting it ahead of the launch in the same block is ordinary front-running from the public mempool: a higher priority fee, or a two-transaction bundle `[jam, launch]` sent to builders, which any searcher can submit using someone else's public transaction. The launch then sells its bounded amount into the attacker's bids, finds the price still above the start and "reverts the whole call". Because the whole call reverts, the attacker's bids are untouched and he withdraws them in a third transaction. His cost is gas only: one v3 pool creation (about 4.5M gas) plus initialize and mint, about 5M gas, which is roughly 0.005 ETH or $11 at 1 gwei and about $1 at 0.1 gwei. The launcher loses the gas of a reverted launch each time. The next attempt has a new prevrandao and a new address, and the same helper handles it with no change.

A second variant needs no bids. The attacker lays dust positions on many ticks between his price and the start. The bounded sale has to cross every one of them. The invariant "The gas launch can use is bounded by what one block's worth of interference can add, and stays under the transaction limit" is stated without a number. An attacker can spend most of a block (60M gas or more) initialising ticks at roughly 85k gas each, and the launch must cross each at roughly 15k to 40k gas inside a single transaction capped at 2^24 = 16.78M gas. At a 60M block the margin is thin, and it shrinks with every increase of the block gas limit while the transaction cap stays. The contract can never be adjusted.

A third variant shows it is not confined to one block: the proposer of slot N-1 knows its own RANDAO reveal, so it knows `prevrandao` of block N while building N-1. A proposer that also builds can leave the launch out of N-1, create the pool there for the address the launch will produce in N, and block it from an earlier block.

What the launcher can do: send through private order flow, so that only the builders that receive it can jam it; or call through a contract so that `msg.sender` is a fresh address. The second does not help against anyone who simulates the pending transaction. The page says neither.

Who is "the caller" when a contract calls: `msg.sender`, the contract. A caller can therefore choose the contract address and grind the token address (vanity, or which side of IMD it sorts on, see M-9). The salt gives the caller control and gives nobody else any uncertainty.

Damage. Launch is delayed for as long as someone pays about 5M gas per attempt and wins ordering. With no token there is "no stake, no screener and no activation" (`#review2`), so the whole system waits. The clock does not wait: caps, emissions, the auction and the deposit window all count from GENESIS (M-12).

Fix (keeps every fixed decision, removes code). Make interference impossible instead of bounded: inside `launch`, loop a counter n = 0, 1, 2, ... into the salt and take the first candidate address for which `factory.getPool(candidate, IMD, 10000) == address(0)`, then create the token and the pool in that same transaction. A blocker has to pay a full pool deployment (about 4.5M gas) for each candidate, and can fit at most about 13 in a 60M block, while each check costs the launch about 5k gas. A loop bound of 64 is out of reach of any block. The "bounded price reset", its parameter, the `fundBuyback` call from launch and the gas invariant can then be deleted, because the pool cannot exist when the position is placed. Also correct the two sentences that say only a block builder can interfere.

### M-2. The flag rule lets one third of screeners (three seats while nine or fewer are active) refuse any named depositor, so control of entry costs a third, not two thirds (high)

Text. `[#screening]` criteria file: `"flags": { "source": "ScreenerRegistry.Flag events before epochStart, from active screeners", "quorum": "max(3, one third of active screeners)", "expireAfterEpochs": 14 }` and `{ "deny": "flagged(depositor)" }`. `[#b-contracts]` Registry: "flag(seatId, data), signalCriteria(seatId, hash) | The operator of an active screener | Emit an event and store nothing." `[#screening]`: "If screeners are bought. Whoever holds two thirds can approve a deposit that is at least a day old, and can refuse everyone else's deposits. Whoever holds a majority for a week can remove the rest and then do the same." and "control of the registry is also control of who earns the users' 45%." `[#b-contracts]` Pool: "refund(id, record) | ... Anyone, while the registry marks the deposit refused".

Mechanism. Refusing is not symmetric with approving. A bloc with one third of the active screeners posts a `Flag` event against the depositing address of every pending deposit that is not its own. Deposits are public and wait at least a day, so there is always time. The published rule then says those deposits are refused, and the honest two thirds, who run the deterministic program, compute and reveal exactly that batch themselves. The flaggers reveal the same batch as everyone else, so they collect no misses and can never be evicted for it. Flags expire after 14 epochs and are re-posted. Anyone can then push the refused deposits back with `refund`.

Cost. With 15 honest screeners the bloc needs 8 seats, about 5.6 ETH or $12,000 of resalable capital at the page's 0.70 ETH a seat, plus stakes that are returned. While nine or fewer screeners are active (the launch condition is seven) the quorum is 3, about 2.1 ETH. The address that holds 77 seats meets one third of any registry with fewer than 155 other active screeners and has to buy nothing. A flag is an event, a few thousand gas each if the operator is a batching contract.

Gain. Exactly what the page prices at two thirds or a week-long majority: choosing who may enter, and so who earns the users' 45% and whose deposits dilute the bloc's own rewards. It also allows targeted censorship of one user for the price of three to eight seats.

The page claims the flag rule was fixed in 4.2: "The quorum is a share of the active screeners, flags expire, and a flag reaches only the flagged address, so three seats cannot have an exchange's customers refused" (`#screening`). That closes the spreading of a flag, not this. Note also that "the flagged address" is `msg.sender` of the deposit: a shared deposit helper contract or a smart-account entry point is one address for all of its users.

Fix. Flags are needed for fresh thefts, so keep them but take away their power to exclude: (a) a flag names one deposit id, not an address, and can be used once per deposit; (b) it does not set the refusal bit; it defers coverage of that one deposit for a fixed number of epochs (see M-4 for how a hold has to be represented), after which the deposit is judged by lists alone; (c) the quorum for anything that refuses outright is a majority of the snapshot, the same threshold the page already discloses as controlling the registry. State in the risks table that the cost of refusing is the flag quorum.

### M-3. A bloc of just over one third can hold the registry to one accepted batch a week for ever, at no cost; the eviction rules do not bound a blockade to "about a week" (high)

Text. `[#screening]`: "A group that reveals something other than the majority's batch for seven days running can be removed by anyone, so a third of the screeners can delay deposits for about a week and no longer." `[#b-contracts]` Registry: "evict(seatId) | Anyone | Removes a screener when the seat has changed hands, when it has not revealed for 7 epochs counted from the later of its last reveal and its first active epoch, or when it has 7 consecutive misses." "What a miss is. ... A screener that revealed something else in such an epoch has one more miss, and a screener that revealed the majority hash has none." "Pay. ... An epoch with no accepted batch pays nobody and its fees stay in the pot." and "a day without a batch delays its share and does not destroy it."

Mechanism A (free, permanent, works under every reading). A bloc with more than one third of the snapshot commits the honest batch every day (anyone can compute it) and simply does not reveal on six days out of seven. Not revealing is not a miss, and the silence rule needs seven epochs. On the seventh day it reveals, the batch is accepted, and its counters are clean. The registry therefore accepts one batch every seven days for as long as the bloc likes. Deposits wait up to about nine days instead of two. The bloc loses nothing: the six days of fees "stay in the pot" and are paid out on the seventh day to everyone who revealed, the bloc included, and the token share is "delayed", not lost. A six-day cycle also stays clear of the rule that sends the screeners' 40% to stakers after seven epochs without a batch. The honest screeners pay gas for six useless commits and reveals each week.

Mechanism B (total blockade, longer than a week). The bloc reveals junk on day 1, stays silent on days 2 to 7, reveals junk on day 8, and so on. It never goes seven epochs without a reveal. Its misses are not "seven days running" in any ordinary sense. The page does not say what a silent epoch does to the count of "consecutive misses" (see guess list). The registry settles a screener's record only at its next commit ("Settles the screener's previous reveal"), so the natural implementation skips silent epochs: the bloc then reaches seven misses after 43 days, not 7. If a builder reads "consecutive" as consecutive epochs and resets the count on a silent epoch, the bloc is never evictable and the blockade is permanent, which is the fault revision 4.2 says it closed.

Mechanism C (rotation). `[#b-contracts]`: "Kept apart from the seat, so a seat that changes hands can be registered again at once." An evicted seat can be re-registered at once and counts again 30 epochs later. Six sets of 8 seats (48 seats, about 34 ETH or $72,000 of resalable capital with 15 honest screeners), staggered a week apart, keep a blocking third active all the time. Each set is silent for its seven days, is evicted, re-registers and returns 30 epochs later.

Cost and gain. A: nothing but gas. B: 8 seats, about $12,000 resalable. C: about $72,000 resalable. Gain: the page's own, "blockers already inside the pool keep the rewards to themselves", plus the ability to sell an end to the blockade.

Fix. (1) Count failures to reveal the accepted or majority hash in a window, not in a row: a screener is evictable when it has failed to reveal the epoch's majority hash in 7 of the last 14 epochs in which a majority hash was recorded, where silence counts as a failure. One counter that goes up by one on a failure and down by one on a success, floored at zero, evictable at 7, does this in one storage field. (2) Do not let the pot pay for blocked days to those who blocked: pay each accepted epoch only the fees received since the previous epoch ended, and send a blocked epoch's fees to stakers. (3) Give a re-registered seat a longer wait after an eviction for cause (for example 90 epochs), so rotation needs far more seats. (4) Replace "about a week and no longer" with what the rules actually bound.
