The six contracts

None has an owner, a pause, an upgrade path or a setter for any parameter. Every state-changing function is guarded against re-entry. They call three Poseidon hash contracts that already exist on mainnet. A one-time deployer creates five of them and then has no function left, and the Distribution contract creates the token at launch. Each of the five works out the others' addresses from the Deployer's address. Each reads the token's address from Distribution.token() the first time it needs it and keeps it. Until the token exists, deposits, registrations, stakes and fee sweeps revert.

1. Pool

Holds every asset. Takes deposits, activates them, verifies spends, keeps the note tree, the spent set, the caps and the reward index.

The fee ceiling. In ETH it is (4 × block.basefee + 1 gwei) × 2,000,000 param: enough for a relayer when gas is anywhere near normal, and never enough to be worth using as a way out. In another asset it is eight times that param, converted at a rate frozen in the build: the number of base units one ETH bought on the freeze day, and for the pool's token the rate at the starting valuation. The factor of eight is room for prices to move. The 0.2% exit fee is charged on the relay fee like on everything else that leaves, so nothing is saved by leaving through it.

The reward index. Advanced before any change to a shielded balance, and at every roll. Nothing is advanced before START or after emissions end.

t         = now − START
step      = min(t / 90 days, LAST_STEP)                 // the same clamp in every formula. LAST_STEP is 5 with the recommended stop
cap(a)    = cap0(a) × 2^step
rate      = USERS × 2^step / D   while t < 730 days, and 0 afterwards        // tokens per second; USERS is the users' 45%
            D = the sum of 2^step over every second of the 730 days, a constant
g        += seconds × rate × 2^64 / max(weighted, floor)       // piece by piece across step changes, rounded down, held at 2^90 − 1
released += seconds × rate × weighted / max(weighted, floor)   // rounded up, and never above USERS
weighted  = the sum over ETH, USDT, USDC and IMD of weight[a] × shielded[a]
floor     = a quarter of the sum of weight[a] × cap(a)

Invariants.

  • For each capped asset, the pool's balance is at least pending + shielded + fees, plus owed in total for ETH. For the reward token it is at least pending + fees + tokenIn − tokenOut plus the reward budget not yet withdrawn.
  • A spend can never take out more of a capped asset than shielded, and never more of the reward token than tokenIn + released − tokenOut.
  • For each capped asset, the values of all unspent notes add up to at most shielded.
  • shielded[asset] never exceeds the asset's cap at the time it last grew. Caps never fall.
  • Only its depositor can activate a deposit. A refund goes to the depositor whoever calls it. Each deposit ends in exactly one of the two.
  • A nullifier is marked once and never cleared. Zero is never marked. The tree only grows, by one leaf per activation or spend.
  • g never decreases and stays under 290. released never exceeds the users' 45%. gCur and gPrev are never above g.
  • Nothing leaves through the relay fee beyond the ceiling in force in that block.
  • After the deposit window closes no deposit is created. Activation, refund, roll, spend and claimOwed have no end date, and no state the pool can reach makes roll revert.
  • No function sends an asset anywhere except: a refund to its depositor, a withdrawal to the recipient and a relay fee to the relayer named in a verified proof, a credit to the address it was recorded for, and fees to the distributor.
  • No proof is accepted before the Verifier exists: a call to an address without code counts as a failed verification.

2. Verifier

The Groth16 verifier for Spend, generated from the ceremony's output. One view function, verify(proof, inputs[14]). No storage. Its invariant is that it accepts exactly the proofs the verification key accepts, which is tested against the reference verifier with valid, altered and malformed proofs, including public inputs at or above the field modulus. The checks on the key itself are part B of what must be shown.

3. ScreenerRegistry

Decides which deposits may be activated. Holds screener stakes, the screeners' share of fees and the screeners' 15% of the token. It cannot reach anything in the pool. If it fails, deposits stop and nothing already shielded is affected.

An epoch is one day from GENESIS, in three parts: commit in the first 12 hours, reveal in the next 8, and 4 hours in which nothing but apply is left to do. A batch can be applied only during its own epoch. A batch that is accepted and not applied by the end of its day is dropped and pays nobody, and the next day's batch covers the same deposits. So no batch ever depends on another, and the order in which things land cannot matter.

The snapshot. Every state-changing function begins by closing the previous epoch if that has not been done. Closing records that epoch's majority hash, releases its pay if its batch was applied, folds new screeners into activeCount, and fixes the number that counts for the new epoch.

  bytes32 criteriaHash;   // the published criteria file in force

// Data: finalized blocks up to the last block whose timestamp is before the epoch's start. The registry's bitmap is read at that block.
// Only deposits that are still open are evaluated. A deposit that was activated or refunded is never looked at again, and its bit is written as 0.
// A word is listed when the bit of at least one open, covered deposit in it has to change. Words that set a bit come first, then words that only clear bits, each group in ascending order.
// At most 96 words. If more than 96 words set a bit, the batch lists the first 96 of them and lowers upTo to just below the first deposit of the first one left out.
// Words that only clear bits and do not fit wait for the next day.

Strikes. When an epoch is closed, its lead hash becomes the majority hash if more than half of the snapshot revealed it. An accepted batch is always the majority hash. For each epoch that has a majority hash, a screener that was active and revealed it loses one strike, down to zero, and a screener that was active and did not, whether it revealed something else or nothing, gains two. Fourteen strikes allow eviction. That is seven misses in a row, or missing more than one day in three for long enough. An epoch with no majority hash changes nobody's count. Strikes are worked out when a screener is settled, from the ring, and a screener is settled at every commit, exit, withdrawal of pay and eviction attempt.

This is what bounds a blockade by a minority. A group that is more than a third and less than half can stop acceptance on any day by revealing nothing or by revealing something else. The honest majority's hash is still recorded that day, so each member gains two strikes. A week of blocking gets the group removed, and so does any pattern that blocks more than one day in three. What the rule cannot prevent is a delay of one day now and then. It also means a group that holds a majority for a week can remove everyone else.

Pay. An epoch pays when its batch was both accepted and applied. At its closing, the pot and one share of the 15% are divided equally among the screeners who revealed the accepted hash, with any remainder left in the pot, and the amount per head is written to the ring. Each of them is credited when next settled, within 16 epochs, to the holder's address and not to the seat. The 15% is paid in 730 equal shares, one per paid epoch, so a day without a batch delays its share and does not destroy it. After the 730th there is no token share. An epoch that does not pay leaves its fees in the pot.

After deposits close. The registry takes no commits from 30 epochs param after the deposit window closes. Whatever is left of the 15% then stays locked for good.

Events. Registered, ScreenerActive, Committed, Revealed, BatchAccepted(epoch, batchHash, agreed), MajorityRecorded(epoch, hash), BatchApplied(epoch, upTo, dataTime), Flag(seatId, depositId), CriteriaSignal(seatId, hash), ExitRequested, Evicted(seatId, reason), PayWithdrawn.

Invariants.

  • A batch is accepted only with matching reveals from at least two thirds of the snapshot, rounded up, and only when the snapshot is at least seven. At most one batch is accepted per epoch.
  • Only apply writes dataTime, screenedThrough or denied, only during the epoch of the batch it applies, and at most once per epoch.
  • No deposit is approved, and none is refused, unless it was at least one day old at the start of the epoch of the last applied batch. No batch, however it was produced, can decide a deposit made after that time.
  • activeCount equals the number of records that are active. It changes only when an epoch is closed, on an exit and on an eviction.
  • A screener commits and reveals at most once per epoch, only while active, and only while the registered holder still owns the seat.
  • A stake leaves only to the holder who posted it, 14 epochs after exit or eviction. Nothing can reduce it.
  • For each asset, the registry's balance is at least the pot plus everything owed plus, for the token, the stakes, the unbonding stakes and the unpaid part of the 15%. Total token pay never exceeds the 15%.
  • A ring slot is read only when its tag matches the epoch asked for.
  • The registry has no function that moves funds held by the pool.

Invariants.

  • Every receipt is split 40, 40 and 20, with any rounding remainder going to the basket stream.
  • For each asset, the balance is at least accounted: the unpaid staker stream plus everything owed plus the basket and its stream, plus the staked tokens for the token and buybackImd for IMD.
  • A staker stream pays out everything put into it, to within one base unit per update, for any asset and any amount staked.
  • Staked tokens return only to the staker who staked them.
  • buybackImd falls only through a swap in the locked pool, and no function sends it to an address. IMD owed to stakers and screeners is accounted apart from it.
  • A buyback step never exceeds half of the pool's fee rate times the locked position's virtual IMD reserve at the larger of the current and the starting price, and at most one step runs per 12 hours.
  • A buyer receives only assets it named, and only their released basket balance.

5. Distribution

Creates the token, gives the supply out once, and owns the locked liquidity position. The token is always the lower of the two addresses in its Uniswap pool, so there is one set of formulas and the canary runs the same path as mainnet.

Why the launch cannot be blocked. Someone who sees the launch transaction can work out every candidate address and create pools for them first. Each pool costs about 4.5M gas to create, so at most about thirteen fit in a block, and the candidates change with every block and every caller. The launch needs one free candidate out of 256. About one candidate in six is skipped for sorting above IMD, whose address begins 0xd34a. With no pool in existence when the position is placed, there is no price to reset and no interference to bound.

Invariants.

  • launch runs once. Afterwards the five shares add up to the whole supply.
  • The token's address is below IMD's, and its pool did not exist before the launch transaction.
  • No code path reduces the position's liquidity. The contract never calls the pool's burn with a non-zero amount.
  • The founder's and each swarm entry's payout never exceeds the vested amount, and never exceeds the allocation.
  • The position's fees can go only to the distributor and the burn address.

6. Token

A plain ERC-20 with 18 decimals, created by the Distribution contract at launch. The whole supply param is created for its creator in the constructor. There is no owner, no mint, no burn function, no fee on transfer and no blocklist. Its invariants are that total supply never changes and that no address has any power another does not. The name and symbol are constants of the build, and the canary's are different from the final ones.
