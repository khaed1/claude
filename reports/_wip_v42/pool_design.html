Design

This table is the summary. The build specification has the detail. The review and the independent review explain each change from the first version 4.

ElementChoiceWhy
NotesA commitment to asset, value, reward accrued, the reward index at creation, and a hidden owner. One append-only tree for all assets, one leaf per transactionSmall assets hide in the same set as large ones. One leaf per transaction keeps the tree code as simple as Tornado Cash's
CircuitOne, called Spend: two notes in and an optional ETH note for the relay fee, two notes and the fee change out, a reward note, and an optional public amount. Groth16, about 31,000 constraints by estimateEvery circuit is permanent. One circuit means one ceremony, one audit target, and one set of proofs that covers transfers and withdrawals alike
DepositsNo proof. The pool computes the commitment from the public amount and the depositor's hidden owner commitment. The 0.2% is paid on top, so a deposit of a standard size becomes a note of that sizeA deposit is public anyway. A circuit for it proved nothing the contract could not compute
ScreeningA deposit can be activated once a screening batch covers it without refusing it. A batch covers only deposits that were at least a day old when its epoch began. Later notes carry no labelScreeners always have between one and two days to refuse stolen funds, and quick in-and-out use is impossible. Screening at entry avoids carrying labels through every private transfer
RefundThe depositor can take back an unactivated deposit at any time, to the depositing address, in full. Anyone can send a refused deposit backScreening failure or capture can never trap funds, and refused funds need not sit in the pool
Batch acceptanceTwo thirds of the active screeners, by commit and reveal, with at least seven active. A screener that disagrees with a majority seven days running can be removed. With no accepted batch, new deposits wait or are refunded. There is no fallback ruleNo single curator. A minority can delay deposits for about a week and no longer. Whoever holds a majority of screeners for a week controls who may enter
ExitsPublic withdrawals only in a 1, 2, 5 series per asset: 0.005 to 1,000 ETH, 5 to 50,000 IMD, 1 to 1,000,000 USDT or USDC. Every exit of a size pays out the same amountRemoves exact amount matching at the edge. It does not hide a total sent to one address
Note encryptionHPKE over X-Wing, the hybrid of X25519 and ML-KEM-768. 1,312 bytes of ciphertext per spendCiphertexts are public forever. This is the part of the quantum threat that cannot be fixed later
Proof systemGroth16 on BN254 with a new public ceremony. It is not quantum-safeQuantum-safe proofs cost too much to verify on Ethereum today. The deposit window below limits the exposure
Deposit windowDeposits stop at a time fixed in the build, about four years out. Activation, refunds and withdrawals never stopNew money goes to a successor pool before proof forgery becomes realistic. Funds left inside after that stay exposed, and the wallet says so
Per-asset limitsEach asset has its own balance. A spend cannot take out more of an asset than was activated into it, or more of the reward token than was activated plus rewards releasedA fault in one asset cannot drain another. A flaw in the circuit reaches every asset at once, so the exposure is the sum of the caps. Unactivated deposits are outside the caps and are protected only by the plain deposit and refund code
CapsA per-asset ceiling on activated value: 25 ETH, 50,000 USDT, 50,000 USDC and 2,500 IMD, doubling every 90 days in code. Recommended: stop after five doublings, at 800 ETH, 1,600,000 of each stablecoin and 80,000 IMDLimits loss while the circuit is young, with no admin to raise it
AssetsFixed in the build, by the owner's decision of 11 November 2026: ETH, USDT, USDC, IMD and the pool's own token. Either stablecoin's issuer can freeze the pool's balance of it, and that traps only that assetAn immutable pool cannot add assets later. Stablecoins are more than half of what people shield on Railgun and Privacy Pools, and USDT is the largest. IMD's holders are the first crowd the pool can reach
RelayingPermissionless. The relayer is paid a public amount of ETH out of a shielded ETH note that the same proof spends. Seat broadcasters serve it with the wallet policy described under the node and the walletThe relayer gets money it can use at once and learns nothing about the asset or the amounts. A fee paid as a shielded note would cost the relayer a second transaction to collect
TimeEvery schedule counts seconds, not blocksA change in Ethereum's slot time would otherwise shorten every wait and every schedule in a contract nobody can change
DeploymentThrough the deterministic deployment proxy at 0x4e59…956C and a one-time deployer, by anyone, from a reproducible build. The token is created inside the launch transaction. Every contract under 24 KiBNo deployer privilege exists. The token's address cannot be known in advance, so nobody can prepare its market against the launch

Assurance without an external audit

The owner's decision is to rely on IMD and see whether the swarm can secure what it builds. Wallets, L2BEAT and exchanges look for a named firm's report, so adoption will be slower without one. Four measures make the test fair and keep a failure small.

  • IMD audit panels on everything. Every contract and the circuit go through IMD's audit jobs, with each finding and its fix public. Reviewers built on the same models as the builders tend to share blind spots, which is why the next three measures do not depend on reviewers.
  • Machine-checked proofs. The swarm writes formal proofs of the statement and the ten lemmas listed under the circuit. Anyone can re-run the checker. A checked proof holds whoever wrote it, as long as the statement it proves is the right one, so each statement is also published in plain words for review. The first list of statements, in revision 4.1, was too weak: an independent reviewer showed a broken circuit could have passed it. Open-source analysers for under-constrained circuits are run in public as well.
  • A canary. The whole system is deployed on mainnet 90 days before launch with a compressed clock and small caps, funded by the project and exercised by a published script. It is a rehearsal and a standing reward for anyone who can break it. It does not prove the circuit sound: someone who finds a flaw can wait for the larger pool.
  • Low caps. The pool opens at 25 ETH, 50,000 of each stablecoin and 2,500 IMD and doubles every 90 days. The most that a circuit flaw can take is about $160,000 at launch and about $5.1M at the recommended stop. Without a stop the same schedule reaches about $41M after two years.

Nothing in the research shows that IMD has audited a zero-knowledge circuit before. This launch is that test.
