A deposit can be activated between about one day sixteen hours and two days sixteen hours after it is made. The batch that covers it is computed at the start of the second epoch after the deposit, so screeners always have at least one full day, and up to two, in which a flag or a list update still counts. No state exists in which a deposit can be neither activated nor refunded, except a depositor that cannot receive its own asset back, such as an address a stablecoin issuer has frozen.

Empty inputs are never in the tree. A note whose ciphertext was posted wrongly is Created for ever and nobody can spend it. Reward stops growing for everyone when emissions end, and a note keeps what it has accrued. Reward on a small balance may never be worth the gas of withdrawing it.

  • Version 4 overstated the cost of post-quantum encryption. It priced each note at about 70,000 gas "at the calldata floor". A spend pays the ordinary rate and publishes one recipient ciphertext, so the whole encryption overhead is about 31,500 gas, under 3% of a spend, today and under Glamsterdam.
  • The hash contracts. The pool calls the three poseidon-solidity contracts that already exist on mainnet, pinned by code hash, so it deploys none. They are 23,478, 14,189 and 19,260 bytes. Their README says they are unaudited, so the build checks them against the circuit with shared test vectors and the audit reads the generator.
  • Size limit. The build fails if any contract's deployed code exceeds 24,576 bytes. Estimates: Pool 17 to 21 KiB, ScreenerRegistry 14 to 18, FeeDistributor 11 to 14, Distribution 8 to 10, Verifier about 4, Token under 3.
  • Transaction limit. The build fails if any call can exceed 16,777,216 execution gas with the worst state that others can create. The heaviest calls are a relayed spend, apply with a full 96-word batch (about 2.1M today and 10.6M under Glamsterdam, of which 1.2M is execution gas), and launch, which creates the token and the Uniswap pool and is tested with a block's worth of hostile ticks placed in front of it.
  • No refunds are relied on. Clearing a deposit record earns a refund under both rule sets. No limit or estimate in the code depends on it.
  • Deployment. About 60 KiB of new code: roughly 13M gas today and 95M under Glamsterdam across the transactions, under $50 either way at 0.2 gwei.

Deployment and the canary

  1. Build. One reproducible build produces the creation code of the contracts, the circuit and a parameter file. The ceremony runs on that circuit, and the verifier's code is generated from its output. The final build fixes GENESIS, a timestamp, and is published only when the launch gate holds. It differs from the build that was audited and run as the canary in a short list of named constants and nothing else, and a job checks that by comparing bytecode.
  2. Deployer. A small contract is created through the deterministic deployment proxy at 0x4e59…956C. Its constructor fixes five creation-code hashes in order. Its only function, deploy(i, code), creates contract i if code matches hash i and every earlier one exists, and reverts if creation fails. No address is passed to a constructor: each contract works out the others' addresses from the Deployer's address and the fixed order, so the hashes do not depend on themselves. Anyone can send these transactions, and nobody can deploy anything else through it.
  3. One contract per transaction: the Deployer, then Verifier, ScreenerRegistry, FeeDistributor, Pool and Distribution. Under Glamsterdam pricing they do not fit in one transaction or one block.
  4. Launch. From GENESIS, anyone calls Distribution.launch(), which creates the token and the public pool in one transaction. Whoever sends it can also be the first buyer in the same transaction, and nothing in a permissionless launch prevents that. Screeners then buy their stake, register and wait 30 days, so the first deposits can be activated about 33 days after launch.

The canary

The canary is the whole system from the same source, with these constants changed and nothing else: the salt, GENESIS, a cap step of 10 days instead of 90, an emission period and deposit window shortened to match (80 and 85 days), starting caps of 0.5 ETH, 1,000 USDT, 1,000 USDC and 50 IMD, and a screener wait of 3 days instead of 30. In 90 days it passes every cap step, the end of emissions and the close of deposits, and its caps are small enough for the project's own funds to fill. It has its own token and its own pool against IMD, which anyone could buy from, so the lawyer's review comes before the canary. It also needs at least seven seat holders willing to buy and stake a canary token, which the project cannot arrange and can only wait for.

The project funds it with enough of each asset to fill the caps, at least 10 ETH in all by the end, and runs a published script. The canary passes only when every line has a transaction hash beside it:

  • Every external function of every contract, called at least once.
  • Spends of every kind: two real inputs; one real and one empty; through a relayer with the ETH fee note; with a reward claim; against the previous checkpoint; of token notes; of a freshly activated note before and after a roll; and a withdrawal of every asset at its smallest and largest reachable size.
  • A refused deposit refunded by its depositor, another refunded by a third party, and a refusal that a later batch clears.
  • An activation that fails at a full cap and succeeds after a withdrawal.
  • An epoch with no accepted batch, a screener evicted for silence, one evicted for seven misses, and one whose seat was transferred.
  • A basket sale, a sale that leaves one asset out, and buyback steps down to an empty balance.
  • A wallet restored from its seed alone that recovers every note.
  • Spends and refunds after deposits have closed and emissions have ended.

Some paths cannot be reached on mainnet in 90 days and are run on a fork of mainnet state instead, with the results published: a launch that a block builder front-runs with bids and with a block's worth of ticks, a batch with more than 96 changed words, a stablecoin issuer freezing the pool, the end of the two-year vesting, and the 30-day screener wait.

What the canary shows: the deployment procedure works, the contracts and the circuit agree on mainnet, and nobody who looked took the money. What it does not show is that the circuit is sound. The proofs are for that, and the caps limit the cost if the proofs are wrong.
