Murmuration: a swarm-operated privacy stack

Ethereum already has the cryptography for private transfers. What it lacks is a way to relay, screen, prove and read without a single operator who can be subpoenaed, captured or prosecuted. IMD's ~1,100 independently owned, on-chain-identified agents are that missing operator layer. Murmuration turns them into six services for the pools that already exist, and only then into a pool of its own.

Thesis: services first, pool second

Three findings drive the shape of the project. First, anonymity sets are the scarcest resource on Ethereum. Privacy Pools v2 launched on seven chains in October 2026 and holds 470 deposits on mainnet; a new pool on day one inherits that problem. Second, every compromise and every prosecution of Tornado Cash came from the mutable periphery (governance, frontend hash, relayer whitelist, fee token), never from the immutable pools. Third, every live privacy product still has a single operator somewhere: 0xbow runs the only Association Set Provider and the only relayer, Fluidkey holds a viewing node, zk.money's enclave co-signs every send.

IMD gives a privacy project something no earlier one had: around a thousand machines whose owners are structurally not the developers of what they run, each bound to an Ethereum NFT, an ERC-8004 agent id and a device key. Murmuration uses only that identity layer and the idle operator pool. It never routes privacy work through IMD's closed control plane, never puts funds in IMD's unaudited contracts, and never pays anyone in IMD's token.

Design rule. Everything in Layer 1 must be usable by Railgun and Privacy Pools today, so the project thickens existing anonymity sets before it adds one. The Layer 0 pool is a phase 2 deliverable gated on evidence that the services worked.

Wallets talk to swarm services. Services talk to pools. Seats are admitted by the IMD identity chain, not by anyone's whitelist.

The six services

Service 1

Broadcaster mesh

Each participating seat runs a Railgun-style broadcaster. It subscribes to a Waku pubsub topic gated by RLN membership, publishes a signed fee quote per epoch, accepts an encrypted transaction whose recipient, fee and relayer address are bound into the ZK proof, pays gas, and is reimbursed with a note inside the pool. A broadcaster can delay a transaction; it can never redirect it or steal the fee.

Selection is client-side. The wallet reads the on-chain mesh registry, draws three candidates from distinct holder wallets with a locally seeded weighted draw, sends to the first and falls back after a block timeout. No on-chain per-transaction assignment exists, because that would itself publish a timing record.

Solves
Relayer chokepoint: the component that is both the metadata leak and the legal exposure in Tornado and Privacy Pools
Borrows from
Railgun broadcasters over Waku; Tornado's proof-bound recipient, fee and relayer; Nym dApp mode for transport
Needs from IMD
Seat ownership and ERC-8004 id reads; a worker tool declaration so a seat can advertise the role
Phase
0 for Railgun relaying; 1 for the registry and Privacy Pools
Service 2

Panel ASP

A panel of n seats from distinct holder wallets independently screens every deposit against a published criteria file (pinned to IPFS, hash on-chain) using only public data: the Chainalysis sanctions oracle, the lists Railgun's Private Proofs of Innocence already consume, deposit age and hop-count rules. Each panelist signs the approved-label Merkle root. The contract accepts a root only with t-of-n signatures, keeps a ring buffer of historical roots, and records dissent.

IMD's own oracle rule is unanimity. That is wrong here: one adversarial panelist could freeze inclusion. The ASP uses a 2/3 threshold with a public dissent log. Several panels with different criteria become several ASPs, and a withdrawal proves membership in a user-chosen set: the union of two lenient panels for a DEX, the intersection of strict ones for an exchange.

Solves
The single-operator ASP; no multi-party ASP exists anywhere as of November 2026
Borrows from
Privacy Pools' label and root scheme; Railgun PPOI list providers; IMD's EIP-712-signed oracle panels
Needs from IMD
Nothing at runtime; panels are selected from the registry, not by api.imd.fun
Phase
0 as a proof-of-association overlay; 1 as a contract
Service 3

Collaborative delegated proving

A phone generates the witness locally, which is cheap, secret-shares it three ways, and sends each share to three seats from distinct wallets. The seats run a collaborative SNARK (coCircom or coNoir) and jointly produce the proof without any one of them learning the witness. Security is honest-majority among the three; an attacker must control two of the three seats the client picked at random.

TEE attestation is defence-in-depth only. Provers may run inside TDX confidential VMs and post an attestation once per epoch, but a forged quote has already passed a production verifier for about $1,000 of hardware, so attestation proves measurement, never custody.

Memory-bound mobile proving, recursive PPOI proofs, multi-note spends
Borrows from
TACEO collaborative SNARKs (3-party honest-majority, distinct providers); dstack and Automata DCAP for optional attestation
Needs from IMD
A premium hardware tier: proving needs about 8 GB of memory per party

Service 4

Private read layer

Each participating seat runs a Helios light client, so an endpoint can refuse but cannot lie, plus an oblivious eth_call endpoint (TEE with ORAM now, PIR later), reachable over Waku or Nym. A "PIR-lite" mode splits a wallet's note discovery and nullifier queries across k random seats with chaff queries so no endpoint sees the full access pattern. This is a behavioural mitigation; real PIR is not expected in the next few hard forks.

Solves
RPC metadata leaks: default RPC providers log IP and address and can time sign-in against withdrawals
Borrows from
Kohaku's Helios plus TEE-ORAM design; Nym's Helios-over-mixnet fork; Penumbra fuzzy message detection later
Needs from IMD
Workers already run local RPC relays on 127.0.0.1; this adds an outward-facing role
Phase
1, merged with the broadcaster binary
Service 5

Anonymity-set stewardship agents

A standing panel re-runs the published linkage heuristics (address reuse, FIFO timing, amount correlation, wallet fingerprints) against every supported pool daily, publishes a signed pool-health root, and exposes a pre-transaction advisory API that wallets call before shielding or unshielding. A second function schedules withdrawals: the wallet hands a steward a proof, never a secret, and the steward submits it in a randomized window that flattens FIFO ordering.

Honest self-churn (decoy flows that thicken thin amount bands) is the third function and the most delicate. It runs only from a shielded budget, only where stewardship measures a thin band, and only after legal review, because a stream of agent round-trips can be read as structuring by the very vendors the project wants to satisfy.

Solves
The 5 to 35 percent of withdrawals that heuristics link, almost all of it user behaviour
Borrows from
Tutela, FIFO and cross-chain linkage studies; IMD's research-report and oracle skills
Needs from IMD
Existing skills only; paid at 0.5 IMD per request through Intake as any customer

Service 6

Threshold selective disclosure

For each transaction the wallet encrypts a disclosure record (amount, counterparty commitment, time) to an epoch public key whose private key is threshold-shared across a disclosure panel. An on-chain policy registry records per user who may request decryption: the user alone by default, optionally a named auditor, optionally a court order attested by a named panel. The user can change or revoke the policy at any time. Because epoch keys rotate and the panel refuses out-of-policy requests, disclosure is per-transaction, time-bounded and revocable, which a Zcash viewing key is not.

Strictly opt-in and default-off. Any decryption authority is a first-class risk in L2BEAT's privacy ratings and is what Aztec markets against. Position it as a user tool for the EU CASP interface after 10 July 2027, never as a network property.

Solves
No shipped per-transaction, revocable disclosure exists on Ethereum
Borrows from
Zcash viewing keys (what to improve on); Labyrinth's threshold revoker keys (testnet); Panther's forensic escrow (what to avoid)

Layer 0 · phase 2

The immutable pool

A Railgun-style shielded ledger with one Merkle tree across all assets so small assets inherit the large set, arbitrary amounts, free internal transfers, atomic unshield-call-reshield, no fee switch, no admin key, deployed from a throwaway key with ownership renounced. Exits are batched per epoch under one aggregated proof, and once Hegotá's keyed nonces ship the pool itself can submit concurrent exits without nonce collisions. Each asset has a turnstile and a supply-audit path so a soundness bug is bounded to one asset's verified deposits. Freezable stablecoins live in their own subtree so an issuer freeze cannot trap ETH depositors. Note commitments are hash-based, with a post-quantum KEM option for note encryption where gas allows.

Specs and technical implementation

Interfaces below are proposed, not deployed. Solidity is illustrative and omits events, errors and access detail. Everything in Layer 1 is written so that the project team can publish the code and run none of it.

Identity and the mesh registry

Admission is derived, never granted. A seat is eligible if the identity.md NFT (0x0000ec93…ec1d) is owned by the registering wallet and bound to an ERC-8004 agent id through IMD's adapter. The registry stores the device key the wallet authorizes, an availability commitment per epoch and an append-only reputation that anyone can recompute from events. Weighting is by holder wallet, not by seat, because the explorer already shows wallets holding around ten agents.

// MeshRegistry.sol  (immutable; no owner)
interface IMeshRegistry {
    enum Role { Broadcaster, Reader, Prover, Panelist, Steward, Discloser }

    struct Seat {
        uint256 seatId;        // identity.md tokenId
        uint256 agentId;       // ERC-8004 IdentityRegistry id (read via IMD adapter)
        address holder;        // ownerOf(seatId) at registration; re-checked each epoch
        bytes32 deviceX25519;  // derived from the Ed25519 device key (birational map)
        bytes   blsPubkey;     // optional BLS12-381 G1 key for aggregated panel signatures (EIP-2537)
        uint8   roles;         // bitmask of Role
        uint256 bondWei;       // ETH bond; 0.05 ETH min for Broadcaster/Reader, 0.5 ETH for Panelist
    }

    function register(uint256 seatId, bytes32 deviceX25519, uint8 roles, bytes calldata eip712WorkerAuth)
        external payable;                                   // proves seat ownership + device binding;
                                                            // verifies via ERC-1271 when holder has code (EIP-8151-safe)
    function commitAvailability(uint64 epoch, bytes32 commitment) external;   // H(seatId, epoch, salt)
    function revealAvailability(uint64 epoch, bytes32 salt, bytes calldata endpointHint) external;
    function reportOutcome(uint256 seatId, uint64 epoch, bool ok, bytes32 jobHash) external; // client-signed receipts
    function reputation(uint256 seatId) external view returns (uint32 ok, uint32 failed, uint64 lastSeen);
    function activeSet(uint64 epoch, Role r) external view returns (uint256[] memory seatIds);
    function withdrawBond(uint256 seatId) external;         // 7-day delay; blocked while slashable
}
  • Epoch: 7,200 blocks (about one day). Availability is commit-reveal so the active set for epoch E is fixed before E begins, but per-transaction selection stays client-side.
  • Probation: mirrors IMD's own rule. Two failed receipts in the last three put a seat last in client weighting for 24 hours. No seat is ever banned by the contract.
  • Sybil cost: a seat trades near 0.8 ETH (floor, Oct 2026) plus the ETH bond; every panel, proving triple and selection draw requires distinct holder values.

Broadcaster mesh protocol

Transport

  • Waku relay, content topic /murmuration/1/relay-<chainId>/proto, RLN membership per seat (one message per second per publisher).
  • Clients reach Waku through Tor or the Nym mixnet. Broadcasters never see a client IP; they see a mixnet exit.
  • Fee quotes are published to /murmuration/1/quotes-<chainId>/proto once per epoch and signed with the device key.

Client selection algorithm

candidates = activeSet(epoch, Broadcaster)
             .filter(seat => quote(seat).pools includes pool && quote(seat).feeBps <= maxFee)
groupByHolder(candidates)                    // one entry per holder wallet
weights = 1 + log(1 + ok) - 2*failedLast3    // mild, so new seats still get traffic
seed    = H(clientSecret, nullifier)         // deterministic per transaction, invisible on-chain
picks   = weightedSampleWithoutReplacement(groups, k = 3, seed)
send(picks[0]); if no receipt in 8 blocks → send(picks[1]); then picks[2]

Guarantees

  • Recipient, fee and relayer payout address are public inputs of the withdrawal proof, so a copied transaction cannot redirect funds or the fee.
  • The broadcaster's fee is minted as a note to its shielded address inside the pool, so broadcaster income never appears as a public payment linked to a client.
  • Broadcasters run Railgun's PPOI check or the panel ASP check before relaying, which is where screening is enforced for the pools that use it.

Panel ASP: contract, criteria and circuit

// PanelASP.sol  (one deployment per panel; immutable parameters)
interface IPanelASP {
    struct Root { bytes32 root; uint64 epoch; bytes32 criteriaHash; uint32 labelCount; }

    function panel() external view returns (uint256[] memory seatIds, uint8 threshold);   // e.g. 7 of 10
    function criteriaHash() external view returns (bytes32);          // keccak of IPFS-pinned criteria.json
    function submitRoot(Root calldata r, bytes[] calldata eip712Sigs) external;   // >= threshold distinct panelists
    function submitRootAggregated(Root calldata r, bytes calldata blsAggSig, uint256 signerBitmap) external;
                                                                      // one BLS12-381 pairing check (EIP-2537) for any panel size
    function dissent(uint64 epoch, bytes32 altRoot, bytes calldata sig) external;  // public, append-only
    function isKnownRoot(bytes32 root) external view returns (bool);  // ring buffer of last 64 roots
    function slash(uint256 seatId, bytes calldata proof) external;    // two objective conditions only
}

Objective slashing conditions

  1. Equivocation: two valid signatures by the same seat on different roots for the same epoch and criteria hash.
  2. Criteria violation: a signed root that includes a label whose deposit address appeared on a list named in criteria.json at the root's epoch, proven with a Merkle inclusion against the list's own published root. Anything that needs judgment is not slashable; it goes to the dissent log and to user choice of panel.

Withdrawal circuit additions

public inputs : aspRoot, poolRoot, nullifierHash, recipient, feeWei, relayer, chainId
private inputs: label, labelPath[], noteSecret, notePath[], value
constraints   : MerkleVerify(poolRoot, commit(noteSecret, value), notePath)
                MerkleVerify(aspRoot,  label, labelPath)            // membership in chosen panel's set
                nullifierHash == Poseidon2(noteSecret, leafIndex)
                isKnownRoot(aspRoot) checked by the contract, not the circuit

Union and intersection of panels are handled off-chain by publishing combined roots: a "union" panel signs the merge of two approved sets, and a wallet simply chooses which root to prove against.

Collaborative proving

  1. Wallet runs witness generation locally (about 2 GB peak on the reference circuits) and produces the full witness vector w.
  2. Wallet Shamir-shares w over the proof system's scalar field into three shares, picks three Prover seats from distinct holders with the selection algorithm above, and sends share i to seat i as a sealed box over Waku.
  3. Seats run coCircom (Groth16) or coNoir (UltraHonk) in a 3-party honest-majority protocol and return the proof plus a job receipt. Memory per party is about 8 GB; latency is comparable to local snarkjs plus network round trips.
  4. Wallet verifies the proof locally before use. Payment is a micro-note to each prover inside the transaction the proof authorizes.

Optional attestation: a prover inside a TDX confidential VM posts an Automata DCAP attestation to the registry once per epoch (about 0.43 to 0.57 M gas in SNARK-compressed form). The client may prefer attested provers but must never require them as the only guarantee.

Private read layer API

// Served over Waku request-response or a Nym service provider; no HTTP endpoint identity
GET  /v1/proof?block=latest&slot=<keccak(tree,index)>   → Merkle path verified against Helios header
POST /v1/ocall   { "to": "0x…", "data": "0x…", "block": "latest" }   → oblivious eth_call (TEE+ORAM)
POST /v1/notes   { "tags": ["0x…", "0x…", …], "from": 23400000 }    → note hints for tagged commitments
GET  /v1/nullifier?h=0x…                                              → spent? with header proof
  • PIR-lite: the wallet splits a discovery pass across k = 3 readers, pads each request with chaff tags so every reader sees a constant-size batch, and rotates readers per epoch.
  • Verification: every answer carries a Helios-verified header and a Merkle path; a reader can withhold but cannot forge.
  • Archive duty: readers keep the full commitment and ciphertext log of every supported pool from its genesis and pin a per-epoch snapshot to IPFS beside the stewardship root. Full nodes already drop pre-merge history, and the roadmap points toward shorter retention, so note discovery must never depend on a node serving old logs.
  • Later: fuzzy message detection lets readers pre-filter notes with a tunable false-positive rate, so the reader learns only a superset.

Worker integration

One binary, several roles. A seat operator installs murmuration-agent beside the IMD worker and declares it as a tool, the same way media tools are declared today. The agent reads only the seat's device key for signing and keeps its own X25519 key for sealed boxes.

# ~/.identitymd/tools.env  (declares the role so the seat can advertise it)
MURMURATION_ROLES=broadcaster,reader
MURMURATION_CHAIN=1
MURMURATION_TRANSPORT=nym          # or tor
MURMURATION_MAX_GAS_GWEI=3

# hardware tiers
# broadcaster + reader : 2 vCPU, 4 GB, Helios light client, no archive node
# prover               : 4 vCPU, 8 GB+ (coSNARK memory ~5x witness)
# panelist / steward   : 2 vCPU, 4 GB, needs the chain-evidence recipes IMD already ships
  • Systemd hardening from the existing VPS manual applies unchanged: unprivileged user, MemoryMax, read-only /opt, no inbound ports.
  • Logs are off by default. A broadcaster keeps only the receipt it signed and the fee note it received.
  • Nothing in the agent calls api.imd.fun. Role advertisement is on-chain; job intake is over Waku.

Legal gate: requirements every component must pass

The record is consistent. The Fifth Circuit protected only immutable contracts. Storm was convicted on relayer, frontend and fee facts. Samourai's founders ran a coordinator and collected fees. The DOJ's 2025 statements are discretionary and name front ends, upgrades, oracles and gateways as risk. The statutory safe harbour failed Senate cloture 49 to 50 in September 2026.

  • Core contracts are immutable: no admin, pause, upgrade or fee switch.
  • The project team writes software and operates nothing: no relayer, frontend, panel, reader or discloser.
  • No developer fee of any kind, in any token.
  • Frontend is built reproducibly, pinned to IPFS and named under ENS; the ENS name is held by a time-locked multi-party process.
  • Screening is real, published and executed by independent panels, with objective slashing only.
  • No component is brokered through IMD's control plane; admission is derived from on-chain ownership.
  • Disclosure is user-held, opt-in and revocable; there is no network viewing key.
  • Operators are jurisdictionally diverse and keep no logs beyond signed receipts.

Invariants the contracts must not assume

  • That a new storage slot costs about 22k gas, or that any opcode's price is fixed. Budget state and execution gas separately.
  • That refunds reduce the cost of a transaction or free block space.
  • That a fixed gas stipend forwarded to an external call is enough.
  • That ecrecover returns an address for every signer.
  • That a single transaction can carry more than 16,777,216 execution gas.
  • That full nodes serve logs from years ago.
  • That the mempool, builders or proposers will include a transaction in the next slot.

Before any mainnet deployment the contracts should be run against the Foundation's published gas-invariant checks for Glamsterdam, and the EIP-8037 and EIP-8038 parameters should be re-read, since the Foundation has said spec changes are being evaluated.

Goals integration

On 3 November 2026 IMD's developer previewed "Goals": the swarm is given one objective, splits into teams, pays agents per accepted item from an escrow with no admin, wakes on chain events, and commits proposals only after a jury (100 of 150 in the demo) simulates them, checks them against a mandate and signs. The tagline was "One multisig. No single key." Nothing about it is documented or deployed yet. What is already visible in the worker's release notes and shipped schemas (versions 0.1.11 to 0.1.12, 2 to 4 November 2026) is enough to say how Murmuration should and should not use it.

What the code already shows

  • Custody is standard Safe tooling, not IMD contracts. A vetted custody table lists Safe 1.4.1, MultiSend, Zodiac Roles v2, ModuleProxyFactory, Multicall3 and DelegationManager per chain, and a "fork rehearsal" moves every surfsurf.eth role to a custody Safe.
  • A mandate is a Zodiac-style scope. MandateScope carries targets, functions, argument rules, allowances, spenders, recipients and a gas budget, with a 90-day maximum, signed as EIP-712 typed data. A FORBIDDEN_SIGNATURES list bars Safe owner, threshold, module and guard changes, Roles admin, multicall, universal-router and ERC-4337 execute calls from any mandate.
  • Payroll is an epoch document. imd-epoch@1 holds a Merkle root of per-wallet and per-seat lines, an IPFS CID, a 72-hour challenge window, a 90-day minimum claim window, and a funding source of either treasury_safe or budget_escrow.
  • Keepers and goal skills exist. Keeper recipes (fees.claim, fees.withdraw, router.forward, distributor.sweep, gas.topup) and goal roles (orchestrator, lead, judge, check, code, chain, task) ship in the worker, switched by imd rpc goals on|off.

Read together, this is a protocol-level treasury Safe with scoped delegation to agents and a challengeable payroll. Per-agent multisigs, per-goal Safes, jury selection and any custody Safe address are not public.

On the jury size

The 100-of-150 figure is the demo's jury, not a Murmuration choice. A Safe verifies every signature on-chain, so cost scales with the signer count. Gas is cheap today; the real cost of a large jury is liveness, because ASP roots must refresh daily. A large jury suits rare treasury actions like the demo's buyback. A daily root wants a small panel of distinct holder wallets, rotated per epoch. If the Safe uses an aggregated threshold signature, the size question disappears.

Funding the security budget

The security spend cannot come from protocol revenue, so it needs an outside source and a custody shape that keeps the team's hands off it. Two IMD features bear on this.

  • An audit-fund Goal. The shipped schemas (budget escrow or treasury Safe, mandates with whitelisted recipients, allowances and a 90-day scope, distribution and vesting intents, jury refusal of anything outside the mandate) fit an audit fund: ETH is raised into the escrow, the mandate names the audit firms' payout addresses and releases tranches on milestones. Conditions: raise ETH, never a new token (a funding token recreates the TORN capture problem); audit firms sign engagement letters with a legal entity, so a minimal foundation or fiscal sponsor signs and is reimbursed by the Goal; whoever posts a public raise is an identifiable promoter, so publish the mandate first and take legal advice; and Goals is unreleased, so grants (EF protocol cluster, EthSystems, ecosystem programs) are the phase 1 fallback.
  • IMD audits as the continuous layer, not the only layer. IMD's audit template, adversarial-review panels of four specialists and a judge, and Foundry fuzz campaigns cost about 0.5 IMD (~$1.30) per request. Run them on every pull request from phase 0 and record findings in the WorkRegistry. They do not replace external audits: the bugs that break privacy systems are under-constrained circuits (Orchard undetected for four years; Aztec V4 and V5; Intmax, Firo and Beam in 2026), found by specialist cryptographers and formal methods; model-based reviewers share blind spots; and L2BEAT, Immunefi, exchanges and courts look for a named firm's report produced by an inspectable process, which IMD's closed verifier is not.

Budget effect: phase 1 holds only bonds, so continuous IMD review plus a funded bounty plus a reduced-scope external review of the registry and ASP contracts can bring phase 1 security to roughly $80k to $200k. Phase 2 does not shrink; IMD's contribution there is cleaner, better-fuzzed code going into the external engagements and regression review between them.

Capital that is locked rather than spent: 100 broadcaster bonds total 5 ETH (~$11k) and 30 panelist bonds total 15 ETH (~$33k), all returnable. For comparison, Tornado's DAO treasury holds about $26M in TORN and 0xbow raised $3.5M in November 2025 to run Privacy Pools with a team; Murmuration's structural advantage is that its operators are already paying for their machines for another reason.

Open questions

  1. Will IMD's operator add a tool declaration for an outward-facing role, and ship encrypted briefs and stealth payouts? Without the first, phase 0 broadcasters run beside the worker rather than through it; without the second, "private IMD" stays a specification.
  2. Can Privacy Pools' contract accept an alternative ASP root, or does the panel ASP first ship as an overlay that only wallets and exchanges consume?
  3. Which premium model tier do seat operators actually run? Co-proving needs memory, not model quality, so a separate hardware census is needed.
  4. Does the EBA's definition of "anonymity-enhancing" under AMLR Article 79 recognise user-held, revocable disclosure? This decides whether service 6 is a compliance bridge or a liability.
  5. How does Judge Failla's Rule 29 ruling and the April 2027 retrial narrow or widen the "operated service" theory that the legal gate is built around?
