# Qi's hash peg cannot anchor IMD compute

Quai's Qi is not an energy dollar in any measurable sense: it is a token whose per-block issuance is **linear in proof-of-work difficulty**, calibrated so that **one Qi costs about 8×10^12 KawPoW hashes** to mine, with a hard-coded 2.69-year depreciation schedule standing in for energy efficiency and a miner-preference controller standing in for a price oracle. Eighteen months after activation the design has not been tested: roughly **249,000 Qi exist**, the only market is a DEX pool doing **two trades and $429 a day** at about $0.40 per Qi against a design-time target near $1, and the conversion controller that was supposed to hold Qi at the cost of mining has been **administratively reset at least four times and frozen by the September 2026 fork**. Theory and history say this outcome was likely: production cost is a soft floor that follows price with a one-to-six-week lag, Bitcoin's USD hashprice fell **93% from October 2021 to June 2026**, hashrate tokens lost over 90% and were delisted, and every non-dollar "stable" unit (AMPL, RAI, Nuon, SPOT) has stayed at a few million dollars of float because nobody prices in it. For IMD the transplant fails at the first step, because IMD has no difficulty: its work is counted by a closed control plane and a single writer key, its marginal compute cost is near zero under operators' Claude and Codex subscriptions (11x–164x below metered prices), **97.6% of its tokens are unpaid self-generated oracle load**, and the underlying good deflates roughly 10x a year at fixed capability. The verdict is therefore to keep imdUSD as the unit of account and borrow from Qi only what works: price IMD's work in a published compute-cost index, mint a non-transferable compute credit by burning IMD with Akash-style circuit breakers, and build the per-task payment election that would, for the first time, give IMD a revealed-preference signal worth measuring.

## Qi is pegged to 8 trillion hashes, not to energy or dollars

Quai runs two coins off one proof-of-work emission, and the difference between them is a logarithm. The 2024 Primer states the reward functions verbatim: "BlockReward QUAI = kQUAI × log₂(Difficulty)" and "BlockReward QI = kQI × Difficulty", where difficulty is "the expected number of hashes needed to mine a block at the current difficulty target value" ([Quai Primer PDF, Oct 2024](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)). The same document is explicit that this is not a peg: "There is no fixed peg, backing nor collateralization, between or underlying either the Quai or Qi coins" ([Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)). The go-quai source makes the Qi side concrete. The reward is `difficulty / OneOverKqi(blockNumber)`, returned in Qits (0.001 Qi), and `OneOverKqi` is **8,000,000,000 hashes per Qit** after the Qi activation block 1,220,000 (26,000,000 before it), with the code comment "This is the number of hashes need to get 1 Qit. 8e9 is ~$0.001" ([go-quai protocol_params.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/params/protocol_params.go); [go-quai rewards.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/consensus/misc/rewards.go)). That gives **8×10^12 hashes per Qi**, which matches Quai's marketing shorthand that "100 MH/s per day = 1 QI" (100 MH/s for 86,400 seconds is 8.64×10^12 hashes) ([quai.org](https://www.quai.org/)) and reveals the design-time intent: one Qi was calibrated to roughly one dollar of mining.

The word "energy" enters only through a forecast. The Primer says k_Qi "has a decaying component to account for the projected growth in processing power over time, so that the Qi block rewards more closely reflect the energy cost of mining, rather than the increasing efficiency of computing power" ([Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)). In code, the hashes-per-Qit figure **doubles every 2.69 years** (`doublingPeriodInBlocks = 365 × BlocksPerDay × 2.69`, with 17,280 five-second blocks per day), citing Epoch AI's GPU performance forecast, and is fixed at four times the base after two doublings on the assumption that GPU gains "will plateau sometime after 2030" ([go-quai protocol_params.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/params/protocol_params.go)). Nothing in the protocol reads an electricity price, a hardware price or a joule count. Qi is stable in hashes by construction, stable in energy only if the 2.69-year schedule happens to match real efficiency gains, and stable in dollars only if the dollar cost of a hash happens to stay still. Since the December 2025 fork the "hash" is itself a synthetic quantity: block proof-of-work is KawPoW, SHA-256 and Scrypt merged-mining shares are converted through `ForkAwareKawPowEquivalentDifficulty` using fixed multipliers (`InitialShaDiffMultiple = 167000`, `InitialScryptDiffMultiple = 12`), so the "8×10^12 hashes per Qi" statement is exact only for native KawPoW work ([go-quai rewards.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/consensus/misc/rewards.go); [go-quai protocol_params.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/params/protocol_params.go)).

The stabiliser is the conversion controller, and its oracle is miner behaviour. Miners elect Quai or Qi for each block reward; anyone can burn one coin and mint the other at the ratio of the two current block rewards ("if the current potential block reward amount is x Quai coins and y Qi coins, conversions can be done at an exchange rate of x:y") ([Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf); [docs.qu.ai Qi Emissions](https://docs.qu.ai/learn/tokenomics/qi-emissions)). The protocol steers k_Quai, which is literally the block-header field `ExchangeRate`, so that the two rewards are equal in market value and both equal the cost of mining a block, "such that miners are economically indifferent as to whether they select/receive their reward in either Quai or Qi" ([Quai Primer PDF](https://www.qu.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Quai-Primer_10_14.pdf)). The update rule in the code comments is `k_quai += alpha × (x_b_star / x_d − 1) × k_quai` with **α = 1/1000 per block**, where the target comes from a regression over the last **4,000 blocks (about 5.6 hours)** of miner token choices and a 4,000-block EMA of difficulty ([go-quai rewards.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/consensus/misc/rewards.go); [docs.qu.ai Conversions](https://docs.qu.ai/learn/tokenomics/token-dynamics/conversions)). Conversions are deliberately slow and expensive at scale: every conversion pays a **minimum 20 basis points** plus a 30 bps minimum slip tolerance, a **cubic discount** `(1 − (value / (10 × average))³)` that reaches 100% when a single conversion exceeds ten times the recent mean flow, a floor guaranteeing the user at least 10% of value, a 100 Quai minimum flow, a 2x cap on flow growth, a directional "K-Quai discount" against the trend, and a **two-week lock** (241,920 blocks) on everything received ([go-quai rewards.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/consensus/misc/rewards.go); [go-quai protocol_params.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/params/protocol_params.go); [docs.qu.ai Conversions](https://docs.qu.ai/learn/tokenomics/token-dynamics/conversions)). Qi itself lives on a scriptless UTXO ledger with **15 fixed denominations from 0.001 to 1,000,000 Qi**, started from zero genesis supply, has no cap, and can only be minted by miner choice or conversion and only burned by conversion ([go-quai utxo.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/core/types/utxo.go); [FintechNews mirror of BusinessWire, Apr 2025](https://www.fintechnews.org/quai-network-launches-qi-the-first-decentralized-energy-dollar-new-flat-coin-backed-by-network-mining-power-begins-production/)).

| Qi parameter | Value in go-quai `main` (13 Oct 2026) | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Qi reward | `difficulty / OneOverKqi` | Linear in expected hashes per block |
| `OneOverKqi` | 8×10^9 hashes per Qit, doubling every 2.69 yr, capped at 4x | 8×10^12 hashes per Qi at activation; scheduled, not measured, efficiency correction |
| Quai reward | `ExchangeRate × log2(D) / 2^64` (minus log2 of a 3×10^11 floor post-KawPoW) | k_Quai is the steered header field |
| Controller gain | α = 1/1000 per block; 4,000-block windows | Full adjustment takes thousands of blocks |
| Conversion cost | 20 bps cubic min + 30 bps min slip; cubic to 100%; ≥10% floor | Small trades cheap, large trades punitive |
| Conversion lock | 2 weeks (241,920 blocks) | Arbitrage against external markets is slow |
| Supply rule | Σ emissions ± Σ conversions; zero genesis; no cap | Inflation bounded only by hashrate and miner choice |

The mechanism is coherent on its own terms, but every link in the chain from "hash" to "dollar" is an assumption rather than a measurement: hashes approximate energy via a GPU forecast, energy approximates cost via an unmeasured electricity price, and cost approximates market value via the premise that miners act as rational, prompt arbitrageurs between two thinly traded coins.

## Quai's controller was reset at least four times and frozen in September 2026

Production has not been kind to the controller leg. Qi activated on **16 April 2025**, two months after mainnet genesis on 4 February 2025 ([qu.ai/roadmap](https://www.qu.ai/roadmap); [FintechNews mirror of BusinessWire](https://www.fintechnews.org/quai-network-launches-qi-the-first-decentralized-energy-dollar-new-flat-coin-backed-by-network-mining-power-begins-production/)). The code then accumulates a series of hand-set overrides of k_Quai that no release note explains: a `KQuaiChangeTable` that cuts k_Quai to 50% at prime block 752,000 and to 75% at three subsequent intervals; a v0.48.0 "kQuai reset to baseline" activated 19 August 2025; an `ExchangeRateResetValueAfterKawpowFork` of **30x the initial rate** held for five months after the 17 December 2025 KawPoW/SOAP fork; an `ExchangeRateAfterShaEquivalentDifficultyFork` of **60x** held for four months from prime block 1,755,000; a `ConversionStabilityForkBlock` at 1,872,600; and finally a `KQuaiFreezeForkBlock` at prime block 2,237,000 whose comment reads that it "freezes the controller at its parent value until a future fork explicitly resumes or replaces it" ([go-quai protocol_params.go](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/blob/main/params/protocol_params.go); [qu.ai/roadmap](https://www.qu.ai/roadmap)). Between those overrides, v0.54.0 (June 2026) lengthened the EMA window so that rewards and conversion rates would be "more resilient to short-term hash rate swings, such as those from NiceHash" ([go-quai v0.54.0](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/releases/tag/v0.54.0)), and v0.56.0 (activated **15 September 2026**) removed Qi's share of the Quai fee pool so that Qi rewards "are calculated directly from the hash-proportional Qi reward" while Quai rewards and conversion pricing absorb a lagged, capped fee EMA ([go-quai v0.56.0](https://github.com/dominant-strategies/go-quai/releases/tag/v0.56.0); [2Miners, Oct 2026](https://2miners.com/blog/september-2026-work-progress-kaspa-reward-cut-and-quai-reward-changes/)). With k_Quai frozen and the Qi reward linear in difficulty, the Quai:Qi conversion ratio now moves only with difficulty and fees; the miner-preference feedback that was the whole point of the design is, per the code, switched off.

The pattern of resets tracks the arrival of merged mining. Once Bitcoin and Litecoin pools could contribute SHA-256 and Scrypt shares at near-zero marginal cost, "difficulty" had to be normalised across algorithms with fixed multipliers, and the controller that read miner choice as a cost signal was reading pools whose cost structure had nothing to do with Quai. Quai's own October 2024 mining-strategy post had already shown the ratio was gameable by difficulty timing: in a testnet example difficulty fell from about 415 billion to 344 billion, "Quai emissions were roughly 1% higher at the peak and Qi was emitting 20% more", and the recommended play was to convert Quai to Qi at the peak, mine Qi on the way down, convert back and "end with more Quai than at the start" ([qu.ai blog, 22 Oct 2024](https://www.qu.ai/blog/maximizing-mining-strategy-in-quai-network-choosing-between-quai-and-qi-tokens)). That is the project documenting that its stabiliser rewards those who trade against it.

The market record is too thin to test anything. Qi circulating supply was **about 248,912** on 20 July 2026 against roughly 1.041 billion QUAI, and QUAI's market capitalisation was "near $9 million" on 4 September 2026 ([Disruption Banking, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.disruptionbanking.com/2026/09/04/quai-network-supply-check-249000-qi-a-1-4-billion-quai-cap-and-unlocks-into-2029/)). Qi has no listing on CoinGecko or CoinMarketCap; the only quote is a QuaiSwap WQI/WQUAI pool at **$0.3972 per WQI with $429 of 24-hour volume, about $91,000 of liquidity and two transactions in a day** ([GeckoTerminal WQI/WQUAI](https://www.geckoterminal.com/quai-network/pools/0x001f91029df78af6d13cbffa8724f1b2718da3f1); [CoinGecko QUAI](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/quai-network)). That prices the entire Qi float near $100,000, under 2% of a system whose base coin has itself fallen from an all-time high of $0.3634 on 23 February 2025 to an all-time low near $0.0074 on 2 September 2026 ([CoinGecko QUAI](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/quai-network)). A $0.40 quote against a design calibration of about $1 looks like a 60% shortfall, but a two-trade-a-day pool cannot establish a price, let alone a depeg; it mostly echoes the protocol conversion rate. The only evaluative independent assessment reaches the same conclusion: Qi is "a working design with yet to be proven economics" that has "not shown stability under load", has "no reserve and no redemption promise", and the right test, whether supply and implied price "hold a steady relationship as the float expands", has not been run ([Disruption Banking, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.disruptionbanking.com/2026/09/04/quai-network-supply-check-249000-qi-a-1-4-billion-quai-cap-and-unlocks-into-2029/)). Quai's own language has retreated in step: the August 2024 blog called Qi "Quai's stablecoin alternative" and "the new category for stablecoins" ([qu.ai blog, 29 Aug 2024](https://www.qu.ai/blog/a-new-era-of-stablecoin-qi-the-energy-dollar)), the April 2025 launch release called it a "flat-coin" with no stability claim in the body ([FintechNews mirror of BusinessWire](https://www.fintechnews.org/quai-network-launches-qi-the-first-decentralized-energy-dollar-new-flat-coin-backed-by-network-mining-power-begins-production/)), while current docs still say "By pegging Qi to energy costs, we create a currency with real-world stability" ([docs.qu.ai Qi Emissions](https://docs.qu.ai/learn/tokenomics/qi-emissions)). No third party has published a formal critique, which means the design has also never faced adversarial review.

## A century of energy money says cost anchors from below, and leaks

The intellectual lineage of Qi is long and uniformly unimplemented. Henry Ford's 1921 proposal to finance Muscle Shoals with currency backed by hydro horsepower ("This energy is wealth in a productive form") was withdrawn in 1924; Technocracy Inc.'s 1930s energy certificates were denominated in ergs but were non-transferable rations that expired and could not circulate; Hayek's 1943 commodity-reserve currency, building on Benjamin Graham, was redeemable in a warehoused basket, which is exactly what a hash peg lacks ([Steve Keen, Ford and Edison](https://profstevekeen.substack.com/p/ford-and-edison-the-anti-gold-bugs); [Wikipedia, Technocracy movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocracy_movement); [AIER, Hayek's Commodity Reserve Currency](https://aier.org/article/hayeks-commodity-reserve-currency/)). Szabo's "unforgeable costliness" argues that cost makes a collectible hard to forge, not that price equals cost ([Nakamoto Institute, Shelling Out](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/shelling-out/)). The sharpest statement of the problem came from Satoshi on 7 August 2010: "The price of any commodity tends to gravitate toward the production cost... In later years, when new coin generation is a small percentage of the existing supply, market price will dictate the cost of production more than the other way around" ([bitcointalk, 7 Aug 2010](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=721.msg8114#msg8114)). That second sentence is the whole case against hash-pegged money: once a stock exists, causality runs from price to cost.

The empirical literature confirms the direction. A 2025 survey calls it "a robust finding across multiple studies" that Granger causality runs **unidirectionally from price to hashrate with lags of one to six weeks** ([arXiv 2508.06071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06071)); a 2020 study finds causality "always unidirectional going from the bitcoin price to the hashrate", on the oil-price-to-rig-count analogy ([MDPI JRFM 13(11):263](https://mdpi.com/1911-8074/13/11/263/html)); Prat and Walter's structural model in the JPE shows free entry capping mining revenue so that price forecasts hashrate ([IDEAS/JPE 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/doi10.1086-714445.html)). Production cost does operate as a floor, but a leaky one: Capriole's electricity-cost model says price "never quite reaches" electrical cost but came "very close" in November 2018, and its Energy Value fair-value estimate has seen price range from 70% below to 600% above it ([Capriole, Dec 2019](https://capriole.com/bitcoins-production-cost/); [Cryptonomist, Feb 2020](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2020/02/17/bitcoin-energy-value/)). The floor also moves: when inefficient miners capitulate, difficulty falls, cost per coin falls, and the floor follows price down ([Unlock-BC](https://unlock-bc.com/en/bitcoin-energy-value-miners-profitability)). A formal result closes the loop: "without an external anchor, stablecoins may have no value even with full commitment" ([d'Avernas, Maurin and Vandeweyer, FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/analysis/cfr/bank-research-conference/annual-21st/papers/davernas-paper.pdf)). For a dual-token system the relevant market price is Quai's, so the "energy anchor" is downstream of Quai speculation; a Quai rally raises difficulty and Qi emission, a crash lowers both, which makes Qi supply pro-cyclical, the opposite of what a stabiliser needs.

### Hashprice fell 93% in five years, so a hash unit is not a dollar

The dollar value of a fixed quantity of hashing is one of the most volatile prices in crypto. Bitcoin's USD hashprice peaked at about **$412 per PH/s per day on 19–20 October 2021**, fell 76% in 2022 to $59.42, recovered 71% in 2023, broke below $50 for the first time after the April 2024 halving, hit a five-year low of $38.2 in November 2025, and reached roughly **$27.70 at the end of June 2026** before recovering to about $40 in early October 2026 ([Hashrate Index 2021 Year-End Report](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/hashrate-index-2021-year-end-report-the-year-of-the-greath-hashrate-migration/); [Hashrate Index 2023 Year in Review](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/content/files/2024/01/Hashrate_Index_2023-Year-in-Review.pdf); [CoinDesk, 18 Nov 2025](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/11/18/bitcoin-hashprice-falls-to-five-year-low); [Hoge.gg, Oct 2026](https://hoge.gg/hashprice-2026-difficulty-ceiling-miner-recovery/)). A token defined as "X hashes" would have lost about 93% of its dollar value over that window, and Luxor's head of derivatives describes hashrate as having "one of the highest volatilities of major commodities", which is why a regulated hashrate futures market exists at all ([CoinDesk, 19 Mar 2024](https://coindesk.com/business/2024/03/19/luxor-looks-to-help-bitcoin-miners-hedge-halving-risk-with-new-hashrate-futures/)). The products that most resembled a hash-denominated token confirm it: Binance's BTCST (0.1 TH/s per token) fell about 97% in 2022 and was delisted that December, and Poolin's pBTC35A halted payouts in June 2021 when Chinese provinces shut mining down, a reminder that "hash backing" is jurisdiction-specific physical capacity ([CoinCodex, BTCST](https://coincodex.com/crypto/bitcoin-standard-hashrate-token); [TokenInsight](https://tokeninsight.com/en/news/binance-will-delist-mith-tribe-rep-and-btcst); [The Block, Jun 2021](https://www.theblock.co/linked/109600)).

Nor is "energy per hash" a constant that the 2.69-year schedule can rescue. ASIC efficiency improved from about 29.5 J/TH (S19j Pro) to 9.5 J/TH (S23 Hydro), electricity for live mining ranges from under 6 cents per kWh in Texas to over 20 cents in Western Europe, and electricity is only about 60% of all-in cost ([Hoge.gg](https://hoge.gg/hashprice-2026-difficulty-ceiling-miner-recovery/); [CoinShares Q1 2026](https://coinshares.com/insights/research-data/bitcoin-mining-report-q1-2026/); [Capriole](https://capriole.com/bitcoins-production-cost/)). Applied to Quai, where an RTX 3080 does roughly 42 MH/s KawPoW at 250 W, the "100 MH/s for a day" Qi works out to about 14 kWh, which is **$0.70 of electricity at 5 cents and $2.90 at 20 cents** before hardware, and before noting that merged SHA-256 and Scrypt shares have an entirely different energy content ([Kryptex RTX 3080](https://pool.kryptex.com/device/gpu/nvidia/nvidia-geforce-rtx-3080-12gb); [quai.org](https://www.quai.org/)). The energy content of one Qi is thus ambiguous by at least 4x on electricity price alone.

### Every non-dollar stable unit has stayed tiny

Even designs with far better oracles than miner preference have failed to become money once they abandoned $1. Ampleforth targets a CPI-adjusted 2019 dollar through daily rebases, trades at about $1.23–1.28 in mid-2026 and has a market capitalisation variously reported between $4 million and $35 million ([Chainlink case study](https://chain.link/case-studies/ampleforth); [Coinranking](https://coinranking.com/coin/_cgMK4FnC+ampleforth-ampl/historical-data); [Blockspot](https://blockspot.io/coin/ampleforth-ampl/price-prediction/)). RAI, the closest cousin to the reader's Liquity-style design, floats its redemption price through a PI controller; its founder called the ETH-only choice a "mistake", negative redemption rates near −10% a year were common, Vitalik Buterin netted about $92,000 shorting it, and supply has shrunk to about 550,000 ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/rai-regrets-mistake-eth); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/205078/wsiew@theblock.co); [CoinGecko RAI](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/rai/usd)). Nuon and SPOT have no visible traction ([Benzinga, Oct 2022](https://benzinga.com/markets/cryptocurrency/22/10/29389763/laguna-labs-launches-the-worlds-first-flatcoin-pegged-to-the-cost-of-living); [The Block, SPOT](https://www.theblock.co/post/193230/ampleforth-developer-debuts-ethereum-based-inflation-resistant-token)). The common pattern is that a drifting unit imposes an opportunity cost that holders refuse to bear, no venue prices in it, and the team eventually pivots to a harder peg (Reflexer's contributors forked to multi-collateral HAI) or stagnates. A hash-denominated unit inherits all of this plus a reference that is far more volatile in dollars than CPI.

The "compute dollar" literature adds one more hazard specific to AI. No production network pegs a token to a compute unit; the dominant live pattern (Helium, Render, Akash, io.net) is the reverse, an external USD oracle used to burn the volatile token for a **non-transferable dollar credit** spent only inside the network ([Helium docs, Data Credit](https://docs.helium.com/tokens/data-credit); [Render Knowledge Base, BME](https://know.rendernetwork.com/basics/burn-mint-equilibrium); [Akash AEP-76](https://akash.network/roadmap/aep-76/)). The financial plumbing for a GPU index now exists (Silicon Data's H100 rental index SDH100RT, on Bloomberg since 20 May 2025, moved only $2.00–2.26 between August 2025 and January 2026, and CME announced cash-settled compute futures for 5 October 2026 pending CFTC review), but the same H100 rents for **$1.79 to $10 an hour across providers**, and the index's weighting and manipulation resistance are proprietary ([Silicon Data launch](https://www.silicondata.com/news-room/silicon-data-launches-world-s-first-daily-gpu-rental-index-to-bring-transparency-to-the-ai-infrastructure-economy-copy); [Finance Magnates, CME](https://www.financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/cme-group-says-it-will-launch-first-regulated-compute-futures-contracts/); [Social Capital brief](https://thebrief.socialcapital.com/briefs/compute-financialization)). More fundamentally, the price of inference at fixed capability falls about **10x per year** by a16z's estimate and between 9x and 900x per year across benchmarks by Epoch AI's ([a16z LLMflation](https://a16z.com/llmflation-llm-inference-cost/); [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends)). A unit defined as "a million tokens of GPT-4-class work" is therefore a hyper-deflationary asset in dollar terms; a unit defined as "one H100-hour" is stable-ish in dollars but silently loses capability. Qi's 2.69-year doubling schedule looks leisurely next to that.

## IMD's work has no cost that is expensive to fake

Transplanting Qi requires an analogue of difficulty: a measure of work that every participant can verify and that is expensive to forge because something real is burned. IMD has none of the three properties. The network is a centrally orchestrated control plane at api.imd.fun that sells six action types for a **flat 0.5 IMD each**, plans them into jobs, offers tasks to NFT-gated "seats" running the open-source worker on the operator's own Claude Code or Codex subscription, verifies results with a central verifier plus peer review or oracle-panel agreement, and records accepted work as reputation ([imd.fun docs](https://imd.fun/docs/); [Bankless, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.bankless.com/read/inside-imd-ethereum-s-new-ai-swarm-experiment)). The control plane is closed-source (the public worker is "generated from the main development repository"), Intake completions on all three chains are written by a **single writer key** (0x3d3c…25cb), and oracle work is committed on-chain only as one Merkle root per UTC day ([github.com/Identity-md](https://github.com/Identity-md); [api.imd.fun/health](https://api.imd.fun/health); [imd.fun docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). Every headline counter (1,027 agents online, 1,041 seats enrolled, 5,603 accepted in the last day, **198.3 billion inference tokens** as of 13 October 2026) is self-reported by that plane ([api.imd.fun/swarm](https://api.imd.fun/swarm)). Nothing is costly to fake the way hashrate is, because the cost of a false counter is zero and the cost of a true one is borne by someone else's subscription.

The cost structure is the second disqualifier. Operators are not paid per task; their IMD income is a 4.5% share of POOL4 burn trims and a slice of the 10% launch allocations, and Bankless notes that "Seats don't earn much yet" ([Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/inside-imd-ethereum-s-new-ai-swarm-experiment); [imd.fun docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). Compute is externalised to $20–200 a month Claude and ChatGPT plans, and the INFER whitepaper's measurement from api.imd.fun/contributors (642 seats, 644,734 attempts) finds effective subscription rates of **$0.0163–0.2334 per million tokens against metered rates of $0.518–2.662**, an 11x–164x gap; one $20 Claude Pro seat sustained 1.23 billion tokens a month before hitting its session limit ([INFER whitepaper §6.5, App. C](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/)). Marginal cost is therefore near zero until a quota binds, then jumps to API overage, and the supply ceiling is set by two vendors' rate limits rather than by physics: the fleet logged 61 rate-limit events in one peak hour, and the VPS guide has already downgraded its recommended model from gpt-6-astra at high effort to gpt-5.6-luna at medium ([INFER §8.9](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/); [imd-worker-vps-manual](https://github.com/identitynode40/imd-worker-vps-manual)). Any currency anchored to IMD work would, in effect, be anchored to Anthropic and OpenAI consumer-plan policy, which can be repriced overnight.

The third disqualifier is what the work is. As of early October, oracle self-assessment was **88% of all jobs and 97.6% of all tokens** (119.2 billion tokens, $61,132 metered-equivalent) with no payer, while paid demand was 0.6–1.6 billion tokens from ten wallets and lifetime fee revenue was $1,590 from 530 paid actions ([INFER §6.1](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/)). Paid orders then grew to 4,202 by 13 October, but the health endpoint does not break them down by action and `schedule.topup` is charged per run ([api.imd.fun/health](https://api.imd.fun/health)). A "unit of work" exists only statistically: an oracle node-attempt is about 114,000 tokens by regression (151,000 measured on Codex, 255,000 on Claude), a build node-attempt about 380,000, which at OpenRouter rates is **$0.08–0.45 per oracle attempt and about $9.50 per workflow attempt** ([INFER App. C.3, §6.2](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/)). Against a $3.00 fee (0.5 IMD at $6), a panel-of-five oracle request is over-recovered 7.4x and a full workflow is under-recovered 3.2x, delivered workflows cost $24.59 against $7.75 of fees, and failed workflows consume 114% of the tokens of completed ones ([INFER §6.2–6.4](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/)). The counters are also unstable in meaning: Bankless reported about 29,600 accepted submissions in 24 hours on 25 September, while the swarm endpoint showed 5,603 "accepted in last day" on 13 October, which is either a change in what "accepted" counts or an 80% throttling of oracle volume in two weeks, and both readings are IMD's own ([Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/inside-imd-ethereum-s-new-ai-swarm-experiment); [api.imd.fun/swarm](https://api.imd.fun/swarm)).

The oracle weaknesses already visible in imdUSD show how this plays out. Its launch parameters accept a signed answer reporting a panel of at least 25 agents with 15 agreeing, but "the contract checks the thresholds but does not see individual agent answers"; its price is a median of 13 readings from the same pool, not independent of spot; and its Network Health Index panel reads api.imd.fun/swarm while the feed "cannot re-fetch the counters itself" and the docs describe no safeguards against manipulating them ([imdusd.com swarm-evidence](https://imdusd.com/docs/economics/swarm-evidence); [imdusd.com network-health](https://imdusd.com/docs/governance/network-health)). The implemented NHI (participation 40%, service availability 30%, reliability 30%) also differs materially from the whitepaper's (acceptance rate 40%, online ratio 25%, 7d/30d volume 20%, median completion 15%), a sign the design is still moving; on 13 October's counters the implemented formula reads about 0.99, so it currently discriminates nothing ([imdusd.com network-health](https://imdusd.com/docs/governance/network-health); [INFER §4 L6](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/); [api.imd.fun/swarm](https://api.imd.fun/swarm)). Verifiable-inference technology does not close the gap: zkML proofs for 70B-class models take hours to weeks, Proof-of-Sampling schemes give probabilistic correctness, and GPU TEEs run with under 5% overhead but require trusting the hardware vendor, and all of them attest that an output is right, not what it cost ([arXiv 2405.00295](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00295); [Equilibrium, State of Verifiable Inference](https://equilibrium.co/writing/state-of-verifiable-inference); [arXiv 2409.03992](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.03992v2)). Bittensor is the cautionary tale for subjective work scoring: its emission allocation swung between price-based and flow-based models within a year ([Bittensor emissions docs](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/concepts/emissions); [learnbittensor, Aug 2026](https://guides.learnbittensor.org/learn/emissions)).

## Verdict: index IMD's prices to compute cost, do not mint a compute coin

The evidence supports a clear answer. A Qi-style "compute dollar" for IMD should not be built, for four reasons that compound. Qi itself has not demonstrated that an endogenous cost peg holds; its controller needed four manual resets and a freeze on a network with a verifiable, costly work signal. The dollar value of a fixed quantity of work is among the most volatile prices in crypto (hashprice −93% in five years) and the dollar value of a fixed quantity of inference deflates about 10x a year, so a compute-denominated unit would be a short on AI progress, not a stable medium. IMD lacks the one ingredient that made Qi's anchor coherent, an objectively verifiable, expensive-to-fake measure of work. And the market has already voted: every non-dollar unit has stayed at a few million dollars of float, while imdUSD, a dollar unit collateralised by sIMD, is live on mainnet since 11 October 2026 and gives IMD the numeraire users actually hold. The reader's design study reached the right conclusion in preferring a Liquity-style ETH-collateralised coin with a Uniswap V4 peg hook; Qi's record strengthens that preference rather than challenging it.

### What transfers from Qi

Four ideas survive the transplant. The first is Qi's **zero-genesis, emission-only-through-work** supply rule: a credit that can only come into existence when verified work is done, and only leave when it is spent or converted, keeps supply tied to activity rather than to a treasury. The second is the **burn-and-mint two-way conversion with volume-dependent friction**: Quai's 20 bps minimum, cubic discount, flow caps and lock period are a reasonable template for discouraging arbitrage at scale between IMD and any internal credit, and Akash's AEP-76 shows the same ideas in a production system with a 0–25 bps mint spread, a remint vault of previously burned tokens, and collateral-ratio circuit breakers that warn at 0.95 and halt mints at 0.90 ([Akash AEP-76](https://akash.network/roadmap/aep-76/)). The third is **revealed preference as a cost signal**: Quai's insight that the choice workers make between two reward assets encodes their relative valuation of them is the only oracle-free cost signal found anywhere, and it is the one thing an agent network could in principle generate. The fourth is the **scheduled efficiency correction**: Qi's k_Qi doubling is an attempt to make the unit track energy rather than hashes as hardware improves, and Arweave's choice to assume the slowest plausible storage-cost decline (0.5% a year against a historical 30%) is the conservative version of the same move ([permaweb-journal, storage endowment](https://permaweb-journal.ar.io/reference/storage-endowment.html)). For IMD, with 10x-a-year inference deflation, the lesson is to rebase a cost index continuously from measured prices rather than to hard-code a schedule.

### What does not transfer

Difficulty does not transfer; IMD has no proof of work and no equivalent of a hash that every node can verify. Energy does not transfer; IMD's cost is subscription policy, the operators' electricity is negligible under a 1 CPU / 3 GiB worker profile, and the dollar cost of a token of inference varies 5.5x between model blends measured on a single day ([imd-worker-vps-manual](https://github.com/identitynode40/imd-worker-vps-manual); [INFER App. C.2](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/)). The miner-choice oracle does not transfer today, because IMD operators are not paid per task and so make no reward choice that could be observed. The "stablecoin" framing does not transfer; Qi is at best stable in hashes, and IMD would be at best stable in attempts. And Qi's refusal of collateral and redemption does not transfer: the reader's collateralised design already avoids the trap that d'Avernas and co-authors identify and that Terra demonstrated, where a two-token mint-and-burn system with no outside asset hyperinflated the backing token from 343 million to 32 billion units in four days ([arXiv 2207.13914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.13914.pdf)).

### The oracle and measurement problem

Whatever IMD anchors to, the measurement chain today terminates in IMD's own keys. The swarm counters, the daily Merkle roots, the Intake completions and the EIP-712 oracle attestations that imdUSD verifies are all produced by platform-operated services, and the INFER paper itself describes "the platform's oracle signer" aggregating and countersigning panel responses ([INFER §4](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/)). Three measurement gaps need closing before any cost signal is trustworthy. The semantics of "accepted" must be fixed and documented, because the 29,600-versus-5,603 discrepancy makes any index built on it unstable. Per-attempt token counts must be evidenced rather than self-reported, which means the control plane collecting vendor-side usage receipts from Claude Code and Codex sessions (or TEE-attested worker runs) rather than trusting the worker's own report; this remains vendor-trusted, but it is at least a second party. And the paid, externally demanded 2.4% of tokens must be separated from the unpaid 97.6% of self-generated oracle load, because an index that averages both is mostly measuring the network talking to itself. Until those three are done, imdUSD's NhiFeed and any future compute index are measuring the honesty and uptime of IMD's operator, not the cost of compute.

### What to test or build next

The first thing to build is a **cost-indexed tariff priced in imdUSD**, replacing the flat 0.5 IMD. The data to do it exist: INFER's measured tokens per attempt by skill and its proposed schedule (0.10 IMD plus 0.02 per panel member for oracle requests, 1.0 for contract and frontend stages, 0.5 for sites, 0.25 per revision, 2.85 IMD for a full three-stage workflow) can be re-expressed in dollars and repriced monthly from published model rates ([INFER §5.1, §6.3](https://whitepaper.imdusd.com/)). This captures the real benefit of Qi, pricing work at its cost, without issuing a new money, and it fixes the current 7.4x over-recovery on small oracle requests and 3.2x under-recovery on workflows.

The second is a **non-transferable IMD compute credit** on the Akash ACT pattern: burn IMD at a 30-minute TWAP taken as the median of the Uniswap V4 pool and an external feed, mint a dollar-denominated credit spendable only on IMD actions, settle operator pay from a remint vault first and new mints second, and pause mints when the vault's collateral ratio falls below 0.90 ([Akash AEP-76](https://akash.network/roadmap/aep-76/)). Quai's cubic discount and two-week lock are the right friction for the IMD-to-credit leg. Non-transferability is what makes this safe: credits cannot be arbitraged on exchanges, so the oracle only needs to be honest at mint and settlement.

The third is **per-task operator pay with a payment-asset election**. Pay accepted work in either IMD or imdUSD at the operator's choice, record the election per attempt, and publish the running ratio. This is the only way IMD can ever generate a Qi-like revealed-preference signal, and the signal should be observed for months, across at least one IMD drawdown of the size already seen (a 32% single-day fall in October), before anything is wired to it ([LiveCoinWatch IMD](https://www.livecoinwatch.com/price/Identitymd-IMD)).

The fourth is the **empirical test on Qi that nobody has run**. The `ExchangeRate` header field and difficulty are in every go-quai block; computing the implied Quai:Qi conversion ratio over time, overlaying the fork heights and the WQI/WQUAI pool price, and checking whether the ratio ever tracked a plausible cost of mining would settle, cheaply, whether Quai's controller ever worked before it was frozen. The result would also calibrate expectations for any revealed-preference controller at IMD.

The fifth is **hardening imdUSD's existing oracle path**, which matters more than any new unit: move Intake completions and oracle attestations behind a multisig or threshold key, have the NhiFeed carry a reproducible input (the daily Merkle root and the counters it was computed from) so third parties can re-derive it, and replace the 13-reading same-pool median with a feed that includes at least one independent source. Finally, before any of the above goes live, **simulate the reflexive case**: operators electing the credit over IMD during a price fall, subscription quotas exhausting fleet-wide, and the remint vault draining, with Akash's breakers as the baseline defence and Terra's May 2022 path as the failure to avoid.

## Conclusion

The deeper lesson of Qi is that "oracle-free" is not the same as "measurement-free". Quai replaced a price feed with a behavioural signal and found that the signal needed constant manual correction once the people generating it (merged-mining pools) had cost structures unrelated to the network; the freeze in September 2026 is the protocol admitting that a controller cannot infer cost from choices when the choosers are not the ones bearing the cost. IMD is in the same position in a purer form: the operators bearing the cost are paid nothing per task, so there are no choices to read, and the counters that exist are produced by the party that would benefit from them being high. A compute-indexed unit for IMD is not blocked by cryptography or controller design but by the absence of a credible, independently reproducible measurement of paid work, and that absence is also the biggest open risk in imdUSD as launched.

That reframing points to the productive path. The question "can IMD mint a compute dollar?" should become "can IMD measure its compute well enough to price it?", and the answer to that can be yes within months rather than years: fix the semantics of accepted work, evidence token counts with vendor receipts, separate paid from self-generated load, and publish a cost index that reprices fees in imdUSD. Once that index exists and operators are paid per task, IMD will have, as a by-product, exactly the revealed-preference data that Quai tried to bootstrap from mining, and will be able to test rather than assume whether a compute-anchored unit could hold. Until then, the dollar is the right numeraire, compute is the right thing to index, and the coin and the index should remain two different objects.
