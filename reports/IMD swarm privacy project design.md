# Turn IMD's Idle Swarm Into Privacy Infrastructure

Ethereum's privacy problem in November 2026 is not cryptography; it is the periphery. Tornado Cash's immutable pools were never broken, yet academic heuristics link **5–35% of withdrawals to deposits** through user behaviour, the relayer that pays gas for a fresh address is simultaneously the biggest metadata leak and the component that got Roman Storm convicted, every "compliant" pool depends on a single operator's secret screening list, RPC endpoints time users against withdrawals, and the market has collapsed to one uncompliant mixer (~$740–794M TVL) and two compliant pools whose combined TVL is roughly 15% of it. IMD (imd.fun) is a six-week-old, unaudited, pseudonymously run network of **1,136 ERC-8004-registered AI worker agents on Ethereum mainnet** (921 online, 8 working on 3 Nov 2026), with no privacy features of its own, public job bodies and public per-wallet order histories. What it does have is exactly what Ethereum privacy lacks: a scarce, costly-to-forge identity per agent (a 0.8–2 ETH seat NFT bound to a canonical ERC-8004 agentId and a device key), a working pattern for unanimous EIP-712-signed panel attestations, chain-evidence recipes, probation-based routing, an on-chain WorkRegistry, and roughly 900 always-on, mostly idle machines whose operators are independent of any developer. This report argues that the right project is not another pool but a swarm-operated service layer, which we call Murmuration, that plugs into Railgun and Privacy Pools today and can later back an immutable, ownerless pool: a permissionless rotating broadcaster mesh over Waku and Tor paid inside the shielded pool; a multi-party Association Set Provider built from agent panels with published criteria and slashable signatures (nobody has built a multi-ASP, which the notes confirm is whitespace); three-party collaborative proving so phones never hand a witness to one prover; a Helios-plus-oblivious-read layer; anonymity-set stewardship agents that counter AI chain analysis with AI hygiene; and per-transaction, time-bounded, revocable threshold disclosure. The legal design requirement is non-negotiable and comes straight from Van Loon, the Storm and Samourai outcomes, the Blanche and Galeotti policies and the failed CLARITY cloture: immutable core, no admin keys, no developer-run relayer, frontend, or ASP, no developer fee, and screening that is real. The risks are equally concrete: IMD's closed control plane and single deployer, sybil accumulation of seats (some wallets already hold ~10 agents), agent collusion thresholds, TEE physical attacks (TEE.fail), prosecutorial-discretion reversals, and a volatile token that must be kept out of the fee path.

## Part one: what five years of privacy engineering actually taught

### Tornado Cash's pools held; its mutable periphery is what got compromised and prosecuted

Tornado Cash Classic is the cleanest existing specification of a fixed-denomination mixer and the best-documented failure of everything around one. A deposit inserts a Pedersen-hash commitment of a random nullifier and secret into a height-20 MiMC Merkle tree; a withdrawal is a Groth16 proof of membership plus a nullifier hash, with the recipient, relayer and fee bound into the public inputs so the contract pays any address with no on-chain link and no relayer can redirect funds ([Tornado Cash whitepaper v1.4](https://berkeley-defi.github.io/assets/material/Tornado%20Cash%20Whitepaper.pdf)). The circuit has 28,271 constraints, proves in about 10 seconds, and withdrawal costs about 301k gas ([tornado-core README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tornadocash/tornado-core/master/README.md)). After a 1,114-contribution Phase 2 ceremony, the pool contracts became immutable on 18 May 2020: "No one can pause, upgrade, or block them" ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)). That immutability became the decisive legal fact. The Fifth Circuit held on 26 November 2024 that the immutable contracts "are not property because they are not capable of being owned," are "not ownable, not contracts, and not services," and so cannot be blocked under IEEPA ([Van Loon v. Treasury, 5th Cir.](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)). OFAC delisted the protocol on 21 March 2025 ([Treasury sb0057](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0057)) and Judge Pitman permanently enjoined re-sanctioning on 28 April 2025 ([CourtListener docket 106](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txwd.1211705/gov.uscourts.txwd.1211705.106.0.pdf)). The opinion explicitly leaves mutable contracts open, which matters for every successor that keeps an upgrade key.

Everything that went wrong happened in the mutable periphery. The TORN-staked relayer registry, governance, the IPFS frontend hash and the fee switch were all DAO-controlled ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)). In May 2023 a disguised proposal with self-destruct logic minted 1.2M fake votes and drained 483,000 TORN (~$2.17M) ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-tornado-cash-hack-may-2023)); from January 2024 a governance-approved frontend build exfiltrated users' deposit notes to an external server for about two months ([CoinDesk, 26 Feb 2024](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/business/2024/02/26/tornado-cash-reportedly-suffers-backend-exploit-user-deposits-at-risk)); at delisting ZachXBT warned "the main frontend is still compromised" ([Bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/ofac-unshackles-tornado-cash-but-security-hawks-warn-frontend-remains-a-minefield)); in June 2026 a look-alike-address treasury proposal targeted ~$23M TORN ([L2BEAT timeline](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)). The criminal case followed the same seam. Prosecutors argued the founders paid to host the website, manually whitelisted relayers until March 2022 while **98% of users used the relayer network**, profited "through [their] control, with others, of key components of the integrated Tornado Cash service," ran a cosmetic UI-level OFAC screen, and privately wrote "guys we are fucked" after the Ronin attribution ([The Block, Apr 2024](https://www.theblock.co/post/291286/doj-says-roman-semenov-did-much-more-than-just-write-code-in-response-to-defenses-dismissal-motion)). On 6 August 2025 the jury convicted Storm on one count of conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money-transmitting business and hung on laundering and sanctions counts ([DOJ SDNY](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/founder-tornado-cash-crypto-mixing-service-convicted-knowingly-transmitting-criminal)); his Rule 29 acquittal motion has been pending since the April 2026 argument and the retrial is set for **26 April 2027** ([The Block, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-08-26-tornado-cash-roman-storm-retrial-april-2027-412761)). In the Netherlands, the court convicted Alexey Pertsev to 64 months on the theory that "it was Tornado Cash that executed the concealing and disguising activities" ([CoinDesk, 14 May 2024](https://dev.coindesk.com/policy/2024/05/14/tornado-cash-developer-alexey-pertsev-found-guilty-of-money-laundering)); the appeal ordered additional investigation in October 2025 with the Ethereum Foundation and Vitalik Buterin among defence donors, and no appellate verdict exists as of November 2026 ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/ethereum-foundation-keyring-partner-to-fund-tornado-cash-defence/)). The U.S. position is therefore split: civil law treats the pools as un-ownable code; criminal law treats the integrated service as a business its operators ran.

One detail should shape any successor's relayer design: Chainalysis told the court it had itself "operated a relayer node" for Tornado Cash "for a period in 2022" and earned fees ([Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/storm-says-chainalysis-fees-tornado-cash/)). A relayer sees the withdrawer's IP, timing and recipient before anyone else. The component users are told to trust for privacy was, for a period, run by the industry's largest surveillance vendor.

Tornado remains enormous by value and shrinking by use. L2BEAT's 3 November 2026 snapshot shows **$739M TVL, with the 100 ETH pool alone around $660M and 38 relayers active over 30 days** ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)); DefiLlama's API returned $794M the same day ([DefiLlama API](https://api.llama.fi/tvl/tornado-cash)) and its page showed $675M on 17 October with an all-time high of $1.23B on 10 November 2025 ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/tornado-cash)); the three figures differ by methodology and the notes do not reconcile them. The November 2025 record came from **≥21 PulseX/Richard Heart-linked addresses depositing >116,000 ETH, roughly a third of deposits**, which, as MetaMask's Taylor Monahan noted, makes it harder for smaller users to hide ([DL News, 7 Nov 2025](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/privacy-protocol-tornado-cash-volumes-hit-record-high/)). By transaction count Railgun now takes about 70% of 2025 mixer transactions to Tornado's 25% and Privacy Pools' 5% ([Cambridge CCAF, Feb 2026](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2026/crypto-privacy-after-sanctions-the-return-of-coin-mixers/)). Tornado Nova, the 2021 arbitrary-amount, shielded-transfer variant on Gnosis, was governance-upgradeable, bridge-dependent, capped at 1 ETH and thinly relayed; it lost 91% of daily transactions after the sanctions and no 2026 source confirms whether it still operates ([tornado-nova README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tornadocash/tornado-nova/master/README.md); [CCAF](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2026/crypto-privacy-after-sanctions-the-return-of-coin-mixers/)).

### Heuristics, not cryptanalysis, link 5–35% of Tornado withdrawals

Four independent studies converge. Tutela (January 2022, ~97k deposits through October 2021) flagged **~42.8k deposits (~44%) as potentially compromised**: 18.6k by exact address match, 18.9k by linked addresses, 16.2k by multi-denomination portfolio matching within 24 hours, with the anonymity set shrinking **37% (±15%) across pools** ([Tutela, arXiv 2201.06811](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.06811)). Wang, Chaliasos et al. found mixers' anonymity-set claims "mostly inaccurate," five heuristics raising a linker's success probability by 51.94% on Tornado, and that TORN anonymity mining "attracts privacy-ignorant users" while leaking each deposit's age ([WWW '23](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~livshits/papers/pdf/www23.pdf)). Cristodaro, Kraner and Tessone (October 2025, data through August 2024 on Ethereum, BSC and Polygon) trace **5.1–12.6% of withdrawals** through address reuse and transactional linkage, with FIFO temporal matching adding 15–22 points to reach **34.7% on Ethereum**; the 0.1 ETH pool is "over 60%" matchable overall ([arXiv 2510.09433](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09433v1)). That paper's v3 was withdrawn on 18 November 2025 "due to mistakes in the references" with a corrected version promised, and none of the studies measures a false-positive rate, so the figures are provisional ([arXiv 2510.09433 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09433)). An ACM SAC 2025 paper adds wallet-software fingerprints as a further linkage channel ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3672608.3707896)). The lesson is the one every designer keeps relearning: fixed denominations remove amount correlation but create multi-pool portfolios; arbitrary amounts remove portfolios but create "unusual amount" fingerprints; and the only behaviour that defeats both is never exiting, which is why shielded-ledger designs with internal transfers (Nova, Railgun, Privacy Pools v2, Aztec) replaced pure mixers.

### Privacy coins: decoys underperform, defaults drive adoption, regulators target custody

Monero is the control case for decoy-based anonymity. Nominal ring size is 16, but Rucknium's OSPEAD work estimates an attacker's average success probability under the default decoy selection at 23.5%, an **effective ring size of ~4.2** (random guessing would be 6.25%), fixable only at a hard fork ([OSPEAD docs](https://rucknium.github.io/OSPEAD/CCS-milestone-2/OSPEAD-docs/_book)). Poisoned-output (EAE) attacks reduce a 16-ring with 12 attacker decoys to an effective set of ~4 ([Monero Academy](https://moneroacademy.com/learn/attacks-and-defenses/decoy-and-poisoned-output-attacks)). The fix, FCMP++, replaces rings with a membership proof over all eligible outputs, converging on the Zcash model; it reached beta stressnet v3.1 on 26 October 2026 with no mainnet date, and a non-binding developer draft targets activation around March 2027 ([GitHub release](https://github.com/seraphis-migration/monero/releases/tag/v0.19.0.0-beta.3.1); [CryptoNews](https://cryptonews.com/news/is-monero-traceable-what-the-fcmp-upgrade-changes-and-when/)). Real-world deanonymization has come from the network layer and services, not the math: a leaked Chainalysis presentation described running many nodes across regions to record transaction IPs ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/chainalysis-leak-monero-traceability)), and a 2026 leak at the Wagyu swap service exposed 4,580 Monero orders with IP addresses ([CryptoTicker](https://cryptoticker.io/en/monero-wagyu-data-leak-xmr-orders/)). Monero was delisted by Binance (20 Feb 2024), OKX (Jan 2024) and Kraken for the EEA (31 Oct 2024) ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/kraken-ends-monero-support-european-economic-area)), yet XMR traded around **$593–608 with a ~$11.2–11.4B market cap on 31 Oct–2 Nov 2026** ([Coinpaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/coin/xmr-monero)), roughly double its August 2025 trough.

Zcash is the counter-case, and its 2025–2026 data is the strongest empirical argument in this report. Shielded usage sat near 6% of transactions in 2021 ([arXiv 2105.02793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02793)); after wallets (Zashi, now Zodl) defaulted to shielded via Unified Addresses, **52.7% of non-coinbase transactions touched shielded pools in Q3 2026 and shielded supply reached 29.1%** ([TechFlow summary of Blockworks Q3 2026](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/34628)). Optional privacy plus user-held viewing keys also produced market outcomes Monero cannot match: Binance removed its Monitoring Tag on 9 July 2025 ([Binance](https://www.binance.com/pt/support/announcement/detail/c2536e8b6ed44c7ba4f62c249dbde9bb)), Grayscale's ZCSH spot ETF launched 25 August 2026 and reached ~$996M AUM by 25 September ([Grayscale Research](https://research.grayscale.com/reports/zcash-financial-privacy-in-the-age-of-ai)), and ZEC traded near $1,257 on 2 November 2026 ([FXStreet](https://fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/zcash-price-forecast-zec-rebounds-as-zodl-founder-now-supports-ending-community-grants-202611020346)). The cost is the transparent boundary: exchanges withdraw to t-addresses and shield/unshield events are the main heuristic surface ([Kappos et al., USENIX 2018](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity18/presentation/kappos)), and a shared viewing key "cannot be revoked once shared" ([zkonomics](https://zkonomics.com/content/viewing-keys-explained/)). Zcash also delivered the year's most important containment lesson: a counterfeiting bug in the Orchard circuit, live since May 2022, was found on 29 May 2026 and the pool was retired behind a turnstile that caps withdrawals at verified deposits, replaced by Ironwood on 28 July 2026 with a 2,700-theorem Lean proof ([Coinpaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/news/zcash-seals-17-billion-pool-four-year/); [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/34628)). Zcash's migration tooling (ZIP-318, migrating in 0.1/0.5/1 ZEC denominations to avoid amount linking across the turnstile) is a reusable pattern. The entire Electric Coin Company staff resigned on 7 January 2026 ([Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/news/zcash-dev-team-resigns-in-whole-after-board-clash)) and delivery continued because development was spread across multiple funded entities, which is itself a design lesson for a "zero-person company" like IMD.

The second tier had a security event each in 2026: Firo's Spark multi-input vulnerability forced a hard fork on ~4 September ([Firo release notes](https://firo.org/2026/08/27/firo-v014180-release.html)); Beam hard-forked in June for a Bulletproofs bug ([RootData](https://www.rootdata.com/projects/detail/beam?k=MzI3)); Namada's MASP was drained of ~$600K on 19–20 June and its shielded-pool TVL now reads about **$474** ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/namada-shielded-pools)); Secret Network lost $4.67M in bridged tokens the same day and is "decoupling" from SGX citing WireTap and TEE.fail ([Secret roadmap](https://x.com/SecretNetwork/status/2010859443257593969)). Dash adopted Zcash's Orchard pool ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/dash-brings-zcash-orchard-privacy-to-evolution-chain-for-shielded-transactions)). Two designs are worth borrowing even though their usage is tiny: Penumbra's ZSwap executes every swap in a block as one batch at a public clearing price, removing per-trade ordering advantage, with sealed-bid threshold-decrypted batches specified but not shipped ([Penumbra DEX spec](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/dex.html)), and its fuzzy message detection lets a light client outsource note detection with a sender-chosen false-positive rate ([Penumbra FMD](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/crypto/fmd.html)). Aleo is live with Circle and Paxos stablecoins and sponsored fees but around $5M of DeFi TVL ([Provable explorer](https://explorer.provable.com/)). The pattern across the tier: multi-asset shielded pools without existing liquidity rails did not attract value; pools attached to existing rails did; and shielded rewards (Namada, up to 10% of inflation) bootstrapped nothing durable.

The regulatory deadline that matters is **10 July 2027**, when Article 79 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 bars EU credit institutions, financial institutions and CASPs from keeping anonymous accounts, including "crypto-asset accounts allowing anonymisation of transactions"; the "1 July 2027" date repeated in crypto media is wrong, "anonymity-enhancing coins" is defined in Article 2(1)(25) of the Regulation as crypto-assets with built-in features that make transfer information anonymous "either systematically or optionally" (corrected 10 Nov 2026 against [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401624); an earlier draft of this report said the term was undefined pending EBA standards), and the ban does not extend to self-custody software providers "insofar as they do not possess access to or control over those wallets" ([Soda Labs](https://www.sodalabs.xyz/privacy-hub/regulation/reg-eu-amlr); [Optimisus](https://optimisus.com/news/crypto-news/europe-bans-privacy-coin-custody-in-2027-and-the-exception-is-the-interesting-part/)). The notes conflict here: one researcher's note repeats the 1 July date and cites Art. 79 from secondary summaries; neither retrieved the EUR-Lex text, so verify before relying on the article number. FATF's travel rule has legislation in 91 of 109 jurisdictions but 60% have taken no enforcement action and 93% have identified no "qualifying DeFi arrangement" with an identifiable operator ([Chainalysis on FATF 7th update](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/fatf-7th-targeted-update-crypto-compliance/)). Japan has barred privacy coins from licensed exchanges since 2018; South Korea since March 2021 ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/south-korea-to-ban-privacy-coins-from-q1-2021)). The through-line is that regulators distinguish on custody and disclosure, not on cryptography.

### zk.money's relaunch is a capped, TEE-co-signed DAI wallet, not a token launch

The question of what https://launch.zk.money/ is deserves a precise answer because the name invites misreading. A headless-browser render on 3 November 2026 shows that https://zk.money/ is a stub pointing to https://launch.zk.money, which returns a CloudFront **302 to https://wallet.zk.money/**, whose single-page app client-routes to `/claim` and renders the ordinary logged-out landing of the relaunched wallet: "Money that moves privately. Pay, get paid and save onchain. Your balance and transactions are private by default," with "Create Account" and "Log in" and no token, airdrop, launchpad or sequencer-launch content anywhere in the visible copy; the `/claim` route name refers to claiming an `@tag` handle through a `claimFpc` contract ([zkmoney_render notes, launch.zk.money fetched 2026-11-03](https://launch.zk.money/)). Aztec Labs' 29 September 2026 post says "Claim tags at launch.zk.money. Priority goes to early users of the previous zk.money" ([Aztec Labs](https://aztec-labs.com/blog/zk-money-is-back)). The runtime config fetched that day identifies the deployment as profile `prod-v6` on mainnet chain 1, Aztec rollup version 2070860440, published 2026-11-03T12:24:29Z, the same day Aztec announced V6 "Tierra" ([cdn.zk.money profile](https://cdn.zk.money/profiles/v6/current.json); [The Block, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-03-ethereum-l2-aztec-tierra-v6-upgrade-private-apps-432399)). The operator of record is Obsidion Labs Limited (BVI) per the terms, while the blog footer names Spilsbury Holdings Ltd (Aztec Labs); the notes do not resolve the relationship ([docs.zk.money terms](https://docs.zk.money/terms)).

Architecturally zk.money is a shielded-ledger wallet with a TEE co-signer and application-level screening, not a trustless mixer. It holds only DAI (USDC/USDT swapped on arrival via Curve), with a Sky sUSDS savings account; every deposit, payment and withdrawal is capped below **$5,000 per transaction with a shared $100,000 daily deposit ceiling** fixed in an unowned, non-upgradeable portal contract (the launch-week caps were $2,500 and $50,000) ([docs.zk.money Limits](https://docs.zk.money/docs/limits); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379621/ethereum-privacy-aztec-brings-back-zk-money)). "Oxide," an AWS Nitro enclave layer, co-signs every token operation so that theft "requires two failures together: a soundness fault in Aztec and a compromised enclave," a sponsoring fee-paying contract pays Aztec fees and "if it runs empty, transactions stop," and "deposit and withdrawal addresses are screened against a sanctions policy" applied by the wallet and relayer rather than the contract ([docs.zk.money Trust](https://docs.zk.money/docs/trust); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/aztec-revives-its-private-payments-wallet-capping-every-transfer-below-2500/)). Its own privacy page is unusually candid: "anything crossing into or out of Ethereum is public," an on-chain AccountRegistry lets anyone who guesses your tag read your Aztec and Ethereum addresses, an XMTP binding is published "with no opt-out," and the hosted node "can tell that one account is behind a set of note requests" ([docs.zk.money Privacy](https://docs.zk.money/docs/privacy)). The caps exist because the CEO chose to launch before the fix for a critical V5 proving-system flaw disclosed on 7 August 2026 ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/aztec-revives-its-private-payments-wallet-capping-every-transfer-below-2500/)).

Aztec itself is the most ambitious privacy platform on Ethereum and the most volatile. The Ignition consensus chain went live 19/20 November 2025, the token TGE passed governance on 12 February 2026 at a FDV just over $308M, the Alpha network with private smart contracts launched 31 March 2026 "with known vulnerabilities," V5 activated 21 July 2026 and V6 "Tierra" on 3 November 2026 as "a new deployment from genesis, not an upgrade," with 3,000+ validators and a claimed 1-second native client-side proof ([Aztec Alpha](https://aztec.network/blog/announcing-the-alpha-network); [Aztec V5](https://aztec.network/blog/introducing-alpha-v5); [Baltex](https://baltex.io/blog/ecosystem/aztec-vs-railgun-privacy-fees-2026-comparison); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-03-ethereum-l2-aztec-tierra-v6-upgrade-private-apps-432399)). Every release in 2026 shipped with or soon disclosed a critical flaw, the legacy Aztec Connect contracts, ownerless and unpausable, were drained of ~$2.1M in June 2026 through a proof-verification bug ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/hacks/aztec-connect-deprecated-contract-exploit-2-1m-zk-proof)), and no independent anonymity-set study of Aztec exists ([Baltex](https://baltex.io/blog/ecosystem/aztec-vs-railgun-privacy-fees-2026-comparison)). Three Aztec ideas transfer directly to an L1 design: client-side proving in a PXE with user-held incoming and outgoing viewing keys and "no privileged viewing keys," note tagging for discovery (which still leaks the querying IP to the node, with OMR/PIR "impractical"), and fee-paying contracts that abstract gas ([Aztec PXE docs](https://docs.aztec.network/aztec/concepts/pxe); [Aztec note discovery](https://docs.aztec.network/developers/docs/concepts/advanced/storage/note_discovery)). The first-generation zk.money and Connect reached 100,000 wallets and ~$17–20M TVL before a 2023 sunset the founders attributed to commercial reasons, with half of a 40-person team running a single node ([CoinDesk via Yahoo](https://au.finance.yahoo.com/news/regulatory-pressure-did-not-shut-172629416.html)); operator cost, not regulation, killed it.

### Ethereum's pool market has collapsed to one mixer and two compliant pools

L2BEAT now tracks 18 privacy projects across pools, shielded ledgers, stealth addresses, confidential amounts and anonymous access ([L2BEAT Privacy](https://l2beat.com/privacy)), but the TVL distribution is bimodal. On 3 November 2026 DefiLlama's API gave Tornado Cash $794M, **Railgun $110M, Privacy Pools $9.1M**, Hinkal $254K and zkBob $224K ([DefiLlama API](https://api.llama.fi/tvl/railgun)). Nocturne raised $6M with Vitalik's participation in October 2023 and wound down in June 2024 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/vitalik-buterin-nocturne-labs-shuts-down)); Intmax paused in February 2026 after a balance-proof circuit vulnerability with no recorded resumption ([L2BEAT INTMAX](https://l2beat.com/scaling/projects/intmax)); Hinkal fell from a $26.8M peak and was exploited for ~$820K in July 2026 ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/hinkal)); Labyrinth remains on testnet; Elusiv, Light, Manta Atlantic, Espresso CAPE and Webb pivoted or died ([Permissionless taxonomy, Apr 2026](https://permissionless-technologies.com/blog/privacy-landscape-2026)). Survivors share two traits: their own wallet surface and an Ethereum Foundation distribution path through Kohaku.

Railgun is the design to beat on L1. Shielding moves tokens into a single contract; balances become encrypted notes in one shared Merkle tree across all assets (147 tokens shielded), so the anonymity set grows with total shielded value; spends are Groth16/BN254; "RelayAdapt" unshields, executes a swap and reshields atomically; shield/unshield carry a 0.25% fee and internal transfers are free ([ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/)). Its broadcaster network is the most decentralized relayer design shipped: independent nodes publish fee quotes over Waku, receive encrypted transactions, pay gas and are reimbursed in any accepted token; the broadcaster "cannot read the contents of the interaction, apart from the gas amount paid to it," and users switch if one refuses ([Railgun docs](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/learn/privacy-system/community-broadcasters)). Private Proofs of Innocence let wallets generate blinded per-provider exclusion proofs against lists from Elliptic, ScamSniffer, PureFi, SlowMist and the Chainalysis Sanctions Oracle, carried recursively through later transfers, after a 1-hour unshield-only standby ([Railgun PPOI docs](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/assurance/private-proofs-of-innocence.md)). Weaknesses are at the edges: shield/unshield events are public, so timing and amount correlation at entry and exit is the main analysis surface, and quick round-trips weaken privacy ([ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/)). Revenue is real but pro-cyclical: ~$323K of fees in 30 days, a trailing-year pace of ~$4.1M, down from ~$1.4M per quarter in Q1 2025 ([DefiLlama Railgun](https://defillama.com/protocol/railgun)).

Privacy Pools, from 0xbow, implements the 2023 Buterin–Illum–Nadler–Schär–Soleimani paper: deposits are public, an Association Set Provider publishes a Merkle root of approved deposit labels, withdrawals prove membership without revealing which deposit, and rejected depositors "ragequit" publicly to origin ([Privacy Pools ASP docs](https://docs.privacypools.com/layers/asp)). It launched 31 March 2025, raised $3.5M in November 2025 with ~$6M volume and >1,500 users, and shipped v2 on 20 October 2026 across seven chains with private transfers, swaps via LI.FI and Aave yield ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/11/18/3190435/0/en/0xbow-Closes-3-5M-Round-for-Compliant-Crypto-Privacy-Technology-Following-Ethereum-Foundation-Integration.html); [EtherWorld](https://etherworld.co/privacy-pools-v2-brings-private-payments-onchain/)). Its gaps are structural. Only "authorized postmen" can post roots, screening criteria are unpublished, and multi-ASP support is not specified ([Privacy Pools docs](https://docs.privacypools.com/layers/asp)). L2BEAT's Ethereum v2 snapshot shows **$510K TVL, 470 total deposits, 2 active relayers in 30 days**, a 2/4 Safe with no-delay upgrade and pause, a 20% cut of Aave yield, the right to set a vetting fee up to 99.99%, and the warning that "0xbow can pair sessions across its ASP, relayer, and Alchemy logs" ([L2BEAT Privacy Pools v2](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)). Nobody has built a multi-party or user-selectable ASP ([ethereum_privacy_ecosystem notes, KQ5 inference](https://docs.privacypools.com/)); that is the whitespace this report's design occupies.

Stealth addresses are the one primitive with a finished standard: ERC-5564's singleton Announcer and ERC-6538's registry, with view tags cutting scanning ~6x ([EIP-5564](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5564)). They hide the recipient only; "hiding the fact that a transfer happened is impossible," and a fresh stealth address "has 0 ETH in it, and so there is no way to pay transaction fees" ([Vitalik, stealth guide](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/01/20/stealth.html)). Umbra has 25.5K all-time deposits (~$143M) but only ~9 per week, a public registry of 11,983 recipients that bounds the set, an owner-adjustable toll and a closed-source relayer ([L2BEAT Umbra](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/umbra)); an academic analysis found "for the majority of Umbra payments, it is straightforward to establish the recipient" ([DeepAI](https://deepai.org/publication/anonymity-analysis-of-the-umbra-stealth-address-scheme-on-ethereum)). Fluidkey wraps each stealth address in a counterfactual Safe for sponsored gas and claims 30,000+ users and $900M+ volume, at the price that the operator holds a shared viewing node and can enumerate every receive address ([Fluidkey docs](https://docs.fluidkey.com/technical-documentation/technical-walkthrough/); [fluidkey.com](https://www.fluidkey.com/)). Every production stealth system solves gas by introducing a party that sees the mapping.

The Ethereum Foundation's push peaked with Vitalik's April 2025 "maximally simple L1 privacy roadmap" (shielded balances in existing wallets, one address per app, private RPC reads, network-level anonymization, FOCIL and EIP-7701 so privacy protocols can work "without relays or public broadcasters") ([Ethereum Magicians #23459](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/a-maximally-simple-l1-privacy-roadmap/23459)), the October 2025 Privacy Cluster of 47 people and the Kohaku wallet SDK that integrated Railgun, Privacy Pools and Tornado in October 2025, sending RAIL up ~300% ([EF blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/2025/10/08/privacy-commitment); [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/railgun-token-soars-as-ethereum-foundation-integrates-protocol-into-kohaku/)). Kohaku's roadmap pairs an in-browser Helios light client with oblivious `eth_call` "achieved with TEE+ORAM" now and pure PIR later, P2P transaction broadcast bypassing RPC, and 4337 relaying ([Kohaku roadmap](https://notes.ethereum.org/@niard/KohakuRoadmap)). Then on 23 June 2026 the EF cut 54 roles and ~40% of its budget and wound down PSE; privacy survives as an "L1 privacy" research line and an Access cluster mandate, the institutional work spun out as EthSystems on 14 July 2026 with no named paying client, and Kohaku has no public release date as of late October ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318949/20260623/ethereum-foundation-cuts-54-jobs-shuts-zk-research-lab-slashes-budget-40.htm); [TechTimes, EthSystems](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320511/20260715/ethereum-foundations-privacy-spinout-targets-bank-confidentiality-gap.htm); [Finwire](https://finwire.io/news/crypto-news/ethereum-foundation-backs-default-wallet-privacy-as-kohaku-work-advances)). EF endorsement and distribution are available; EF engineering capacity is not. Meanwhile confidential-compute tracks went to production with a decryption authority each: Zama (mainnet 30 Dec 2025, ~20 TPS, KMS committee), Fhenix CoFHE (21 Oct 2026), STRK-20 (one auditor key decrypts every history), Privacy Boost on Base (operator TEE) ([L2BEAT Privacy](https://l2beat.com/privacy); [Zama changelog](https://docs.zama.org/protocol/changelog)). ZK shielded pools remain the only designs with no decryption authority, which is the positioning axis for anything new.

## Part two: a privacy project on top of IMD

### IMD is a 1,136-agent identity and attestation substrate with zero privacy

Honesty about the substrate comes first. IMD describes itself as "a network of AI agents that build, review and ship software, paid in IMD, with the record on Ethereum" ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). It descends from the August 2023 Base game token Fren Pet, renamed $VIBE and then $IMD in May 2026 when "identity.md" minted 2,000 free seat NFTs, one per wallet; the agent network opened to NFT holders on 20 September 2026 ([IMD Token page](https://imd.fun/token/); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/ru-imd-token-community-owned-ai-agents)). The only named individual is Fren Pet's developer "Adam" (@surfcoderepeat); the seat contract was deployed by surfsurf.eth on 14 May 2026 with "No Contract Security Audit Submitted," and release notes mention a "custody Safe" and "a fork rehearsal that moves surfsurf.eth roles to it" ([Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/address/0x0000ec93127baa929e58e97dd0095a2bfb38ec1d); [RELEASE_NOTES](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Identity-md/worker/main/RELEASE_NOTES.md)). There is no whitepaper, roadmap, legal entity or named team; "Backend source and service credentials are not distributed" and the worker repo is a release mirror ([Identity-md/worker](https://github.com/Identity-md/worker)). TokenPost noted "The public documentation does not show recurring production work for outside protocols" ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/24260)). The "zero-person company" framing is marketing, not an organisational fact.

The on-chain pieces are concrete. The seat NFT "identity.md (IDMD)" lives at 0x0000ec93127baa929e58e97dd0095a2bfb38ec1d (1,999 items, 792 owners, floor 0.796 ETH on 3 Nov 2026) ([OpenSea](https://opensea.io/collection/identitymd)). "Register my agent" is one mainnet transaction through an IMD-deployed ERC-8004 adapter at 0xde152afb7db5373f34876e1499fbd893a82dd336 that binds the seat to an agentId in the canonical ERC-8004 IdentityRegistry (0x8004a169fb4a3325136eb29fa0ceb6d2e539a432) with the ReputationRegistry at 0x8004baa17c55a88189ae136b182e5fda19de9b63, neither deployed by IMD ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). Pairing binds the seat to an Ed25519 device key via an EIP-712 `WorkerAuthorization` signature; "One NFT authorizes one active device" and "Independent reviewers must use different wallets" ([worker README](https://github.com/Identity-md/worker)). A WorkRegistry at 0xb6d0a187b050fa5bb0b87033a203f37becf4a775 holds "append-only receipts for work, and the reviews written with them"; Intake v2 at 0xa43e6f75ee006411f79ac1c84120606c2330de82 takes a 0.5 IMD payment and a request in one call, via x402 with Permit2 ([imd_pages_fetched notes](https://imd.fun/docs/)). IMD's docs say none of the sources are verified on explorers; a check on 9 and 10 Nov 2026 found the seat NFT, the ERC-8004 adapter and the WorkRegistry source-verified on Etherscan, with no audits (see the addendum).

The attestation primitive is the oracle: "You pose a typed question. A panel of agents answers it, and the answer is signed in EIP-712 for your contract. Every panel member must match for the quorum to be met ... If the panel disagrees, there is no answer," with chain-evidence recipes `log-sum`, `log-count`, `log-rank`, `call-compare`, `v4-volume-rank` and `univ4-spot`, re-run by the deployer at pinned blocks before signing, and daily Merkle-root records ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). Routing goes to "the machine that ranks best on tools, load, recent failures, and time since its last similar task"; "Nobody reviews their own work. Three failures in a row pause a machine for 15 minutes"; contract work carries 30-day probation; audits use four specialists and a judge; a centrally operated verifier checks every result "before it goes onchain under the agent" ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). Workers already run local RPC relays (`imd rpc add`, served on 127.0.0.1 so "Tasks never see the URL"), sandbox contract, research and oracle tasks in a read jail, and can deploy sites to IPFS pinned under ENS through the `build-website` skill ([worker README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Identity-md/worker/main/README.md)). The explorer on 3 November 2026 showed **1,136 agents, 921 online, 8 working, 6,570 jobs and 9,012 oracle entries**, with some holders running ~10 agents from one wallet ([explorer.imd.fun/agents](https://explorer.imd.fun/agents)).

Payment rails and economics are launchpad-centric. Every request costs 0.5 IMD; workers are not paid a per-task wage but receive a 10% "swarm share" of every token launched through IMD (2% to wallets with accepted work, 8% split per seat connected at admission) via Merkle distributors, plus 4.5% of Uniswap v4 POOL4 "trims," of which 85% is burned; supply is ~7.1M and falling; "Running a worker does not guarantee earnings" ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/ru-imd-token-community-owned-ai-agents)). Market cap briefly exceeded $39M on 26 September 2026 ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/ru-imd-token-community-owned-ai-agents)). A job brief from October 2026 refers to an incident with a token "SIMD ... built on the Identity.md ecosystem" and an "unverified" Robinhood loss ledger ([launch-2479](https://github.com/identity-md-launches/launch-2479-independent-tokenomics-review-choose)), and a third-party "IMD6900" token with a 10% trade fee exists on Robinhood Chain ([Blockspot](https://blockspot.io/coin/identitymd-6900-imd6900/)); both signal the kind of token volatility and ecosystem noise a privacy project must insulate itself from.

Privacy in IMD today is not merely absent; the system is structurally public. "The body is public forever and goes into the builders' prompts"; the explorer exposes "held by" wallets and a per-wallet order history at `/requests/paid-by/:address`; "Network jobs, outputs and contributor activity currently have public read APIs"; a worker's "JavaScript, prompts, API requests, and assigned task data are inspectable" ([imd.fun/skill.md](https://imd.fun/skill.md); [worker README](https://github.com/Identity-md/worker)). The only privacy gesture is an option to route jobs to Venice models to "keep the work private" from the model provider ([imd_pages_fetched notes](https://imd.fun/docs/)). The risks a builder inherits are therefore unaudited contracts with unverified sources, a single pseudonymous deployer, a closed-source control plane at api.imd.fun that assigns, verifies and records all work, no TEE, no slashing, and a token whose value moves on launchpad activity. The substrate is valuable for exactly one reason, which the rest of this half develops: it is the only live set of roughly a thousand independently operated, on-chain-identified, costly-to-forge, always-on machines on Ethereum mainnet, and Ethereum privacy's unsolved problems are all problems of who operates the periphery.

### Each chokepoint maps to a capability the swarm already has or can cheaply add

The design mapping is one-to-one. Problem 2 (the relayer chokepoint) needs many independent operators who cannot be whitelisted by a developer and who see only ciphertext; the swarm supplies 1,136 seats with distinct wallets and ERC-8004 ids, and Railgun's Waku broadcaster pattern supplies the ciphertext-only protocol ([Railgun docs](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/learn/privacy-system/community-broadcasters)). Problem 8 (stigma without a single gatekeeper) needs a multi-party ASP; IMD's unanimous EIP-712 oracle panels and chain-evidence recipes are a working attestation primitive, and ERC-8004 explicitly delegates slashing to "the specific validation protocol," which an EigenLayer or Symbiotic operator set can be ([EIP-8004](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8004); [BlockEden on EigenLayer slashing](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/18/eigenlayer-avs-slashing-activation-15b-restaking-reality-check/)). Problem 6 (mobile proving) needs witness-hiding delegation; TACEO's collaborative SNARKs run 3-party honest-majority MPC where "MPC nodes must come from different providers," and IMD's "different wallets" rule plus seat scarcity gives a ready provider set ([TACEO Devcon demo](https://core.taceo.io/articles/devcon-demo/); [TACEO private delegation](https://core.taceo.io/articles/private-proof-delegation/)). Problem 3 (RPC metadata) needs a decentralized set of verified, oblivious read endpoints; IMD workers already run local RPC relays and Kohaku's design (Helios plus TEE-ORAM `eth_call`) lacks precisely "a decentralised operator set" ([Kohaku roadmap](https://notes.ethereum.org/@niard/KohakuRoadmap)). Problem 1 and 9 (thin sets and AI analysis) need continuous measurement and behaviour correction; IMD's `research-report` and `oracle` skills already scan chains with `log-sum`/`log-count`/`call-compare` recipes ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)), and no production "AI privacy agent" exists in public sources. Problem 5 (legal) is answered by the fact that the swarm is not the developer: every operator is an independent NFT holder running their own machine and model subscription, which is the one organisational shape Galeotti's remarks and BRCA §604 both bless, provided the project's own team operates nothing. Problem 4 (freezes) and problem 7 (economics) are design choices rather than swarm capabilities, handled below.

### Flagship architecture: Murmuration, a swarm-operated privacy stack

Murmuration is a service layer first and a pool second. The strongest finding of the analysis is that anonymity sets are the scarcest resource on Ethereum and that new pools fragment them; a project that launches a fourth L1 pool on day one would start with the 470-deposit problem Privacy Pools v2 has ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)). So the stack has two layers. Layer 1 is the swarm services, each usable by Railgun and Privacy Pools (and, where the pool allows it, Tornado) through Kohaku-style wallet integration. Layer 0 is an immutable, ownerless pool contract that is added only in phase 2 and only if the services prove out, designed so the services are already its periphery. The six services follow.

**Service 1, the broadcaster mesh.** Each participating seat runs a Railgun-style broadcaster: it subscribes to a Waku pubsub topic gated by RLN membership (one message per second per publisher), publishes a signed fee quote per epoch, accepts an encrypted transaction whose recipient, fee and relayer address are bound into the ZK proof (the Tornado binding, so the broadcaster can only delay, never redirect), pays gas, and is reimbursed with a note inside the pool ([Waku network](https://waku.org/about/waku-network/); [Tornado whitepaper](https://berkeley-defi.github.io/assets/material/Tornado%20Cash%20Whitepaper.pdf)). Selection is client-side but structured: the wallet reads the on-chain mesh registry (seat tokenId, ERC-8004 agentId, epoch availability commitment, aggregate reputation), draws k=3 candidates with a locally seeded weighted random draw across distinct holder wallets, sends to the first and falls back after N blocks. The notes confirm no production relayer uses VRF or commit-reveal and that "selection is client-side" ([imd_and_swarm_tech notes, Part 2 Q2](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/learn/privacy-system/community-relayers)); a commit-reveal epoch lottery is useful only for the "availability" set, not for the per-transaction choice, because an on-chain per-transaction assignment would itself publish a timing record. Transport is Waku over Tor or the Nym mixnet, since Nym's dApp mode already proxies Ethereum wallet traffic and the Tornado CLI's fee checks famously bypassed Tor and exposed IPs ([Nym support](https://support.nym.com/hc/en-us/articles/42202770203537-How-to-Make-Private-Ethereum-Transactions-with-NymVPN-dApp-Mode); [L2BEAT Tornado](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)). Probation mirrors IMD's routing rules on-chain: a broadcaster that fails to include a bound transaction it quoted for loses availability weight for 24 hours, and three failures in an epoch remove it from the active set for that epoch ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). Reputation writes must not leak client-relayer pairs, so the pool contract, not the user, emits aggregate per-epoch success counts into the ERC-8004 ReputationRegistry; ERC-8004 already forbids the agent's owner from submitting its own feedback ([EIP-8004](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8004)). Because a Chainalysis-style broadcaster is assumed, the mesh's privacy claim is only that any single broadcaster sees ciphertext, a fee and a Tor or mixnet exit, never an IP or a recipient in the clear.

**Service 2, the panel ASP.** A panel of n seats, each from a distinct holder wallet, independently screens every deposit against a published criteria file (pinned to IPFS and hashed on-chain) using IMD-style chain-evidence recipes against public data only: the Chainalysis Sanctions Oracle, the lists Railgun's PPOI already consumes from Elliptic, ScamSniffer, PureFi and SlowMist, and deposit-age and hop-count rules ([Railgun PPOI docs](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/assurance/private-proofs-of-innocence.md); [Decrypt on the Chainalysis oracle](https://decrypt.co/94808/crypto-chainalysis-sanctions-screening-tools-defi-daos)). Each panelist signs the resulting approved-label Merkle root with EIP-712; the ASP contract accepts a root only with t-of-n signatures and keeps a circular buffer of historical roots (0xbow's own contracts, read on 9 and 10 Nov 2026, keep an append-only array and accept only the latest root; see the addendum) ([Privacy Pools ASP docs](https://docs.privacypools.com/layers/asp)). Unanimity, IMD's oracle rule, is wrong for this job because one adversarial panelist could freeze inclusion; a 2/3 threshold with a public dissent log is right, and the deliberate departure from IMD's rule should be documented. Multiple panels with different published criteria form multiple ASPs, and a withdrawal proves membership in a user-chosen set, so a user can present the union of two lenient panels to a DEX and the intersection of strict ones to an exchange. This is the multi-ASP design the 2023 paper envisioned that "has not been built by anyone found" ([ethereum_privacy_ecosystem notes, KQ5](https://docs.privacypools.com/)). Misbehaviour is objectively checkable in both directions: a root that includes a deposit matching the published SDN recipe is provable on-chain by re-running the recipe, and a root that excludes a deposit that passes the published criteria is provable the same way; both become slashing conditions for an EigenLayer or Symbiotic operator set in which panelists restake, or, in phase 1 before an AVS exists, a seat-NFT bond held by the ASP contract ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/18/eigenlayer-avs-slashing-activation-15b-restaking-reality-check/)). Ragequit remains the complaint path. Crucially, panelists see only public chain data and never a user's identity or IP, matching Railgun's "providers see only public on-chain data and receive nothing from wallets," which keeps them out of the "knowledge" trigger of §1960(b)(1)(C).

**Service 3, collaborative delegated proving.** A phone generates the witness locally (cheap), secret-shares it three ways, and sends each share over Waku to three seats from distinct wallets that run coCircom or coNoir and jointly produce the Groth16 or UltraHonk proof without any party learning the witness; TACEO reports performance "comparable to snarkJS" for coCircom and ~8 GB of memory for proving versus 2 GB for witness generation ([TACEO primer](https://docs.taceo.io/docs/primer/collabSNARKs-primer/); [TACEO private delegation](https://core.taceo.io/articles/private-proof-delegation/)). The security model is honest-majority among three parties, which the seat-uniqueness rule and client-side random selection across holders make realistic; the attacker must control two of the three specific seats the client picked. TEE attestation is defence-in-depth only: provers may run inside dstack TDX CVMs and post an Automata DCAP attestation once per epoch (~0.43–0.57M gas in SNARK-compressed form), but TEE.fail demonstrated a forged quote passing Automata's verifier with a ~$1,000 DDR5 interposer and "Everyone moved to permissioned," so attestation proves measurement, never physical custody, and must never be the sole root ([ethresear.ch](https://ethresear.ch/t/physical-integrity-attestation-and-the-state-of-permissionless-tees/24964); [automata-dcap-attestation](https://github.com/automata-network/automata-dcap-attestation)). The service matters most for memory-bound devices: in-browser UltraHonk proving is ~6 s on a Galaxy A23 and out-of-memory on iPhone 16 ([Hyli](https://hyli.ghost.io/benchmarking-in-browser-p256-ecdsa-proving-systems/)). It also requires a premium hardware tier, since IMD's VPS manual profiles 3 GiB machines ([imd_and_swarm_tech notes, Part 2 Q3](https://docs.taceo.io/docs/primer/collabSNARKs-primer/)).

**Service 4, the private read layer.** Each participating seat runs Helios, so "the RPC can refuse to respond but cannot lie" ([a16z crypto](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/building-helios-ethereum-light-client/)), plus an oblivious `eth_call` endpoint built the way Kohaku specifies, TEE+ORAM now and PIR later ([Kohaku roadmap](https://notes.ethereum.org/@niard/KohakuRoadmap)), reachable over Waku or Nym. A "PIR-lite" mode splits a wallet's note-discovery and nullifier queries across k random seats with chaff queries so that no single endpoint sees a user's full access pattern; this is a behavioural mitigation, not cryptographic PIR, which Vitalik's January 2026 roadmap says "would not arrive in the upcoming Kohaku release or next few hard forks" ([KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/vitalik-buterin-outlines-2026-ethereum-roadmap-to-restore-core-values)). Nym's experimental Helios-over-mixnet fork shows the transport works, with tens-of-seconds first-response latency ([nymtech/helios-nym](https://github.com/nymtech/helios-nym)). Penumbra-style fuzzy message detection can later let the read layer pre-filter notes for a client without learning which are real ([Penumbra FMD](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/crypto/fmd.html)).

**Service 5, anonymity-set stewardship agents.** A standing panel re-runs the Tutela, FIFO and wallet-fingerprint heuristics against every supported pool every day using `research-report`-style jobs, publishes a signed pool-health root (effective set size per asset and amount band, FIFO-matchable share, whale concentration), and exposes a pre-transaction advisory API that a wallet calls before shielding or unshielding: "this amount has three matches in the last 24 hours," "this recipient has transacted with your deposit address," "wait 31 hours for the set to refresh after the next ASP root." Scheduling is the second function: the wallet hands a withdrawal intent to a steward that holds only the proof (not the secret), and the steward submits it inside a randomized window chosen to flatten FIFO ordering, which the 2025 cross-chain study found adds 15–22 points of linkability ([arXiv 2510.09433](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09433v1)). The third function, honest self-churn, is the most delicate: agents fund decoy shield-transfer-unshield flows from a shielded budget to thicken amount bands that stewardship measures as thin. It is also the costliest and riskiest, which the candidate-idea section addresses.

**Service 6, threshold selective disclosure.** For each transaction the wallet encrypts a disclosure record (amount, counterparty note commitment, time) to an epoch public key whose private key is threshold-shared across a disclosure panel of seats. An on-chain policy registry records, per user, who may request decryption (the user alone by default; optionally a named auditor; optionally "a court order attested by a named panel"), and the policy can be changed or revoked by the user at any time; because epoch keys rotate and the panel refuses requests outside policy, disclosure is per-transaction, time-bounded and revocable, which Zcash viewing keys, "a drastic form of selective disclosure" that "cannot be revoked once shared," are not ([arXiv 2506.04853](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.04853); [zkonomics](https://zkonomics.com/content/viewing-keys-explained/)). The closest existing designs are Labyrinth's threshold "revoker/guardian" keys (testnet only) and Panther's "Forensic Data Escrow" ([Permissionless taxonomy](https://permissionless-technologies.com/blog/privacy-landscape-2026); [Cointelegraph PR](https://cointelegraph.com/press-releases/panther-protocol-deploys-privacy-infrastructure-on-polygon)). This service must be strictly opt-in and default-off, because any decryption authority is what L2BEAT now flags as a first-class risk and what Aztec markets against ("no privileged viewing keys"); its purpose is to give an EU user something to show a CASP after 10 July 2027 without giving the network a backdoor.

**Layer 0, the phase-2 pool.** When built, the pool is a Railgun-style shielded ledger with one Merkle tree across all assets (so small assets inherit the large set), arbitrary amounts, free internal transfers, atomic unshield-call-reshield, and no fee switch; immutable with no admin key, deployed by a throwaway key with ownership renounced; exits batched per epoch with keyed nullifiers so that simultaneous withdrawals do not collide, the mechanism Vitalik named in May 2026 ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/vitalik-buterin-outlines-ethereums-near-term-privacy-roadmap-aa-focil-keyed-nonces-and-kohaku/)); a per-asset turnstile and supply-audit path borrowed from Ironwood and Beam's lustration so a soundness bug is bounded to one asset's verified deposits ([Coinpaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/news/zcash-seals-17-billion-pool-four-year/)); hash-based note commitments and a PQ-KEM option for note encryption where the gas budget allows, since harvest-now-decrypt-later cannot be patched later ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)); and freezable stablecoins isolated in their own sub-tree so that an issuer freeze of the pool address, which Circle executed against Tornado's USDC pool in 2022, cannot trap ETH depositors ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/162172/circle-freezes-usdc-funds-in-tornado-cashs-us-treasury-sanctioned-wallets)). The pool's verifier should prefer a universal or transparent setup over a circuit-specific Groth16 ceremony, accepting UltraHonk's 0.72–2.4M verification gas, which at November 2026 gas prices is cents.

### Verdicts on the eight candidate ideas

**(a) Swarm broadcaster mesh: keep, as the flagship's first service.** It attacks the single component that is both the metadata leak and the legal exposure, and it is buildable from shipped parts (Railgun's Waku broadcaster client, RLN, Tornado's proof-bound fee). Two improvements: drop VRF for per-transaction selection in favour of client-side draws over an epoch availability set, because on-chain assignment publishes timing; and weight by distinct holder wallet rather than by seat, because the explorer already shows wallets holding ~10 agents ([explorer.imd.fun/agents](https://explorer.imd.fun/agents)). One caution: IMD's own control plane must not be the broker; if api.imd.fun assigned relay jobs, the project would have recreated the whitelisted relayer network that prosecutors used against Storm.

**(b) Panel ASP: keep, with a threshold not unanimity, published criteria, and slashing.** It is the clearest whitespace in the notes and the one with institutional pull (0xbow lifted caps "to open doors to institutional users"; EthSystems raised from Bitmine and Sharplink within three weeks of PSE's closure) ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/oxbow-lifts-deposit-limits-on-privacy-pools-opens-doors-to-institutional-users); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320511/20260715/ethereum-foundations-privacy-spinout-targets-bank-confidentiality-gap.htm)). The notes also confirm the ASP concept was co-designed with Chainalysis's chief scientist ([PANews](https://panews.io/articles/m29oozp5)), so a decentralized, criteria-published version is politically legible to compliance vendors.

**(c) Collaborative delegated proving: keep, with TEE demoted to optional.** The idea is sound and the enabling stack is in production (TACEO:Proof). The improvement is to scope it: Aztec's V6 claims 1-second native proofs and Zcash's mobile proof is ~200 ms ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-03-ethereum-l2-aztec-tierra-v6-upgrade-private-apps-432399); [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/34628)), so the service's value is for memory-bound devices and for larger circuits (recursive PPOI proofs, multi-note spends), not for every transfer.

**(d) Private IMD first: keep as phase 0, but it depends on the operator.** Encrypting job briefs to assigned workers' Ed25519 device keys, paying swarm-share claims to ERC-5564 stealth addresses, and returning oracle answers encrypted to the requester with only a commitment on-chain are all straightforward. The honest caveat is that job assignment, the explorer and the paid-by API live in a closed control plane the project does not control; "private IMD" therefore requires IMD's operator to ship it, and the privacy project's contribution is a specification and a reference implementation of the stealth-payout distributor. It is still worth doing: it dogfoods ERC-5564 scanning at ~1,000-recipient scale and removes the per-wallet order history that currently makes every IMD customer's activity public.

**(e) Anonymity-set stewardship: keep measurement and scheduling; gate self-churn.** Measurement and pre-transaction warnings are pure upside and need only existing skills. Scheduling requires the steward to hold a proof but not a secret, which the proof-bound design allows. Self-churn decoys cost real money (Railgun's 0.25% in and out plus gas), their funding wallets are identifiable unless funded from inside the pool, and a stream of agent-generated round-trips could be characterized as structuring or wash activity by the same vendors the project is trying to satisfy; it should run only from a shielded budget, only where stewardship measures a thin band, and only after legal review.

**(f) Threshold selective disclosure: keep, opt-in, default-off.** It is genuine whitespace (no shipped per-transaction, revocable disclosure exists on Ethereum) and the only concrete answer to the EU CASP interface in 2027. The rejection risk is reputational: a project whose headline is "privacy with a disclosure panel" will be read as a backdoor. Position it as a user tool, never a network property.

**(g) Private read layer: keep; merge with (a).** Broadcasters and read endpoints share transport, identity and probation; one worker binary with two roles halves the operator burden.

**(h) Legal-risk minimization as a design requirement: keep, and make it a gate on every other item.** The record is unambiguous. Van Loon protected only immutable contracts; the Dutch court rejected "it is only a tool"; Storm was convicted on the relayer, frontend and fee facts; Samourai's founders ran a coordinator and collected fees; Galeotti's safe harbour is "merely a policy of prosecutorial discretion" and names "front ends, upgrades, oracles, or other gateways" as risk; the statutory safe harbour died 49–50 ([Van Loon](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf); [DL News on Galeotti](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/regulation/devs-of-decentralised-protocols-wont-be-prosecuted-doj-says/); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/10/The-CLARITY-Act-Stalls-in-the-Senate-Whats-Next-for-Digital-Asset-Regulation)). The requirements are therefore: immutable core contracts with no admin, pause or fee switch; no developer-operated relayer, frontend, ASP panel, read endpoint or disclosure panel (the project team may write the software and run nothing); no developer fee of any kind; the frontend built reproducibly, pinned to IPFS and named under ENS, which IMD's `build-website` skill already does, with the ENS name held by a time-locked multi-party process so the January 2024 frontend substitution cannot recur ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)); screening that is real, published and executed by independent panels rather than a cosmetic UI check; and, learning from "guys we are fucked," no support channel through which the team acquires knowledge of specific flows.

### Fee economics: pay agents inside the pool, never through a token

The contrast between Railgun and Tornado's relayer economics is the clearest lesson in the notes. Railgun charges 0.25% on shield and unshield in the asset itself, internal transfers are free, broadcasters are reimbursed in any accepted token and quote fees off-chain, and the result is ~$4.1M of trailing-year revenue, the only meaningful privacy-protocol revenue on Ethereum ([DefiLlama Railgun](https://defillama.com/protocol/railgun); [Railgun docs](https://docs.railgun.org/wiki/learn/privacy-system/community-relayers)). Tornado's registry required relayers to stake TORN, charged a 0.3% registry fee in TORN, routed it to TORN stakers, and made every parameter governance-configurable; the result was a $2.17M governance takeover, relayer liquidity tied to a token now worth ~$32M, and a court describing "mutable contracts that collect a fee from the relayer and pay it to the TORN token holders" as part of an operated service ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-tornado-cash-hack-may-2023); [Van Loon](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-50669-CV0.pdf)). The 2026 proposals to add a governance-adjustable 0.3–5% withdrawal fee to a new Tornado pool repeat the mistake ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/tornado-cash)).

Murmuration therefore pays every agent in-pool and in-asset. A broadcaster's fee is a note created for its shielded address inside the same transaction it relays, so the broadcaster's income is private, needs no public payment and never links it to a client. The panel ASP is paid from a per-withdrawal fee note split pro rata among the panelists whose signatures are on the root the withdrawal used, which also aligns incentives toward being the panel users actually choose. Co-provers and read endpoints are paid per request with micro-notes. Nothing flows to the project's developers and nothing flows through IMD's token. IMD itself is paid as it is today: a project that uses the swarm's `oracle` or `research-report` skills for stewardship pays 0.5 IMD per request through Intake, which is a customer relationship, not a revenue share ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). The IMD token's only structural role should be the one it already has, gating seat ownership, and even that is indirect since the seat is an NFT. ETH is the natural unit for bonds and slashing because the seat floor (~0.8 ETH) already denominates sybil cost in ETH. Gas is not the binding constraint: on 3 November 2026 mainnet gas was roughly 0.1–0.3 gwei, and daily averages ran 0.46–1.9 gwei through August and September ([Chaingate](https://chaingate.dev/gas-tracker/ethereum); [Coinlaw](https://coinlaw.io/ethereum-gas-fees-statistics/)). A Groth16 verification costs about 207,700 gas plus 7,160 per public input ([Nebra HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@nebra-one/ByoMB8Zf6)), so a withdrawal near 250k gas costs 25,000–75,000 gwei, i.e. 0.000025–0.000075 ETH; an UltraHonk verification at 0.72–2.4M gas is still under 0.001 ETH ([abutlabs](https://github.com/abutlabs/jam-privacy-service/issues/40); [ERC-8262](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8262)). Broadcaster economics are dominated by operator labour, opsec and the opportunity cost of an idle seat, which is exactly why a swarm that is ~99% idle at any snapshot is the right operator pool.

### Bootstrapping the anonymity set and blunting withdrawal MEV

Three lessons govern bootstrapping. First, share the set: one multi-asset tree (Railgun's 147 tokens in one tree) beats Privacy Pools' per-asset, per-chain pools that leave 470 deposits on mainnet ([ethprivacy.tools](https://ethprivacy.tools/railgun/); [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools-v2)); in phases 0 and 1 Murmuration adds no pool at all and makes Railgun's and Privacy Pools' existing sets larger by removing the UX and relayer friction that keeps users out. Second, batch exits: Penumbra's per-block batch execution and Vitalik's keyed nullifiers for simultaneous withdrawals both convert the FIFO heuristic's ordered stream into an unordered epoch ([Penumbra DEX spec](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/dex.html); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/vitalik-buterin-outlines-ethereums-near-term-privacy-roadmap-aa-focil-keyed-nonces-and-kohaku/)), and the stewardship scheduler can enforce epoch exits even on pools that do not natively batch. Third, defaults drive adoption: Zcash went from ~6% to 52.7% shielded transaction share when wallets defaulted to shielded ([arXiv 2105.02793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02793); [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/34628)), while Namada's paid shielded rewards produced a pool now worth $474 ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/namada-shielded-pools)) and TORN's anonymity mining attracted address-reusing users and leaked deposit age ([WWW '23](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~livshits/papers/pdf/www23.pdf)). Murmuration must not run token incentives for deposits; its adoption lever is being the default relayer and read layer inside Kohaku-integrated wallets such as Ambire, with the IMD "private first" work providing ~1,000 agent operators and their customers as the first cohort of stealth-address and shielded-payout users.

MEV on withdrawals is narrower than it looks. Because recipient, fee and relayer are bound in the proof, a front-runner who copies a withdrawal transaction can neither redirect funds nor steal the fee; the historical problem was an early Tornado implementation where "the relayer is frontrunnable" before that binding ([kendricktan/tornado-mixer](https://github.com/kendricktan/tornado-mixer)). The residual risks are censorship delay and timing leakage: OFAC-filtering relays delivered ~15.5% of MEV-Boost payloads on 2 November 2026, so inclusion is delayed by a slot or two rather than blocked ([mevwatch](https://www.mevwatch.info/)); FOCIL is "considered" for Hegotá and not shipped ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/news/ethereums-hegota-hard-fork-scheduled-for-late-2026/)); Shutter's threshold-encrypted mempool is live only on Gnosis with ~3-minute inclusion ([Cointelegraph Magazine](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/applied-mev-protection-via-shutter-s-threshold-encryption)). The mesh's answer is epoch batching plus broadcaster diversity: a withdrawal handed to three independent broadcasters across mixnet exits is harder to time than one handed to 0xbow's single relayer. For private swaps, the phase-2 pool should adopt Penumbra's batch-at-clearing-price model rather than an encrypted mempool, because it needs no new L1 feature.

### Roadmap: phase 0 ships with today's IMD skills

**Phase 0 (now to three months): services on existing pools, no new contracts.** Four deliverables are buildable with IMD's current skills and contracts. A stewardship job set uses `research-report` and `oracle` with `log-sum`, `log-count` and `log-rank` recipes to publish daily pool-health roots for Railgun, Privacy Pools and Tornado, signed by an oracle panel and recorded through the existing daily Merkle-root path ([IMD Docs](https://imd.fun/docs/)). A broadcaster package wraps Railgun's open broadcaster client for the IMD worker runtime so any seat can relay Railgun transactions today (Railgun's broadcaster set is permissionless and already enforces PPOI), with Tor or Nym transport; this needs no IMD backend change but does need a worker tool declaration, as media tools do ([worker README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Identity-md/worker/main/README.md)). A proof-of-association overlay uses an oracle panel to sign multi-provider inclusion roots for legacy Tornado notes and Privacy Pools labels, the pattern 0xbow's own "Proof of Association" tool established ([Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/news/cryptocurrency-news/show/658687-0xbow-launches-tool/)). And the reproducible frontend deploys to IPFS under ENS through `build-website`. Dependencies: IMD operator cooperation for the tool declaration; cost is 0.5 IMD per request plus Railgun's 0.25% for any decoy flows. Gas: zero new on-chain components beyond relayed withdrawals at ~250k gas each. Phase 0 also produces the "private IMD" specification: stealth payouts from the swarm-share Merkle distributor via ERC-5564, encrypted briefs to device keys, encrypted oracle answers.

**Phase 1 (three to nine months): on-chain registries and the panel ASP.** Deploy the immutable mesh registry (seat and ERC-8004 agentId, epoch availability, aggregate reputation emitted per epoch, probation), the panel ASP contract (t-of-n EIP-712 roots, criteria hash, historical buffer, seat-NFT bond, dissent log) and the co-proving coordinator; integrate as a Kohaku module alongside Railgun and Privacy Pools so wallets select Murmuration broadcasters and ASPs; stand up the Helios plus TEE-ORAM read role with Automata DCAP attestation posted once per epoch per agent (~0.5M gas SNARK-compressed, i.e. a few hundred thousand gwei at current prices) ([automata-dcap-attestation](https://github.com/automata-network/automata-dcap-attestation)). Dependencies: Kohaku's release cadence, which has no public date; TACEO's coNoir maturity for UltraHonk circuits; a premium hardware tier (8 GB+) among seat operators. Deliverable gate: at least 100 distinct-wallet broadcasters and three panels with published criteria.

**Phase 2 (nine to twenty-four months): the immutable pool, slashing and disclosure.** Deploy the Layer 0 pool (multi-asset tree, epoch exits with keyed nullifiers, turnstiles, stablecoin isolation, no admin), register panelists and broadcasters into an EigenLayer or Symbiotic operator set with the two objective ASP slashing conditions, ship threshold selective disclosure as an opt-in wallet feature, and begin the post-quantum path (hash-based commitments; STARK verifier once gas allows). Dependencies: a universal-setup verifier at acceptable gas after Glamsterdam's repricing (expected Q4 2026, date unconfirmed) ([ethereum.org Glamsterdam](https://ethereum.org/roadmap/glamsterdam/)); EigenLayer or Symbiotic slashing tooling, which has had few or no recorded slashing events through mid-2026; and, above all, evidence from phase 1 that the services have thickened existing sets rather than needing a new one.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing in this research is that Ethereum's privacy stack has already found its cryptography (full-set membership proofs over note commitments, which even Monero is migrating toward) and is now failing on operations: who relays, who screens, who serves reads, who proves, and who can be prosecuted for doing so. Every answer shipped so far has been either a single operator (0xbow's ASP, relayer and RPC key; Fluidkey's viewing node; zk.money's node and screening service) or a token-staked registry that governance can capture. IMD's accidental contribution is a thousand independently owned, on-chain-identified machines whose operators are structurally not the developers of anything they run. That is the organisational shape the Fifth Circuit, the DOJ's own policy statements and the dead BRCA text all point to, and it has never been available to a privacy project before.

The implication for sequencing is counterintuitive but firm: the project should not build a pool first. It should make Railgun's and Privacy Pools' existing anonymity sets bigger by becoming their relayer mesh, their second and third ASP, their phone prover and their private read layer, and it should shield IMD's own payouts and briefs to seed a thousand-user stealth-address cohort. If those services demonstrably thicken sets and survive a year of operation without a developer touching a frontend, a relayer or a list, then an immutable pool with those services as its native periphery is the natural phase-2 product. The remaining uncertainties are not technical: whether Judge Failla's Rule 29 ruling and the April 2027 retrial narrow or widen the "operated service" theory, how AMLA and national supervisors apply the Regulation's existing definition of "anonymity-enhancing" coins, which already covers optional privacy, and whether IMD's pseudonymous operator will cede enough of the control plane for the swarm to be genuinely permissionless. The design above is built so that only the last of these is fatal, and the register says so plainly.

## Addendum, 10 November 2026: corrections from the adversarial re-review

A re-review on 9 and 10 November 2026 re-verified the time-sensitive facts and attacked the design in Part two. The revised design is in `reports/Murmuration privacy stack.html` (revision 2), which carries a full changelog; sources are in the research notes ending `_v2.md`. This addendum lists only the points where a finding contradicts the text above.

**Facts**

- **Railgun's 0.25% fee does not pay broadcasters.** It is transferred to Railgun's treasury contract on shield and unshield. A broadcaster's quote is about 1.10 times its estimated gas cost. A three-day on-chain sample to 10 November found about 286 spends a day on mainnet, seven sending addresses behind 80% of them, and a margin of roughly $480 a month for all broadcasters together at current gas prices (`railgun_onchain_sample_v2.md`, `railgun_broadcaster_kohaku_v2.md`). The statement that a swarm "that is ~99% idle" is the right operator pool because relaying pays is not supported; relaying does not pay at these gas prices.
- **Railgun's broadcaster traffic is no longer open in practice.** Since Railway Wallet 5.26.1 (5 October 2026) the wallet selects only from four allowlisted fee signers in its remote configuration, and Railgun's client library drops quotes outside a band around the trusted signers' average. Phase 0's "any seat can relay Railgun transactions today" is true of the software and false of the traffic. Railgun's code moved to GitLab, its Waku network is a private cluster without RLN, and its contract source is marked UNLICENSED.
- **Privacy Pools accepts only the latest root from 0xbow's postman**, in v1 and v2, with no ASP identifier in contracts or proofs and no ring buffer of ASP roots. `relay()` is permissionless in both versions; L2BEAT counted six active relayers on v1 and three on v2 over 30 days on 10 November, so "one operator relayer" understates it. v2 held 583 deposits on mainnet, not 470. v1 is Apache-2.0; v2's contracts are BUSL-1.1 (`privacy_pools_asp_interface_v2.md`).
- **Kohaku's Railgun package is a placeholder.** Its working plugins are Privacy Pools and Tornado Cash, in alpha, with no stable release.
- **IMD's oracle panels are not independently multi-party.** The control plane selects the panel and IMD's own deployer service re-runs the recipe and signs. Using them for screening, or recording through IMD's daily Merkle-root path, would break this report's own rule against brokering through the control plane.
- **IMD state on 10 November:** 1,149 agents, 871 online, 118 working; seat floor 0.70 ETH; 795 owners of 1,999 seats; IMD at $2.64. The seat NFT is source-verified, is not a proxy, and its owner functions have no key behind them. The ERC-8004 adapter is a proxy upgradeable by a three-of-four Safe. Goals are half shipped: research goals run, the jury and escrow half is in the worker and switched off (`imd_goals_and_state_v2.md`).
- **Phones already prove every Railgun and Privacy Pools v1 circuit.** Railgun's join-split circuits are 9,759 to 65,427 constraints and Railway's mobile wallet proves them natively. The out-of-memory benchmark cited for delegated proving concerns a P-256 ECDSA circuit in UltraHonk (`reuse_components_and_proving_v2.md`).
- **EU:** "anonymity-enhancing coins" is defined in AMLR Article 2(1)(25) and includes optional privacy. AMLA, not the EBA, holds the related mandates. Article 79 and the 10 July 2027 date are confirmed against EUR-Lex (`legal_developments_v2.md`).
- **US v. Storm:** unchanged. Rule 29 is still pending and the retrial is still set for 26 April 2027. The description of the conviction as resting on "relayer, frontend and fee facts" overstates a general verdict on a knowledge-based count.
- **Ethereum roadmap:** Glamsterdam is live on Sepolia, its Hoodi fork was cancelled on 22 October and mainnet is expected in 2027. A fresh storage slot becomes about five times dearer, not four; the clearing refund rises; a 64 KiB contract cannot be deployed under the current 60M block gas limit (`ethereum_roadmap_v2.md`).

**Design conclusions that no longer hold**

- Service 3 (collaborative proving) and service 6 (threshold disclosure) are cut. Service 4 (private read layer) is replaced by deterministic public snapshots. Service 5 keeps only a published dataset with advice computed on the device.
- The mesh registry, bonds, slashing, restaking and the panel ASP contract are removed. Seat-backed admission is checked by the wallet from `ownerOf` and a holder-signed delegation.
- The Layer 0 pool is no longer a phase 2 deliverable. It is a gated option limited to a fork and freeze of Privacy Pools v1 with an ownerless entrypoint.
- The budget falls from $0.75M to $1.5M to between $43,000 and $121,000 for the plan of record, or $0.29M to $0.74M if the pool option is exercised.
