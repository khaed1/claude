A deposit waits between one and two days: the batch that covers it is computed at the next epoch boundary and accepted during that epoch. No state exists in which a deposit can be neither activated nor refunded by its depositor, except a depositor that cannot receive its own asset back, such as an address a stablecoin issuer has frozen.

A note

StateOn-chain meaningNotes
CreatedIts commitment is one of four in a leaf of the treeA note made by a spend carries that spend's checkpoint and is spendable in the next transaction. A note made by activation carries the exact index of its block and becomes spendable at the next 300-block boundary, at most an hour later
SpendableA root that contains the leaf is among the last 64, and gCur is at least the note's indexA proof stays valid until 64 more leaves have been added or two checkpoints have passed, whichever comes first. After that the wallet proves again
SpentIts nullifier is in the spent setFinal. Nothing links the nullifier to the leaf

Padding inputs are never in the tree and go straight to Spent. A note whose ciphertext was posted wrongly is Created for ever and nobody can spend it. Reward stops growing for everyone when emissions end, and a note keeps what it has accrued.

  • Version 4 overstated the cost of post-quantum encryption. It priced each note at about 70,000 gas "at the calldata floor". A spend pays the ordinary rate, and the design now publishes one recipient ciphertext per spend, so the whole encryption overhead is about 31,500 gas, under 3% of a spend, today and under Glamsterdam.
  • The hash contracts. The pool calls the three poseidon-solidity contracts that already exist on mainnet, pinned by code hash, so it deploys none. They are 23,478, 14,189 and 19,260 bytes. Their README says they are unaudited, so the build checks them against the circuit with shared test vectors and the audit reads the generator.
  • Size limit. The build fails if any contract's deployed code exceeds 24,576 bytes. Estimates: Pool 16 to 20 KiB, ScreenerRegistry 12 to 16, FeeDistributor 10 to 13, Distribution 7 to 9, Verifier about 4, Token under 3.
  • Transaction limit. The build fails if any call can exceed 16,777,216 execution gas. The heaviest calls are a spend at about 1.3M, and apply with a full 96-word batch, which writes 96 new slots: about 2.1M today and 10.6M under Glamsterdam, of which 1.2M is execution gas.
  • No refunds are relied on. Clearing a deposit record earns a refund under both rule sets. No limit or estimate in the code depends on it.
  • Deployment. About 60 KiB of new code in six transactions: roughly 13M gas today and 95M under Glamsterdam, under $50 either way at 0.2 gwei.

Deployment and the canary

  1. Build. One reproducible build produces the creation code of the six contracts, the circuit, and a parameter file. The ceremony runs on that circuit, and the verifier's code is generated from its output.
  2. Deployer. A small contract is created through the deterministic deployment proxy at 0x4e59…956C. Its constructor fixes the six creation-code hashes in order. Its only function, deploy(i, code), creates contract i if code matches hash i and every earlier one exists. Addresses follow from the deployer's address and a counter, so each constructor receives the others' addresses before they exist. Anyone can send these transactions, and nobody can deploy anything else through it.
  3. One contract per transaction. Under Glamsterdam pricing the six do not fit in one transaction or one block.
  4. Launch. Anyone calls Distribution.launch(). Screeners who register in the first week count from epoch 7, so the first deposits can be activated about eight days after deployment.

The canary

The canary is the whole system from the same build, deployed with a different salt and three different time constants: a cap step of 50,400 blocks instead of 648,000, and an emission period and deposit window shortened to match. In 90 days it passes all eight emission steps, the last cap step, the end of emissions and the close of deposits. Screening runs at its real pace with real seats. It has its own token and its own pool against IMD, which anyone could buy from, so the lawyer's review comes before the canary.

The project funds it with at least 10 ETH and the equivalent in each other asset, and runs a published script. The canary passes only when every line of the script has a transaction hash beside it:

  • Every external function of every contract, called at least once.
  • A spend with two real inputs, one with a padding input, one paying a relay fee note, one that turns reward into a token note, and a withdrawal of every asset at its smallest and at its largest reachable size.
  • A refused deposit, its refund, and a refusal that a later batch clears.
  • An activation that fails at a full cap and succeeds after a withdrawal.
  • An epoch with no accepted batch, a screener evicted for silence, and one whose seat was transferred.
  • A basket sale, a sale that skips one asset, and buyback steps down to an empty balance.
  • A token pool that a third party created and priced wrongly before launch.
  • Spends and refunds after deposits have closed and emissions have ended.

What it shows: the deployment procedure works, the contracts and the circuit agree on mainnet, and nobody who looked took the money. What it does not show is that the circuit is sound. The proofs are for that, and the caps limit the cost if the proofs are wrong.
