Assurance without an external audit

The owner's decision is to rely on IMD and see whether the swarm can secure what it builds. Wallets, L2BEAT and exchanges look for a named firm's report, so adoption will be slower without one. Four measures make the test fair and keep a failure small.

  • IMD audit panels on everything. Every contract and the circuit go through IMD's audit jobs, with each finding and its fix public. Reviewers built on the same models as the builders tend to share blind spots, which is why the next three measures do not depend on reviewers.
  • Machine-checked proofs. The swarm writes formal proofs of the five properties listed under the circuit: no value created, no note spent twice, no reward created, nothing left unconstrained, no wrap-around. Anyone can re-run the checker. A checked proof holds whoever wrote it, as long as the statement it proves is the right one, so each statement is also published in plain words for review. Open-source analysers for under-constrained circuits are run in public as well.
  • A canary. The whole system is deployed on mainnet 90 days before launch with a compressed clock, funded with at least 10 ETH, and exercised by a published script. It is a rehearsal and a standing reward for anyone who can break it. It does not prove the circuit sound: someone who finds a flaw can wait for the larger pool.
  • Low caps. The pool opens at about 25 ETH per asset and doubles every 90 days. With three capped assets the most that can be lost is about $160,000 at launch and about $5.1M at the recommended stop of 800 ETH. Without a stop the same schedule reaches about $41M after two years.

Nothing in the research shows that IMD has audited a zero-knowledge circuit before. This launch is that test.
